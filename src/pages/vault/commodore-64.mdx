## Overview
The Commodore 64 (C64) arrived in 1982 with a 64 KB price tag and a multimedia feature set rivaling arcade cabinets. It spread through bedrooms, schools, and living rooms worldwide, anchoring the scenes this curriculum celebrates.

## Fast facts
- **CPU:** MOS Technology 6510 running at ~1 MHz.  
- **Graphics:** VIC-II chip delivering sprites, smooth scrolling, and 16 colours.  
- **Sound:** SID 6581/8580 tri-voice synthesizer with filters and modulation.  
- **Storage:** Datasette cassettes, 1541 floppy disk, cartridges.

## Why it mattered
- **Affordability:** Commodore controlled chip production, letting them undercut rivals and dominate sales (12–17 million units).  
- **Creativity platform:** bundled BASIC invited experimentation; POKE lists and magazines nurtured a generation of coders.  
- **Ecosystem:** thriving third-party hardware, music, and [demo scenes](/vault/demo-scene-101) kept the machine relevant well into the 1990s.  

## Lesson connections
- BASIC Blocks 1–4 happen entirely on the C64, mirroring the experience of 1980s learners.  
- Transition course peeks under BASIC’s hood to expose how the 6510, KERNAL, and ROMs cooperate.  
- Assembly course dives into VIC-II timing, SID sound design, and 1541 fastloaders built for C64 hardware.

## Modern legacy
- **Emulation:** VICE, Frodo, and MiSTer recreate the machine with high accuracy.  
- **Hardware clones:** THE C64 Maxi/Mini, Ultimate 64, and FPGA boards keep the platform alive.  
- **Community:** new games, demo competitions, and music releases appear annually, proof that the “64” still inspires.
