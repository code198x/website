A concrete example helps us improve the material. You can answer any of these
questions, send a short note or send nothing. There is no requirement to finish
an activity before telling us about it.

## Your experience

- Which activity and lesson did you try?
- Were you exploring alone, with someone at home or in a group? What programming
  experience was relevant to the activity?
- What did you expect, and what happened?
- Where did you need help? What explanation, hint or action helped?
- Were you able to keep your work and check the saved copy?
- What did you want to try or understand next?

If a tool failed, include the page, the action you tried, the message you saw
and the device/browser you used. Describe the symptom even if you do not know
its cause. A paper-only session is useful feedback too; say which parts you
could investigate and which remained predictions.

## Share what you choose

You can give your notes to the person who shared this pack. If you want to
report directly, the site's [correction routes](/standards#corrections)
include a GitHub issue route. A parent or teacher can report on a shared session
without requiring each learner to create an account.

Leave out learner names, school details and other identifying information. If
including a screenshot or program, check it for personal information first.
A description of the relevant steps is enough; recording the session is not
required.
