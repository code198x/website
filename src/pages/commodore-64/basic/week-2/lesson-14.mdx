## Why Nested Loops Matter

A single loop goes through one list. But what about a grid? Or a checkerboard? You need a loop for rows AND a loop for columns - one inside the other. This is called **nesting**.

## Simple Nested Loop

Print a 3×3 grid of numbers:

```basic
NEW
10 FOR ROW=1 TO 3
20 FOR COL=1 TO 3
30 PRINT ROW;COL;
40 NEXT COL
50 PRINT
60 NEXT ROW
```

Output:
```
11 12 13
21 22 23
31 32 33
```

The outer loop (ROW) runs 3 times. For each ROW, the inner loop (COL) runs 3 times.

## Drawing a Grid on Screen

Fill a 10×10 area with dots:

```basic
NEW
10 PRINT CHR$(147)
20 FOR Y=0 TO 9
30 FOR X=0 TO 9
40 POKE 1024+Y*40+X,46
50 NEXT X
60 NEXT Y
```

**Lines explained:**
- Line 20: Outer loop for rows (Y from 0 to 9)
- Line 30: Inner loop for columns (X from 0 to 9)
- Line 40: Draw a dot (character 46) at position Y,X

## WOW Moment: Checkerboard Pattern

Create an 8×8 checkerboard:

```basic
NEW
10 PRINT CHR$(147)
20 FOR Y=0 TO 7
30 FOR X=0 TO 7
40 C=(X+Y) AND 1
50 CH=32:IF C=0 THEN CH=160
60 POKE 1024+(Y+8)*40+(X+16),CH
70 CL=1:IF C=0 THEN CL=0
80 POKE 55296+(Y+8)*40+(X+16),CL
90 NEXT X
100 NEXT Y
```

![Checkerboard pattern](/images/lessons/commodore-64/basic/week-2/lesson-14/lesson14-checkerboard.png)

A perfect checkerboard! The pattern alternates black and white squares.

## How the Checkerboard Works

**Line 40**: `(X+Y) AND 1` - This clever trick alternates values:
- When X+Y is even, result is 0 (black square)
- When X+Y is odd, result is 1 (white square)

**Lines 50-70**: Set character and colour based on the alternation
- Line 50: Default to space (32), change to solid block (160) if black
- Line 70: Default to white (1), change to black (0) if black square

**Line 60/80**: Offset by 8,16 to centre it on screen

## Loop Order Matters

Try reversing the loops:

```basic
NEW
10 FOR X=0 TO 3
20 FOR Y=0 TO 3
30 PRINT X;Y;" ";
40 NEXT Y
50 NEXT X
```

Output: `00 01 02 03 10 11 12 13 20 21 22 23 30 31 32 33`

Same coordinates, different order! Usually you want Y (rows) on the outside, X (columns) on the inside.

## Multiplication Table

```basic
NEW
10 PRINT CHR$(147)
20 FOR Y=1 TO 5
30 FOR X=1 TO 5
40 PRINT X*Y;
50 IF X*Y<10 THEN PRINT " ";
60 NEXT X
70 PRINT
80 NEXT Y
```

Output:
```
1 2 3 4 5
2 4 6 8 10
3 6 9 12 15
4 8 12 16 20
5 10 15 20 25
```

## Common Patterns

**Fill a rectangle:**
```basic
FOR Y=0 TO 9
FOR X=0 TO 19
POKE 1024+Y*40+X,160
NEXT X
NEXT Y
```

**Draw a border:**
```basic
FOR I=0 TO 39
POKE 1024+I,160
POKE 1024+24*40+I,160
NEXT I
FOR I=0 TO 24
POKE 1024+I*40,160
POKE 1024+I*40+39,160
NEXT I
```

## Quick Reference

```basic
FOR ROW=0 TO 9          REM Outer loop (rows)
FOR COL=0 TO 9          REM Inner loop (columns)
REM ... do something with ROW and COL
NEXT COL                REM Close inner loop first
NEXT ROW                REM Then close outer loop
```

**Remember**: Close loops in reverse order - inner first, then outer.

## What You've Learnt

- Nested loops run one loop inside another
- Outer loop = rows, inner loop = columns (usually)
- Perfect for grids, boards, and patterns
- Loop order affects the sequence
- AND operator works for alternating patterns
- Always close inner loop before outer loop
- Every game board uses nested loops!
