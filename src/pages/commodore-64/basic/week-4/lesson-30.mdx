## Lesson 30 – Random Events

Arcade games thrive on unpredictability. This lesson shows how to use `RND` to scatter pickups, spawn hazards, and keep rooms fresh each run.

> [📷 suggested: screenshot with randomly placed collectibles and hazards]

---

### The One-Minute Tour

- `RND(1)` returns a decimal between 0 and &lt;1.  
- Multiply, `INT`, and offset to fit screen coordinates.  
- Keep a safe margin so items don’t spawn inside walls.  
- Use random colours or point values for variety.

---

### Random Pickups

```basic
NEW
10 PRINT CHR$(147)
20 FOR I=0 TO 9
30 X=INT(RND(1)*30)+5
40 Y=INT(RND(1)*18)+4
50 LOC=1024+Y*40+X
60 POKE LOC,81
70 POKE 55296+Y*40+X,INT(RND(1)*16)
80 NEXT I
```

Spawns ten dots in a safe window away from the edges.

---

### Random Hazard or Reward

```basic
90 FOR TURN=1 TO 5
100 X=INT(RND(1)*30)+5
110 Y=INT(RND(1)*18)+4
120 LOC=1024+Y*40+X
130 IF RND(1)<0.7 THEN POKE LOC,120 ELSE POKE LOC,42
140 NEXT TURN
```

70% chance of a hazard (`120`), 30% chance of a bonus (`42`). Adjust the probability as needed.

### Experiment Section

- Give each pickup a random score value (`SCORE=SCORE+INT(RND(1)*15)+5`).

- Combine with the HUD: show remaining random pickups by scanning the map for character `81`.  
- Seed the timer bonus: add `RULEBONUS(ROOM)=RULEBONUS(ROOM)+(INT(RND(1)*40))` for extra variety.

### Circular Motion with SIN and COS

Random placement is one thing; smooth, predictable paths are another. Use `SIN` and `COS` to move sprites in circles, spirals, or angled trajectories. These trigonometric functions take angles in **radians** (not degrees) and return values between -1 and 1.

```basic
NEW
10 PRINT CHR$(147)
20 CX=20 : CY=12 : REM centre
30 RADIUS=10
40 ANGLE=0
50 POKE 53280,0 : POKE 53281,0
60 FOR I=0 TO 360 STEP 10
70 A=I*3.14159/180 : REM convert degrees to radians
80 X=CX+RADIUS*COS(A)
90 Y=CY+RADIUS*SIN(A)
100 LOC=1024+INT(Y)*40+INT(X)
110 POKE LOC,81
120 NEXT I
```

**Lines 70–90** convert a degree angle to radians, then calculate X and Y offsets from the centre using `COS` and `SIN`.
**Line 100** plots the result on screen.

> **Tip:** For projectiles, use a fixed angle: `X=X+SPEED*COS(A)`, `Y=Y+SPEED*SIN(A)` each frame for straight-line motion at any angle.

**Common uses:**
- **Orbital enemies:** Move around a fixed point with `ANGLE=ANGLE+0.1` each frame.
- **Angled shots:** Fire projectiles in 8 directions using pre-calculated angles (`0`, `45`, `90`, etc.).
- **Wave patterns:** Combine `SIN` with time for smooth side-to-side motion: `X=BASE+AMPLITUDE*SIN(TI/30)`.

---

### Concept Expansion

Randomness fuels replayability. Applied carefully, it keeps rooms lively without breaking the layout. Later, you'll use the same techniques for procedural map generation. Combine randomness with physics math (SIN/COS) for enemies that orbit unpredictably or fire at calculated angles.

---

### Game Integration

- Scatter power-ups and traps each time a room loads.  
- Randomise patrol routes by picking direction deltas at spawn.  
- Adjust difficulty on the fly: fewer safe zones as the score climbs.  
- Log random seeds (store the result of `RND(-TI)`) to reproduce interesting runs during testing.

### Quick Reference

```basic
INT(RND(1)*W)       : REM 0..W-1
INT(RND(1)*W)+O     : REM O..O+W-1
IF RND(1)<P THEN    : REM probability test
A=DEGREES*3.14159/180 : REM convert degrees to radians
X=CX+R*COS(A)       : REM circular X position
Y=CY+R*SIN(A)       : REM circular Y position
```

- Use SIN/COS for smooth circles, spirals, and angled movement.
- Angles must be in radians; multiply degrees by π/180 to convert.
- Pre-calculate common angles (0°, 45°, 90°) for projectiles.
