You've learnt to play single notes on voice 1. Now discover what made the SID legendary—three independent voices working together. Play rich chords, create harmonies, and build the foundation for complete musical compositions.

**Three voices, three frequencies, one POKE to volume.** When all three play together, the C64 transforms into a synthesizer.

## The One-Minute Tour

- **SID has three independent voices** at addresses 54272 (voice 1), 54279 (voice 2), and 54286 (voice 3)
- **Each voice has identical capabilities** including frequency, waveform, and ADSR envelope
- **Voices share master volume** at address 54296 but can have different frequencies and waveforms
- **Musical chords combine three notes** like C major (C-E-G) or A minor (A-C-E)
- **All three voices can play simultaneously** creating harmony, counterpoint, or sound effects
- **Register layout repeats every 7 addresses** making multi-voice programming systematic

## First Example: C Major Chord

Type this and hear three voices in harmony:

```basic
10 REM THREE VOICE HARMONY - SIMPLE C MAJOR CHORD
20 PRINT CHR$(147);CHR$(5)
30 PRINT "THREE VOICE HARMONY"
40 PRINT
50 PRINT "PLAYING C MAJOR CHORD..."
60 PRINT "(C-E-G)"
70 PRINT
80 REM SETUP SID VOLUME
90 POKE 54296,15
100 REM SETUP VOICE 1 ADSR (FAST ATTACK)
110 POKE 54277,8:POKE 54278,0
120 REM SETUP VOICE 2 ADSR
130 POKE 54284,8:POKE 54285,0
140 REM SETUP VOICE 3 ADSR
150 POKE 54291,8:POKE 54292,0
160 REM PLAY C MAJOR CHORD (C-E-G)
170 REM VOICE 1: C (262HZ = 4286 SID)
180 POKE 54272,190:POKE 54273,16
190 POKE 54276,17
200 REM VOICE 2: E (330HZ = 5401 SID)
210 POKE 54279,25:POKE 54280,21
220 POKE 54283,17
230 REM VOICE 3: G (392HZ = 6412 SID)
240 POKE 54286,12:POKE 54287,25
250 POKE 54290,17
260 PRINT "PRESS ANY KEY TO STOP..."
270 GET K$:IF K$="" THEN 270
280 REM STOP ALL VOICES
290 POKE 54276,0:POKE 54283,0:POKE 54290,0
300 PRINT
310 PRINT "DONE!"
```

**Run this** and hear a rich, full C major chord.

## How Multi-Voice Programming Works

**The SID chip has three identical voice channels, each with 7 control registers.**

**Voice Register Layout:**

| Voice | Base Address | Frequency Low | Frequency High | Control | ADSR Registers |
|-------|-------------|---------------|----------------|---------|----------------|
| Voice 1 | 54272 ($D400) | +0 | +1 | +4 | +5, +6 |
| Voice 2 | 54279 ($D407) | +0 | +1 | +4 | +5, +6 |
| Voice 3 | 54286 ($D40E) | +0 | +1 | +4 | +5, +6 |

Each voice's base address is 7 apart (54272, 54279, 54286). Within each voice, registers follow the same pattern: frequency low/high at +0/+1, control at +4, ADSR at +5/+6.

**Lines 90: Master Volume**

```basic
90 POKE 54296,15
```

All three voices share the master volume register at address 54296 ($D418). Set this once to maximum (15) and all voices will be audible. You can't control individual voice volumes directly—that requires using the filter or ADSR envelope.

**Lines 110-150: ADSR Setup for All Voices**

```basic
110 POKE 54277,8:POKE 54278,0    REM Voice 1
130 POKE 54284,8:POKE 54285,0    REM Voice 2
150 POKE 54291,8:POKE 54292,0    REM Voice 3
```

Each voice needs its ADSR envelope configured. Here we use identical settings for all three (Attack=0, Decay=8, Sustain=0, Release=0) which creates a quick, percussive envelope. The envelopes could be different—voice 1 might sustain longer while voice 3 fades quickly.

**Lines 180-250: Playing the C Major Chord**

A C major chord consists of three notes: C (the root), E (the major third), and G (the fifth). We assign one note to each voice:

```basic
180 POKE 54272,190:POKE 54273,16    REM Voice 1 = C (4286)
190 POKE 54276,17                    REM Gate on, triangle wave

210 POKE 54279,25:POKE 54280,21     REM Voice 2 = E (5401)
220 POKE 54283,17                    REM Gate on, triangle wave

240 POKE 54286,12:POKE 54287,25     REM Voice 3 = G (6412)
250 POKE 54290,17                    REM Gate on, triangle wave
```

The frequency values are 16-bit, split into low and high bytes. For C (4286): LOW=190, HIGH=16 because 4286 = 16×256 + 190. The control register value 17 means triangle wave (16) + gate on (1).

**How Frequency Values Work:**

The SID uses a formula to convert 16-bit values to audio frequencies:

**Frequency (Hz) = (SID_VALUE × 985248) / 16777216**

You don't need to calculate these—use a frequency table. Common notes:

| Note | Frequency | SID Value | Low Byte | High Byte |
|------|-----------|-----------|----------|-----------|
| C4   | 262 Hz    | 4286      | 190      | 16        |
| D4   | 294 Hz    | 4811      | 203      | 18        |
| E4   | 330 Hz    | 5401      | 25       | 21        |
| F4   | 349 Hz    | 5713      | 81       | 22        |
| G4   | 392 Hz    | 6412      | 12       | 25        |
| A4   | 440 Hz    | 7200      | 32       | 28        |
| B4   | 494 Hz    | 8085      | 141      | 31        |
| C5   | 523 Hz    | 8556      | 108      | 33        |

**Line 290: Stopping All Voices**

```basic
290 POKE 54276,0:POKE 54283,0:POKE 54290,0
```

Setting each voice's control register to 0 clears the gate bit and waveform selection, silencing that voice. This is simpler than individually clearing just the gate bit (POKE 54276,16).

**Subroutine Structure (Lines 1000-2020):**

The second example uses subroutines for each chord. This is crucial for multi-voice programming:

```basic
1000 REM C MAJOR CHORD (C-E-G)
1010 REM VOICE 1: C
1020 POKE 54272,190:POKE 54273,16
1030 POKE 54276,17
1040 REM VOICE 2: E
1050 POKE 54279,25:POKE 54280,21
1060 POKE 54283,17
1070 REM VOICE 3: G
1080 POKE 54286,12:POKE 54287,25
1090 POKE 54290,17
1095 RETURN
```

Each chord subroutine sets all three voices to specific frequencies, then returns. This makes it easy to play different chords in sequence without repeating code.

## Why This Matters

Three-voice harmony is what made the C64 sound professional. Games used multiple voices for:

- **Chords in title music**: Full harmonies instead of single-note melodies
- **Bass + melody**: Voice 1 for bass, voices 2-3 for melody/harmony
- **Sound effects + music**: Music on two voices, effects on the third
- **Dynamic arrangements**: Voices move independently for counterpoint

Understanding multi-voice control is essential for:
- **Lesson 24**: Creating melody sequencers with harmony
- **Lesson 25**: Background music with simultaneous melody lines
- **Lesson 27**: Complete music composition with all SID features

## Experiments to Try

**1. Different chord types**

Change the frequencies in the C major subroutine to create other chords:

**C minor (darker, sadder sound):**
```basic
1010 REM VOICE 1: C (262HZ = 4286)
1020 POKE 54272,190:POKE 54273,16
1040 REM VOICE 2: EB (311HZ = 5091)
1050 POKE 54279,227:POKE 54280,19
1070 REM VOICE 3: G (392HZ = 6412)
1080 POKE 54286,12:POKE 54287,25
```

Minor chords use a flattened third (Eb instead of E), creating a melancholy feel.

**2. Different waveforms per voice**

Try mixing waveforms for different timbres:

```basic
190 POKE 54276,17    REM Voice 1: Triangle (mellow bass)
220 POKE 54283,33    REM Voice 2: Sawtooth (bright)
250 POKE 54290,65    REM Voice 3: Pulse (hollow)
```

Different waveforms create richer, more complex sounds. Triangle works well for bass, sawtooth for melody, pulse for counterpoint.

**3. Arpeggios (broken chords)**

Instead of playing all three notes simultaneously, play them in sequence:

```basic
180 POKE 54272,190:POKE 54273,16:POKE 54276,17    REM C
185 FOR I=1 TO 100:NEXT
190 POKE 54276,16:REM STOP
200 POKE 54279,25:POKE 54280,21:POKE 54283,17     REM E
205 FOR I=1 TO 100:NEXT
210 POKE 54283,16:REM STOP
220 POKE 54286,12:POKE 54287,25:POKE 54290,17     REM G
225 FOR I=1 TO 100:NEXT
230 POKE 54290,16:REM STOP
```

This creates the classic "broken chord" sound used in countless game soundtracks.

**4. Sustained bass with changing melody**

Keep voice 1 playing continuously whilst voices 2-3 change:

```basic
180 POKE 54272,190:POKE 54273,16
190 POKE 54276,17    REM Voice 1 stays on C
200 REM Now change voices 2-3 for different harmonies
210 POKE 54279,25:POKE 54280,21:POKE 54283,17     REM E
220 FOR I=1 TO 400:NEXT
230 POKE 54279,76:POKE 54280,22:POKE 54283,17     REM F
240 FOR I=1 TO 400:NEXT
```

This creates a drone bass with melody movement above—a common technique in electronic and folk music.

**5. Seventh chords (using voice reuse)**

Play four notes with three voices by quickly switching one voice:

```basic
180 POKE 54272,190:POKE 54273,16:POKE 54276,17    REM C
190 POKE 54279,25:POKE 54280,21:POKE 54283,17     REM E
200 POKE 54286,12:POKE 54287,25:POKE 54290,17     REM G
210 FOR I=1 TO 200:NEXT
220 POKE 54290,16    REM Stop voice 3
230 POKE 54286,117:POKE 54287,30:POKE 54290,17    REM Bb (seventh)
240 FOR I=1 TO 200:NEXT
```

This creates a C7 chord (C-E-G-Bb) by alternating between G and Bb on voice 3.

## Quick Reference

**Voice Base Addresses:**
```basic
REM Voice 1: 54272 ($D400)
REM Voice 2: 54279 ($D407) = Voice 1 + 7
REM Voice 3: 54286 ($D40E) = Voice 1 + 14
```

**Register Offsets from Base:**
```basic
REM +0: Frequency low byte
REM +1: Frequency high byte
REM +4: Control (waveform + gate)
REM +5: Attack/Decay
REM +6: Sustain/Release
```

**Play Chord on All Three Voices:**
```basic
100 REM Setup volume and ADSR
110 POKE 54296,15
120 POKE 54277,8:POKE 54278,0
130 POKE 54284,8:POKE 54285,0
140 POKE 54291,8:POKE 54292,0
150 REM Play C major chord
160 POKE 54272,190:POKE 54273,16:POKE 54276,17    REM Voice 1: C
170 POKE 54279,25:POKE 54280,21:POKE 54283,17     REM Voice 2: E
180 POKE 54286,12:POKE 54287,25:POKE 54290,17     REM Voice 3: G
```

**Stop All Voices:**
```basic
POKE 54276,0:POKE 54283,0:POKE 54290,0
```

**Calculate Low/High Bytes from SID Value:**
```basic
F=4286:FL=F AND 255:FH=INT(F/256)
POKE 54272,FL:POKE 54273,FH
```

## What You've Learnt

- The SID chip has three independent voices that can play simultaneously
- Each voice has identical register layout (frequency, control, ADSR) offset by 7 addresses
- Musical chords combine three notes played together on separate voices
- All three voices share master volume but have independent frequencies and waveforms
- Subroutines organize multi-voice programming for easy chord changes
- Different waveforms per voice create richer, more complex sounds
