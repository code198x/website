## Your Sound Synthesiser

You've been pushing pixels—now you'll push air molecules. The C64's SID chip (Sound Interface Device) isn't background music hardware; it's a three-voice analogue synthesiser designed by Bob Yannes, who later founded Ensoniq. While other computers beeped, the C64 sang, and you're about to take direct control of this legendary sound engine.

---

### The One-Minute Tour

- SID chip base address: 54272 (Voice 1 starts here)
- Volume register: 54296 (bits 0-3 control master volume)
- Frequency: Two bytes (low/high) set pitch for each voice
- Waveform: Triangle, sawtooth, pulse, noise (select with bits 4-7)
- Attack/Decay/Sustain/Release: Envelope shaping (covered in Lesson 11)

---

### Example Program

```basic
10 PRINT CHR$(147)
20 POKE 54277,9:POKE 54278,0
30 POKE 54296,15
40 POKE 54273,25:POKE 54272,177
50 POKE 54276,17
60 FOR I=1 TO 1000:NEXT I
70 POKE 54276,16
80 END
```

Sample output:

```
[Screen clears, single beep tone plays]

READY.
```

**Line 10** clears screen. **Line 20** sets Attack/Decay (A=0, D=9) and Sustain/Release (S=0, R=0) for the envelope. **Line 30** sets volume to maximum (15). **Line 40** sets frequency: high byte (25) and low byte (177) for combined value of 6577. **Line 50** activates triangle waveform (17 = gate on + triangle). **Line 60** delay loop. **Line 70** gate off (16 = triangle only, no gate).

> **Tip:** The "gate" bit (bit 0 of the waveform register) triggers the sound. Setting it to 1 starts the note, 0 stops it. The waveform bits (4-7) determine the tone colour.

### Experiment Section

Try these variations:

- **Different pitches**: Change line 40 to `POKE 54273,28:POKE 54272,214` (higher) or `POKE 54273,19:POKE 54272,63` (lower)
- **Waveforms**: Change line 30 to 33 (sawtooth), 65 (pulse), 129 (noise)
- **Higher frequencies**: Try `POKE 54273,50:POKE 54272,0` for very high pitch
- **Simple melody**: Play multiple notes in sequence with delay loops between each
- **Volume control**: Try `POKE 54296,7` for half volume

Play a simple three-note melody:

```basic
10 PRINT CHR$(147)
20 POKE 54277,9:POKE 54278,0
30 POKE 54296,15
40 FOR N=1 TO 3
50 READ HF,LF
60 POKE 54273,HF:POKE 54272,LF
70 POKE 54276,17
80 FOR I=1 TO 500:NEXT I
90 POKE 54276,16
100 FOR I=1 TO 100:NEXT I
110 NEXT N
120 DATA 25,177,28,214,32,94
130 END
```

### Concept Expansion

**Bob Yannes' masterpiece.** The SID chip wasn't designed by committee—it was the vision of engineer Bob Yannes, who was frustrated by the primitive sound capabilities of other computers. He designed a true analogue synthesiser on a chip with three independent voices, programmable filters, ring modulation, and sync. After Commodore, he co-founded Ensoniq, which dominated the professional synthesiser market in the 1980s.

**Three voices, one chip.** SID has three completely independent voices (Voice 1: 54272, Voice 2: 54279, Voice 3: 54286). Each voice has its own frequency, waveform, and envelope registers. You can play chords, harmonies, or three completely different sound effects simultaneously. Most games used one voice for music, one for continuous sound effects, and one for spot effects.

**Frequency bytes.** The two frequency bytes (low and high) form a 16-bit value that controls pitch. The formula is: frequency = note_frequency × 0.06097. For middle C (261.6 Hz), that's approximately 261.6 × 0.06097 = 15.95, which rounds to 16 (low byte). The high byte extends the range for higher pitches. Most game music used lookup tables of pre-calculated values.

### Quick Reference

**SID Chip Base Addresses:**
```
Voice 1: 54272    Voice 2: 54279    Voice 3: 54286
Master Volume: 54296 (bits 0-3, range 0-15)
```

**Voice 1 Registers:**
```
54272 = Frequency Low Byte     (0-255)
54273 = Frequency High Byte    (0-255)
54274 = Pulse Width Low        (for pulse wave)
54275 = Pulse Width High       (for pulse wave)
54276 = Waveform + Gate        (see below)
54277 = Attack/Decay           (Lesson 11)
54278 = Sustain/Release        (Lesson 11)
```

**Waveform Register (54276) Bits:**
```
Bit 0 = Gate (1=on, 0=off)     Bit 4 = Triangle (16)
Bit 1 = Sync                   Bit 5 = Sawtooth (32)
Bit 2 = Ring Modulation        Bit 6 = Pulse (64)
Bit 3 = Test                   Bit 7 = Noise (128)

Common values:
17 = Triangle wave + gate on   (16 + 1)
33 = Sawtooth + gate on        (32 + 1)
65 = Pulse + gate on           (64 + 1)
16 = Triangle wave, gate off   (16 + 0)
```

**Volume Register (54296):**
- Bits 0-3: Volume (0=silent, 15=maximum)
- Always set this first before playing sounds

### What You've Learnt

- The SID chip at 54272 is a three-voice analogue synthesiser
- Volume register 54296 controls master volume (0-15)
- Frequency registers (low/high bytes) set the pitch
- Waveform register selects tone colour and controls the gate
- Gate bit (bit 0) starts and stops notes
- Bob Yannes designed SID, later founded Ensoniq
