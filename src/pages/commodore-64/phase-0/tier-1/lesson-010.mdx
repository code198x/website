You've controlled the VIC-II chip to change colours and draw graphics. Now meet the SID—the Sound Interface Device, one of the most celebrated sound chips ever created. With three independent synthesizer voices, programmable waveforms, and envelope control, the SID powered everything from simple beeps to complex game soundtracks.

**POKE 54296,15** sets the volume to maximum. **POKE 54276,17** starts a triangle wave on voice 1. You're about to make the C64 sing.

## The One-Minute Tour

- **SID chip** (6581/8580) has 3 independent voices for music and sound effects
- **Voice 1 starts at address 54272** ($D400) with 7 registers controlling frequency, waveform, and envelope
- **Frequencies are 16-bit values** requiring two POKEs (low byte, high byte)
- **Waveforms** include triangle (soft), sawtooth (bright), pulse (hollow), and noise (percussion)
- **ADSR envelope** (Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release) shapes how notes start, hold, and end
- **Gate control** (bit 0 of waveform register) turns sound on (1) or off (0)

## First Example: Single Note

Type this and hear your first SID note:

```basic
10 REM PLAY MIDDLE C
20 POKE 54296,15
30 POKE 54272,84:POKE 54273,32
40 POKE 54277,0:POKE 54278,240
50 POKE 54276,17
60 FOR D=1 TO 500:NEXT D
70 POKE 54276,16
```

**Run this** and you'll hear a clean, sustained note for about a second.

## How SID Programming Works

**The SID chip lives at memory addresses 54272-54303 ($D400-$D41F).**

Voice 1 occupies the first 7 addresses:

**Line 20: POKE 54296,15**

Address 54296 ($D418) controls master volume for all three voices. Values 0-15, where 0 is silent and 15 is maximum. You only need to set this once at the start of your programme.

**Lines 30 and 60: Frequency Control**

Musical notes have specific frequencies. Middle C vibrates at roughly 262 Hz, but the SID uses a 16-bit frequency value (0-65535) that controls an internal oscillator.

```basic
30 POKE 54272,84:POKE 54273,32
```

This writes the 16-bit value 8276 (32×256 + 84) to the frequency registers. The formula is:

**Frequency value = (note Hz × 65536) / 985248**

For middle C (262 Hz): (262 × 65536) / 985248 ≈ 8276

You don't need to calculate these values yourself—use a frequency table (see Quick Reference below).

**Lines 40 and 30 (second example): ADSR Envelope**

ADSR controls the "shape" of the sound over time:

- **Attack**: How quickly the sound reaches full volume
- **Decay**: How quickly it drops to sustain level
- **Sustain**: The held volume level
- **Release**: How quickly it fades after gate off

```basic
40 POKE 54277,0:POKE 54278,240
```

This sets Attack=0 (instant), Decay=0 (none), Sustain=15 (full), Release=0 (instant). The result is a sharp attack and immediate release—good for demonstration. Later lessons will experiment with different envelopes for smoother or more percussive sounds.

**Lines 50 and 70: Gate Control**

The waveform register (54276, $D404) controls which waveform plays and whether the gate is open:

```basic
50 POKE 54276,17    REM Triangle wave, gate on
70 POKE 54276,16    REM Triangle wave, gate off
```

**Bit breakdown:**
- Bit 0 (value 1): Gate on/off
- Bit 4 (value 16): Triangle wave
- Bit 5 (value 32): Sawtooth wave
- Bit 6 (value 64): Pulse wave
- Bit 7 (value 128): Noise

So 17 = 16 + 1 (triangle + gate on), and 16 = just triangle (gate off, sound stops).

**Line 60 and 80/100: Timing**

Delay loops control how long notes play and the gaps between them:

```basic
80 FOR D=1 TO 300:NEXT D    REM Note duration
100 FOR D=1 TO 50:NEXT D    REM Gap between notes
```

These loops aren't precise timing—they just burn CPU cycles. Later lessons will use interrupt-driven timing for proper music synchronisation.

## Why This Matters

The SID chip was revolutionary. While contemporaries like the ZX Spectrum had simple beepers and the Apple II had basic tone generators, the C64 had a programmable synthesizer. Games used the SID for:

- **Title music**: Complex multi-voice compositions
- **Sound effects**: Explosions, lasers, jumps, pickups
- **In-game music**: Background tracks during gameplay
- **Dynamic audio**: Music that changes based on game state

Later lessons will teach multi-voice harmony, non-blocking music (so the game doesn't pause), and complex sound effects. Understanding voice 1 basics is essential—everything else builds on frequency, waveform, and envelope control.

## Experiments to Try

**1. Different waveforms**

Change line 50 to try other waveforms:
```basic
50 POKE 54276,33    REM Sawtooth (bright, buzzy)
50 POKE 54276,65    REM Pulse (hollow, reedy)
50 POKE 54276,129   REM Noise (percussion)
```

Each waveform has a distinct character. Sawtooth is bright and harsh, pulse is hollow (like a clarinet), and noise is for percussion and explosions.

**2. Play a melody**

Replace the DATA line in example 2 with these values to play "Mary Had a Little Lamb":
```basic
120 DATA 52,41,182,36,84,32,182,36
130 DATA 52,41,52,41,52,41,0,0
```

Add more DATA lines for longer melodies. Each pair is (low byte, high byte) for one note.

**3. Adjust the envelope**

Change line 30/40 to make notes fade in or out:
```basic
30 POKE 54277,9:POKE 54278,0    REM Slow attack, fast release
40 POKE 54277,0:POKE 54278,240  REM Fast attack, slow release
```

The ADSR values are packed as 4-bit nibbles (0-15 each). Experiment with different combinations to hear how they affect the sound.

**4. Pitch bend effect**

Add this after line 60 in example 1:
```basic
62 FOR B=32 TO 40
64 POKE 54273,B
66 FOR D=1 TO 20:NEXT D
68 NEXT B
```

This sweeps the high byte of the frequency upward, creating a rising pitch effect.

## Quick Reference

### SID Voice 1 Registers

| Address | Hex    | Function | Range |
|---------|--------|----------|-------|
| 54272 | $D400 | Frequency low byte | 0-255 |
| 54273 | $D401 | Frequency high byte | 0-255 |
| 54274 | $D402 | Pulse width low | 0-255 |
| 54275 | $D403 | Pulse width high | 0-15 |
| 54276 | $D404 | Control (waveform + gate) | 0-255 |
| 54277 | $D405 | Attack/Decay | 0-255 |
| 54278 | $D406 | Sustain/Release | 0-255 |

### Waveform Control Values

| Value | Waveform | Character | Gate |
|-------|----------|-----------|------|
| 16 | Triangle | Soft, mellow | Off |
| 17 | Triangle | Soft, mellow | On |
| 32 | Sawtooth | Bright, buzzy | Off |
| 33 | Sawtooth | Bright, buzzy | On |
| 64 | Pulse | Hollow, reedy | Off |
| 65 | Pulse | Hollow, reedy | On |
| 128 | Noise | Percussion | Off |
| 129 | Noise | Percussion | On |

### Musical Note Frequencies (C Major Scale)

| Note | Hz | Freq Value | Low Byte | High Byte |
|------|-----|------------|----------|-----------|
| C | 262 | 8276 | 84 | 32 |
| D | 294 | 9398 | 182 | 36 |
| E | 330 | 10548 | 52 | 41 |
| F | 349 | 11176 | 168 | 43 |
| G | 392 | 12544 | 0 | 49 |
| A | 440 | 14080 | 0 | 55 |
| B | 494 | 15804 | 188 | 61 |
| C | 523 | 16744 | 168 | 65 |

**Formula:** 16-bit value = (Hz × 65536) / 985248

### Standard ADSR Values

```basic
REM Fast attack, instant release (good for testing)
POKE 54277,0:POKE 54278,240

REM Slow attack, slow release (smooth fade in/out)
POKE 54277,9:POKE 54278,9

REM Instant attack, long release (piano-like)
POKE 54277,0:POKE 54278,200

REM Organ sustain (no decay, full sustain)
POKE 54277,0:POKE 54278,240
```

### Simple Note Pattern

```basic
10 POKE 54296,15               :REM volume
20 POKE 54277,0:POKE 54278,240 :REM adsr
30 POKE 54272,FL:POKE 54273,FH :REM frequency
40 POKE 54276,17               :REM on
50 FOR D=1 TO 500:NEXT D       :REM duration
60 POKE 54276,16               :REM off
```

## What You've Learnt

- SID chip has 3 voices for music and effects (voice 1 starts at 54272)
- Master volume (54296) controls overall loudness (0-15)
- Frequencies are 16-bit values requiring two POKEs (low/high bytes)
- Waveforms include triangle (soft), sawtooth (bright), pulse (hollow), noise (percussion)
- ADSR envelope shapes the sound attack, decay, sustain, and release
- Gate control (bit 0) turns sound on (1) or off (0)
- Delay loops provide simple timing for note duration
