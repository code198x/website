Lattice puzzles have nodes — fixed points that the player connects with lines. Dots are too small to see clearly, so we draw each node as a circle. The CIRCLE command takes a centre point and a radius, and draws a perfect circle at that position.

## How It Works

**Line 10** clears the screen.

**Lines 20-30** draw a single circle with `CIRCLE x, y, r`. The first two arguments are the centre position in pixel coordinates. The third is the radius in pixels. A radius of 8 gives a circle about the size of a character cell — large enough to see, small enough to fit many on screen.

**Lines 40-70** draw a 4x4 grid of circles using nested FOR/NEXT loops. The circles are spaced 40 pixels apart, starting from position (60,30). This gives a feel for how a puzzle layout might look — sixteen nodes in a regular grid, each clearly visible and well separated.

## Choosing a Radius

The radius controls how large each node appears. Too small and nodes are hard to see. Too large and they overlap or crowd the screen. For Lattice, a radius of 6 to 8 pixels works well. The grid spacing must be large enough that circles do not touch — a spacing of 40 pixels with a radius of 8 leaves 24 pixels of clear space between node edges.

## CIRCLE and the Coordinate System

CIRCLE uses the same pixel coordinate system as PLOT and DRAW. The centre point is specified as (x,y) with the origin at the bottom-left. A circle at (128,88) sits in the centre of the screen.

Unlike DRAW, CIRCLE does not change the current drawing position. After drawing a circle, the "pen" position is undefined — you must PLOT before the next DRAW command.
