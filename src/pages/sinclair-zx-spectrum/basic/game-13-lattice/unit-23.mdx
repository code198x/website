A single score number does not tell the player much. Stars are immediately understood — three stars means perfect, two means good, one means room for improvement. Each completed puzzle earns 1 to 3 stars based on how efficiently the player solved it.

## How It Works

**Lines 170-190** calculate the star rating after puzzle completion. The rating depends on the number of undo moves used:

- **3 stars:** zero undos — the player solved the puzzle perfectly
- **2 stars:** 1-2 undos — minor backtracking
- **1 star:** 3 or more undos — significant trial and error

**Lines 200-220** display the rating. Asterisks represent stars: "***" for three, "** " for two, "* " for one. The display uses PRINT AT on the HUD area, with INK 6 (yellow) to make the stars stand out.

**Lines 230-240** store the rating in array `st()`, indexed by puzzle number. This allows the program to track the best rating for each puzzle across the session.

## Why Stars?

Stars provide replay motivation. A player who finishes a puzzle with one star knows they can do better. Three stars on every puzzle is a completionist goal that extends the game beyond simply solving each puzzle once.

The thresholds are deliberately generous. Three stars for zero undos rewards planning ahead. Two stars for minor backtracking acknowledges that some experimentation is normal. One star for heavy undo use still rewards completion — the player solved the puzzle, just not efficiently.

## Storing Ratings

Array `st()` persists across puzzles within a session. When the player returns to a previously completed puzzle (added in a later unit), the stored rating can be displayed. The array is dimensioned to match the total puzzle count, so every puzzle has a slot.

## Try This

**Earn three stars.** Pick a puzzle you have solved before and try to complete it with zero undos. Plan your connection order before drawing.

**Adjust the thresholds.** Change the undo cutoffs — perhaps 3 stars for 0-1 undos, 2 stars for 2-3. Which thresholds feel fair?
