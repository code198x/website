Sound reinforces every action in the game. A rising tone when a connection is drawn. A low buzz when a crossing is rejected. A multi-note fanfare when a puzzle is completed. These audio cues make the game feel responsive and rewarding.

## How It Works

**Lines 1500-1510** play a connection sound — a short ascending tone. `BEEP 0.05, 20` followed by `BEEP 0.05, 24` creates a quick two-note rise. The pitch increases, suggesting progress and success.

**Lines 1520-1530** play the rejection sound — a low descending tone. `BEEP 0.1, 5` followed by `BEEP 0.1, 2` drops in pitch, signalling failure. The longer duration (0.1 seconds per note) makes it more noticeable than the connection sound.

**Lines 1540-1570** play the completion fanfare — four ascending notes that span a wider pitch range. `BEEP 0.1, 20`, `BEEP 0.1, 24`, `BEEP 0.1, 27`, `BEEP 0.2, 32` builds to a satisfying conclusion. The final note is held longer for emphasis.

**Lines 1580-1600** play the cursor movement sound — a very short, quiet tick. `BEEP 0.02, 30` is brief enough not to be annoying when the player moves rapidly between nodes.

## BEEP Syntax

`BEEP duration, pitch` plays a tone for the specified duration in seconds at the specified pitch. Pitch is measured in semitones above middle C. A pitch of 0 is middle C. Each increment of 1 is one semitone higher. Negative values go below middle C.

Key pitches to remember:
- 0 = middle C
- 12 = C one octave up
- 24 = C two octaves up
- -12 = C one octave down

## Sound as Feedback

Each sound maps to a specific game event:

| Event | Sound | Purpose |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Connection drawn | Rising two-note | Confirms success |
| Crossing rejected | Falling two-note | Signals error |
| Puzzle complete | Four-note fanfare | Celebrates achievement |
| Cursor move | Short tick | Acknowledges input |
| Hint used | Single mid tone | Neutral acknowledgement |

The sounds are short and distinct. The player learns to associate each sound with its event, which provides feedback even when they are not looking directly at the relevant part of the screen.

## Try This

**Play with sound off.** Mute the speaker and play a puzzle. Does the game feel less responsive without audio feedback?

**Design your own fanfare.** Write a five or six-note completion melody using BEEP. Try to create something that feels celebratory without being too long.
