Drawing a connection with DRAW is instant — the line appears all at once. An animated line that extends gradually from start to end looks more polished and gives the player a moment to appreciate the connection being made. This unit replaces the instant DRAW with an incremental animation.

## How It Works

**Lines 200-260** replace the single DRAW command with an animated sequence. Instead of drawing the full line in one step, the subroutine breaks it into small segments drawn one after another.

**Lines 210-220** calculate the total distance between the start and end nodes in x and y. The number of animation steps is the larger of the two distances divided by a step size (4 pixels works well). More steps means smoother animation; fewer steps means faster drawing.

**Lines 230-250** loop through the steps. At each step, the program PLOTs a short segment from the current position toward the end node. The step offset is calculated as `(dx * i) / steps` and `(dy * i) / steps`, where `i` is the current step and `steps` is the total count. DRAW extends by one step's worth of movement each iteration.

**Lines 255** has no PAUSE between steps. The loop itself, with the PLOT and DRAW calculations, is slow enough at BASIC speed to create a visible animation. On the Spectrum, each iteration takes a few milliseconds — fast enough to look smooth, slow enough to see.

## Why Animate?

Instant lines are functional but abrupt. Animated lines add three things:

1. **Visual satisfaction.** Watching a line extend is more rewarding than seeing it pop into existence.
2. **Directional clarity.** The animation shows which node is the start and which is the end, though this does not affect gameplay.
3. **Pacing.** The brief animation gives the player a moment between connections, preventing accidental rapid-fire selections.

## BASIC Speed as a Feature

In assembly language, you would need to add deliberate delays to slow down an animation. In BASIC, the interpreter is slow enough that a simple loop naturally produces a visible animation. This is one of the rare cases where BASIC's speed is a benefit rather than a limitation.

## Try This

**Adjust the step size.** Change the step divisor from 4 to 2 (more steps, slower) or 8 (fewer steps, faster). Which speed feels best?

**Animate in reverse for undo.** When the player presses U, animate the line shrinking from end to start before removing it. This makes undo feel as deliberate as drawing.
