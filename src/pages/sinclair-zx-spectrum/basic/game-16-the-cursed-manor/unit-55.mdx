Talking to guests is one-directional. GIVE adds a two-way interaction — the player offers an item, and the guest responds. Most items are politely declined (the game needs them), but the gesture deepens the relationship between player and characters.

## The GIVE Command

GIVE [item] TO [person] involves an item and a target. The parser extracts the item name (before "TO") and the person name (after "TO"). Both must be present and valid.

## How It Works

**Lines 1900-1960** handle the GIVE verb. The noun is split at "TO" — everything before is the item, everything after is the recipient. The code checks: is the player carrying the item? Is the recipient in the room and free?

**Item matching** uses the same partial-match system as TAKE and EXAMINE. "GIVE KEY TO COLONEL" matches the silver key and Colonel Blackwood.

**Most gifts are declined.** The Colonel says "I appreciate the gesture, but you may need that more than I." Dr Hartley says "Keep it. I am more interested in studying it from a distance." The responses are polite and in-character.

**The grimoire to Dr Hartley** is a special case. If the player gives the grimoire to the Doctor, he responds with additional insight: "Remarkable binding work. The symbols here suggest the curse was cast with great precision — and great anger. Elara must have had powerful reasons." This is flavour, not a required puzzle step — the game works without it.

**Giving items to frozen guests** produces: "You hold the [item] out, but the stone figure cannot take it." The futility reinforces the curse.

**Giving items to the cat** produces: "The cat sniffs the [item] and turns away. Cats have no use for such things."

## Try This

**Give every item to every guest.** Note the responses. Are they in character? Does any response feel generic or mismatched?

**Give the grimoire to the Doctor.** Read his analysis. This is optional content that rewards curious players — the kind of detail that makes a text adventure feel deep.

**Try GIVE without carrying the item.** The error should say "You are not carrying that." Try GIVE with no item specified: "Give what?" Good error messages for every input variation.
