Text adventures that include illustrations feel richer. The Spectrum's PLOT and DRAW commands can create simple line drawings — a doorway, a window, a piece of furniture. This unit adds an illustration to the entrance hall that appears above the text description.

## Drawing in the Top Half

The screen is 256 pixels wide and 176 pixels tall. Reserve the top portion (roughly 80 pixels) for illustrations and the bottom for text. PLOT sets a starting point; DRAW draws a line relative to it. A few lines create a recognisable scene.

## How It Works

**Lines 700-740** contain the drawing subroutine for room 1 (entrance hall). PLOT places the pen at a coordinate. DRAW moves it by a relative amount — `DRAW 40, 0` draws a horizontal line 40 pixels right. Combining horizontal, vertical, and diagonal lines creates the outline of a doorway with a pointed arch.

**Line 210** calls the drawing subroutine after CLS but before the text prints. `GO SUB 700` jumps to the illustration. Each room will have its own drawing subroutine, selected by the room number.

**The illustration area** uses the top 10 character rows (80 pixels). Text starts at `PRINT AT 11, 0` to avoid overwriting the drawing. This split keeps graphics and text separate.

**INK 5** (cyan) gives the drawings a blueprint quality — line drawings on a dark background. The consistent colour ties all illustrations together visually.

The drawing is deliberately simple. You are not trying to create detailed pixel art — just a recognisable sketch that supports the room description. A few lines suggesting an arched doorway communicate "entrance hall" faster than words alone.

## Try This

**Draw a different room.** Create a subroutine at line 750 for room 2 (corridor). A long horizontal line with doorways branching off captures the shape. Use `ON r GO SUB` to select the right drawing for each room.

**Add detail.** Draw a small table or chair in the entrance hall illustration. Each object is just a few PLOT and DRAW commands. Keep it minimal — the Spectrum's resolution means simple shapes read better than complex ones.

**Experiment with CIRCLE.** `CIRCLE 128, 60, 20` draws a circle. A clock face, a mirror, or a plate on a table — circles add variety to the line drawings.
