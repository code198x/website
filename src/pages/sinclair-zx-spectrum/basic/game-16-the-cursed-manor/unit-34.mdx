## How It Works

**The tower room description** (DATA line for room 14) reads: "A circular room at the top of the tower. Wind howls through narrow windows. In the centre, a stone pedestal holds a leather-bound book." The description establishes the grimoire's importance through its placement — alone on a pedestal, the focus of the room.

**The grimoire** (item 3) starts in room 10 (the study) in the current version. This unit moves it to room 14 (the tower). The player must unlock the tower before they can access it. This creates a clear dependency chain: find key, unlock tower, take grimoire.

**A tower illustration** draws the circular room with narrow slit windows and the pedestal at centre. The curved walls use short DRAW segments to approximate a circle. The pedestal is a simple rectangle with a small rectangle (the book) on top.

**The wind sound** plays when entering the tower — a sustained low BEEP suggesting howling wind through the narrow windows. The tower should feel high and exposed, contrasting with the enclosed rooms below.

## Try This

**Experience the full sequence.** Start a new game. Find the key, unlock the tower, climb the stairs, and enter the tower room for the first time. The sequence should feel like a narrative arc: discovery, challenge, solution, reward.

**Examine the grimoire here.** Before taking it, examine it on the pedestal. The examine text should describe it in context — "The leather binding is cracked with age. Strange symbols are embossed on the cover." After taking it, examining it again should feel different — you are holding it now, not viewing it from a distance.

**Look out the windows.** Add "WINDOW" as scenery in the tower room. Examining it should describe the view — the manor grounds far below, the dark forest surrounding the estate. Scenery that rewards curiosity.
