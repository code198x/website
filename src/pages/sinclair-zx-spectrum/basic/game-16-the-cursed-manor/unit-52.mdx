The room descriptions were written for function — they identified the room and listed its features. Now rewrite them for atmosphere. Good descriptions engage multiple senses: sight, sound, smell, touch, temperature. They create a feeling, not just a picture.

## Sensory Writing

A functional description: "A large kitchen with a table and stove." A sensory description: "The kitchen smells of old grease and cold stone. A scarred oak table dominates the centre. The stove is dark — no fire has burned here in a long time." Both convey the same information, but the second one places the player in the room.

## How It Works

**All 20 DATA lines** are rewritten. The new descriptions follow these principles:

**Lead with the dominant sense.** The cellar leads with smell ("Damp earth and mildew"). The tower leads with sound ("Wind screams through the narrow windows"). The garden leads with temperature ("The cold night air is a relief after the manor's oppression").

**One sentence for atmosphere, one for detail.** The first sentence sets the mood. The second gives the player something to interact with. "The library is silent as a tomb. Leather-bound books line every wall from floor to ceiling." Mood, then detail.

**Consistent tone.** Every room feels like part of the same cursed manor. Indoor rooms are cold, dusty, and dark. Outdoor rooms are cold but less oppressive. The sealed chamber is actively unpleasant — the cold is supernatural, not just weather.

**Brief descriptions** (`b$` array) are also rewritten. "The dusty entrance hall" becomes "The cold entrance hall." "The library" becomes "The silent library." Each brief description is 3-5 words that capture the room's essence.

**Length constraint** — descriptions must fit within the 80-character string limit. This forces tight writing. Every word earns its place. "A grand entrance hall with a sweeping staircase and dust on every surface and portraits on the walls" becomes "Dust covers the entrance hall. Portraits watch from the walls, their eyes dark."

## Try This

**Read every description aloud.** If a description sounds flat when spoken, rewrite it. Text adventure descriptions are essentially spoken narration — they should have rhythm.

**Check for repetition.** Do multiple rooms use the word "dark"? "Cold"? "Dusty"? Some repetition is fine — the manor is dark, cold, and dusty — but vary the vocabulary. "Dim" instead of "dark." "Chill" instead of "cold." "Neglected" instead of "dusty."

**Test the brief descriptions.** Walk through the manor visiting rooms you have already seen. The brief descriptions should jog your memory without slowing you down.
