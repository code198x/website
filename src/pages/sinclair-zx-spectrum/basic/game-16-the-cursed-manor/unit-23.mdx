Six items and two hands. A carry limit forces the player to think about what they need and where they are going. Carrying everything at once removes decision-making. A limit of four items means the player must leave two behind and return for them when needed.

## Counting Carried Items

Before allowing a TAKE, count how many items have `ic(i) = 1`. If the count equals the limit, refuse the take and tell the player they are carrying too much. The player must drop something first.

## How It Works

**Line 11** sets the carry limit: `LET ml = 4`. Four items is generous enough that the player does not feel punished, but tight enough that they cannot hoover up everything.

**Lines 550-555** count carried items before a TAKE. A quick loop sums up `ic(i)` values. If the total equals `ml`, the game prints "You are carrying too many things. Drop something first." and skips the pickup.

**The INVENTORY command** now shows the count: "You are carrying: (3/4)". This tells the player how much space they have left without making them count manually.

**Strategic implications** — the player must decide what to carry based on where they are going. If they are heading to the tower, they need the key. If they are heading to the cellar, they need the lantern. Planning ahead is part of the challenge.

## Try This

**Hit the limit.** Pick up four items and try to take a fifth. The refusal message should appear. Drop one and try again — it should work now.

**Adjust the limit.** Try `LET ml = 3` and `LET ml = 5`. A lower limit forces more backtracking. A higher limit feels more relaxed. Four is a good balance for six total items.

**Show the limit in HELP.** When you add the HELP command (unit 31), include "You can carry up to 4 items" in the instructions. Players who know the limit can plan better.
