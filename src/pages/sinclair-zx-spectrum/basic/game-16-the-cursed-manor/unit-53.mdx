Each guest appears in three states, and each state needs a distinct description. Frozen guests are eerie stone figures. Waking guests are confused and disoriented. Free guests are fully alive, with personality and purpose. The transition should feel like watching someone return from the dead.

## Three Descriptions Per Guest

The room display selects a description based on `gs(i)`. The frozen description sets the horror. The waking description builds tension. The free description releases it. Together, the three stages tell a mini-story for each character.

## How It Works

**Lines 9600-9650** store three descriptions per guest in DATA statements:

**Colonel Blackwood:**
- Frozen: "The Colonel stands rigid, one hand raised as if proposing a toast. His stone face holds an expression of mild surprise."
- Waking: "The Colonel blinks slowly. Colour creeps back into his cheeks. He lowers his hand, staring at it as if seeing it for the first time."
- Free: "Colonel Blackwood paces the room, muttering about the state of things."

**The Maid:**
- Frozen: "A young woman in a black dress and white apron, frozen mid-step. One hand reaches for a door handle that is not there."
- Waking: "The Maid stumbles, catching herself on the wall. She looks around in confusion, breathing hard."
- Free: "The Maid moves quietly, straightening things as she goes."

**Each frozen description** captures a specific moment — the instant the curse struck. The Colonel was making a toast. The Maid was opening a door. Lady Ashworth was descending the stairs. Dr Hartley was reading. Mrs Pemberton was cooking. These frozen moments tell you who these people are.

**Each waking description** shows physical recovery — blinking, stumbling, breathing. The transition from stone to flesh is disorienting and frightening. The descriptions reflect this.

**Each free description** shows personality through action. The Colonel paces and commands. The Maid cleans and serves. Dr Hartley investigates and analyses. These actions match the NPC behaviour system from units 43-44.

## Try This

**Read each transition sequence.** Mentally play through seeing each guest frozen, then waking, then free. Does each transition feel like a coherent mini-narrative?

**Compare personalities.** Are the five guests distinct? Could you identify each one from their free description alone? If two guests feel similar, differentiate them further.

**Add physical details.** What does the Colonel look like? Tall, silver-haired, military bearing? A few physical details in the frozen description help the player visualise each guest.
