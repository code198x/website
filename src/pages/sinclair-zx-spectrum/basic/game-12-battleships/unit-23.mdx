How efficiently did the winner play? Shot counts tell the story. Sinking all five ships takes a minimum of 17 hits (5+4+3+3+2), but the total shots fired will always be higher because of misses. Tracking and displaying this number adds a competitive element beyond just winning.

## How It Works

**Lines 20-30** initialise the shot counters. `LET s1 = 0` counts Player 1 shots. `LET s2 = 0` counts Player 2 shots.

**Lines 90-100** increment the appropriate counter after each valid shot. If `p = 1`, increment `s1`. If `p = 2`, increment `s2`. The increment happens after the duplicate check, so rejected shots do not count.

**Lines 310-330** display the shot totals on the victory screen. Below the winner message: "Player 1 fired " + `STR$(s1)` + " shots" and the same for Player 2. The winner total shows how efficiently they played.

## Minimum Shots

The five ships occupy 17 cells total (5+4+3+3+2). A perfect game with no misses takes exactly 17 shots. On a 10x10 grid with 100 cells, random shooting averages around 50-60 shots before finding all ships. Skilled players using search patterns can finish in 30-40 shots. The shot counter puts a number on this skill.

## Two Variables vs One Array

We use `s1` and `s2` rather than `DIM s(2)`. Either works, but simple variables are marginally faster to access than array elements in Spectrum BASIC. For two values that are incremented every turn, the speed difference adds up over a full game. The trade-off: with an array, we could write `LET s(p) = s(p) + 1` without an IF. With variables, we need `IF p=1 THEN LET s1=s1+1: ELSE LET s2=s2+1`. Both are fine; we chose variables for speed.

## Try This

**Play and compare totals.** Play a full game and check the shot counts. Were they close, or did one player fire significantly more? Low totals indicate either skill or luck.

**Add hit percentage.** Calculate hits as a fraction of total shots: `LET pct = INT(17 / s1 * 100)`. Display "Hit rate: 34%" alongside the shot count. This normalises the statistic for comparison.
