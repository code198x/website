When a shot hits, the cell turns red — but on a grid with several red cells already, the new hit can be hard to spot. FLASH makes the most recent hit blink, drawing the eye to exactly where it landed. After a brief pause, the flash turns off and the cell settles into its permanent colour.

## How It Works

**Lines 100-110** print the hit cell on the tracking grid with `FLASH 1` enabled. The cell character appears in the right colour (red for hit) but blinks between INK and PAPER colours. This creates a visual pulse that stands out from the static grid.

**Lines 120-130** pause for 50 frames (one second at 50fps) with `PAUSE 50`. During this pause, the cell flashes visibly. The player sees exactly where their shot landed.

**Lines 140-150** reprint the same cell with `FLASH 0` to stop the blinking. The cell is now permanently red, matching all other hit cells on the grid.

## How FLASH Works

The Spectrum attribute system stores FLASH as a single bit per character cell. When FLASH is set, the hardware automatically swaps INK and PAPER colours every 16 frames (about 0.32 seconds). This happens in hardware — no BASIC code runs during the flashing. Setting FLASH is therefore free in performance terms.

The visual effect depends on the INK and PAPER colours. Red PAPER with yellow INK creates a red-yellow alternation, which is eye-catching. Blue PAPER with blue INK would flash invisibly. Choose colour combinations where the flash is clearly visible.

## Temporary vs Permanent Attributes

The flash is temporary — it serves as an animation that draws attention, then settles into the permanent state. This pattern (highlight then revert) is useful whenever you need to show "something just happened here". The alternative, leaving FLASH on permanently, would make the grid increasingly frantic as hits accumulate.

## Try This

**Try different pause durations.** Change `PAUSE 50` to `PAUSE 25` (half a second) or `PAUSE 100` (two seconds). Which duration feels right? Too short and the player misses it; too long and the game feels slow.

**Flash misses too.** Apply the same flash treatment to miss cells. Does it help or does it make misses feel more important than they are?
