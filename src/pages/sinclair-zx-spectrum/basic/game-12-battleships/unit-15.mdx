Sound reinforces what the player sees. A satisfying hit sound makes hitting a ship feel impactful. A dull miss sound confirms the shot went wide. Even with the Spectrum single-channel speaker, two distinct tones make the game more engaging.

## How It Works

**Lines 80-90** play the hit sound immediately after detecting a hit. Two ascending BEEP notes create a rising tone: `BEEP 0.1,20: BEEP 0.1,25`. The first note is at semitone 20 (around B5), and the second jumps up to semitone 25 (around E6). The quick rise sounds positive and rewarding.

**Lines 100-110** play the miss sound. A single low BEEP: `BEEP 0.3,5`. Semitone 5 is a low D above middle C. The longer duration (0.3 seconds vs 0.1) and single note contrast sharply with the hit sound. Players will quickly learn to associate the sounds with outcomes before they even read the text.

## BEEP Timing

BEEP blocks execution — the program pauses while the sound plays. This is usually a problem, but for shot feedback it works well. The sound plays, then the result text appears. The brief pause builds anticipation: the player hears the tone and knows the outcome a fraction of a second before reading it.

Keep the total sound duration short. The hit sound takes 0.2 seconds (two notes of 0.1 each). The miss takes 0.3 seconds. Anything longer would feel sluggish when playing dozens of turns.

## Sound Design Principles

Good game sounds are:

- **Distinct** — hit and miss should be instantly distinguishable
- **Brief** — they punctuate gameplay, not interrupt it
- **Consistent** — the same event always makes the same sound
- **Appropriate** — high/rising for positive events, low/falling for negative

The Spectrum has limited audio capabilities, but these principles still apply. Two different tones at different pitches and durations are enough to create clear audio feedback.

## Try This

**Swap the sounds.** Use the low tone for hits and the rising tone for misses. Play a few rounds. Does the game feel wrong? Sound associations are powerful.

**Add a third sound.** When the player fires at a cell they already targeted, play a different sound — perhaps a quick buzz with `BEEP 0.05,0: BEEP 0.05,2: BEEP 0.05,0`. This gives audio feedback for errors as well as results.
