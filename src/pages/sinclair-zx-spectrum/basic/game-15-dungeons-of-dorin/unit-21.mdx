The status panel shows stats at a glance, but it cannot show everything. An inventory screen gives the player a full picture: current weapon and armour by name, potion count, gold total, and combat stats with equipment bonuses broken down. Press a key to open it, press any key to return to the dungeon.

## How It Works

The inventory screen is a third mode alongside exploration and combat. When triggered from the main loop, it clears the screen and displays all player information in a readable layout.

**Equipment names** are derived from the bonus values. A weapon bonus of 0 means "Fists", 1 means "Rusty Sword", 2 means "Steel Sword", 3 means "Enchanted Blade". Armour follows the same pattern: 0 is "None", 1 is "Leather", 2 is "Chain Mail", 3 is "Plate Armour". These names are selected with IF chains rather than DATA statements, since there are only four values for each.

**Stat breakdown** shows the base stat and equipment bonus separately. "Attack: 3 + 2 (Steel Sword)" tells the player exactly where their numbers come from. This transparency is important — the player should understand why their attack is 5, not just see the total.

**Gold and potions** are displayed alongside a floor counter and kill count. This gives the player context for how their run is going.

## Mode Switching

The inventory screen is the third mode in the game, after exploration and combat. Each mode takes full control of the screen: exploration shows the viewport and status panel, combat shows the fight display, and inventory shows the stat summary. CLS marks each transition, and pressing a key returns to the previous mode.

This pattern — clearing the screen and drawing a new interface — is the simplest way to handle multiple views in a character-based display. More sophisticated approaches would preserve the viewport and overlay the inventory, but that requires careful cursor management and partial redraws. The full-screen approach is clean and reliable.

## Named Equipment

Turning numbers into names makes the game feel richer. "Steel Sword +2" communicates more than "weapon bonus: 2". The names do not affect gameplay — the bonus value drives combat — but they give the player something to discover and something to talk about. "I found an Enchanted Blade on floor 7" is more memorable than "my weapon variable increased."

## Try This

**Add a key count.** If you add a key item system later, show how many keys the player holds. Even without the mechanic, designing the display first helps plan the feature.

**Show floor history.** Display a line showing which floors the player has visited: "Floors explored: 1, 2, 3, 4." This requires an additional array to track visited floors.
