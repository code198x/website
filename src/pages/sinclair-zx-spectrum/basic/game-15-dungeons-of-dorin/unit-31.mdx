Reaching the Heart of Dorin is an achievement that deserves a proper ending. The victory screen shows everything the player accomplished across ten floors: monsters defeated, gold collected, turns survived, and HP remaining. A rising fanfare celebrates the victory. The game ends with STOP — a deliberate, final moment.

## How It Works

**Lines 1100-1126** trigger when the floor counter exceeds 10.

**Line 1104** prints the headline: "THE HEART OF DORIN!" in bright white. This names the achievement. The player found the thing they were told to find on the title screen.

**Lines 1106-1108** describe the outcome in yellow: "You found the ancient heart and restored Dorin!" Two lines of narrative conclusion. The quest is complete. The city is saved.

**Lines 1110-1118** show the stat summary:
- **Floors: 10** — always 10 on a successful run
- **Kills:** the total monster count across all floors
- **Gold:** the total gold collected
- **Turns:** how many moves the player made
- **HP remaining:** how much health survived the journey

Each stat uses a different colour for readability. White for neutral stats (floors, kills, turns), yellow for wealth (gold), cyan for health (HP remaining).

**Line 1120** plays the victory fanfare: four ascending notes that build to a climax. The increasing duration (0.1, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 seconds) gives the fanfare a sense of resolution.

**Line 1126** ends the game with STOP. Unlike the death screen, which offers a restart, the victory screen is terminal. The player has won. The game is over.

## Stats as a Scorecard

The stat summary serves as an informal scorecard. Two players who both complete the game can compare kills, gold, and turns. Fewer turns means a more efficient run. More kills means a more aggressive one. Higher remaining HP means better resource management. These comparisons add replayability — the player might try again to beat their own numbers.

The turn counter is particularly interesting. A player who rushes for the stairs on every floor will have a low turn count but may miss chests and arrive at later floors under-equipped. A thorough explorer will have a high turn count but better gear. The ideal balance is the sweet spot between speed and preparation.

## The Death Screen Comparison

The death screen (lines 1000-1016) shows similar stats but frames them as a record of failure: "YOU HAVE FALLEN" instead of "THE HEART OF DORIN!" The same data tells a different story. On death, the floor number shows how far the player got. On victory, it confirms they went all the way. This parallel structure — same stats, different context — is a simple but effective design pattern.

## Try This

**Add a rating.** Based on turns taken, give the player a grade: "Master Delver" for under 200 turns, "Seasoned Explorer" for under 400, "Cautious Wanderer" for anything above. A text label adds flavour to the raw numbers.

**Track equipment found.** Add a line showing the best weapon and armour found during the run. "Best weapon: Enchanted Blade (+4)" gives the player a sense of the loot they accumulated.
