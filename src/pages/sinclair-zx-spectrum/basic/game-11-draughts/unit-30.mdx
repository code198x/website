Sound tells the player what happened before they read the screen. Each game event needs its own audio signature so the player builds an instinctive map between sounds and actions. This unit replaces the basic clicks with a full palette of distinct sounds for every event type.

## How It Works

**Error sound** at each validation rejection uses `BEEP 0.05,-5`. A short, low-pitched buzz that signals "something went wrong" without being irritating. Players hear this often while learning, so it needs to stay unobtrusive.

**Move sound** at line 1062 uses `BEEP 0.05,10`. A brief, light click confirms the move was accepted. It is the most common sound in the game and the quietest.

**Capture sound** at line 1055 uses `BEEP 0.1,5`. Longer and deeper than the move click, with enough presence to signal that a piece was removed. The difference in pitch and duration makes captures immediately distinguishable from simple moves.

**Promotion sound** at lines 1025-1026 uses `BEEP 0.1,20: BEEP 0.1,24`. Two ascending notes celebrate the piece becoming a king. The rising pitch mirrors the piece's rise in status.

**Victory fanfare** at lines 1380-1390 uses `BEEP 0.2,12: BEEP 0.2,16: BEEP 0.2,19`. Three ascending notes, the longest sound in the game. Reserved for the most significant moment.

**Stalemate loss** at lines 1480-1485 uses `BEEP 0.2,0: BEEP 0.2,-3: BEEP 0.2,-7`. Three descending notes that contrast sharply with the ascending victory fanfare. The falling pitch signals defeat.

## Sound Design Principles

Three rules guide the sound palette:

1. **Duration matches importance.** Quick events (moves, errors) get short sounds. Rare events (wins, promotions) get longer sequences.
2. **Pitch conveys mood.** Positive events ascend. Negative events descend. Neutral events stay low and brief.
3. **Every event is distinct.** No two events share the same sound. The player can identify what happened from audio alone.

## The Spectrum Speaker

The Spectrum has a single-channel beeper with no sustain or envelope control. `BEEP` takes two parameters: duration in seconds and pitch in semitones from middle C. Negative values go below middle C. Playing multiple BEEPs in sequence creates a melody, but each one blocks execution until it finishes.

For a turn-based game, this blocking behaviour is fine. The player waits for the sound to finish before making their next move. In a real-time game, you would need to keep every sound under 0.1 seconds.

## Try This

**Change the victory fanfare.** Try a major chord (0, 4, 7) for a triumphant feel, or a minor chord (0, 3, 7) for something more reflective. Which suits a draughts victory?

**Add an error buzz.** Before each error message, play `BEEP 0.05,-10`. Does the low buzz help the player notice invalid moves more quickly?

**Remove all sound.** Comment out every BEEP line and play a game. Notice how much harder it is to follow the action without audio feedback. Sound carries information that the eye can miss.
