The game works for normal play. But unusual situations reveal gaps: a capture at the edge of the board, king promotion during a multi-jump chain, both players locked with no moves. This unit adds the `REM === DRAUGHTS ===` title line and ensures every edge case is handled robustly.

## How It Works

**Line 1** adds `REM === DRAUGHTS ===` as a program title. This is a convention in Spectrum BASIC programs. The REM statement does nothing at runtime, but it identifies the program in listings and when saving to tape.

**Board edge captures** are handled by bounds checking in the capture scan (lines 1660-1670) and the multi-jump check (lines 1755-1760). Before checking any diagonal direction, the code verifies that the target square is within the 1-8 range. Without this, a piece at column A trying to jump left would access `b(r,0)` or `b(r,-1)`, causing unpredictable behaviour.

**King promotion during chains** works because the promotion check at lines 1025-1026 runs after every capture, not just after normal moves. If a piece reaches the back row during a multi-jump, it becomes a king immediately. The multi-jump check at line 1750 then uses the updated piece value, allowing backward captures from the promotion square.

**Capturing the last piece mid-chain** triggers the win check after the full chain completes. The win detection at line 145 runs after the turn ends, which means it catches the final state after all jumps in a chain have been executed.

## Systematic Testing

Edge cases cluster around boundaries. In a board game, the boundaries are:

- **Board edges** - squares in columns A and H, rows 1 and 8
- **State transitions** - promotion (piece to king), chain start and end
- **Terminal states** - last piece captured, no moves available
- **Arithmetic boundaries** - intermediate square calculation when jumping near edges

For each boundary, the question is: "What is the smallest position that exercises this boundary?" Place a piece there, attempt the action, and verify the result.

## Testing Checklist

Test each of these positions:

1. **Corner captures** - piece at B2, opponent at A1. Can the capture proceed? The landing square would be off the board.
2. **Edge captures** - piece at G6, opponent at H7. Landing at I8 is out of bounds. The bounds check should reject silently.
3. **Promotion during chain** - piece at B6, opponents at C7 and a valid second jump from D8. After promotion, can the king jump backward?
4. **Last piece mid-chain** - two opponent pieces in a double-jump line. After the first capture, one remains. After the second, zero. Win triggers after the chain.
5. **No moves on turn start** - all pieces blocked by board edge and opponent pieces. The no-moves check should declare the winner before asking for input.

## Try This

**Test every board corner.** Place pieces near A1, A8, H1, and H8. Attempt captures in all directions. Every out-of-bounds attempt should be caught cleanly.

**Force a draw position.** With two kings each, create a position where neither side can capture and both can move forever. The game will loop indefinitely. This is a known limitation that tournament draughts addresses with move counters, but it is outside the scope of this implementation.

**Break the input.** Type numbers instead of letters, enter extra characters, or press Enter with no input. Each should be handled with an error message, not a crash.
