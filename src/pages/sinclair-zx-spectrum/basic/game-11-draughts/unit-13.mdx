A capture is only valid when three conditions are met: the landing square is empty, the jumped square holds an opponent's piece, and the jump is in the forward direction. This unit adds captured piece counting and tightens the validation.

## How It Works

**Line 25** introduces two counters: `g` for pieces captured by Player 1, `h` for pieces captured by Player 2.

**Lines 1110-1170** form a chain of capture validation checks:

1. **Landing square empty** (1110): `b(v,u) <> 0` means the destination is occupied. Reject.
2. **Piece to jump** (1130): `b(w,x) = 0` means there is nothing to jump over. Reject.
3. **Enemy piece** (1140-1150): The jumped piece must belong to the opponent. Jumping over your own piece is not allowed.
4. **Forward direction** (1160-1170): Like regular moves, captures must go forward for non-king pieces.

**Lines 1055-1056** increment the correct capture counter after a successful jump.

## Complex Conditional Logic

Each capture validation line checks one specific condition and rejects the move if it fails. The order matters. There is no point checking whether the jumped piece is an enemy if there is no piece to jump. The checks cascade from simple to specific:

1. Is the landing square available?
2. Is there something in between?
3. Is that something an enemy?
4. Is the direction correct?

If all four pass, the capture is valid. This cascade pattern is the Spectrum BASIC equivalent of an AND chain. Without ELSE or complex boolean logic, layered IF-THEN-GOTO is the only practical approach.

## Try This

**Test each error path.** Set up board positions that trigger each specific error. Try jumping to an occupied square, jumping over empty space, jumping over your own piece, and jumping backward. Each should produce a different message.

**Track captures.** Play several turns with captures and watch the counter. The status line now shows how many pieces each player has captured.
