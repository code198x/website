# Jack Tramiel

**Jack Tramiel** (1928-2012) was a Polish-American businessman who founded Commodore International and later led Atari Corporation. His aggressive business tactics and vision for affordable computing changed the industry forever.

## From Holocaust Survivor to Tech Titan

Born Idek Trzmiel in Poland, Tramiel survived Auschwitz and emigrated to the United States in 1947. After serving in the U.S. Army, he founded Commodore in 1954, initially as a typewriter repair company.

## The Commodore Revolution

Under Tramiel's leadership, Commodore:
- Acquired MOS Technology (1976), securing chip production
- Launched the PET, VIC-20, and Commodore 64
- Engaged in aggressive price wars that drove competitors from the market
- Made computing affordable for millions of families

## Famous Philosophy

Tramiel's motto "Computers for the masses, not the classes" drove Commodore's strategy of producing powerful but affordable machines. His aggressive pricing strategies earned him the nickname "The Darth Vader of Computing."

## The Atari Years

After leaving Commodore in 1984, Tramiel acquired Atari's consumer division, launching the successful ST series of computers that competed directly with the Amiga and Macintosh.

## Legacy

Jack Tramiel's impact on computing history is immeasurable. By making computers affordable and accessible, he democratized technology and enabled an entire generation to learn programming and digital creativity.
