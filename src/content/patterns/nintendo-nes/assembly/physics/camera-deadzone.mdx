A camera need not move every time the player takes a step. Keep a quiet area on screen, and scroll only when the player pushes beyond it. The camera should also stop at the edge of the world rather than revealing an empty border.

## Include, initialise and call

Reserve **CAMERA, PLAYER, LEFT_EDGE, RIGHT_EDGE, SCROLL_LIMIT, SHIFTED, VALID** as 7 contiguous private bytes in that order. Include the helper once. Call `camera_deadzone_init` at the intended reset boundary: it clears every named byte, including input fields. Set the starting values afterwards, then call `camera_deadzone_step` for each intended service.

Initialise, establish valid world/view limits and update PLAYER in world coordinates. Service after movement, then pass CAMERA to the renderer’s coordinate conversion or scrolling backend. The normal 40–80 dead zone moves the camera from zero to ten for player 90, then to twenty for player 100.

CAMERA and PLAYER are unsigned world coordinates. LEFT_EDGE/RIGHT_EDGE are permitted screen-relative player thresholds, with LEFT_EDGE ≤ RIGHT_EDGE. SCROLL_LIMIT is the largest camera coordinate; the current CAMERA must already be within 0–SCROLL_LIMIT. Invalid bounds leave CAMERA unchanged and clear SHIFTED/VALID. Otherwise step sets VALID, moves just enough to put the player on the crossed threshold, clamps to 0–SCROLL_LIMIT and sets SHIFTED exactly when CAMERA changes. Player and bounds survive.

## State, registers and ownership

Seven bytes. CAMERA persists; PLAYER and the three bounds are inputs; SHIFTED and VALID are replaced flags. PLAYER − CAMERA is computed without byte-wrap mistakes, including when the player lies to the left of the camera.

A/X/flags may change; Y and decimal mode survive. Clear decimal mode before binary arithmetic; the caller uses CLD. A direct call needs two return-address stack bytes.

The implementation binds fixed symbols and has one mutation owner. Independent users need separate state blocks and scoped/renamed labels, including routine labels; sharing the same bindings means sharing the state. Do not let an interrupt and the main loop mutate the same block without an explicit ownership policy.

The standalone cartridge owns its background/font and test RAM layout. Its bounded packet, preview and completion marker fit below the end of the NES’s 2 KiB internal RAM. The helper itself owns no PPU, DMA or audio registers.

## A caller you can adapt

The buildable program supplies bindings and include order. Its separate client retains state between calls. `checked_step` calls the public `camera_deadzone_step` entry and checks register sentinels; `emit_state` records diagnostics. Your game can call the public entry directly, remove the diagnostics and consume the documented outputs.

## Make it your own

## Common mistake and repair

The wrong client subtracts CAMERA from PLAYER before calling the world-coordinate API. The second request supplies screen position 90 in place of world position 100, leaving the camera at ten. Keep world and screen coordinates separate and subtract the camera only in the rendering path.

## Cost, limits and verification

The measured layout has **90 code bytes, 0 immutable data bytes and 7 private state bytes**. Alignment, stack usage, diagnostic tables and display machinery are additional. The extents come from the loaded program’s symbols, not from counting source lines.

A relative-position comparison and one clamped correction, with no map copy or display transfer. C64/Amiga hardware capabilities are not reduced to this logical byte coordinate; their renderers may need wider and differently split scroll values.

The byte world is a bounded example, not a universal camera range. Wider maps need wider coordinates; smoothing adds fractional state and a time-step policy. Hardware coarse/fine scrolling, exposed map columns and parallax remain separate display-specific work. If bounds shrink, first move CAMERA into the new valid interval.

Four native runs use NTSC mapper-0 NES cartridge, CHR-ROM diagnostic font, NMI/DMC disabled; Asm198x 0.0.72 and Emu198x 0.44.2. Each checks 64 boundary/varied/error cases, dirty initialisation and every state of a separate sequential client against an independent integer model. This is not exhaustive coverage of every possible byte combination. Source/tool/firmware hashes, immutable loaded code/data, native startup/completion, preserved-register sentinels, helper/data sizes, screenshot bytes/pixels and exact repair are checked. Deliberately altered observations must fail. Firmware is hashed, never distributed.
