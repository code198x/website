Press Space: the counter advances. Wait: nothing happens. Press T, though, and waiting becomes a decision. After 240 firmware ticks, about 4.8 seconds in the checked setup, the game takes a turn for you. Space takes your turn sooner and starts that deadline again. Another T gives you your thinking time back.

The game rule still knows nothing about clocks. A small caller decides whether this turn needs a deadline, checks for expiry, and keeps the keyboard working while you think.

## Run it, then take the pieces you need

Build the [maintained source](https://github.com/code198x/code-samples/tree/823df0b65fd64a3a4364adc7d16de8a10695925f/commodore-pet/patterns/assembly/framework/turn-deadline):

```sh
make -C commodore-pet/patterns/assembly/framework/turn-deadline ASM198X=/path/to/asm198x
```

The checked assembler is **Asm198x 0.0.72**. Load **build/demo.prg** as a BASIC program and RUN; its SYS line enters the caller. Reset leaves the demo. The named setup is a **32 KiB, 40-column upgrade-ROM PET**, with the emulator’s default PAL timing and normal firmware interrupt service. The PRG importer enters the stock RUN/SYS path; the observer does not inject game state.

Keep the whole samples checkout: the caller uses relative includes. It shares the twelve-byte [6502 deadline helper](https://github.com/code198x/code-samples/blob/823df0b65fd64a3a4364adc7d16de8a10695925f/cross-platform/patterns/framework/elapsed-deadline/6502.inc) and the BBC/Electron policy dispatcher. The PET keeps its own clock adapter and shares the buffered input sampler. It does not take over the interrupt vector.

## A countdown you can call

<CodeFromFile src="cross-platform/patterns/framework/elapsed-deadline/6502.inc" lang="6502" />

The context at **$7000** owns twelve bytes:

| Offsets | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0–1 | Duration, unsigned little-endian ticks; zero disables |
| 2–3 | Remaining ticks, clamped at zero |
| 4 | Active flag |
| 5 | Expiry event for this poll |
| 6–7 | Elapsed ticks in this poll |
| 8–9 | Previous clock sample |
| 10–11 | Scratch sample |

Set duration, clear decimal mode, sample the clock into **A low, X high**, then call `deadline_init`. It starts disabled. `deadline_start` copies duration into remaining time and enables a nonzero duration. It uses the last sampled baseline, so sample/poll immediately before starting when that instant matters.

Call `sample_clock`, then `deadline_poll`, on every service iteration, even while disabled. Read offset 5 before the next poll clears it. Polling may expire and disable the countdown; the caller decides whether that means a turn, a warning, or something else. The helper changes A/X/P and preserves Y. The adapters and surrounding firmware calls may change A/X/Y/P.

An untimed action stays untimed. This caller restarts an active deadline after Space, and after an automatic expiry. T disables and **discards** remaining time; re-enabling starts fresh. Preserving a paused remainder would be a different caller policy.

## A daily reset is not a binary wrap

<CodeFromFile src="commodore-pet/patterns/assembly/framework/turn-deadline/clock.inc" lang="6502" />

The upgrade-ROM PET stores its clock at **$8D high, $8E middle, $8F low**. Those are different locations from the VIC-20 and C64. The later machines’ RDTIM entry is also not a compatible clock-read API in this PET firmware.

The adapter uses PHP, SEI, three loads and PLP to take a coherent snapshot without letting an IRQ change a byte halfway through the read. It restores the interrupt-enable flag, then normalises the sample. Its output is **A low, X high**; `normalise_clock` accepts raw A low, X middle, Y high. The [PET User Manual’s revision-3 memory map](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1024284/Commodore-Pet.html?page=349) documents the clock locations and daily reset. Original BASIC 1 ROMs need a different map and are outside this entry’s qualification.

Ordinary low-word wrap is easy: previous=65530 and now=5 means eleven ticks. But the KERNAL clock also resets at **5,184,000** jiffies, a nominal day. That is not a multiple of 65536. Simply taking the low word would turn midnight into a large false elapsed interval.

The adapter remembers the previous full sample in eight bytes at **$7030**. If the raw clock moves backwards, it adds the low word of one day to its offset before returning a virtual 16-bit clock. `normalise_clock` is callable separately with a raw A/X/Y sample; the native boundary fixture is a second caller of that same routine.

Clear all eight bytes once before the first sample. Keep samples **less than 65536 jiffies apart**, about 21.9 minutes in this checked configuration. Another part of your program must not write the firmware clock or reset TI$: the adapter would mistake that backward jump for midnight. Reinitialise it and the countdown after an intentional clock change.

This connects directly to [Maths for Games](/craft/maths-for-games/): units, number representation and modular subtraction determine the result. A day wraps at a different modulus from a 16-bit integer. Neither is the same thing as one display field.

Keep normal firmware interrupts running. The sampler does not add clock updates or install an IRQ. Its short atomic read preserves the interrupt-enable flag; the surrounding input/display firmware has its own effects. Long IRQ blocking can lose service ticks. The helper cannot recover ticks which were never counted.

## Keyboard delivery and turn order

<CodeFromFile src="commodore-64/patterns/assembly/framework/turn-deadline/input.inc" lang="6502" />

The caller restores default channels with CLRCHN before using GETIN. Space becomes action bit 0; T becomes toggle bit 1. GETIN is channel-sensitive: a game using CHKIN must restore its keyboard channel or provide another sampler.

These are **delivered characters**, not physical press edges. The firmware buffer and key repeat still exist. Choose a physical-key sampler if holding a key must never repeat an action; this example does not make that promise.

<CodeFromFile src="bbc-micro/patterns/assembly/framework/turn-deadline/dispatch.inc" lang="6502" />

The order is deliberate: poll time, read one character, then dispatch. **Expiry wins** over that iteration's character, including T. It spends one turn and starts one new deadline; missed time does not create a catch-up burst. Otherwise T wins over Space when both action bits are supplied by another sampler. Drawing happens after changes, leaving the service loop free to poll.

## Make it your own

Change **DURATION = 240** to **120** in settings.inc and rebuild. The same caller now gives the player half as many jiffies. That exact change has its own independently executed `custom-duration` record. It changes neither the clock rate nor code size.

The helper can represent durations from 1 to 65535 ticks. That upper limit does not remove the sampling limit. Multiple timers, preserved pauses and catch-up rules are useful extensions, but need separate state and their own qualification.

## When nothing happens at expiry

**Deliberately broken dispatcher:** this version checks for input before consuming the one-poll expiry event.

<CodeFromFile src="bbc-micro/patterns/assembly/framework/turn-deadline/mistakes/input-gated-expiry.inc" lang="6502" />

<Figure kind="capture" fit="column" src="/images/patterns/commodore-pet/firmware-deadline-wrong.png" alt="PET remains at 0001 00 01 after the deadline: one turn, no automatic expiry and a stale active flag." caption="The helper has expired, but the caller missed the event. Its unchanged screen even retains the old active flag." />

The countdown reaches zero and becomes inactive. With no character, though, the dispatcher returns before spending a turn or asking for a redraw. The screen stays `0001 00 01`. That last 01 is stale presentation; native state says the timer is off. A still image alone cannot establish the fault, so the evidence also retains the advancing clock and countdown state.

Use the normal dispatcher above as the fix. The independently run **repaired** program is byte-identical to **working** and shows `0002 01 01` after the same input/wait sequence. Consume expiry every service iteration, whether input arrives or not.

## Costs and what was checked

The normal PRG loads **489 bytes**, including its BASIC stub and padding; the disk file adds a two-byte load address. Inside that total are the **140-byte helper** and **69-byte clock adapter**. The caller owns another **32 bytes**: twelve deadline bytes, two guards, ten caller-state bytes and eight clock-adapter bytes, plus ordinary hardware stack use. The BASIC stub is at $0401, the SYS entry at $0410, and the normal image ends at $05E9. State is at $7000/$7020/$7030, below the 32 KiB RAM limit; the screen is at $8000. BASIC is suspended inside SYS, so reserve those locations against other owners.

Retained runs measured **30 jiffies in 30 fields**. The [released default frame](https://github.com/emu198x/emu198x/blob/2f35ad00f88de688d64ce0e90c6e78e436b261a4/crates/machine-commodore-pet/src/lib.rs#L132) is 20,032 CPU cycles at 1 MHz, about 49.92 fields per second. That makes 240 observed firmware ticks about **4.8 seconds** here. The manual’s nominal 1/60-second wording is not the wall-clock conversion for this named configuration. Count ticks deliberately, and establish the service rate before promising a duration in seconds.

That observation is not a helper-cycle benchmark or a whole-game budget. Rendering, sound and input still have to leave room for service and timely polling.

This PET has seven independent **Emu198x 0.44.2** runs: working, half-duration, wrong dispatcher, repaired, boundaries, wrong equality and wrong midnight correction. The observer checks the whole loaded executable, guarded state and native captures. The retained checker separately decodes the on-screen digits.

A larger native caller exercises twelve helper boundary cases, six raw-clock pairs including midnight, and simultaneous expiry/action/toggle priority. These guest-supplied samples establish arithmetic and dispatch, not an observed 24-hour wait. The altered equality and omitted midnight correction complete their fixtures but fail the independent oracle. Twelve falsified records must also be rejected by the retained checks.
