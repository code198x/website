---
title: "Alan Turing describes the Turing machine"
year: 1936
category: "science"
summary: "Turing's theoretical 'universal machine' establishes the mathematical foundations of computation, defining what it means to compute."
---

In his paper "On Computable Numbers," Alan Turing described an abstract machine that could simulate any algorithmic process. This theoretical construct—now called the Turing machine—became the foundation of computer science.

The concept proved that a single machine could, in principle, compute anything computable, given enough time and memory. Every computer built since is essentially a physical implementation of Turing's idea.
