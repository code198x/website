On October 19, 1987, stock markets around the world crashed. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 508 points, or 22.6%—a record single-day percentage decline that still stands.

Computer-driven trading strategies amplified the crash as automated sell orders triggered more selling. It was an early demonstration of how interconnected, computerised financial systems could cascade.

Despite the crash's severity, markets recovered relatively quickly. The event accelerated discussion of computerised trading's risks and benefits.
