President John F. Kennedy was assassinated in Dallas, Texas, on November 22, 1963. The event was captured on film and broadcast worldwide, creating one of the first shared global media moments.

Kennedy had championed the space program and challenged America to reach the Moon by decade's end. His death galvanised support for that mission, which would be achieved in 1969.

The assassination and its aftermath—including the live televised murder of suspect Lee Harvey Oswald—demonstrated television's power to create immediate, shared experience across vast distances.
