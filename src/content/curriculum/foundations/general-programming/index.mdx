## What this is

The first step, before a machine and before even a language. *General Programming*
teaches the **ideas that go into every program**: that a program is instructions in order,
that a variable remembers, that a loop repeats, that a decision picks a path, and, most
useful of all, how to find out why a program is wrong.

This course writes every idea in plain **pseudocode**: instructions in ordinary words, for
a person to read. No language, no machine, nothing to install. Every real language spells
these ideas its own way, and you will find you already know what its instructions are for
when you pick one up.

## Who it's for

Anyone starting from nothing. If you have never written a line of code, start here. If you
already think in variables and loops you can move straight on, though the debugging thread
running through every unit is worth a look even then.

You do not need a computer to follow this course. You need somewhere to think.

## The shape

- **The basics** — a program is instructions, order matters, and showing things.
- **Memory and input** — variables, and asking the person at the keyboard.
- **Working and choosing** — arithmetic, the counter that grows from its own value, the
  sums that do not come out evenly, decisions that let a program react, keeping a yes or a
  no in a box, and joining questions together.
- **Repeating** — counted loops, the loop-until at the heart of every game, and the loop
  that checks before it starts.
- **Holding many things** — lists, walking along them, and loops inside loops.
- **Structure and the safety net** — naming a job so you can reuse it, telling it what to
  work on, and the one skill that separates getting it done from getting stuck: debugging
  by looking rather than guessing.

By the end you will have built a small guessing game from the ground up, and you will hold
the ideas that every language is only a way of saying. From there you pick one to say them
in: **Meet BASIC**, and the family that follows it. Or, if you are heading down to the
machine, take [**Numbers & Bits**](/foundations/numbers-and-bits) next, the short primer
that turns binary, hex and bit logic from noise into a language, ready for the assembly
that follows.
