One instruction is a program. So is a list of them. Give the computer several
instructions in these examples and it does them in order, from the top.

That order matters as much as the instructions do.

## In order, from the top

Here are three instructions:

```text
SHOW "Put your socks on"
SHOW "Put your shoes on"
SHOW "Go outside"
```

Run it, and the three lines appear in the order you wrote them:

<Output>{`Put your socks on
Put your shoes on
Go outside`}</Output>

The computer started at the top and worked down, one instruction at a time.
These lines display advice; they do not dress anyone or move them outside.

## The order is the program

Now swap the first two lines around:

```text
SHOW "Put your shoes on"
SHOW "Put your socks on"
SHOW "Go outside"
```

<Output>{`Put your shoes on
Put your socks on
Go outside`}</Output>

Those are the same three instructions. Only their order changed. The displayed
advice now puts shoes before socks.

Each SHOW is valid in our notation, so this sequence has no instruction error
to report. It still gives advice in an order we did not intend. We must compare
the output with the purpose of the program.

Real programs go wrong the same way. Add up the score before you add the points, or draw
the player before moving them when we wanted the new position, and the result
can differ from our intention.

<Question prompt="If we swap the first two SHOW lines, does Go outside move to the first output line?">

No. The third instruction stays third. The first two output lines swap, and
Go outside remains last. All three outputs are text, not physical actions.

</Question>

## When it's wrong, see why

- **The lines come out in an order you did not expect.** Read your program from the top.
  The order you read is the order it runs.
- **A line never appears.** Check that you ran the intended version and reached
  that line. A run may have stopped, or earlier output may have scrolled away.
  Compare the first unexpected observation rather than assuming the line vanished.

## What you've learnt

- These examples are **sequences**, followed one instruction at a time.
- The **order is part of the program**. The same instructions in a different order make a
  different program, and often a broken one.
- Valid instructions can still produce an unintended order; check their result.
