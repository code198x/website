A computer can work out `2 + 2` and show `4`. It can also work from values it
did not have when you wrote the program.

Here we use pseudocode: notation for explaining a program. `ASK` stores an answer in
a named variable, and `SHOW` displays a result. Type numbers for these examples.

## A sum from numbers you type

```text
ASK "First number? " INTO a
ASK "Second number? " INTO b
SHOW "The total is ", a + b
```

Type `7`, then `5`:

<Output>{`First number? 7
Second number? 5
The total is 12`}</Output>

The program does not have to know the answer in advance. The numbers come from
whoever is typing. `a + b` is an instruction to work the total out from
whatever the two boxes hold at the moment that line runs.

With inputs `7` and `5`, the line gives `12`; with `100` and `9`, it gives `109`. Arithmetic is `+`, `-`, `*` for
multiply and `/` for divide.

## Why multiply is a star

On paper you may write `×` and `÷`. In this notation, `*` means multiply and `/`
means divide. Many programming languages use those characters too: they are keys
you can type on an ordinary computer keyboard. A system lesson explains the
symbols its language uses.

## Some sums happen before others

What does this come to?

```text
SHOW 2 + 3 * 4
```

<Output>{`14`}</Output>

Not 20. The computer did not work along the line from left to right. It did the
multiplication first and the addition afterwards, following the same rule you were taught
for sums on paper: multiply and divide go before add and subtract.

You cannot change that rule. What you can do is say exactly what you want, by putting
brackets round the part that should happen first:

```text
SHOW (2 + 3) * 4
```

<Output>{`20`}</Output>

Brackets mean **work this out first**. Use them even when the rule is already on your side.
You will read this line again in a month, and it should not need you to remember anything.

## When the operators have equal priority

Multiplication and division have equal priority here. Work them from left to right:

```text
SHOW 20 / 5 * 2
SHOW 20 / (5 * 2)
```

<Output>{`8
2`}</Output>

The first line divides 20 by 5, then multiplies 4 by 2. The second divides 20 by 10.
Addition and subtraction also share a priority and work from left to right.
Brackets let you choose a different grouping.

<details>
<summary>What will 20 - 5 - 2 show? Where would brackets make it 17?</summary>

It shows **13**: first 20 - 5 is 15, then 15 - 2 is 13.
`20 - (5 - 2)` shows **17**, because the bracketed part gives 3 first.

</details>

## When it's wrong, see why

- **You get a wrong total.** Inspect both input values and the expression. Was a value
  missing, entered wrongly or overwritten before the sum? Set both inputs before using them.
- **The answer is the two numbers stuck together**, like `75` instead of `12`. The computer
  joined them as text instead of adding them as numbers. Check how your target language reads numbers and joins text. Here `+` adds
  numbers; commas in `SHOW` place values side by side.
- **The answer is right but not the one you meant.** Check the order. `2 + 3 * 4` is 14, and
  if you wanted 20 the brackets were missing.
- **Someone typed something that is not a number.** `hello + 5` is not a sum anyone can do. A
  finished program checks the answer before trusting it; for now, type numbers.

## What you've learnt

- The computer does **arithmetic**, working a new value out from ones it already has.
- It can work from values the **player gives**, so the same program gives different answers.
- `*` means multiply and `/` means divide in this notation.
- **Multiply and divide happen before add and subtract**, and **brackets** say what to work
  out first. Equal-priority operations work from left to right here.
