## What this is

A truth table lists the output for each combination of 0 and 1 inputs. A gate
implements a rule such as AND, OR or XOR. Join those rules and we can describe
something that adds binary numbers.

By the end, you can trace a one-bit adder and follow its carry through three
columns. The interactive drawings use an ideal Boolean model; understanding
that model is a useful step towards understanding physical hardware.

## Who it's for

Anyone who has ever wondered what a processor *is*.

Not what it does, which you can read anywhere. What it is made of, at the bottom, where the
explanations usually stop and say "and then it adds the numbers".

## What you need first

You will use 0/1 values, small sums and binary place values: 1, 2, 4, 8. We recall
the rules as they appear. [Truth Tables](/foundations/truth-tables/) and
[Counting in Twos](/foundations/counting-in-twos/) offer more practice; prior
attendance is not required.

This gates nothing in turn. Nothing later in the curriculum requires it. It is here because
it is one of the most satisfying things a beginner can learn, and because after it the word
"hardware" stops sounding like a different subject.

## The shape

- **The same table, drawn** — a gate's Boolean rule, drawn with wires. Set the inputs and watch
  the output follow.
- **The half adder** — add two bits and you need two answers, a sum and a carry. One is
  `XOR` and the other is `AND`. That is the entire circuit.
- **The full adder and the ripple** — accept a carry as well as produce one, then chain them
  together and add whole numbers.

## The model we use

We treat inputs and outputs as settled 0 or 1 values. These drawings do not
measure voltages, power or the time an output takes to settle. A physical part
needs electrical and timing specifications as well as a Boolean table.

BASIC and assembly remain independent entry points. Use this module when you
want to look inside an arithmetic operation; completing it is not a condition
for starting a system track.
