The counted loop is for when you know how many times. Often you do not. You keep going
**until something happens**: until the player guesses right, runs out of lives, or gives
up. You cannot count that in advance.

Our guessing examples assume ASK supplies a valid number. Checking typed text or
rejecting an invalid answer is extra behaviour, separate from repeating the work.

## One guess, then stop

Here is the guessing game from *Decisions*:

```text
ASK "Guess my number? " INTO guess
IF guess < 7 THEN
  SHOW "Too low"
ELSE IF guess > 7 THEN
  SHOW "Too high"
ELSE
  SHOW "Correct!"
END
```

<Output>{`Guess my number? 4
Too low`}</Output>

It tells you the guess was low, and then it is over. To guess again you run the whole
thing again. We want several guesses within one round.

## Repeat until you get it

```text
REPEAT
  ASK "Guess my number? " INTO guess
  IF guess < 7 THEN
    SHOW "Too low"
  ELSE IF guess > 7 THEN
    SHOW "Too high"
  END
UNTIL guess = 7
SHOW "Correct! Well done."
```

Everything between `REPEAT` and `UNTIL` happens over and over. At the bottom the computer
asks its question. If the answer is no, it goes back to the top and runs the whole block
again. Only when the answer is yes does it carry on to the line below.

Play it. Guess `4`, then `9`, then `7`:

<Output>{`Guess my number? 4
Too low
Guess my number? 9
Too high
Guess my number? 7
Correct! Well done.`}</Output>

This is a useful **game-loop model**: do some work, check whether it is over,
and repeat if it is not. Real games can organise repeated work differently; this
small loop does not prescribe one structure for every game.

## An exit we can reach is not a promise

The winning case is reachable: submitting 7 makes the condition true. That
does not guarantee the player will submit it. Valid wrong guesses can keep the
loop going. If the player has not submitted the next answer, ASK can wait
without reaching the condition at all. Waiting for input differs from repeatedly
executing a body that makes no progress.

<Question prompt="The player submits 4, then 4 again. Does a reachable winning case guarantee this guessing loop will finish?">

No. Both guesses are wrong, so neither makes guess = 7 true. The program asks
again and may wait for another submission. If the player later submits 7, it
finishes normally. A few successful trials do not show that every possible
input sequence finishes; the rule depends on the answers supplied.

</Question>

## A secret worth keeping

The answer is always seven. Across rounds, you already know what to guess.
Choosing a secret for each round gives us another answer to search for.

`RANDOM` chooses a number from a range:

```text
LET secret = RANDOM 1 TO 10
REPEAT
  ASK "Guess my number? " INTO guess
  IF guess < secret THEN
    SHOW "Too low"
  ELSE IF guess > secret THEN
    SHOW "Too high"
  END
UNTIL guess = secret
SHOW "Correct! Well done."
```

`RANDOM 1 TO 10` picks a whole number from 1 to 10, and both ends can come up. It uses the
same range that `FOR` does, so there is nothing new to read.

Notice the tests changed too. They ask about `secret` now rather than about 7, so the
program works for any secret in the range. A new round makes a fresh choice, but that
choice may be the same number as before. Random does not mean different every time.

The assignment to `secret` comes **before** `REPEAT`, so it happens once when this
round starts. Wrong guesses go back to `ASK`, not to the assignment. The secret
stays the same while the player works towards it.

<details>
<summary>If the last round chose 7, can the next round choose 7 too?</summary>

Yes. Both choices are in the range. Seeing the same answer twice does not by itself
show that the program is broken. Choosing a new secret after every wrong guess
would be a different rule: the clues would no longer lead towards one fixed answer.

</details>

## When it's wrong, see why

- **It has not finished.** First check whether it is waiting at ASK or handling
  more wrong guesses. Verify that a submitted winning guess reaches the exit.
  A reachable exit alone does not guarantee the input needed to take it.
- **It stops after one go.** Something outside the loop ended the program, or the work you
  meant to repeat sits below the `UNTIL` rather than above it.
- **It leaves at the wrong moment.** Read the question out loud. "Until the guess is
  seven" leaves exactly when the guess is seven, which is what you want. Turn it around
  and the loop either never runs twice or never stops.

## What you've learnt

- A **loop-until** repeats until something becomes true, for when you do not know how many
  times in advance.
- The question sits at the **bottom**, so the work always happens at least once.
- This do-it, check, repeat shape models one kind of repeated game work.
- For a loop intended to finish, ask what makes it leave and whether that event
  is guaranteed, input-dependent or prevented by a bug.
