## What this is

A briefing, not a course. Before you write anything for the Spectrum — in BASIC, in assembly, in anything — it helps to know what the machine was and what it couldn't do. That's all this is.

You won't write a line of code here. You'll meet a machine that had sixteen colours but could only show two in any eight-by-eight square, a speaker that could be on or off and nothing in between, and 48K to hold your program, your data and the screen at once.

## Why the limits come first

Every one of those limits shows up later as a decision someone had to make. The reason Spectrum games have that particular look — the colour bleeding at the edges of a sprite — isn't taste. It's the attribute file. The reason the music sounds the way it does isn't ambition. It's one bit.

Meet those constraints now and the rest of the course stops being a list of rules. It becomes a set of consequences.

## Who it's for

Anyone heading for the Spectrum, whichever language you're going to use. This briefing feeds [Meet BASIC](/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic) and the assembly track alike — the hardware is the same either way, so it's taught once.

New to programming entirely? Start with [General Programming](/foundations/general-programming). Nothing here assumes it, but everything after does.
