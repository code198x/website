SPACE no longer advances traffic. Leave the controls alone: the lane keeps moving. Hold J or L and the person steps repeatedly until the boundary. Contact is still unenforced, so you can inspect movement without losing.

## A clock outside the loop

PEEK reads a byte from memory. Address 23672 is the lowest byte of the Spectrum's FRAMES counter. On our PAL target, the counter advances every 20 milliseconds. We read it without changing it.

At initialisation, tick remembers the current byte. Line 280 subtracts tick from the current reading. Because this byte cycles through 0..255, a negative difference needs 256 added back. For example, a start value of 250 followed by 10 gives −240; adding 256 gives sixteen elapsed frames.

Line 290 waits until elapsed reaches 32. R and Q are still checked during that wait. At the update boundary, the program reads the movement key and resets dx/dy. Line 300 records the next starting time **before** doing the update's drawing and traffic work.

## One clock, two rates

The player gets one movement opportunity per update. Traffic uses a separate countdown t, initially v=2. Each update reduces t by one. A positive value skips traffic movement; zero reloads t from v and advances the lane.

| Update | t before decrement | Result |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 2 | t becomes 1; traffic stays put |
| 2 | 1 | t becomes 0; reload 2 and move |
| 3 | 2 | t becomes 1; traffic stays put |

steps counts completed updates, not seconds. The v value counts updates too. Neither measures how long the BASIC work takes.

<Question prompt="Does v=2 guarantee that traffic moves once every 64 video frames?">

No. Thirty-two frames is the target spacing between update starts. If an update's work takes longer, the next opportunity arrives later. The lane moves every second update, whatever those updates cost.

</Question>

## Work can take longer than the target

We do not add a fresh 32-frame pause after drawing. Time spent updating already counts towards the next start. Nor do we run several catch-up moves after a slow update; the next update records a new tick and does one piece of work.

The low-byte subtraction assumes the loop returns before a whole 256-frame cycle passes. It is suitable for this bounded game loop, not a timer for arbitrarily long pauses. Shorter teaching programs and the eventual six-lane game need not feel equally fast.

Try a very short tap while a vehicle row is being redrawn. It may have ended before line 291 asks for a key. Holding a direction works because it remains present at that sample. We will address recognised short presses later; do not repair this stage by making it wait for key release again, because that would also stop traffic while waiting.

If nothing moves, check that lines 210 and 480 were deleted. If SPACE still controls traffic, remove 240. If the speed alternates unexpectedly, inspect the decrement, reload and skip at 350–370 before changing the frame target.
