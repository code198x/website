I/J/K/L still move the person. Press and release SPACE to move traffic left. Vehicles and player may overlap at this stage: we have not written a contact rule.

## One phase describes three vehicles

p is the starting cell of one vehicle. The other two begin five and ten cells further around a fifteen-cell ring. Each vehicle occupies two cells, leaving three-cell gaps.

Initially p=10. The starts are 10, 0 and 5 after wrapping. Direction d=−1 moves the phase left; a phase of −1 becomes 14. The rightward branch is also present: when d=1, phase 15 becomes 0. Neither edge is a wall for traffic.

The vehicle artwork occupies four character columns and two rows. Its eight DATA lines fill the eight UDG slots reserved in the first lesson. The two-row person still uses the following four slots, so changing the loader to start at 7200 keeps all the character codes consistent.

## Build two rows of picture

`DIM a$(30)` and `DIM b$(30)` create fixed-length strings filled with spaces. a$ will draw the vehicle tops; b$ draws their bottoms. Their thirty character positions cover fifteen logical cells.

The nested loops visit three vehicles, j=0..2, and four character columns per vehicle, k=0..3. The starting character offset is `2*p+10*j+k`. The factor two converts cells to character columns; the ten separates successive vehicle starts by five cells.

Line 150 wraps that offset into 0..29, then adds one because string positions begin at 1. With p=14, the first vehicle's four columns land at positions 29, 30, 1 and 2. Its two halves appear at opposite edges, but it is still one wrapping vehicle.

## Rotate the picture with the phase

A string slice such as `a$(3 TO 30)` includes both end positions. Moving left joins positions 3–30 to positions 1–2. Moving right joins positions 29–30 to positions 1–28. In either case, the row stays thirty characters long and moves by one two-character cell.

The phase changes first, then both strings rotate in the same direction. Numeric position and drawing must agree. Printing a row does not tell the program where its vehicles are; p still owns that fact.

<Question prompt="Why does rotating by one character produce the wrong movement for this board?">

It moves only half a logical cell. The picture would shift eight pixels while the phase says it moved sixteen. The later collision rule would then disagree with what the player sees.

</Question>

## Keep the player visible

Printing a lane can cover the person. Line 420 restores the player afterwards when y=1. Moving off the lane calls its drawing routine to restore whatever traffic was underneath; restoring plain spaces would leave holes in vehicles.

Advance fifteen times. The phase and picture return to their starting positions. Move onto a vehicle, advance once, then move away. Its artwork should be restored. If a vehicle gains a blank half, check both row strings and the restoration at 3100. If SPACE keeps advancing while held, check that the release gate is still present.
