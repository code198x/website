## Give each lane its own state

A scalar such as p stores one value. `DIM p(6)` creates six indexed entries. p(1) is the first lane's phase; p(6) is the last lane's phase. We do the same for direction d, countdown t, period v and logical row r.

The string arrays have a different final dimension: `DIM a$(6,30)` provides six strings of thirty characters. a$(i) is lane i's whole upper row, while a$(i,c) addresses one character. b$ holds the lower rows. The [Foundations collection lesson](/foundations/structure/unit-01/) explores indexed storage further; here each shared index identifies one lane across all seven arrays.

RESTORE 7100 positions the DATA reader at the lane records. For each i, line 120 reads phase, direction, period and row, then copies the period into the countdown. The records are:

| Lane | Row | Phase | Direction | Period |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 0 | Right | 3 |
| 2 | 2 | 9 | Left | 4 |
| 3 | 3 | 11 | Right | 2 |
| 4 | 5 | 7 | Left | 3 |
| 5 | 6 | 1 | Right | 4 |
| 6 | 7 | 10 | Left | 2 |

This table is the level design. The bottom lane retains the practice lane's phase, direction and period, but its row changes from 1 to 7. R re-creates the arrays and reads every record again. Without RESTORE, a later retry would continue reading from the wrong place.

## Update only what is due

The new outer loop visits i=1..6. It reduces t(i), skips movement when that value is positive, and reloads only that lane's countdown when it reaches zero. Phase changes and string rotations use the same i.

At the opening update no lane moves: all countdowns exceed one. On update two, lanes 3 and 6 move. On update three, lanes 1 and 4 move. The shared clock does not force every lane to advance together.

Drawing uses `row=2+2*r(i)`. The arrow and colour follow direction, while the dotted strips mark places to wait. After printing a due lane, line 420 restores the person immediately if that is the player's row. A player waiting on safe ground need not be erased while other lanes redraw.

## Find the lane beneath a cell

The contact query rejects rows 0, 4 and 8 before touching an array. In rows 1–3, lane=ny. In rows 5–7, lane=ny−1. The missing index is not a missing lane: logical row 4 belongs to the resting strip.

<Question prompt="A candidate move reaches logical row 6. Which phase entry must the contact query read, and why not p(6)?">

It reads p(5), because rows 1,2,3,5,6,7 belong to lanes 1,2,3,4,5,6. The central safe row shifts the mapping for the lower bank. p(6) describes row 7 instead.
