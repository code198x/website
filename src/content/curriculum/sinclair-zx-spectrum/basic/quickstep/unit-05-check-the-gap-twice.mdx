Reach the centre exit without touching traffic. R retries with the same starting pattern; Q quits. You can fail by stepping into a vehicle or by letting a vehicle reach you while you wait.

## Ask about cells, not colours

The contact routine receives nx/ny and returns hit. Rows other than 1 are safe on this practice board, so it returns immediately there. In the lane it compares nx with the numeric phase p.

Our pattern repeats every five cells: two occupied cells followed by three empty ones. Begin with `delta=nx-p`. Line 2015 replaces delta with `delta-5*INT(delta/5)`, leaving a remainder from 0 to 4. Values 0 and 1 are vehicle cells; 2, 3 and 4 are gaps.

INT rounds down, including for negative numbers. With p=14 and nx=0, the difference is −14; INT(−14/5) is −3, so the remainder is −14−5*(−3)=1. Cell 0 is the second cell of the wrapping vehicle, exactly as the drawing shows.

This compact test depends on our regular five-cell pattern. It is not a general test for vehicles of arbitrary widths or spacing. The full two-cell rectangle counts, including dark corners inside the artwork.

## The order makes the rule

Each update follows this sequence:

1. Propose nx/ny and reject an out-of-bounds move.
2. Test the destination against traffic's **current** phase.
3. If clear, erase the old player, accept the position and draw it.
4. Advance traffic if its countdown is due, restoring the player overlay afterwards.
5. Test the player's position against traffic's **new** phase.
6. If still safe, test for the centre exit.

The first test prevents entering a vehicle that is about to leave. The second catches a vehicle arriving after a legal player step, including when that step was to stay still.

<Question prompt="Traffic is due to move left. The player is at x=11 in the lane, and a vehicle occupies x=9 and x=10. May the player move left into x=10 because that cell will become empty?">

No. The first test finds x=10 occupied and rejects the move. After traffic moves to cells 8 and 9, a final-position-only test would find x=10 clear. Our rule requires the destination to be clear before traffic advances as well as afterwards.

## Freeze the answer

A failed first check displays “That gap was not clear.” A failed second check displays “The lane caught you.” Reaching x=7, y=0 displays the success message. Other cells on the top strip are safe but do not win.

All three results jump to 4000, away from the update loop. The world stops there. Line 4020 waits for release before the R/Q result controls, so the key held during arrival does not immediately choose another action.

The erase routine can now restore blanks in the lane because a continuing player cannot be standing on an occupied cell. It no longer needs the inspection stage's special handling for leaving the exit: a successful arrival ends the crossing before another movement.
