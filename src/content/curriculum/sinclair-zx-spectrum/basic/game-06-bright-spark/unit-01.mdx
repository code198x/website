Before the game can flash panels, the panels need to exist. Four large coloured rectangles, filling the screen.

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-06-bright-spark/unit-01/panels.bas" />

Each panel is drawn by setting `PAPER` to a colour, then printing rows of spaces with `PRINT AT`. Spaces have no visible character, but they carry the paper colour — so a row of 14 spaces in `PAPER 2` creates a solid red stripe. Eight rows of stripes make a solid red rectangle.

Four panels: red (top-left), blue (top-right), green (bottom-left), yellow (bottom-right). Each fills roughly a quarter of the play area. The last line labels them 1 through 4 — the keys the player will press.

## Colour as identity

Each panel is immediately recognisable by colour alone, from across the room. That distinctness is essential — the game works by recognition, not by reading labels. Red is red. Blue is blue. If the player hesitates to identify a colour, the game breaks.

Try swapping colours. Red and magenta are too similar — they would cause confusion. The four chosen colours (red, blue, green, yellow) are the most distinct set the Spectrum offers.

## PRINT AT for rectangles

This is a new use of `PRINT AT`: not for displaying text at a position, but for *filling area with colour*. The character printed is a space — invisible — but the paper colour behind it is solid. This technique creates rectangles, borders, status bars, and backgrounds throughout BASIC games on the Spectrum.
