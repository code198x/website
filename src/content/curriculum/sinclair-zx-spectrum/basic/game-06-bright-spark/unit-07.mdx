Line 320 changes from printing "Wrong!" and stopping to `GO TO 380`. Lines 380–440 are the game over screen: a low buzz (`BEEP 0.5, -10`), a `CLS` to clear the board, the game title, and the score. Line 450 then stops the program — the same wall you built in unit 2, so execution does not run on into the drawing subroutine at line 540.

The score is `LEN s$ - 1`. Why minus one? Because line 150 already added the digit the player failed on — the string includes the round that was not completed. If the player matched five panels correctly and missed the sixth, `s$` is six characters long but the score is five.

## Endings as rewards

A game over screen is not just an ending — it is the player's reward. The score tells them how they did. "The sequence was 7 long" is an invitation to try again and beat 7. Without a score, the player has nothing to measure against.

Line 380 plays a low-pitched buzz — the opposite of the bright, high panel tones. Sound tells the player the game is over before they read the text. Line 400 clears the board so the score gets the screen to itself, just as `CLS` gave the story its own screen in Story Builder.
