## The game-over sequence

Line 300: `BEEP 0.5, -10` — a low, harsh buzz. Unmistakable failure, but brief.

Line 310: `LET score = LEN s$ - 1` — the score is the sequence length *minus one*, because the player failed on the current round. If the sequence was "2143" (length 4) and the player got the fourth one wrong, they successfully completed a sequence of 3.

Lines 320–410 clear the screen and display the result: "GAME OVER", the sequence length, and a colour-coded rating.

## Ratings

| Score | Rating | Colour |
|-------|--------|--------|
| 10+ | Amazing! | Green |
| 6–9 | Good memory! | Cyan |
| 3–5 | Not bad | Yellow |
| Under 3 | Keep practising | Red |

The thresholds are calibrated to human short-term memory. Psychologists have measured it at roughly 7 items — the "magical number seven." A score of 7 is genuinely good; 10 is exceptional. The ratings reflect this without naming the science.

## Replay

Lines 420–440: "Press any key to play again" + `PAUSE 0` + `GO TO 10`. The game restarts from the title. The player does not need to type `RUN` — the replay is built into the program. This small convenience is the difference between "run it again if you want" and "play again."

## Is the game-over moment respectful?

"The sequence was 7 long" acknowledges achievement. "You failed" does not. "GAME OVER" is neutral — it is the universal signal for "this round is done." The tone is set by the words that follow: "Good memory!" says "you did well." The player is more likely to play again if the ending respects their effort.
