## Catching a two-pixel ball

A candidate ball spans **nx to nx+1**. The paddle spans **8*p to 8*p+31**. They are separated if the ball's rightmost pixel is left of the paddle, or the ball's leftmost pixel is right of it.

Those are the two failure tests in line 400. OR means either separation is enough for a miss. Equality is a catch: one shared pixel counts.

With the paddle at p=14, its span is 112–143:

| Candidate ball x | Ball span | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 110 | 110–111 | Miss: no shared pixel |
| 111 | 111–112 | Catch at the left edge |
| 143 | 143–144 | Catch at the right edge |
| 144 | 144–145 | Miss: no shared pixel |

These are coordinate examples. The moving program reaches only some integer positions with its four-pixel steps, but the overlap rule is defined for all of them.

## Turn down into up

Line 390 skips the paddle test while ny is at least 32. Once ny is below 32, a catch reflects that candidate around y=32: `ny=64-ny`. For example, ny=28 becomes 36. Then dy becomes 4, sending the ball upwards.

We are using a catching plane just above the artwork, not asking which coloured pixels are underneath the ball. This keeps the rule independent of the rounded corners and the character-cell colour attributes.

Horizontal direction is unchanged for now. If the ball arrived moving right, it leaves moving right. Later we will use the contact position to aim.

## A miss leaves a valid picture

A failed overlap sets the result message and jumps away before erasing or committing the proposed ball. x and y still describe the last safe position. Retry returns to the same initialisation used at the start: centred paddle, waiting ball and upward-right direction.

<Question prompt="Would checking only nx against the paddle miss a legal catch at its left edge?">

Yes. At nx=111, the ball's left pixel is outside a paddle beginning at 112, but its right pixel is 112. The ball overlaps by one pixel, so nx+1 must take part in the test.

</Question>

RUN and keep the ball in play for several returns. Then deliberately move away and miss. Hold a direction while the result appears, release it, and press R. Check that SPACE is needed to serve again; Q should leave from both ready and result screens.

If a catch passes through, inspect both span comparisons and the jump in line 390. If the ball travels downwards after a catch, check the positive dy in line 410. Changing a paddle's colour should not change any of these decisions.
