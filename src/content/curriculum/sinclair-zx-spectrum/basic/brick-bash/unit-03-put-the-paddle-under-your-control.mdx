## Draw a rounded paddle

The paddle contains four characters: a left end, two copies of the middle and a right end. Its first four pixel rows are visible; its other four are blank. For the left end, the row bytes are 63,127,127,63,0,0,0,0:

```text
..######
.#######
.#######
..######
........
........
........
........
```

Across a row, the bit values are 128,64,32,16,8,4,2,1. The first row is 32+16+8+4+2+1=63. The middle uses four rows of 255; the right end mirrors the left with 252,254,254,252.

`USR "a"` gives the address of the first user-defined graphic. Each character needs eight consecutive bytes. Offsets 24–47 therefore hold characters d, e and f, codes 147–149. We leave a, b and c free for bricks later. This string form of USR locates graphic storage; it is not a machine-code call.

RESTORE 7230 positions the DATA reader. The loop READs each row byte and POKEs it into that storage. `CHR$` turns a character code into a one-character string. Line 7020 joins four of them into p$, ready for PRINT.

## Columns become pixels

The paddle begins at character column p. Its pixel left edge is 8*p; four characters make it 32 pixels wide. At p=14 it covers x=112–143. The attached ball starts at 8*p+15=127, so its two columns occupy the middle of the paddle's width.

The paddle is printed at row 18. That character row spans pixel y=24–31; our four inked rows occupy its upper half. The ball waits above it at y=40.

O subtracts one from p; P adds one. Lines 260–270 clamp p to 2–26. That keeps the paddle's full width between the side walls. We remember oldp so a change can erase the old four-character string and draw the new one. If p did not change, there is nothing to redraw.

## Waiting and flying

`served=0` means ready. In that state, moving the paddle toggles the old ball off, recalculates its x and draws it again. SPACE sets served to 1 and enters the movement part of the loop. Once served, line 290 bypasses the attachment code, so the paddle no longer drags the ball along.

The paddle does not catch anything yet. Serving still ends at the bottom stop from the previous lesson.

<Question prompt="Why does one paddle step change the attached ball's x by eight rather than one?">

p counts character columns. Each column is eight pixels wide, so increasing p by one increases 8*p+15 by eight.

</Question>

Hold O and P to reach both limits. Serve, then move the paddle while the ball flies. Try SPACE again: it should not restart the flight. R restores the centred paddle and waiting ball.

If the paddle is letters or scrambled blocks, check the three DATA lines and the offset 24 in the loading loop. If the attached ball leaves a trail, check that line 320 erases before changing x.
