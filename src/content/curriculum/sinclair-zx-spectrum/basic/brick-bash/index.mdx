Eighteen bricks. One ball. Catching it is only half the job: where it meets the paddle decides where it goes next.

Use **O/P** to move left and right and **SPACE** to serve. Catch near the paddle's left end for a leftward return, its centre for a vertical return, or its right end for a rightward return. Clear all eighteen bricks to finish. A miss ends the attempt; **R** restores the wall and **Q** quits. **S** starts from the title.

This game runs in Sinclair BASIC on a stock **48K ZX Spectrum**. It is deliberately silent. Three coloured rows help us read the wall, while disappearing bricks and the BRICKS count show progress without relying on colour alone.

## Pictures, positions and memory

The ball moves in pixels; the paddle and bricks are drawn in character cells. We connect those coordinate systems, check rectangular overlap and keep a separate array of live bricks. Screen colour never decides a collision.

We start with a new short program and explain the BASIC needed as it grows. A keep-up game comes before a one-brick challenge. Then a temporary wall inspector lets us enter positions and see which brick or gap they identify. Reconnecting that query to the moving ball gives us the full wall; aiming adds a choice to every catch.

The final lessons finish the instructions, retry and exit cycle, then save a tape that reconstructs the game in a fresh session. There is no earlier game's source to import and no machine-code helper to install.
