What changes when you roll a die twelve times instead of twelve hundred? Dice Roller lets you watch counts and percentages develop, then compare one sample with the next. The bars stay on the same scale, so a larger count does not automatically look like a larger share.

Choose **1–4** for batches of 12, 60, 300 or 1,200. At a result, **R** starts fresh, **A** adds another batch and **N** chooses a size. **Q** stops rolling early and retains the counts; at a result or menu it quits. The previous sample stays beside the live chart.

This is an optional probability experiment in **Sinclair BASIC on a stock 48K ZX Spectrum**, with deliberate silence. There is no score or winning distribution. A finite sample does not prove a generator fair, and a larger sample need not look more balanced every time.

## From rolls to comparisons

Five lessons build five runnable programs: numbered rolls, six tallies, percentages, a live chart, then batch controls and previous-sample comparison. The final lesson saves the finished program and checks a fresh tape load.

Counts remain exact. Percentages round to one decimal place; twenty-cell bars round to the nearest five percentage points. Small batches refresh every roll. Larger batches refresh every ten rolls, plus the opening roll and the exact result. Added samples can grow to 9,600 rolls.

Variables, loops, arrays, string slices and display routines are explained where they are used. [Meet BASIC](/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/) supplies a slower route through entering and saving programs. You do not need to complete another game first.
