Four new lines. Line 90 announces the roll. Line 100 adds spacing. Lines 110 and 250 are the loop: `FOR i = 1 TO 5` starts it, `NEXT i` ends it. Everything between runs five times. Line 190 changed from "You rolled:" to printing each die inline with `;`.

`FOR i = 1 TO 5` starts a counted loop. The variable `i` begins at 1. Every time the program reaches `NEXT i`, it adds 1 to `i` and jumps back to the line after `FOR`. When `i` passes 5, the loop ends.

## FOR/NEXT vs GO TO

`GO TO` loops until a *condition* is met — unknown count. `FOR/NEXT` loops a *specific* number of times — known count. Both are loops; the choice depends on what you need.

You previewed `FOR/NEXT` in Reflex (the telegraph bar), but this is the formal introduction. From here on, both kinds of loop are in your toolkit.

One quirk to know: the Spectrum only allows **single-letter** variable names for `FOR`/`NEXT` and for string variables (`a$` to `z$`). You can use long names like `score` with `LET`, but `FOR score = 1 TO 10` gives a "Nonsense in BASIC" error.

Single-letter names work well in short programs — you can see the whole program on one screen, so `t` for total and `d` for die are clear enough. As programs grow, longer names like `total` or `count` become worth the extra typing. Use the clearest name the Spectrum will accept.
