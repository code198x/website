The lander falls at a constant rate — 1 unit per tick. Real gravity does not work that way. Things fall faster the longer they fall.

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-08-touchdown/unit-02/speed.bas" />

Two new variables: `spd` starts at 0 and increases by 1 each tick (line 40 — gravity). Altitude now drops by `spd`, not by 1 (line 50). The lander falls slowly at first, then faster and faster.

Line 100: `IF alt = 0 AND spd <= 5 THEN PRINT "TOUCHDOWN!"` — if you reach the ground at speed 5 or less, you land. Line 110: if speed is over 5, you crash.

The outcome depends on how fast you are going at the moment of contact. Right now there is no way to slow down — every landing is a crash, because gravity keeps adding speed. The player needs thrust.

## Acceleration

`LET spd = spd + 1` is **acceleration**: velocity changes over time. Each tick, speed increases. After 1 tick, speed is 1. After 5 ticks, speed is 5. After 10, speed is 10. The altitude drops faster and faster because `alt = alt - spd` subtracts a growing number.

This is the simplest physics model: constant acceleration (gravity) producing increasing velocity. It is the same physics behind every platformer's jump arc, every falling object, every ball bouncing in every game.
