Thrust is free — the player can hold space forever and hover safely. That is not a game. Fuel makes it one.

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-08-touchdown/unit-04/fuel.bas" />

Line 40: `LET fuel = 50` — fifty units of fuel.

Line 60: `IF INKEY$ = " " AND fuel > 0 THEN LET spd = spd - 2: LET fuel = fuel - 1`. Each thrust burns one unit. When fuel reaches zero, pressing space does nothing. The player coasts on whatever speed they have — and gravity keeps adding.

The `AND fuel > 0` check is essential. Without it, the player could thrust with empty tanks. The compound condition gates the action: you can only thrust if you *have* fuel to burn.

## Finite resources

Fuel is the game's tension mechanism. With unlimited fuel, the player can always recover. With 50 units, every burn is a decision: slow down now and spend fuel, or wait and hope you can brake later with less?

Try 100 fuel. The game is relaxed — you can hover, experiment, take your time. Try 20. Every burn is critical — one wasted thrust and you crash. The number of fuel units is the difficulty dial.

## The three forces

The game now has three forces:

1. **Gravity** — adds 1 to speed each tick, always, unconditionally.
2. **Thrust** — subtracts 2 from speed, but costs fuel.
3. **Fuel** — depletes with each thrust, eventually to zero.

The player's job is to balance thrust against gravity while fuel lasts. This three-force model — gravity, thrust, fuel — is the Lunar Lander archetype. Every version of the game since 1969 uses it.
