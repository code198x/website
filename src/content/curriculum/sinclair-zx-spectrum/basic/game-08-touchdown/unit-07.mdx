## Altitude in the border

Lines 160–200 map altitude to border colour — the same distance-to-colour pattern from Lucky Number:

| Altitude | Border | Feeling |
|----------|--------|---------|
| Above 70 | Blue (1) | High, safe |
| 41–70 | Cyan (5) | Descending |
| 21–40 | Yellow (6) | Getting close |
| 11–20 | Red (2) | Low, urgent |
| 10 or less | White (7) | Critical |

The player sees the border shift in their peripheral vision. Blue is calm; white is alarm. The colour carries urgency without the player reading a number.

## Low-fuel warning

Line 210: `IF fuel < 10 AND fuel > 0 THEN BEEP 0.02, 30` — a rapid high-pitched tick when fuel drops below 10. The sound says "you are running out" before the number reaches zero.

## Thrust sound

Line 80 adds `BEEP 0.02, 15` when the player thrusts. Each press makes a sound — the player hears their fuel burning.

## Ambient feedback

The embedded concept for Touchdown is **ambient feedback** — information delivered through the environment rather than through explicit UI. The border colour, the warning tick, and the thrust sound all communicate game state without the player looking at the dashboard.

Play the game and cover the numbers with your hand. Can you still tell how high you are? Can you tell when fuel is running out? If the ambient feedback works, the answer is yes — the border and the sounds carry enough information to play by feel alone.

This is the same principle from Lucky Number (border colour as proximity) scaled up to a full game. The difference: in Lucky Number, you had one feedback channel (distance). Here you have three running simultaneously — altitude in the border, fuel in the beeper, thrust in the keypress sound.
