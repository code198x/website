Which letter would help most? Cipher hides a familiar word and reveals every match when you guess a letter. You have seven mistakes; repeating a guess costs nothing. Unused letters stay visible so you can consider the next choice.

<Figure src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/cipher/teaching/board.png" width={352} alt="Cipher shows a partly revealed word, six remaining mistakes, an unused alphabet and Z in its separate miss list." caption="The word, unused alphabet and missed-letter list describe different parts of the same round." />

Press **S** at the title, then guess with letter keys. There is no Enter after a guess. **Enter pauses**: C continues, R resets words and scores, and Q quits. Q and R remain guesses during play. At a result, Space chooses another word, R resets and Q quits.

The finished game chooses from twenty-four everyday words. It avoids the immediately preceding word during a session; earlier words can return. Wins and losses carry between rounds. The game runs in **Sinclair BASIC on a stock 48K ZX Spectrum**, with deliberate silence and no timer.

## A word becomes a game

Six lessons use five runnable programs. Begin with a fixed guess that reveals both Ts in BOTTLE. Read a letter key, remember attempts, count distinct misses and recognise a result. Build the board around those rules, then move the words into DATA and add random selection, a title and replay. The final lesson saves the complete program and checks a fresh load.

The fixed word makes early experiments repeatable. The finished word list introduces uncertainty without changing the guessing rules. Word length alone does not determine difficulty; familiarity and the possible alternatives matter too.

String positions, loops, keyboard input and screen coordinates are explained where they are used. [Meet BASIC](/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/) offers a slower route through entering, editing and saving programs. Other games are optional connections, not prerequisites.
