Restart and press S, E. At the Old camp, the east exit should say Cold draught. Press E again: the expedition ends in the shaft. The room and result remain visible; R starts afresh and Q quits.

## Check before collecting

pit holds the dangerous room number. Line 410 compares it with dest after the action has been validated and counted, but before the normal move and collection statements. If they match, record the destination in rm, select ending 1 and jump to the result routine.

Recording rm lets the final screen show where the expedition ended. Jumping past collection prevents later parts of the turn from running after a fatal move. The result routine redraws the room, replaces the status and controls, then waits for R or Q.

The pit is a stored rule, not a property of the room's wording or screen colour. Renaming the black shaft would not make it safe. Changing pit would change which destination is dangerous, so the clue also compares v with pit.

## A warning and its consequence must agree

The tunnel table says Cold draught beside the relevant direction. This warning is specific enough to act on: a different open tunnel avoids the pit. The cave is fixed, so the same room remains dangerous on every visit.

The final input loop returns to line 5090 for ignored keys. It does not resume movement or repeatedly apply the ending. R starts at line 200, rebuilding the room data and restoring the treasure flags.

<Question prompt="Why does line 410 appear before copying dest into rm on line 430?">

It gives the fatal case its own route. That route records the room and ends immediately. The ordinary route continues to move and collect. What matters is keeping the later turn work from running after the ending; both paths deliberately record the destination.
