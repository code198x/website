Line 100 adds `RANDOMIZE` so each game places the pit differently. Line 170 picks a random room between 2 and 20 for the pit — never room 1, so the player always starts safe. Line 360 shows the pit's location for debugging (we will remove this later).

Lines 370–470 add sensory clues. Line 380 sets `chk` to the pit's room number and calls the adjacency subroutine at line 740. If the subroutine finds that the pit is in a room connected to the player's current position, `adj` comes back as 1, and line 390 prints "You feel a cold draft..." Line 470 prints "All quiet." if no clues fired.

## The adjacency subroutine

Lines 740–770 are the heart of the game. The subroutine checks whether room `chk` is adjacent to room `rm` — meaning one of `rm`'s exits leads directly to `chk`. Line 760 checks all four directions in a single condition: if `n(rm)`, `s(rm)`, `e(rm)`, or `w(rm)` equals `chk`, the rooms are connected.

This subroutine will be called for every hazard and every treasure. Writing it once as a `GO SUB` means we never repeat the four-way check. The subroutine does not know or care what it is checking — pit, creature, treasure — it just answers: "Is room `chk` next to room `rm`?"

## Indirect sensing

The player never sees the pit. They sense a draft when they are one room away. This is indirect information — the draft tells you something is nearby, but not exactly where. If a room has three exits and you feel a draft, the pit could be behind any of them. You must reason about which direction is dangerous based on where you have already been and what you already know. The clue does not solve the problem; it frames it.
