## The order is the rule

The program validates a key, works out dest and rejects a wall. Then it counts the action and checks entry into a pit or the creature's current room. A surviving player moves and collects. Only then does the creature advance and the screen show the new state.

If we ended the game immediately after the creature entered the player's room, the preceding display could not always warn about that outcome. Here the arrival changes the next decision. The player's next accepted destination must leave the occupied room.

Invalid keys and blocked exits return before the turn count and creature update. Even during an encounter, a mistyped key or wall does not silently consume the escape opportunity. There is also no real-time deadline: MOVE NOW means “make your next valid action an escape”, not “type quickly”.

<Question prompt="At the arrival in room 10, what happens if you press A, then S, then Space?">

A is not a command. S has no tunnel from room 10. Neither advances the turn. Space is a valid wait, so it keeps the destination in the occupied room and the creature catches you.

</Question>

Try going north from that arrival instead of west. You leave room 10, but the creature's next patrol room is 6, where you have just gone. It arrives again and gives another escape turn. Leaving an encounter and ending the turn away from the creature are different outcomes.

The twelve-room map and patrol offer an exit at every reachable arrival. That is a property of this authored combination; adding rooms or changing the route means checking it again. A generous rule cannot rescue a map that gives the player nowhere to go.
