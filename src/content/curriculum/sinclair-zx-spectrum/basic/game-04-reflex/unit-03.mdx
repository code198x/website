The random PAUSE from Unit 1 created unpredictability. But the player stares at a static screen — nothing tells them something is building. The telegraph bar fixes that.

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-04-reflex/unit-03/telegraph.bas" />

Lines 50–70 draw the bar:

```
FOR x = 0 TO 200
PLOT x, 88
NEXT x
```

`PLOT x, 88` places a single pixel at horizontal position `x`, vertical position 88 (roughly the middle of the screen). The `FOR` loop runs `x` from 0 to 200, drawing one pixel per iteration. The result: a horizontal line that fills left to right, one pixel at a time.

This is your first use of `PLOT` — putting a pixel at a specific coordinate on the Spectrum's 256×176 pixel display. The screen's origin (0, 0) is the bottom-left corner. `PLOT 0, 0` is the bottom-left pixel; `PLOT 255, 175` is the top-right.

## Telegraphing

The bar creeps across the screen. The player watches it approach the right edge. They know the signal is coming — but not exactly when. The bar **telegraphs** the event: it shows that something is about to happen without revealing the exact moment.

Take the bar out (delete lines 50–70, replace with `PAUSE INT (RND * 100) + 50` as before). Run both versions. The bar version creates a different quality of tension — you are watching something happen, not just waiting.

This is the embedded concept: **Telegraphing** — showing the player what is about to happen so they can prepare. The telegraph bar does not make the game easier (the signal is still sudden), but it makes the waiting active rather than passive.

## FOR/NEXT with PLOT

You have not formally learnt `FOR/NEXT` yet — that comes in Dice Roller (Game 5). But the pattern here is simple enough to use before the formal introduction: "do this 200 times, with `x` counting from 0 to 200." Each time through the loop, `PLOT` uses the current value of `x` to place one more pixel.

Try changing 200 to 100 — the bar is shorter and fills faster. Try 255 — it spans the full screen width. The bar length is the tension dial.
