Line 80: `PAPER 2: BORDER 2: CLS` — the screen turns red. Everything — background, border, the entire visible area — goes red in one frame. Then "NOW!" prints on top of it.

## PAPER as event

Until now, `PAPER` and `BORDER` have been palette choices — you set them once at the start and left them. Here, the colour change *is* the event. The screen was dark, now it is red: something happened. The player's body reacts to the colour change before their mind reads the word.

This is the design concept **Tension** at work: the telegraph bar built anticipation slowly, and the red flash releases it violently. The contrast between the slow build and the sudden flash is what makes the reaction feel urgent.

Try `PAPER 4` (green) instead of red. Does it feel as urgent? Try `PAPER 6` (yellow). Red screams "now." Green says "go." Yellow says "caution." The colour you choose shapes the emotional tone of the signal.

## Contrast is the tool

The telegraph bar was subtle — a thin line of pixels creeping across a dark screen. The signal is bold — the entire screen changes colour. Subtlety before, boldness after. The design lesson: if everything is bold, nothing stands out. Reserve the loud moments for when they matter.
