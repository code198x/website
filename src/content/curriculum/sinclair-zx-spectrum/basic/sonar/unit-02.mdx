Lines 130–170 place three targets. Each one picks a random row and column, checks whether that cell is already a target (`IF g(r,c) = 9 THEN GO TO 140` retries if so), and marks the cell with 9. The value 9 means "target here."

Lines 290–300 update the grid drawing: cells with value 9 show as `X`, everything else as `.`. Run it a few times — three Xs appear at different positions each run.

## Why retry on collision?

Line 150 checks `IF g(r,c) = 9`. Without it, two targets could land on the same cell — the grid would have two targets but only two Xs instead of three. The retry loop guarantees three distinct positions.

The Xs are a debugging aid. In the finished game, targets will be invisible — the player finds them through clues, not by seeing them on the grid. For now, the Xs let you verify that targets are placed correctly and that later logic (hits, distances) works as expected.
