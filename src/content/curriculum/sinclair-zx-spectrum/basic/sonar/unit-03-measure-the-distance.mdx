## Count rows and columns

`ABS` returns the magnitude of a number without its sign. For a probe at row 1 and target at row 3, the difference is -2, but the separation is two rows. `ABS (r-tr)` gives that separation whichever position is larger. Do the same for the columns, then add them.

| Probe | Target | Row steps | Column steps | Distance |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `(1,2)` | `(3,6)` | 2 | 4 | 6 |
| `(1,6)` | `(3,6)` | 2 | 0 | 2 |
| `(3,4)` | `(3,6)` | 0 | 2 | 2 |
| `(3,6)` | `(3,6)` | 0 | 0 | 0 |

This game counts horizontal and vertical steps, not diagonal shortcuts. It is a chosen rule for a deduction game, not a model of how sound travels through water. A clue supplies no direction: several cells can be the same distance from a probe.

The main drawing routine no longer paints the target. The long-input recovery routine also loses its target drawing, otherwise a typing mistake would reveal the answer. Both ordinary drawing and recovery must follow the same rule.

## Fit the number in its cell

`STR$ d` turns the distance into text for `b$`. A distance below ten gets a leading space. A two-digit result already fills both positions. Zero replaces the number with `*`. The separate `>` still occupies the first of the cell's three character columns.

Opposite corners of an 8×8 board are seven rows and seven columns apart, giving the maximum distance 14. The display has room for it even though our ordinary fixed target does not produce that maximum.

<Question prompt="Does a clue of 2 mean that the target is two columns to the right?">

No. It could be two columns in either direction, two rows away, or one row and one column away. The clue is the sum of the separations, without a direction.

</Question>

Try the four probes in the table and compare their results. To check the maximum, temporarily replace line 20 with target row 1, column 1 and probe `(8,8)`. Restore `(3,6)` afterwards.

Exact clues can make a very efficient search. From the two ends of the top row, one clue gives the sum of the target's offsets and the other helps separate them.
