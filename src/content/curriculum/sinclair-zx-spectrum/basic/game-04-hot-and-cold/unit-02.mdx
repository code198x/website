To move something on screen, you need two ingredients: a character to display and variables to track where it is.

## A Cursor on Screen

Variables `r` and `c` store the cursor's row and column. `PRINT AT r, c` draws a bright yellow "+" at that position — the player's marker on the grid.

## Position Variables

Instead of fixed numbers in `PRINT AT`, we use variables:

- `r` — the **row** (vertical position, 0 = top)
- `c` — the **column** (horizontal position, 0 = left)

To move down, increase `r`. To move up, decrease `r`. To move right, increase `c`. To move left, decrease `c`.

## The Erase-Update-Draw Pattern

Moving a character on screen takes three steps:

1. **Erase** — print a space at the old position
2. **Update** — change `r` or `c`
3. **Draw** — print the character at the new position

Without erasing first, you'd leave a trail of "+" characters behind. The old one stays on screen because the Spectrum doesn't clear it automatically — you have to do it yourself.

## Try This

**Move it down.** After displaying the cursor, add `PRINT AT r, c; " "` then `LET r = r + 1` then `PRINT AT r, c; "+"`. The cursor jumps down one row.

**Different starting position.** Change `r` and `c` to start the cursor in a corner instead of the centre.

**Different character.** Replace "+" with "@" or "#". Pick something that stands out against a black background.

## What You've Learnt

- **Position variables** — `r` for row, `c` for column
- **Erase-update-draw** — print a space at the old position, change the variable, print at the new position
- **BRIGHT 1** — makes the cursor stand out against the background
