Touchdown has been numbers and a falling letter. Now it becomes a view from the cockpit.

This is the visual overhaul. The lander becomes a custom-drawn ship, stars fill the sky, the ground turns solid green, and the readout runs along the top. The three outcomes also gain a shared results screen.

## A ship of your own

Lines 20–50 define two custom characters. `CHR$ 144` and `CHR$ 145` are blank slots you fill yourself: eight bytes per character, one per pixel row, loaded from the `DATA` lines by the `READ`/`POKE USR` loop on line 50. Character 144 is the lander — a small rocket; character 145 is the thrust flame the final unit draws beneath it when the engine fires. You met this technique in Hi-Lo's suit cards; here it draws a spacecraft. Line 470 prints the lander with `CHR$ 144`, where the falling "V" used to be.

## Stars and ground

Line 150 scatters a starfield: `FOR i = 1 TO 20: PLOT INT (RND * 256), INT (RND * 100) + 60` plots twenty pixels at random positions across the upper sky. Line 170 lays down the surface — a row of 32 solid blocks (`CHR$ 143`) in green along the bottom. Between them, the lander falls through space toward the ground.

Line 160 prints the instruments in an inverse bar across the top — black text on white — so the readout reads like a cockpit panel rather than ordinary output.

## One results screen for three endings

The biggest structural change is at the bottom. Each outcome — perfect (line 600), bumpy (640), crash (680) — still sets its own border, sound, and message, but instead of stopping, all three jump to line 710. Lines 710–720 print the landing speed and the fuel remaining; line 730 stops.

It is a funnel: three entrances, one exit. The outcome-specific drama fires first, then every path converges on the same summary. Speed decides the outcome — the player already knows that. Fuel remaining tells a different story: it measures efficiency. Landing perfectly with 30 fuel left means barely touching the thrusters; landing with 1 left means cutting it fine. The player starts chasing a second goal — not just "can I land?" but "can I land with fuel to spare?"
