Gravity keeps changing the craft's speed while you decide when to fire its engine. Reach the pad, arrive gently and keep enough fuel to make the corrections that matter. Each attempt begins from the same situation, so another try can use what the last one taught you.

<Figure src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/touchdown/teaching/flight.png" width={352} alt="A small white lander above a green ridge, with a yellow pad on its far side and fuel and speed instruments above." caption="The full challenge uses one fixed landscape. We begin with vertical landings on flat ground." />

Use a **48K ZX Spectrum** or an emulator configured as that machine. The completed game uses O/P to steer and Space to thrust; those controls can be held together. S launches, R retries after contact, and Q alone quits after release. Fuel exhaustion disables the engine but leaves the flight going.

Touchdown follows [Volley](/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/volley/). We recall its continuous updates, candidate positions and consecutive erase/draw, then explore velocity, fuel and a landscape whose shape affects contact. Each lesson explains its BASIC locally; neither assembly nor Foundations is a prerequisite.

## Learn the approach in smaller games

First watch a constant fall, then add gravity and unlimited thrust. Make that vertical game complete before limiting fuel. A generous pad on flat ground gives steering room to develop before the ridge and narrower target appear.

Every lesson has a complete runnable checkpoint. Additional checkpoints let us inspect simultaneous keys without flying, change terrain representation without changing its shape, and build the custom craft before its flame. Keep the simpler versions as useful practice and comparisons.

The full approach is demanding. The simpler games give us room to understand and practise its demands; there is no requirement to land on the first attempt. The final lesson compares difficulty changes one at a time and ends with a saved game that can be reopened and shared.
