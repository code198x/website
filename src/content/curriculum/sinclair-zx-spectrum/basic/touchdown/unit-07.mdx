## Erase-then-draw

This is the fundamental animation pattern: **erase the old position, draw the new position**. Every animated game does this, from Pong to Pac-Man. The object appears to move because the eye sees it in a new place and the old place is empty.

The order matters. Erase first, draw second. If you draw first and erase second, you erase what you just drew — the object flickers and vanishes for a frame. The eye catches the flicker even at speed.

You need the previous position to know where to erase. That is why line 180 captures `prev` before line 190 updates `alt`. Without that snapshot, the old position is lost once the altitude changes. The general rule: **save before you update** whenever you need to undo the old state.

Lines 370 and 400 both clamp with `IF row < 4` — this keeps the lander from overwriting the dashboard at the top of the screen. The clamping on both `row` and `prow` ensures the erase never hits an impossible position either.
