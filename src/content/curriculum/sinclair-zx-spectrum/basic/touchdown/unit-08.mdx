The dashboard shows altitude as a number. The border can show it as a feeling.

Lines 290–330 change the border colour based on altitude. Above 70: blue — high up, nothing to worry about. 40–70: cyan — descending, time to pay attention. 20–40: yellow — getting close, start managing thrust. 10–20: red — danger, every frame counts. Below 10: white — the ground is right there. The colours track the player's anxiety, not just the lander's height.

Line 340 adds a fuel warning: when fuel drops below 10 and is not yet zero, a high-pitched beep sounds each frame. The warning is impossible to ignore. It says "you are almost out — every thrust counts."

## Ambient feedback

The border is peripheral vision. The player is watching the lander and the dashboard; they do not look at the border directly. But they see the colour shift from blue through yellow to red, and that shift creates tension without requiring attention. It is ambient information — always present, never demanding.

This is the same principle as the green/red feedback in Hi-Lo, but continuous rather than binary. The colour is not telling the player "correct" or "wrong" — it is telling them "how close to danger am I?" The answer changes every frame.

The border is the only part of the Spectrum display that extends to the edge of the screen. When it turns red, the entire frame around the play area glows — the effect is visceral in a way that a number on the dashboard cannot match.
