The game starts with a V falling from the top of the screen. A new player has no idea what is happening.

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/touchdown/unit-10/titled.bas" />

Lines 10–80 add a title screen. Line 10 sets the stage black. Lines 20–60 explain the game in four lines: land the spacecraft, hold SPACE to fire thrusters, land slowly or crash, fuel is limited. Line 70 prompts, line 80 waits.

This title screen matters more than Bright Spark's or Hi-Lo's. Those games had simple controls (press a number, press H or L). Touchdown has real-time physics and a resource to manage — the player needs to understand all three concepts before the lander starts falling. "Hold SPACE to fire thrusters" and "Fuel is limited. Use it wisely." are the two sentences that turn confusion into a game.

Notice that all the game code is now pushed to line 90 onward. The title screen occupies lines 10–80, the game logic starts at 90, and the subroutine-style outcome screens start at 450. The program has a clear structure: introduction, game, endings.
