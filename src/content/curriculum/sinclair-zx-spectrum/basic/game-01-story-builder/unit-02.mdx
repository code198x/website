## How it works

Line 50: `INPUT "What is your name? "; n$` does two things. It prints the prompt (the text in quotation marks), then waits for you to type. Whatever you type is stored in `n$`.

The `$` at the end of `n$` means it holds text, not a number. The letter `n` is just a name you chose — you could call it `x$` or `z$`.

Line 130: `PRINT "Hello, "; n$` joins two pieces. The fixed text `"Hello, "` and whatever `n$` contains. The semicolon glues them onto the same line.

## Why the big gaps?

The line numbers jump — setup on 10, the question on 50, the output on 130 — and that is on purpose. Numbering in tens with room to spare lets a later unit drop new lines straight into a gap without renumbering anything. More questions will go between 50 and 130 (you start that in the next unit), and a title screen will go between 10 and 50 (a few units on). Leaving the room now is cheaper than shuffling every line later — it is the habit from unit 1, used in earnest.
