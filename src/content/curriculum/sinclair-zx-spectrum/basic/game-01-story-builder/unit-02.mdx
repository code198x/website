## How it works

`INPUT "What is your name? "; n$` does two things:

1. It prints the message in quotation marks — the prompt.
2. It waits for you to type something, then stores your answer in `n$`.

The `$` at the end of `n$` means it holds text, not a number. The letter `n` is just a name you chose — you could call it `name$` or `x$` or anything.

Then `PRINT "Hello, "; n$` joins two pieces together: the fixed text `"Hello, "` and whatever `n$` contains. The semicolon `;` glues them onto the same line.

## What you have built

Two new tools:

- **INPUT** asks a question and waits for an answer. The answer goes into a variable.
- **String variables** (`n$`, `a$`, `b$`) remember text. You can use them anywhere you would use a quoted string.

And one technique: **semicolons** join pieces of text together on the same PRINT line. `PRINT "Hello, "; n$` becomes one continuous line of output.
