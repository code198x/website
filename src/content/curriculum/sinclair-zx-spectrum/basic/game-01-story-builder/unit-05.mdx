One new line: `90 CLS`. After the last question, the screen clears. The story prints on a blank screen — a clean reveal.

Run it. Answer the five questions. The screen goes blank, then the story appears fresh. The questions are gone. The output has the stage to itself.

## Why this matters

The program does exactly the same thing as before — same questions, same output. But the experience is different. The blank screen before the story creates a moment of anticipation. The player waits half a second, wondering what the computer will say. Then it appears.

This is the simplest possible version of a design idea you will use in every game: **separate input from output**. The player's actions happen in one phase. The result appears in another. The gap between them — even a fraction of a second — is what makes the result feel like a response, not just more text scrolling past.
