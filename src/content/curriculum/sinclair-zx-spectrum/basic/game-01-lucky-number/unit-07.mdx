The guessing game works, but it has a problem: the secret number is always 7. Once you know it's 7, there's no game. You need the computer to pick a different number every time.

That's what `RND` does. It generates a random number.

The screen fills with numbers like `0.43719482` and `0.79025269`. Every number is different, but they're all between 0 and 1. That's what `RND` produces — a random decimal, never quite reaching 0 or 1.

Press **BREAK** (Caps Shift + Space) to stop it.

Decimal numbers between 0 and 1 aren't useful for a guessing game. You need whole numbers between 1 and 100. You have to transform what `RND` gives you.

## From Decimal to Whole Number

Here's the trick:

```basic
INT (RND * 100) + 1
```

Work through it step by step:

1. `RND` gives a random decimal between 0 and 1 — say `0.437`
2. `RND * 100` multiplies it by 100 — giving `43.7`
3. `INT (RND * 100)` chops off the decimal part — giving `43`
4. `INT (RND * 100) + 1` adds 1 — giving `44`

`INT` stands for "integer." It removes everything after the decimal point, leaving a whole number. It always rounds down: `INT 3.9` gives `3`, not `4`.

Why add 1? Because `INT (RND * 100)` gives numbers from 0 to 99. Adding 1 shifts the range to 1 to 100 — exactly what the game needs.

## REM — Notes to Yourself

Line 10 is new: `REM Lucky Number`. `REM` stands for "remark." The Spectrum ignores everything after `REM` on that line. It's a note for you — a reminder of what the program does.

REM lines are useful when programs get longer. They don't affect how the program runs, but they help you remember what you were thinking when you wrote it. Use them whenever a section of code might be confusing later.

## Different Ranges

The formula `INT (RND * 100) + 1` gives numbers from 1 to 100. You can change the range:

| Formula | Range |
|---------|-------|
| `INT (RND * 10) + 1` | 1 to 10 |
| `INT (RND * 50) + 1` | 1 to 50 |
| `INT (RND * 100) + 1` | 1 to 100 |
| `INT (RND * 6) + 1` | 1 to 6 (a dice roll) |

The number you multiply by sets the range. The `+ 1` makes it start at 1 instead of 0.
