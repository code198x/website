Reporting "hit" or "miss" is useful, but the player also needs to see their shot history on the tracking grid. Each shot updates the tracking array with a marker value, and the grid redraws to show the result. Over time, the tracking grid builds up a picture of where the opponent ships are hiding.

## How It Works

**Lines 20-40** check the opponent ship array as before. If the cell value is greater than 0, it is a hit.

**Lines 50-70** update the tracking array. For Player 1 (using tracking array `c()`), a hit sets the cell to 6 and a miss sets it to 7. These values match the colour scheme from Unit 4: value 6 draws in red (hit), value 7 draws in cyan (miss).

**Lines 80-100** also update the opponent ship array on a hit. Setting the ship cell to 6 marks it as damaged. This serves two purposes: it prevents the cell from registering as a "new" hit if fired at again, and it lets the ship grid show damage to the ship owner.

**Lines 110-130** redraw the tracking grid to show the updated state. The player sees their growing map of hits and misses.

## Two Arrays, One Shot

A single shot touches two arrays. The tracking array (`c` or `d`) records what the current player has learned. The ship array (`a` or `b`) records damage to the opponent fleet. Both updates are necessary:

- Without the tracking update, the player would not see their shot history.
- Without the ship array update, subsequent shots to the same cell would register as fresh hits.

The value 6 means "hit" in both contexts. On the ship grid, it shows the ship owner where they have been damaged. On the tracking grid, it shows the shooter where they hit something.

## Try This

**Fire several shots.** Take a few turns and watch the tracking grid fill in. Hits should appear in red, misses in cyan. The ship grid should show damage in red where the opponent hit you.

**Check both grids.** After a hit, look at both the tracking grid and the ship grid. The hit should appear on both — in the tracking grid for the shooter, and in the ship grid for the ship owner (visible during their turn).
