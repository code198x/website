Sinking a ship is the highlight of the game. The two-note hit sound is not enough for this moment. A multi-note fanfare — a short ascending melody — marks the event as special. It plays after the sunk message appears, giving the player a brief celebration.

## How It Works

**Lines 160-180** play the fanfare immediately after displaying the sunk message. The sequence uses four ascending notes: `BEEP 0.15,10: BEEP 0.15,14: BEEP 0.15,17: BEEP 0.3,22`. The notes rise in pitch, creating a triumphant upward melody. The final note is longer (0.3 seconds) to give the phrase a sense of arrival.

**Lines 190-200** keep the sunk message on screen during the fanfare. Since BEEP blocks execution, the message remains visible while all four notes play. Total fanfare duration is about 0.75 seconds — long enough to feel celebratory, short enough to keep the game moving.

## Choosing the Notes

The four semitone values (10, 14, 17, 22) form an ascending pattern with unequal intervals. This avoids sounding like a scale exercise. The jumps are: +4, +3, +5. The wider final jump creates a sense of lift at the end, as if the melody is leaping up in triumph.

You could use a simple scale (10, 12, 14, 17) but equal steps sound mechanical. Musical interest comes from varying the intervals. Even with four notes on a single-channel speaker, the shape of the melody communicates emotion.

## Sound Hierarchy

The game now has three sound levels:

1. **Miss** — single low note (0.3 seconds). Understated, slightly disappointing.
2. **Hit** — two ascending notes (0.2 seconds). Positive, energetic.
3. **Sunk** — four ascending notes (0.75 seconds). Triumphant, celebratory.

Each level is more elaborate than the last, matching the significance of the event. The player learns the hierarchy quickly: more notes means a bigger deal.

## Try This

**Design your own fanfare.** Try different note combinations. A descending pattern sounds ominous rather than triumphant. A repeating note sounds like an alarm. What four notes best capture "you sank a battleship"?

**Scale the fanfare by ship size.** Play more notes for larger ships: two notes for the Destroyer, five for the Carrier. The Carrier sinking should feel like a bigger event because it took more effort.
