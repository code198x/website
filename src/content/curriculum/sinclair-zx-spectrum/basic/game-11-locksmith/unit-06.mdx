Each guess disappears when the next one starts. The player cannot remember the clues from guess 1 by the time they reach guess 5. A visible history solves this.

Line 80 adds a title at the top. Line 110 moves the input prompt to row 20 with `PRINT AT`. Line 300 is the key change: `PRINT AT 2 + t, 2` — each guess prints at a row offset by the attempt number. Guess 1 goes to row 3, guess 2 to row 4, guess 3 to row 5. The history grows downward, one line per attempt.

The guess text and its bull/cow counts sit side by side: `1234  1 bull  0 cow`. The player can scan the column and compare clues across guesses. "Guess 3 gave me 2 bulls and guess 4 gave me 1 — what did I change between them?"

## The visual record of reasoning

This is the design concept behind Locksmith. The game is not just about guessing — it is about reasoning. And reasoning requires a record. A chess player sees the board. A scientist keeps a lab notebook. The Locksmith player has a history column.

The display restructuring is a teaching unit in itself. The program moves from scrolling text to a fixed-position layout. Each piece of information has an address on screen. The title stays at the top. The history grows in the middle. The input prompt lives at the bottom. The screen is now designed, not just printed to.
