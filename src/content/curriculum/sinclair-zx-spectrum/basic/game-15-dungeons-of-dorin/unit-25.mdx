All the systems are built. Now they need to work together at the right pace. Floors 1-3 should feel safe enough to learn the controls. Floors 4-6 should test the player combat skills. Floors 7-9 should demand careful resource management. Floor 10 should feel like a genuine final challenge. This unit is about adjusting numbers until the curve feels right.

## How It Works

Difficulty scaling touches every system:

**Monster count per floor** increases gradually. Floors 1-3 have 2-3 monsters. Floors 4-6 have 3-4. Floors 7-9 have 4-5. Floor 10 has the dragon plus supporting creatures. More monsters means more combat, which means more HP loss per floor.

**Monster type selection** (lines 1700-1710) restricts which creatures appear at each depth. This is already in place from unit 16 — rats and goblins early, trolls and wraiths late. The scaling here is verified and tuned rather than rewritten.

**Chest contents** scale with floor depth. Gold amounts increase. Weapon and armour bonuses get larger. Potions remain equally likely on all floors, providing consistent healing opportunities.

**Fountain frequency** drops on later floors. Early floors offer free healing to help the player learn. Later floors demand potion management and careful combat decisions.

## Playtesting

Difficulty tuning is not a coding exercise — it is a playing exercise. Run the game ten times. Note where you die most often. If floor 3 kills most attempts, the early game is too hard. If floor 9 is trivial, the late game needs more pressure.

Common findings from playtesting this game:

- **Floor 4** is often the first death point. The transition from rats/goblins to skeletons is a stat jump. A weapon find on floors 1-3 softens this.
- **Floor 7** is the resource crunch. By now, potions are running low and the player may not have found armour. A fountain on floor 5 or 6 helps.
- **Floor 10** should feel like a boss level. The dragon alone is dangerous. Adding even one wraith alongside it makes the floor very hard — perhaps too hard without good equipment.

## The Balance Spreadsheet

Even in a BASIC game, thinking about numbers systematically helps. Consider the player expected state at each floor:

| Floor | Expected HP | Expected Attack | Expected Defence | Typical Monster |
|-------|-------------|----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| 1 | 20/20 | 3 | 2 | Rat |
| 3 | 15/20 | 4 | 2 | Goblin |
| 5 | 12/20 | 5 | 3 | Skeleton |
| 7 | 10/20 | 6 | 3 | Troll |
| 10 | 8/20 | 7 | 4 | Dragon |

These are estimates, not guarantees — RND makes every run different. But they show the intended progression: the player gets stronger but takes attrition damage faster than equipment compensates.

## Try This

**Play with a fixed seed.** Sinclair BASIC does not support fixed seeds, but you can note the room layout on floor 1 and compare runs. Does the same layout always feel the same difficulty?

**Remove all potions and fountains.** Play with combat damage as the only mechanic. How many floors can you survive? This reveals the raw difficulty curve without healing.
