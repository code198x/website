## How It Works

**Lines 830-840** handle the player attack. The damage formula is `INT(RND * 6) + 1 + a + w - t3`, where `a` is the player base attack, `w` is the weapon bonus, and `t3` is the monster defence. The random component (1-6) simulates a dice roll. If the total drops below 1, it is clamped to 1 — every attack deals at least 1 damage.

**Line 836** subtracts damage from the monster HP. Line 838 prints the result: "You hit for X!" in green INK.

**Line 842** checks if the monster HP has dropped to 0 or below. If so, the fight is won and control jumps to the victory handler at line 890.

**Lines 844-856** handle the monster attack. The same formula applies in reverse: `INT(RND * 6) + 1 + t2 - f - r`, where `t2` is the monster attack stat, `f` is the player base defence, and `r` is the armour bonus. The result is subtracted from the player HP.

**Line 856** checks for player death. If HP reaches 0, control jumps to the death handler at line 1000.

**Line 858** updates the displayed HP values, and line 862 loops back to let the player choose again.

## The Combat Formula

The formula `roll + attack - defence` is a classic RPG pattern. The random element (1-6) means the outcome is not purely deterministic — a weak character can occasionally land a strong hit, and a powerful one can whiff. The stats shift the odds. Higher attack means dealing more damage on average. Higher defence means absorbing more.

The minimum damage of 1 prevents stalemates. Without it, a heavily armoured player fighting a weak monster could take 0 damage every round, making combat risk-free. Similarly, a rat with 0 defence can always be hurt, preventing frustrating misses.

## Victory and Rewards

**Lines 890-906** handle monster defeat. The monster HP is set to 0 in the array, which removes it from the dungeon — the viewport renderer only draws monsters with HP above 0. The player receives gold from the monster DATA, and the kill counter increments. A brief victory fanfare plays before the screen clears and control returns to the exploration loop.
