Base attack of 3 is enough for rats and goblins. It is not enough for trolls and dragons. Weapons bridge the gap. Found in treasure chests, a weapon adds a bonus to the player attack stat. Better weapons appear on deeper floors, and finding one replaces the current weapon. The combat formula already includes the weapon bonus — `a + w` — so equipping a weapon changes every future fight.

## How It Works

**Line 26** initialises the weapon bonus `w` to 0. The player starts unarmed (fists only).

**Line 758** in the chest subroutine handles weapon drops. The weapon bonus is calculated from the current floor: `INT(e / 3) + 1`. On floors 1-2 this gives +1, floors 3-5 give +2, floors 6-8 give +3, and floors 9-10 give +4. The check `IF t > w` ensures the weapon is only equipped if it is better than the current one. If the rolled weapon is weaker or equal, the chest falls through to gold.

**Line 504** in the status panel shows the total attack as `a + w`. When the player equips a weapon, they see the combined number increase — immediate feedback that the upgrade matters.

## The Upgrade Check

Only better weapons are equipped. If the player already carries a +3 weapon and finds a +2, the chest gives gold instead. This prevents frustrating downgrades and means every weapon pickup is a genuine improvement. The player never needs to compare items and choose — the game handles it automatically.

This simplicity is a design choice. A more complex system would let the player carry multiple weapons and swap between them. But that adds inventory management, comparison screens, and more variables. The single-slot auto-upgrade keeps the focus on exploration and combat rather than menu navigation.

## Stat Modifiers

The weapon bonus `w` is added to the base attack `a` everywhere combat damage is calculated. Line 832 in the player attack formula uses `a + w` directly. This means the weapon affects every fight from the moment it is equipped, with no additional code needed. The modifier pattern — a base stat plus an equipment bonus — is clean and extensible. Armour uses exactly the same approach for defence in the next unit.

## Floor-Based Scaling

Tying weapon quality to floor depth serves two purposes. First, it ensures the player gets stronger as monsters get tougher. A weapon found on floor 7 is appropriate for floor 7 monsters. Second, it creates a progression curve — early floors offer small upgrades, later floors offer dramatic ones. This parallels the monster difficulty scaling from unit 16.
