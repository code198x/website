"You hit for 4" works, but "You slash the goblin for 4 damage with your Steel Sword" tells a story. Combat polish replaces generic messages with descriptive ones that use the monster name, weapon name, and colour to make each exchange feel distinct. The combat mechanics are unchanged — only the presentation improves.

## How It Works

**Player attack messages** now include context. Instead of "You hit for 5!", the message names the action and the target. The weapon name appears when the player has one equipped. Misses (damage reduced to minimum 1) get a different message emphasising the glancing blow.

**Monster attack messages** name the creature: "The Troll strikes you for 3!" replaces the anonymous "Enemy hits for 3!". Colour coding makes damage easy to read — green INK for player damage dealt, red INK for damage taken.

**The combat header** shows the monster name, its HP, and the player HP side by side. After each round, both values update. The player can watch HP drain from both sides and judge how the fight is going.

**Miss messages** appear when the damage roll is very low. "The rat barely scratches you!" communicates that the hit was minor, even though 1 damage was still dealt. These flavour messages make combat feel less mechanical without changing the underlying maths.

## Colour as Information

Colour serves two purposes in combat text. First, it distinguishes player actions (green) from monster actions (red). The player can glance at the colour to know who is acting without reading the full message. Second, it conveys severity — a critical hit might use BRIGHT 1 to make the text stand out.

The status display during combat uses the same colour scheme as the exploration HUD: white for HP, green for attack, cyan for defence. Consistency helps the player read information quickly across different screens.

## String Building in BASIC

Sinclair BASIC does not concatenate strings easily. Building a message like "You hit the Goblin for 5!" requires multiple PRINT statements or careful use of semicolons. The approach here uses sequential PRINT statements on the same line with `AT` positioning, or the semicolon separator to chain values: `PRINT "You hit for "; c; "!"`.

The monster name `i$` is read from DATA at the start of combat and reused throughout. Since BASIC variables persist until overwritten, the name remains available for every message in the combat loop.

## Try This

**Add a critical hit message.** When the dice roll is 6 (maximum), print "Critical hit!" in bright yellow. The mechanical effect is the same, but the message makes it feel special.

**Vary the verb.** Instead of "hit" every time, use "slash", "strike", or "smash" based on the weapon type. A random selection from a small set adds variety without changing the formula.
