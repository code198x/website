The screen fills with horizontal stripes — the rainbow repeating every eight rows. Row 0 is black (invisible against the black background), row 1 is blue, row 2 is red, all the way to row 7 (white), then it wraps back to black and repeats.

## How It Works

The outer loop (`r`) counts rows 0 to 21. The inner loop (`c`) counts columns 0 to 31. For each cell, `PRINT AT r,c` places a coloured space at that exact position.

The colour formula `PAPER (r-INT(r/8)*8)` wraps the row number into the 0-7 range:

| Row | Formula | Colour |
|-----|---------|--------|
| 0 | 0-0=0 | Black |
| 1 | 1-0=1 | Blue |
| 7 | 7-0=7 | White |
| 8 | 8-8=0 | Black (wraps) |
| 9 | 9-8=1 | Blue (wraps) |

`INT(r/8)*8` rounds down to the nearest multiple of 8, then subtracts it. The result is always 0-7, cycling endlessly. This is the **modulo** pattern — you'll use it whenever you need a number to wrap around.

## Why Rows 0-21?

The Spectrum screen has 24 rows, but the bottom two (rows 22-23) are reserved for system messages and INPUT prompts. Printing there causes errors. Rows 0-21 are safe.

## Try This

- Change `r` to `r+1` in the formula: `PAPER ((r+1)-INT((r+1)/8)*8)`. The stripes shift down by one — blue on top instead of black.
- Change the range to `FOR r=0 TO 21 STEP 2`. Every other row is filled — striped with gaps.
- Replace the colour formula with a fixed colour: `PAPER 2`. The entire screen turns red. One number, one colour, no formula needed.

## What You've Learnt

- **Screen filling** — a nested loop with `PRINT AT r,c; PAPER n;" "` fills the screen cell by cell
- **Colour formula** — `(r-INT(r/8)*8)` wraps a number into the 0-7 range (modulo 8)
- **Row-based colour** — changing the colour per row creates horizontal stripes
- **Rows 0-21** — the safe area; rows 22-23 are reserved for the system
