Colour Flood is a memory game built on colour and sound. The border floods with a colour, a tone plays, and the player has to remember both. This unit builds the core visual — the Spectrum's border cycling through all seven colours, each with its own musical note.

## Border + BEEP

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-03-colour-flood/unit-02/border-beep.bas" />

Type `RUN`. The entire border — that thick frame around the screen — lights up blue with a low tone. It drops to black for a beat, then flashes red with a higher note. Then magenta. Green. Cyan. Yellow. White. Seven colours, seven rising tones, each separated by a moment of darkness. It looks and sounds like the Spectrum is showing off.

One loop variable — `i` — drives everything:

- `BORDER i` — sets the border colour (1=blue, 2=red, ... 7=white)
- `BEEP 0.3,i*3` — plays a tone at pitch `i*3` semitones (3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21)
- The gap between flashes (`BORDER 0`, `PAUSE 8`) makes each colour distinct — without it, the colours would blur together

## The Seven Colours

| Number | Colour | Pitch (i×3) |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| 1 | Blue | 3 |
| 2 | Red | 6 |
| 3 | Magenta | 9 |
| 4 | Green | 12 |
| 5 | Cyan | 15 |
| 6 | Yellow | 18 |
| 7 | White | 21 |

Colour 0 (black) is excluded — it's invisible against the black screen. Each remaining colour has a unique pitch, evenly spaced. After a few rounds of the game, you'll start recognising colours by their sound alone.

## Why the Border?

The border is the largest single-colour area on the Spectrum — a thick frame that surrounds the entire screen on all four sides. When it flashes green, you can't miss it, even if you're not looking directly at the screen. That makes it perfect for a memory game: the signal is bold and unmistakable. No squinting at tiny characters — the whole room changes colour.

## Try This

- Change `i*3` to `i*5`. The tones spread further apart — more dramatic.
- Change `PAUSE 10` to `PAUSE 25`. Each flash lingers longer. Change it to `PAUSE 3` — barely a flicker.
- Add `GO TO 10` after `NEXT i`. The rainbow cycles endlessly. Press BREAK to stop.
- Reverse the loop: `FOR i=7 TO 1 STEP -1`. The tones descend.

## What You've Learnt

- **BORDER with a loop** — `BORDER i` uses the counter as a colour number
- **Colour + sound pairing** — each colour has a pitch; `i*3` spaces them evenly
- **Flash pattern** — colour on, pause, colour off, pause — makes each flash distinct
- **Seven colours** — 1-7 are the playable colours; 0 is invisible on black
