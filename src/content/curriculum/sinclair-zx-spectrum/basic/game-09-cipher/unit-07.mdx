One word per game is not enough. The player should keep going — word after word, tracking wins and losses.

Line 170 initialises two counters: `wins` and `losses`, both at 0. Line 270 shows them at the top of the screen: `"Won: 3  Lost: 1"`.

Lines 530–540 change from `STOP` to `GO TO 180`. When the player wins, `wins` increments and the program jumps to line 180 — pick a new random word, build a fresh underscore string, reset lives and tried letters, start again. When the player loses, `losses` increments and the same thing happens.

## Two counters, one story

Hi-Lo tracked a single streak. Cipher tracks both wins and losses separately. The pair tells a richer story than either alone: "Won: 5, Lost: 2" means the player is good but not perfect. "Won: 3, Lost: 0" means they are on a clean run. "Won: 0, Lost: 3" means they should try more common letters first.

Both counters are initialised on line 170, before line 180 where the word-pick loop starts. The replay jumps to 180, skipping 170 — so the counters persist across words. This is the same position trick from Hi-Lo's `best` score: variables before the loop boundary survive; variables inside it reset.
