The random PAUSE is gone — but the randomness is not. It moves into the bar. Line 155 picks a random stopping point `e`, somewhere between 88 and 227. Lines 160–190 are a FOR/NEXT loop: they repeat the PLOT command for every value of `x` from 28 up to `e`, drawing one pixel per step. We'll explore FOR/NEXT properly in Dice Roller; for now, read it as "draw a pixel, step right, repeat until you reach `e`." When the bar reaches its endpoint, "NOW!" appears.

## PLOT

`PLOT x, 87` places a single pixel at horizontal position `x`, vertical position 87. The FOR loop runs `x` from 28 up to the random endpoint `e`, drawing a horizontal line that fills left to right — a different length every time.

The Spectrum's screen is 256×176 pixels. Origin (0, 0) is the bottom-left corner.

## Telegraphing

The bar creeps across the screen and the player watches it grow, knowing the signal is coming. But because line 155 chose the stopping point at random, they cannot see *where* it will stop — so they cannot guess *when* "NOW!" will appear. That is the trick: the bar **telegraphs** the event (something is building) without revealing the exact moment (it could stop anywhere).

The old `PAUSE` made the wait random but invisible; the bar makes it random and visible. A fixed-length bar would give the timing away the moment the player learned its length — the randomness is what keeps the reaction test honest.
