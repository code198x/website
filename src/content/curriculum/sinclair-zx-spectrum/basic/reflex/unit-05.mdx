"NOW!" is just text. A proper signal should hit you visually — the entire screen changing colour.

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/reflex/unit-05/flash.bas" />

Two new lines. Line 210: `PAPER 2: BORDER 2: CLS` — the screen flashes red. Line 270: `PAPER 0: BORDER 0: CLS` — the result screen goes black. Line 240 changed its target from 300 to 270 (the new result CLS).

The red flash IS the signal. "NOW!" still prints on the red screen, but the colour change is what the player reacts to — it is faster to register than text.

<Figure src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/reflex/unit-05/screenshot.png" alt="The entire ZX Spectrum screen and border flashed red, with NOW! in the top-left" caption="The signal: the whole screen flashes red." />

## PAPER as event

In Story Builder and Oracle Stone, `PAPER` set a background colour that stayed the whole program. Here it changes *during* the program — the shift from white to red to black is three distinct visual states. Colour is not just decoration; it is information. Red means "react." Black means "here is your result."
