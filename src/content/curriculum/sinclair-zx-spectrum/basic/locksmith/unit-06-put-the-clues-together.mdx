## Find the contents first

With 1111, every possible place for a 1 has been tried. Two matches therefore tell us the code contains exactly two ones. The clue does not identify their positions: any two of the four places could hold them.

The 2222 result tells us there are exactly two twos. Together, these clues fill all four places. No 3, 4, 5 or 6 remains possible. We have reduced the question to arranging two ones and two twos.

The six distinct arrangements are 1122, 1212, 1221, 2112, 2121 and 2211. Repeated digits are why there are only six: swapping the two ones does not make a different code.

## Ask where they can go

The third guess, 2211, receives 0 EXACT and 4 OTHER. We already know all four digits belong. The new information is that none occupies its correct position.

<Question prompt="Which of the six arrangements survives the third clue, and why?">

1122 survives. The first two positions cannot hold 2 because 2211 earned zero exact matches. They must therefore hold the two ones. The last two positions must hold the two twos. Every other arrangement would share at least one position with 2211.

</Question>

The positions follow from the combined clues. A single OTHER count does not label individual digits, and the display never points to a particular matching cell. We supplied that reasoning.

## Check the scorer by reasoning backwards

Clear the board and submit 3456. Against 1122 it should give 0 EXACT and 0 OTHER. That tells us none of those four digits appears anywhere. It leaves only 1 and 2, but it does not tell us their counts or their order: 1111, 1122 and 2222 would all agree with that clue alone.

Explain why 1212 gives 2 EXACT and 2 OTHER against 1122 before submitting it. The first and fourth positions match; the remaining 1 and 2 can each be paired once elsewhere. Then try your own sequence and explain what each row rules out.

This visible-code exercise checks our interpretation of clues. It does not claim that three guesses will solve every hidden code. A useful guess can distinguish possibilities even when you do not expect it to be the answer.
