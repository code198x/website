## Four places in memory

DIM c(4) creates four numeric elements, addressed as c(1) through c(4). The LET statements put 1, 1, 2 and 2 into those places. Their order is part of the answer. The caption tells us the practice code, but the scorer reads c, never the caption.

The scoring routine creates a second four-element array, g. A character such as `"2"` and the number 2 are different kinds of value. `VAL g$(i)` converts one typed digit into a number so it can be stored in g(i) and compared with c(i). Our editor already restricts each character to 1–6.

`bulls` is the program's name for the EXACT count. It begins at zero on every submission. FOR visits positions 1 through 4; IF adds one only when g(i) equals c(i). NEXT sits on a separate line, so it runs whether that comparison succeeds or fails.

## Compare positions, not totals

With 1111, positions 1 and 2 match. Positions 3 and 4 do not. With 1212, positions 1 and 4 match. Both guesses earn 2 EXACT, although their matching places differ.

The clue reports a count. It does not mark the individual digits that earned it. Green makes the count easy to find, while the EXACT heading explains what it means.
