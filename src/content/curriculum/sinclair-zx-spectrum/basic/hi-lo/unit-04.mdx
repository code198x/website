The game plays, but a correct guess passes almost unnoticed — the next card just appears. A
hit should *feel* like a hit. With tools you already have — `BORDER`, `BEEP`, `INK`, `PAUSE` —
you can mark the win with a flash of colour, a bright note, and a beat before play moves on.

## Marking the win

The new lines all hang off a correct guess. Line 360 flashes the border green and sounds a
bright `BEEP 0.1, 10`; line 370 prints "Correct!" in `INK 4` (green) so the word matches the
flash. Line 380's `PAUSE 30` holds that celebration for about half a second — long enough to
register — before line 390 puts the border back to black and line 400 wipes the "Correct!" so it
does not linger over the next round.

That little sequence — **flash, sound, pause, clear** — is the same shape you used for the
telegraph signal in Reflex and the panel flash in Bright Spark. A moment made deliberately
different, then tidied away. It costs four lines and turns a silent tick into a reward.

## Why feedback matters

A game that reacts feels alive; one that does not feels broken, even when it works. The flash
and beep tell the player "yes — that counted", and the half-second pause gives the win room to
breathe before the pressure of the next deal. Sound and colour are not decoration here; they are
how the game talks back.
