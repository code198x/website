Line 160 draws a row of the four suit symbols on the title screen — the custom graphics you defined back in The Cards. `CHR$ 144` to `CHR$ 147` are the four user-defined characters; `INK 2` paints the first pair red for hearts and diamonds, and `INK 7` leaves the other pair white. A single `PRINT` turns a text title into a branded one.

## Custom graphics, reused

The suit shapes were defined once — the `DATA`/`READ`/`POKE USR` setup at the top of the program, from The Cards. Once a user-defined character exists, printing it costs nothing: it is just another `CHR$`. The same four shapes that mark the cards during play now decorate the title. Define once, use anywhere.

## What you have built

Hi-Lo is complete: a title screen with custom suit graphics, playing cards drawn from user-defined characters, higher/lower guessing with the flag pattern, streak tracking, a best score that persists across games, green/red multi-sensory feedback, a game over screen with ratings, and a replay loop. Seventy-seven lines.

The structure is becoming familiar — title, game loop, game over, ratings, replay. Each game adds its own concept (the flag pattern, the carry-forward with `LET a = b`, user-defined graphics), but the scaffolding is the same. That is not a limitation; it is a vocabulary. You now know the shape of a game.
