Lines 140–180 use `PRINT AT` to pin each piece of information at a fixed row and column. Line 140 puts the title at row 1. Line 150 puts the current number at row 3. Line 160 puts the streak at row 5. Line 180 positions the input prompt at row 9. Line 290 shows "Correct!" at row 13.

The loop starts with `CLS` (line 130), which wipes the screen, then redraws everything at the same positions. The effect is an updating dashboard rather than a scrolling log.

## Why fixed positions matter

Without `PRINT AT`, each round adds more lines and the screen scrolls. After five or six rounds, the earlier information is gone — pushed off the top. The player cannot see the current number and the streak at the same time.

With `PRINT AT`, the program owns the screen. The title stays at the top. The streak stays visible. The current number updates in place. Each piece of information has an address, and the program writes to that address every round. This is how every dashboard-style game works: a fixed layout that refreshes, not a scrolling feed.

The trailing `"   "` on lines 150 and 160 clears leftover digits. If the streak was 10 last round and 2 this round, the old "10" would leave a ghost "0" behind the new "2". Three spaces after the number wipe the extra characters.
