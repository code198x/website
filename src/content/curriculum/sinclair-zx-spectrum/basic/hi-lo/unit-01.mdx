Hi-Lo is a card game. The computer shows a number. You guess: will the next be higher or lower?

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/hi-lo/unit-01/two-numbers.bas" />

Seven lines. Line 110 generates a random number from 1 to 13 and stores it in `a`. Line 150 prints it. Line 200 generates a second random number in `b`. Line 210 prints that too. The range 1–13 mirrors a deck of cards: Ace through King.

Run it several times. Sometimes the second number is higher, sometimes lower, sometimes equal. That unpredictability is the game — if you could predict the next number, there would be nothing to play.

<Figure src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/hi-lo/unit-01/screenshot.png" alt="Black ZX Spectrum screen: 'Number: 1' above 'Next: 13'" caption="Two numbers, 1–13 — the ranks of a deck, before they become cards." />

`INT (RND * 13) + 1` is the formula you have used since Lucky Number. `RND` gives a decimal between 0 and just under 1. Multiply by 13 to stretch the range, `INT` drops the decimal part, and `+ 1` shifts the range from 0–12 to 1–13. Line 20 `RANDOMIZE` makes sure the sequence is different each run.
