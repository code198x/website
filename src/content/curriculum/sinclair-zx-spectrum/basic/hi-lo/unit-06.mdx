The game plays from the first card to game-over and round again. The last touch is a front
door: a title screen that names the game, explains it in a line or two, shows off the four card
suits you designed, and waits for the player to begin.

## The front door

Lines 100–150 build the title: `GO SUB 9000` centres "HI-LO" with the subroutine from Oracle
Stone, then a few `PRINT AT` lines give the rules — guess higher or lower, press H or L, see how
long your streak runs. Line 160 is the flourish: it prints all four suit symbols in a row, red
for hearts and diamonds, black for clubs and spades, so the player sees the custom graphics
before the first deal. Line 170's `PAUSE 0` holds the screen until they press a key.

Because the title sits at the top of the program (before line 180, where a round begins),
it shows once at the start — and the game-over screen loops back to line 180, *not* line 100, so
the player drops straight into a new game without sitting through the title again.

## What you built

Hi-Lo started as two random numbers and a guess, and became a card game with a streak to
protect. Almost all of it you already knew — `RND` to deal, a streak counter, a rating, a title,
a replay loop. The two new ideas carried the rest: **`AND`** let a single `IF` judge a guess on
two facts at once, gathered through an `ok` flag; and **user-defined graphics** let you design a
suit symbol, `POKE` it into the character set, and deal real cards instead of bare numbers.
Logic that weighs several conditions, and the power to draw your own shapes — both will be back
in almost every game from here.
