This is the biggest single step in the game. Three new ideas arrive together: **user-defined graphics** (characters you draw yourself), a **card-drawing subroutine**, and a **face-down card** for the unknown next one.

## User-defined graphics

The Spectrum lets you design your own characters. `CHR$ 144` to `CHR$ 164` are blank slots — twenty-one characters waiting for a shape. Hi-Lo claims the first four for the card suits.

A character is an 8×8 grid of pixels, stored as eight bytes — one per row. Each byte's bits switch the pixels on or off: `255` is a full row (`11111111`), `24` is two pixels in the middle (`00011000`). Lines 20–50 hold the four suit shapes as `DATA`: eight numbers each.

Line 60 installs them:

`FOR u = 0 TO 3: FOR j = 0 TO 7: READ b: POKE USR CHR$ (144 + u) + j, b: NEXT j: NEXT u`

`USR CHR$ (144 + u)` is the memory address where character `144 + u` lives. `READ b` pulls the next number from the `DATA` list, and `POKE` writes it into that character's grid, one row at a time. The outer loop picks the character; the inner loop writes its eight rows.

`DATA` and `READ` are a pair. `DATA` lists values anywhere in the program; `READ` walks through them in order, like dealing from a deck. It is the tidy way to load a table of numbers — pixel rows, level maps, lookup tables — into a program.

## Drawing a card

The card-drawing subroutine starts at line 800. Give it a value `v`, a suit `s`, and a position `cx, cy`, then `GO SUB 800`. It draws a white rectangle (`PAPER 7`, lines 810–820), prints the rank in the top-left and bottom-right corners (lines 850–860), and stamps the suit in the middle (line 880).

The rank comes from a string. Line 70 sets `f$ = "A234567890JQK"` — the thirteen faces in order. `f$(v TO v)` picks the `v`-th character: card 1 is "A", card 11 is "J", card 13 is "K". Line 840 handles the one awkward case — card 10 needs two characters, "10".

The suit is one of your custom characters: `CHR$ (143 + s)` is suit 1 to 4. Line 870 paints hearts and diamonds (suits 1 and 2) red with `INK 2`; clubs and spades stay black.

## The face-down card

The next card is a mystery until the player commits. The subroutine at line 910 draws a face-down card: a blue rectangle (`PAPER 1`) with a `?` in the middle. Line 240 draws it on the right; line 290 turns it over — drawing the real card once the guess is in.

Run it. Two cards appear — one face-up, one face-down. Guess higher or lower, and the second card turns over. The game plays exactly as before, but now it looks like cards on a table.
