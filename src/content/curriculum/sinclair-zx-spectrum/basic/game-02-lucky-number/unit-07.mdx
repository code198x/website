The game tells you "Too high!" and "Too low!" But it only speaks in words. Real games speak with colour too.

Six new lines. Line 140 computes the distance between the guess and the secret. Lines 150–190 set the border colour based on that distance. Everything else is unchanged.

Run it. Guess 50 when the answer is 73. The border turns blue — cold. Guess 70 — yellow. Guess 72 — white, burning hot. You know you are close before you read the text.

## How it works

`LET d = ABS (g - n)` calculates the **distance**. `ABS` gives the absolute value: `ABS(70 - 73)` is 3, and `ABS(76 - 73)` is also 3. Direction does not matter — only how far.

| Distance | Colour | Feeling |
|----------|--------|---------|
| Over 50 | Blue (1) | Cold |
| 26–50 | Cyan (5) | Cool |
| 11–25 | Yellow (6) | Warm |
| 6–10 | Red (2) | Hot |
| 5 or less | White (7) | Burning |

The border is **ambient feedback** — information delivered through the environment rather than text. The player reads it peripherally, without looking away from their guess.

## Two feedback channels

The game now has words ("Too low!") and colour (blue border). Both say "you are far away" but in different ways. Words give direction. Colour gives distance. Together they give more information than either alone.
