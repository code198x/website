One mistake and the game is over — that's too harsh. The player now gets three lives. Detection costs a life and resets positions. Lose all three and the game ends with a final score.

## How It Works

**Line 60** sets the starting values. `v = 3` gives three lives. `e = 0` starts the score at zero.

**Line 410** deducts a life on detection. `v = v - 1` brings the player closer to game over.

**Line 440** checks if lives have run out. `IF v = 0 THEN GO TO 500` sends the player to the game over screen.

**Lines 450-496** handle the reset. Clear the sightline, erase the guard and player, reset both to their starting positions, reset the patrol counter, and redraw the HUD. Then jump back into the game loop. The map doesn't need redrawing — only the characters moved.

**Lines 500-550** show game over. Clear the screen, display "GAME OVER" in red, show the final score, and play a descending three-note melody that sounds like defeat.

**Lines 856-860** draw the HUD. Lives and score display on the top line of the screen, always visible. The player knows how many chances remain.

## Reset Without Redraw

When the player loses a life, the game resets positions but doesn't redraw the map. The walls haven't changed — only the player and guard need to be erased and repositioned. This makes the reset instant. Redrawing the entire map would be slow and visually jarring.

## The HUD as Information

The HUD (`Lives:3 Score:0`) sits above the map. It uses the two rows left empty by the map's `+ 2` offset. The player always knows their current state without guessing.

## Try This

**More lives.** Change `v = 3` to `v = 5`. Does the game feel too easy? The number of lives is a difficulty dial.

**Score for surviving.** Add `LET e = e + 1` inside the main game loop. The longer you survive, the higher your score.
