The guard catches you by contact — walk into the same cell and it's over. But real guards don't need to bump into you. They can *see* you. The guard now projects a sightline: a line of dots extending in its facing direction until hitting a wall.

## How It Works

**Lines 700-736** draw the sightline. Starting from the guard's position, the subroutine steps one cell at a time in the guard's facing direction (`f$`). At each cell:

- **Line 728** checks boundaries — if we've gone off the map, stop.
- **Line 730** checks walls — if the cell is `#`, the wall blocks the view. Stop.
- **Line 732** checks for the player — if the player is in the sightline, detection triggers immediately.
- **Line 734** draws a yellow dot at the cell. The sightline is visible.

**Lines 760-786** clear the sightline. Before the guard moves and changes direction, erase all the dots by printing spaces along the old sightline path. This subroutine mirrors the draw subroutine but prints spaces instead of dots.

**Line 150** draws the sightline every frame with `GO SUB 700`.

**Line 245** clears the old sightline before the guard moves with `GO SUB 760`.

## Two Subroutines, One Pattern

The draw and clear subroutines use the same loop logic. Both start at the guard's position and step outward in the facing direction. The only difference is what they print: dots or spaces.

This is why subroutines exist. The sightline logic is complex enough that duplicating it would be error-prone. `GO SUB 700` and `GO SUB 760` let you call the same pattern from different places.

## Detection by Line of Sight

The sightline check on line 732 is elegant: if the stepping loop reaches the player's cell, `GO TO 400` triggers detection. The player doesn't need to touch the guard — they just need to be visible. This transforms the game from "avoid contact" to "avoid being seen."

## Try This

**Longer sightlines.** Remove some internal walls from the map data. The sightline extends further in open areas, making the guard more dangerous.

**Different dot character.** Replace `"."` with `"-"` or `CHR$ 130`. Some characters look more like a torch beam than others.
