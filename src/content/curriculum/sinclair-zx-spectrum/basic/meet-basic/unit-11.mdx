The Spectrum makes sound with a single keyword. `BEEP` plays one tone — you tell it **how
long** and **how high** — and that's enough to build a fanfare, a warning, or the blip
under a footstep. This unit is for the ears: each result is a clip to play.

## Milestone 1 — one note

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/unit-11/steps/step-01.bas" lang="sinclair-basic" />

`BEEP 1, 0` plays for **1 second** at pitch **0**. The pitch is counted in **semitones
from middle C**, so `0` is middle C itself. One line, one note:

<AudioClip
  src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/unit-11/step-01-note.wav"
  title="BEEP 1, 0 — one second of middle C"

The first number is the length in seconds; the second is the pitch. Change either and the
note changes.

## Milestone 2 — a fanfare

Put `BEEP` inside a `FOR` loop and the count becomes the pitch. Each pass plays a note one
semitone higher, so the notes climb:

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/unit-11/steps/step-02.bas" lang="sinclair-basic" />

`n` runs 0 to 12 — a full octave — and `BEEP 0.1, n` plays each step for a tenth of a
second. The title card has its fanfare:

<AudioClip
  src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/unit-11/step-02-fanfare.wav"
  title="A rising octave — thirteen short beeps climbing one semitone at a time"
  source="ZX Spectrum beeper · BEEP in a FOR loop"
/>

Twelve semitones is an octave, so the last note is exactly one octave above the first.
The loop you learned in Unit 7 just became music.

## Milestone 3 — a low buzz

A **negative** pitch goes *below* middle C. A short, deep note makes a "wrong answer"
buzz:

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/unit-11/steps/step-03.bas" lang="sinclair-basic" />

`BEEP 0.4, -24` is two octaves below middle C (24 semitones down), short and low:

<AudioClip
  src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/unit-11/step-03-buzz.wav"

A high quick beep reads as "yes"; a low buzz as "no". You'll use both in every game —
for a hit, a win, a loss, a tick of the clock.

## When it doesn't work

- **No sound at all.** Check the length isn't `0` — `BEEP 0, 0` plays for no time. And
  the volume on your machine or emulator needs to be up.
- **The program froze for a moment.** That's `BEEP` working — it holds the program while
  the note plays. A `BEEP 5, 0` ties it up for five whole seconds.
- **`Integer out of range` or a silent note.** Extreme pitches fall outside what the
  beeper can play; keep within a few octaves of middle C.

## Before and after

You started with a silent machine and finished giving it a voice — a single note, a
rising fanfare, and a low buzz — all from one keyword. The idea underneath: **`BEEP
length, pitch` plays a tone; pitch is semitones from middle C, negative for lower; and a
loop turns notes into a tune.**

## Try this

- **A falling buzz.** Loop `n` from 0 down to -12 with `STEP -1` for a descending run.
- **Two-tone alert.** Play a high note, then a low one, twice — a two-note alarm.
- **Tune the fanfare.** Change `0.1` to `0.05` for a quicker flourish, or widen the range
  past 12 for more than an octave.

## What you've learnt

- `BEEP length, pitch` plays one tone: **length in seconds**, **pitch in semitones from
  middle C**.
- **Negative** pitch is below middle C; **12 semitones** is an octave.
- `BEEP` **holds the program** until the note finishes.
- A `FOR` loop over the pitch turns single notes into a tune.
