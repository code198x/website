An immediate answer and a slowly revealed answer feel different, even when their
words are identical. We’ll try a brief visible rhythm, then express the repeated
job with a loop.

Continue with [Choose an answer](/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/unit-09-choose-an-answer/), or enter this complete listing in an
empty program. Keep its random selection in line 70; fixed answers are only for
testing particular replies.

## Repeat it by writing it again

Add **lines 81–86 and 88**:

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/oracle/unit-10/steps/step-01.bas" lang="sinclair-basic" />

After line 80 clears the screen, `PRINT ".";` writes a dot. The semicolon keeps
the next printed item on the same line. `PAUSE 10` waits for ten display frames
unless a key press cuts the wait short. Enter `PAUSE` with M after a line number.
The dot-and-pause pair appears three times. The bare `PRINT` at line 88 moves
printing to the next line before the answer heading.

On our 50-frame-per-second 48K target, ten frames are about a fifth of a second.
The program also takes time to execute its other instructions. This is a pacing
experiment, not an accurate stopwatch. Pressing a key after a pause starts can
shorten it, so keep your hands off the keys while comparing the ordinary rhythm.

Run it. Watch three dots arrive before the answer. Compare with the immediate
answer in the preceding lesson: does the wait make the reveal pleasant, or does
it get in the way after repeated consultations?

## Describe the repetition once

Replace **lines 81–84** and delete **85 and 86** by entering each line number
on its own. Keep line 88:

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/oracle/unit-10/steps/step-02.bas" lang="sinclair-basic" />

`FOR i=1 TO 3` gives the loop variable `i` the value 1. BASIC runs lines 82–83,
then `NEXT i` adds one and repeats the body while the loop still has a value to
visit. It uses 1, 2 and 3, then continues at line 88. Sinclair BASIC requires a
single-letter numeric variable for a `FOR` loop, which is why we use `i` here.

Enter `FOR` with F, `TO` with **left Option/Alt+F**, and `NEXT` with N after its
line number. `NEXT i` refers to the same loop variable as `FOR i`.

[Foundations: Doing It Again](/foundations/repeating/unit-01/) follows a counted
loop step by step. Our loop ends with `NEXT`, rather than pseudocode's `END`.
The principle is the same: one body, a stated number of visits.

<details>
<summary>Does this loop print 3, or print three dots?</summary>

Three dots. `i` controls repetition, but line 82 prints a quoted dot, not `i`.
Replacing that quoted dot with `i` would change the output, not the repeat count.

</details>

Try changing the final value in line 81 to 5. Expect five dots and a longer wait.
Restore 3. To adjust the pace, change line 83 once; all three visits use the new
pause. The repeated source version required three separate edits.

## Mark the arrival with a sound

Add **line 85**:

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/oracle/unit-10/steps/step-03.bas" lang="sinclair-basic" />

This `BEEP` lasts 0.05 seconds at middle C. It runs **after** the loop, so we hear
one note rather than three. Enter extended mode with **Shift+left Option/Alt**,
release it, then press **left Option/Alt+Z** for `BEEP`.

Listen with the volume low, then try the toy muted. The dots and answer heading
still show the change. Sound is optional feedback, not the only announcement.
To remove it, enter `85` on its own; the visible sequence remains complete.

Keep the short version for the next lesson. A longer wait is not automatically
more dramatic: the right length depends on what the player is waiting for and
how often it happens.
