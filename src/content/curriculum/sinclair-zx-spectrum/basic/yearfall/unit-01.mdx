Yearfall has no maze, no opponent, nothing hidden. The whole world is three numbers, and learning to
read them — and live with how they change — is the game. Before any decision, you need the kingdom on
screen.

## Three numbers, one world

Lines 110–120 set the starting state: `pop = 100`, `grain = 2800`, `land = 1000`. These are plain
variables, the kind you have used since Lucky Number — but their *role* here is new. Population is who
depends on you. Grain is what you spend, both food and seed from one store. Land is potential — acres
you could plant if you had the grain and the workers. The dashboard (lines 140–190) just prints them.

## State that lasts

Every earlier game's state was momentary — a guess, a board, a room — reset or replaced each turn.
Yearfall's three numbers **persist**: the grain you have left after this year is the grain you start
next year with. That is the idea the whole game rests on. There is nothing to discover and nothing
hidden; the difficulty is entirely in what you choose to do with what you can already see, knowing it
carries forward.
