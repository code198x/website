Lines 1030–1050 add the replay loop. Line 1030 prompts "Press any key to play again." Line 1040 waits. Line 1050 jumps back to line 10, which re-runs the title screen and resets all state variables.

The replay restarts from scratch — population 100, grain 2800, land 1000. Every game gets the same starting conditions but different harvests. The player brings what they learnt from the previous game: which allocation ratios work, when to buy land, how much underfeeding is survivable.

## A kingdom you can see

The replay loop makes Yearfall endlessly playable. The rest of this unit makes it look like a kingdom.

Line 70 draws a small castle on the title screen — a row of battlements built from `PLOT` and `DRAW`. It is the first thing the player sees, and the only picture in an otherwise text game.

Lines 140 and 640 replace the centred title with an inverse banner across the top of the screen: white-on-black, with the year built into it. The dashboard and the year-end report now share the same banner, so every screen states which year you are ruling.

Line 170 turns the population number into a picture. A green bar of solid blocks (`CHR$ 143`) grows one block for every ten people — `FOR j = 1 TO INT (pop / 10)`. A second `FOR` loop pads the rest of the row with spaces, clearing last year's longer bar so the gauge shrinks as well as grows. The number still prints below it, but the bar is what you feel: it lengthens in good years and pulls back when people starve.

Line 680 flashes the border red (`BORDER 2`) for the moment the starvation message shows, then restores it to black. It is a failure you see at the edges of the screen, not just read in the text.

## What you have built

Yearfall is complete. About sixty lines of BASIC: a title screen with a castle, a three-variable kingdom (population, grain, land), three allocation decisions per year (trade, feed, plant) with constraint validation, starvation and harvest calculations, population growth from births, a 10-year simulation loop with a coloured year-end report, a five-tier rating system, collapse detection, a replay loop, and a layer of visual polish — inverse-bar banners, a living population gauge, and a red border flash when people starve.

This is the first game with no opponent, no puzzle, and no hidden state. Every number is visible. Every constraint is calculable. The challenge is entirely in the trade-offs: grain spent feeding cannot seed fields, land bought now costs grain that could feed people, and a good harvest this year does not guarantee one next year. The game teaches through compounding consequences — not the consequences of a single choice, but the way ten years of choices interact.

The V2 design concept is **Patterns of State**: turn-based games where the player reasons about structured information. Yearfall is the purest expression of this — three variables, three decisions, ten iterations, and a kingdom's fate in the balance.
