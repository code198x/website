RUN. The title explains food, workforce, seed and harvest rules, and says that results are reviewed every ten years. S starts and Q quits. Other keys leave the invitation in place. Once planning begins, B buys and S sells; the same letter can have different meanings on different screens because each screen has its own input branch.

## Keep entry separate from reset

The title occupies lines before RANDOMIZE and the settlement setup. Starting seeds the random sequence, then creates the initial resources. R during play returns to 200, recreating the settlement and its traveller schedule. It does not promise to repeat the same random prices, harvests or visits.

The shared input routine still waits for release and then a fresh character. There is no fast key requirement. Yearfall is deliberately silent, and all costs and choices are expressed in text.

The finished listing is the accepted prototype, including the separate buying and selling controls, cost/proceeds feedback, traveller choices and decade continuation. Compare its complete source with the preceding checkpoint: the additions invite the player rather than silently changing the economy.

<Question prompt="The title says S starts, while the plan says S sells. Why is that not an input conflict?">

Only the active screen's input branch runs. The title handles S before settlement setup; the planning loop handles S as an editor choice. A key's meaning comes from the current interaction, not a global rule attached to the letter.

</Question>

Try the whole route: start, edit a proposal, cancel another edit, commit a harvest and restart. Check that the population, grain and land return to 60, 360 and 100, and that loss and traveller totals return to zero. Quit from a report as well as from the title.

This game has a small simulation model, not a promise of fair weather or a universally rescuable position. Its reports make the rules inspectable. Preserve this working version before changing crop yields, food requirements or arrival costs: those values interact across many years, so one successful season is a weak test of a revised economy.
