Consider 60 people, 220 grain and 100 acres, with land priced at eight grain per acre. You want to feed everyone and plant the land. Food costs 180 and planting 100 costs another 100: the plan is short by 60.

## A sale changes two sides of the budget

Selling one acre brings in eight grain and, if you plant all remaining land, saves one seed grain. Each acre sold closes nine grain of that particular shortfall. Six acres close 54, which is insufficient. Seven close 63.

| Item | Grain |
|---|---:|
| Opening store | 220 |
| Sell seven acres at eight each | +56 |
| Feed sixty people | −180 |
| Plant the remaining ninety-three acres | −93 |
| Reserve before harvest | 3 |

The plan is now valid. A yield of two produces 186 grain, leaving 189. A yield of five produces 465, leaving 468. The display's possible balance should therefore be **189 to 468**.

With no starvation, three newcomers arrive. Next year's 63 people need exactly 189 grain for food. At the poorest yield, feeding them would leave nothing for seed. Seven acres sold made this year affordable; it did not establish a safe recovery.

<Question prompt="Why is selling eight acres not automatically the best answer?">

It leaves twelve grain before harvest and ninety-two planted acres. That gives a slightly larger reserve against the poorest harvest, but permanently removes another acre unless you later buy it back. Its value depends on the next price, harvest and choices. The minimum valid sale and a preferred strategy are different questions.

</Question>

For a reproducible inspection, quit with Q, then enter `LET grain=220`, `LET price=8` and `GO TO 220` as direct commands. This deliberately sets up the example; it is not an ordinary playthrough. Set planting to 100 to see the sixty-grain shortfall. Then sell seven and plant ninety-three. Use R afterwards to restore a normal settlement.

Watch the food requirement, planted area and possible post-harvest balance during play. The program reveals the range, not the next random outcome. Some positions may already be beyond recovery; “ready” only means the current proposal satisfies its rules.
