RUN. B buys acres and S sells acres; both editors take a positive amount. The screen shows the planned acreage in the appropriate row and states how much grain will be spent or received. Entering zero clears the land plan. Cancelling or leaving an entry blank keeps the previous plan.

## One proposed land change

Internally, trade is positive for a purchase and negative for a sale. `cost=trade*price` is therefore positive when spending grain and negative when receiving it. Subtracting cost from grain handles both directions. The input controls supply the sign; the player never types it.

A new buy or sell entry replaces the previous planned transaction. It does not buy first and then sell. No land changes until Space commits a valid combined plan. The year-end report labels purchases and sales and displays their amounts without requiring the reader to interpret a negative cost.

The price is `6+INT (RND*5)`: six through ten grain per acre, fixed while that year's plan is edited. Both buying and selling use that price. `land+trade` is the proposed acreage. Validate that it is nonnegative and that planting fits it. Selling land can therefore make an unchanged planting proposal invalid.

<Question prompt="At eight grain per acre, what does selling ten acres do to the draft budget?">

trade becomes −10, so cost is −80. The proposal has ten fewer acres and eighty more grain available. Food and seed have not changed. If planting still exceeds the reduced land, Space must reject the plan.

</Question>

Try buying five acres, then cancelling a sale. The purchase should remain. Now sell ten and reduce planting to ninety before harvesting. Check that the report counts the sale proceeds once.

The new trade does not guarantee recovery. Selling assets buys breathing room but can shrink later harvests. The next lesson reads that tension from a concrete budget.
