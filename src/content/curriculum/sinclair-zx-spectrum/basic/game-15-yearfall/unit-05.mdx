A kingdom that feeds everyone attracts newcomers. A kingdom that starves people does not.

Lines 230–234 add population growth. Line 232 is the condition: if nobody starved this year (`starved = 0`), between 1 and 6 newcomers arrive. Line 234 adds them to the population. Line 240 checks for total collapse — if population reaches zero, the game ends immediately.

## The growth incentive

The births mechanic creates a clear incentive: feed everyone, and your population grows. Let people starve, and it shrinks. But growing the population increases next year's food requirement — 106 people need 2120 grain, not 2000. Growth is both reward and burden.

This creates a second-order decision. A smaller population is easier to feed. If you deliberately let population shrink by underfeeding, the survivors are cheaper to maintain. But fewer people means fewer workers (line 119 caps planting at `pop * 10` acres), which means smaller harvests. The game punishes both extremes: starve too many and you spiral down; grow too fast and you cannot keep up.

## Collapse detection

Line 240 ends the game immediately if `pop <= 0`. Without this check, the game would continue with zero people — a kingdom with no subjects, still asking the player to allocate grain to nobody. The early exit is both mechanically necessary and narratively appropriate: when the last person dies, there is no kingdom left to rule.
