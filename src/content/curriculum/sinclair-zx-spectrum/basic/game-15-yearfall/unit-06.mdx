## Investment under uncertainty

Land trading adds a third dimension to each year's decisions. Before feeding and planting, the player can reshape their kingdom. Buying land increases planting capacity but costs grain now. Selling land generates grain immediately but shrinks future potential.

The variable price creates timing decisions. At 17 grain per acre, land is cheap — buying is attractive. At 26, selling might be wiser if grain is scarce. But the player does not know next year's price. Buying cheap and selling dear requires luck, not just strategy.

## Three allocations, one supply

The trade INPUT comes first (lines 200–230), before feeding (line 240) and planting (line 260). This order matters: buying land reduces available grain before the player decides how to split the remainder between food and seed. A large land purchase might leave too little grain to feed everyone. The player must think across all three decisions before committing to the first.
