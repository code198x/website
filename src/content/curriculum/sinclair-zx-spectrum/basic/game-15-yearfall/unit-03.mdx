Grain does two things: it feeds people and it seeds fields. Every bushel you feed is a bushel you cannot plant.

Lines 260–290 add the planting INPUT with three constraints. Line 270: you cannot plant more acres than you own. Line 280: planting costs 1 grain per acre, so you cannot plant more than your remaining grain after feeding. Line 290: each person can work 10 acres, so you cannot plant more than `pop * 10`.

## The tension between feeding and planting

Line 400 deducts both feeding and planting costs: `grain = grain - feed - plant`. With 2800 grain and 100 people, full rations cost 2000, leaving only 800 for planting. But planting 800 acres at an average yield of 3 produces 2400 grain — less than you started with.

The player must find a balance. Underfeed to plant more, and people die — but the larger harvest might sustain the survivors. Overfeed and everyone lives this year, but the grain supply shrinks. This is the core of the game: every allocation has an opportunity cost.

## Random yield

`INT(RND * 5) + 1` gives a yield between 1 and 5 grain per planted acre. The player controls how many acres to plant but not how productive they are. A yield of 1 is devastating; a yield of 5 is a windfall. Over 10 years, the average is 3, but any single year can surprise you. Planning for the average and getting the worst is how kingdoms fall.
