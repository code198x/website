Line 270 clears the screen. Line 280 prints the game title. Line 300 tells the player where they are — room 1. Line 310 tells them where they can go — south or east.

That is the complete interface for a text adventure. No graphics, no sprites, no animation. Just two pieces of information: where you are, and where you can go. The player reads, imagines, and decides.

## The room as a unit

In Sonar, the grid was visual — you could see every cell at once. In The Caverns, you can only see the room you are standing in. The rest of the cave exists in your memory and imagination. This changes everything about how the game feels. A 20-room cave is tiny on paper. In the dark, with no map, it feels vast.

Right now, room 1 is hardcoded text. The exits are just the word "S E" — the program does not know what lies south or east. The next unit changes that. The room stops being a description and becomes a position in a data structure.
