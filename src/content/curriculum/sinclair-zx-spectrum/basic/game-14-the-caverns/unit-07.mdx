Lines 630–720 add the creature's movement. After the player moves and survives all hazard checks, the creature takes its turn. Line 650 picks a random direction (1–4). Lines 660–690 check whether that direction has a valid exit from the creature's current room. If not, line 700 loops back to pick again. Line 710 moves the creature to its new room.

Line 720 checks whether the creature just walked into the player's room. If so, the same death screen at line 840 fires — "The creature got you!"

## Random walk

The creature's movement is a random walk — each turn, it picks a random valid exit and takes it. It has no intelligence, no pathfinding, no awareness of the player's position. It wanders blindly, just like the player wanders in the dark.

The retry loop at lines 650–700 handles rooms where some exits do not exist. If the creature is in a room with only two exits (say, south and west), it might randomly pick north or east first. Those exits are 0 (no connection), so `nm` stays at 0 and the loop tries again. Every room has at least two exits, so the loop always terminates.

## The hunt goes both ways

Before this unit, danger was one-directional: you could walk into the creature, but it could not walk into you. Now the creature can arrive in your room between turns. You might enter a room with "All quiet." — no clues at all — and on the next turn, the creature wanders in and catches you.

This changes the meaning of "All quiet." Before, silence meant safety. Now, silence only means safety *right now*. The creature might be two rooms away, moving closer. The smell clue updates each turn because the creature's position changes. A room that was silent last turn might show a smell warning this turn — not because you moved toward the creature, but because the creature moved toward you.
