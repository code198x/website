## Colour as a sensory code

Each clue type has a distinct colour and sound. Yellow and low for the draft — cold, environmental, geological. Red and high for the creature — urgent, biological, threatening. Green and bright for treasure — rewarding, valuable, worth pursuing.

The player learns these associations within a few turns. After that, they react to the colour and pitch before they read the text. A flash of red triggers caution. A flash of green triggers excitement. The colours encode meaning the same way the clue text does, but faster.

## The title screen as frame

The title screen explains the game in six lines: how many rooms, what to collect, what to avoid, and how to survive. "Listen for clues..." is the most important line. It tells the player that the game's information system is auditory, not visual. You survive by paying attention to what the game tells you, not what it shows you.

With the debug line removed, the player enters the cave blind. They know the rules but not the layout. The dark blue screen and the silence before the first clue set the mood: you are alone in the dark, and something else is down here.
