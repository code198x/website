## A centred title — with a subroutine

Lines 30 and 160 stop printing the title directly. Instead they set up a string and call a **subroutine**:

`LET a$ = "*** THE ORACLE STONE ***": LET y = 3: GO SUB 9000`

`GO SUB 9000` jumps to line 9000, runs it, and `RETURN` (line 9010) comes straight back to where it left off. Line 9000 does the centring: `PRINT AT y, (32 - LEN a$) / 2; BRIGHT 1; a$` places the text at row `y`, in the column that leaves equal space on each side. `LEN a$` is the length of the string; the screen is 32 columns wide; so `(32 - LEN a$) / 2` is the left margin that centres it.

Both the opening screen and the answer screen want a centred title, so the work lives in one place and is called twice. That is what a subroutine is for: write it once, call it from anywhere.

## Glowing bars — a loop and a graphic

Lines 180 and 350 replace the rows of equals signs with a solid bar:

`INK 5: FOR i = 1 TO 28: PRINT CHR$ 143;: NEXT i: INK 7`

`CHR$ 143` is the Spectrum's solid block character. `FOR i = 1 TO 28 ... NEXT i` prints it twenty-eight times across — a `FOR`/`NEXT` loop, counting from 1 to 28. The trailing `;` keeps each block on the same line. In cyan, the bars match the answer.

## A pulsing answer — FLASH

Line 220 adds `FLASH 1` alongside the cyan; line 330 turns it off with `FLASH 0`. `FLASH 1` makes the ink and paper swap back and forth, so the answer pulses — pulling the eye straight to the Oracle's word.

## Make it yours

Write your own answers. Change the colours — `PAPER 0` (black) with `INK 6` (yellow) for a different mood. Change the prompt: "O Wise One, I ask thee:". Show it to someone, ask them to ask a question, and watch their face when the answer flashes up. The ceremony works — even when they know it is random.
