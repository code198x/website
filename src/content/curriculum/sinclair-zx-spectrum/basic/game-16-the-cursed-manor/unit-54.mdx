Free guests can move and act, but the player cannot speak to them. TALK TO COLONEL opens a dialogue — the Colonel responds with a line of speech that varies based on the game state. Dialogue makes NPCs feel like characters, not furniture.

## The TALK Command

TALK TO is a two-word verb (TALK TO) followed by a name. The parser needs to handle this — the verb is "TALK" and the noun includes "TO COLONEL". Strip the "TO " prefix from the noun to get the target name.

## How It Works

**Lines 1800-1860** handle the TALK verb. First, strip "TO " from the noun if present. Then search the guest array for a matching name. Check the guest's state:

- **Frozen:** "You speak, but the stone figure does not respond."
- **Waking:** "They mumble something incoherent, still caught between stone and flesh."
- **Free:** Display the guest's dialogue line.

**Lines 9700-9730** store dialogue lines in DATA. Each free guest has 3-4 dialogue options, selected randomly:

- **Colonel:** "There was a woman — Elara. She cursed us all." / "I remember the toast. Then... nothing." / "We owe you our lives."
- **Maid:** "I was just opening the door, and then..." / "The house feels different now. Warmer." / "Thank you. I did not think anyone would come."
- **Dr Hartley:** "Fascinating. The binding used sympathetic resonance." / "I must write this up. A genuine curse, broken by name and flame." / "My research was right — names have power."

**Dialogue reflects the aftermath.** The guests remember being frozen. They are grateful, confused, and processing what happened. The Colonel is stoic. The Maid is emotional. The Doctor is analytical. Each voice is distinct.

**Talking to the cat** produces: "The cat looks at you impassively." Animals do not talk in this game.

## Try This

**Talk to every free guest.** Do their lines feel natural? Does each voice sound different from the others?

**Talk multiple times.** The random selection means you might get the same line twice. Add enough options (4-5 per guest) that repetition feels rare.

**Talk to frozen guests.** The response should feel eerie. The player is speaking to a statue. The silence is the point.
