Five clues, one puzzle chain. Each clue should point the player toward an action or location. Together, they form a breadcrumb trail: find the key, unlock the tower, read the grimoire, discover the combination, open the sealed chamber, retrieve the amulet, light the cold fire, speak the true name, and perform the ritual. This unit refines the clues until they guide without giving everything away.

## Clue Design

A good clue narrows the search without eliminating the challenge. "The answer is in room 14" is too direct. "Look to the stars" is too vague. "The tower holds the answer" tells the player where to go without telling them what they will find there.

## How It Works

**The refined clue chain** works as follows:

1. **The Maid** points to the cellar: "She gestures downward with frozen hands." The cellar contains the tinderbox.
2. **Mrs Pemberton** reveals symbols: "The back of her recipe card shows three symbols — moon, crown, star." This is part of the combination.
3. **Colonel Blackwood** mentions the tower: "His note reads: If you seek to end this, climb." The tower holds the grimoire.
4. **Dr Hartley** explains the ritual: "The book describes a binding of name and flame." You need the true name and fire.
5. **Lady Ashworth** whispers the key word: "Her lips form a silent word — it looks like 'Elara'." This is the true name, confirmed by the portrait.

**The clues interlock.** The Maid leads to the tinderbox (needed for fire). The Colonel leads to the tower (where the grimoire waits). Dr Hartley explains what you need (name and flame). Mrs Pemberton gives the combination (for the sealed chamber). Lady Ashworth gives the name (confirmed by the portrait).

**No single clue solves the puzzle.** The player must find and combine multiple clues. This creates a satisfying "aha" moment when the pieces fit together.

## Try This

**Map the clue chain.** Draw a diagram showing how each clue leads to the next step. Every puzzle step should be reachable through at least one clue. If any step has no clue pointing to it, the player might get stuck.

**Test with fresh eyes.** Read only the clues, without knowing the puzzle solution. Can you work out what to do? If a clue is too obscure, reword it. If it is too obvious, add ambiguity.

**Add redundancy.** If the player misses one guest, can they still solve the puzzle? Consider adding a secondary clue source — a note in a drawer, writing on a wall — that duplicates one of the guest clues.
