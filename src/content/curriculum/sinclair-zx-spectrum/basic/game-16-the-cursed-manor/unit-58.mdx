The cat has been wandering the manor since unit 12, but its behaviour is basic — random movement and a static description. Now give it personality. The cat reacts to its environment: it avoids the cellar, it watches the frozen guests with suspicion, it follows the player after the ritual, and it purrs near the fireplace.

## Cat Behaviours

The cat's turn now includes context checks. Instead of always moving randomly, it considers where it is and what is nearby. These are not complex AI decisions — just a few IF checks that make the cat feel more alive.

## How It Works

**Lines 2700-2740** replace the cat's simple random movement with contextual behaviour:

**Avoid the cellar.** If a random exit leads to a dark room, pick again. Cats dislike the dark (in this game, at least). This prevents the cat from disappearing into rooms the player has not lit yet.

**Linger near the fireplace.** If the cat is in the drawing room (room 7), there is a 60% chance it stays put instead of moving. "The cat stretches lazily by the fireplace." Cats like warmth.

**React to frozen guests.** If the cat enters a room with a frozen guest, the description changes: "The cat sits near the stone figure, watching it with unblinking eyes." This is eerie and reinforces the curse.

**Follow the player after the ritual.** Once `rf = 1`, the cat has a 40% chance of moving toward the player's room instead of randomly. "The cat pads alongside you." A freed manor means a friendlier cat.

**Room-specific descriptions** replace the generic "A black cat sits here, watching you." In the kitchen: "The cat sits on the table, cleaning a paw." In the library: "The cat is perched on a bookshelf, peering down at you." In the garden: "The cat stalks through the long grass."

## Try This

**Observe the cat in different rooms.** Walk around and note the cat descriptions. Each should feel appropriate to the room. A cat on a bookshelf makes sense; a cat on a bookshelf in the cellar does not.

**Check the post-ritual behaviour.** After the ritual, does the cat follow the player more often? The shift from independent to companionable should be noticeable over several turns.

**Try EXAMINE CAT.** Add an examine description for the cat: "A lean black cat with bright green eyes. It seems perfectly at home here, as if the curse never touched it." The cat's immunity to the curse is a small mystery that the game never explains.
