Visual atmosphere is half the story. Sound completes it. The Spectrum's BEEP command is limited — one channel, one note at a time — but short, carefully chosen sounds create mood. A low creak when a door opens. A high whistle for wind in the tower. A rhythmic tick for the clock in the hallway.

## Sound as Feedback

Sound serves two purposes: atmosphere and feedback. Atmospheric sounds play when entering certain rooms or during events. Feedback sounds confirm actions — a satisfying click when picking up an item, a dull thud when a door is locked. Both make the game feel more responsive.

## How It Works

**Lines 900-920** define sound effect subroutines. Each is a short sequence of BEEP commands. A door creak uses a falling pitch: `BEEP 0.1, 20: BEEP 0.1, 18: BEEP 0.1, 15`. The wind is a longer, lower tone: `BEEP 0.5, -10`. Footsteps alternate two quick notes: `BEEP 0.05, 5: BEEP 0.05, 3`.

**Lines 410-412** add a door creak sound when the player moves between rooms. The sound plays before the new room description appears, creating the sensation of a door opening.

**Lines 260-270** add room-specific ambient sounds. The entrance hall has a ticking clock. The tower has wind. The cellar has dripping water (quick high-low notes). These play during the display phase, after the description.

**Sound is optional.** Every BEEP takes real time — the game pauses while the sound plays. Keep effects short (under 0.3 seconds total) to avoid breaking the rhythm of play. The player should never feel like they are waiting for a sound to finish.

## Try This

**Create a new sound effect.** Design a sound for the chapel — perhaps a low, resonant tone suggesting an echo. Test different pitch and duration values until it feels right.

**Add a movement sound.** Play a short footstep sound whenever the player moves, regardless of destination. Two quick BEEPs at slightly different pitches create a convincing step.

**Make sounds conditional.** The wind should only play in the tower, not every room. The clock should only tick in rooms adjacent to the entrance hall. Use the room number to control which sounds play where.
