One illustration proves the concept. Now add drawings for the rooms that matter most — the library (books on shelves), the kitchen (a table and stove), the garden (trees and a path), and the tower base (a spiral staircase). Not every room needs a unique illustration, but the key locations should have one.

## A Drawing Per Room

Each room that has an illustration gets its own subroutine. `ON r GO SUB` dispatches to the correct one. Rooms without illustrations skip the drawing phase — the text description carries the load alone.

## How It Works

**Lines 700-740** remain the entrance hall drawing from the previous unit.

**Lines 750-780** draw the library: vertical lines for bookshelves, small rectangles for books. The shelves fill the right side of the frame, creating an immediately recognisable scene.

**Lines 790-820** draw the kitchen: a rectangle for the table, a box for the stove with a chimney line rising from it. Simple shapes, but the player knows exactly what room they are in.

**Lines 830-860** draw the garden: a wavy ground line, two triangular trees, and a winding path. The outdoor scene feels different from the interior drawings, reinforcing the transition from inside to outside.

**Lines 870-900** draw the tower base: a narrow vertical rectangle for the doorway, and a spiral of connected lines suggesting a winding staircase. The height of the drawing communicates the tower's verticality.

**Line 210** uses `ON r GO SUB` to select the correct drawing. Room numbers without illustrations map to a dummy subroutine that returns immediately.

## Try This

**Add one more illustration.** Choose a room that matters to the story — perhaps the chapel or the cellar. Draw something recognisable in under 10 lines of PLOT and DRAW.

**Refine an existing drawing.** Pick the illustration you like least and improve it. Add a detail, adjust proportions, or change the composition. Small changes make a big difference in simple line art.

**Test all rooms.** Walk through every room and check that illustrated rooms show their drawing and non-illustrated rooms display cleanly without a blank gap at the top.
