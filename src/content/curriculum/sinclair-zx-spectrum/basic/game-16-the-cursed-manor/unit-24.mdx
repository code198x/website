The manor has rooms and items. Now it needs people. Five guests came to the manor and were frozen by the curse — turned to stone mid-action. They stand in different rooms, each holding a clue to breaking the curse. The player must find them, examine them, and eventually free them.

## Guest Data

Each guest has a name, a room location, a state (frozen or free), and a clue. Like items, guests are stored in parallel arrays. Unlike items, guests cannot be picked up — they are part of the scenery until the ritual frees them.

## How It Works

**Lines 34-37** dimension the guest arrays. `DIM g$(5,20)` for names, `DIM gl(5)` for locations, `DIM gs(5)` for state (0 = frozen, 1 = free), `DIM gc$(5,80)` for clues.

**Lines 92-96** initialise the five guests:

| Guest | Name | Room |
|-------|------|------|
| 1 | Colonel Blackwood | 7 (drawing room) |
| 2 | Lady Ashworth | 9 (landing) |
| 3 | Dr Hartley | 10 (study) |
| 4 | Mrs Pemberton | 6 (kitchen) |
| 5 | The Maid | 4 (corridor) |

**Lines 285-295** display guests in the room description. If a guest is in the current room and frozen, the game prints "Colonel Blackwood stands here, frozen like a statue." If the guest is free (after the ritual), the description changes to show them moving and speaking.

**All guests start frozen.** `gs(i) = 0` for all five. The game displays them as stone figures — eerie, motionless, mid-gesture. This visual reinforces the curse and gives the player a goal: free these people.

## Try This

**Find all five guests.** Walk the manor and note which room each guest occupies. Their positions should feel natural — the Colonel in the drawing room, the Maid in the corridor, the Doctor in the study.

**Read the frozen descriptions.** Each guest is frozen in a specific pose. The Colonel has his hand raised as if giving a toast. Lady Ashworth is reaching for the banister. Dr Hartley is bent over a book. These details make the curse feel real.

**Think about the clues.** Each guest holds information about breaking the curse. What kind of clues would make sense? A name, a location, a sequence, a warning? Plan the clue chain before writing it.
