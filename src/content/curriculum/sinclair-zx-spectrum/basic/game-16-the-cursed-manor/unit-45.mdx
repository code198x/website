When guests move or act in other rooms, the player should sometimes hear them. Footsteps on the floor above. A voice echoing down the corridor. A door creaking in a nearby room. Distant events make the manor feel larger than the current room and remind the player that NPCs exist even when unseen.

## Adjacent Room Awareness

After each NPC turn, check whether any NPC acted in a room adjacent to the player. If so, print a vague sensory message — not "The Colonel walked into the library" but "You hear heavy footsteps in a nearby room." The player does not know exactly what happened, just that something is alive out there.

## How It Works

**Lines 2300-2340** check for distant events. After all NPC turns, the code loops through NPCs that acted (moved or performed an action) and checks whether their room is adjacent to the player's room. "Adjacent" means connected by any exit from the player's current room.

**Lines 2350-2370** select a distant event message. The message depends on the type of action and the direction. If a guest moved in a room to the north: "You hear footsteps from the north." If a guest acted in a room above: "You hear movement upstairs." If the cat moved nearby: "You hear a soft padding of paws."

**Vertical distance** creates distinct messages. Footsteps above feel different from footsteps in the next room. "Something creaks on the floor above" versus "You hear movement to the east." The direction tells the player where the sound comes from.

**Frequency control** — not every adjacent NPC action produces a distant event. A 50% chance prevents message overload. When the manor has five free guests and a cat, distant events could fire every turn. The random filter keeps it occasional and atmospheric.

**Distant events do not identify the source.** "Heavy footsteps" hints at the Colonel, but the player cannot be certain. This ambiguity is intentional — it creates curiosity and encourages exploration.

## Try This

**Free all guests and listen.** Stay in a central room (the corridor) and take turns. Adjacent-room events should appear occasionally, coming from different directions.

**Note the direction.** Are the direction labels correct? If you hear footsteps "from the north" and go north, is there a guest there? The directions should be geographically accurate.

**Adjust the frequency.** Try 30% and 70% chances. Too few distant events and the manor feels empty. Too many and they become noise. Find the balance where each event feels noteworthy.
