## Why centre, then corners?

The centre is the strongest opening position. It sits on four winning lines (row 2, column 2, both diagonals). A corner sits on three. An edge sits on only two. Controlling the centre gives the computer more opportunities to build threats and more lines to watch for blocks.

After the centre, corners are stronger than edges because they share diagonals with the centre. The priority list `5 → 1,3,7,9 → edges` encodes decades of Noughts and Crosses wisdom in six lines of BASIC.

Play against it now. The computer takes the centre if you do not, blocks your lines, and takes its own wins. Most games end in draws. That is correct — with optimal play on both sides, Noughts and Crosses always draws. The fun is understanding why.
