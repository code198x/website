Three in a Row is Noughts and Crosses on the Spectrum — but you are not playing against a friend. The computer is your opponent, and it gets smarter as you build it.

You start with a dumb opponent that picks random squares. Then you teach it to block your winning moves. Then to spot its own winning opportunities. Then to prefer the centre and corners. By the end, the computer plays well enough that most games end in draws — and understanding why is the point.

**What you will learn:**

- Arrays as a game board — nine positions, three states
- DATA as a lookup table — eight winning lines stored as triples
- `GO SUB` for reusable win detection
- Game AI built in layers: random, reactive, proactive, strategic
- Why draws are the correct outcome of perfect play

**11 units. About 10–14 hours.**
