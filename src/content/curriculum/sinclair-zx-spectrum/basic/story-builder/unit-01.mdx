### Changing a line

Now change the word. Type line 10 again, with a new message:

Press Enter, then type `RUN`. The old line 10 is gone — replaced. The computer now says `Goodbye`. You did not start over; you re-used the line number, and the new line took its place.

<Figure src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/story-builder/unit-01/goodbye.png" alt="The Spectrum screen showing the word Goodbye, with the report 0 OK, 10:1." caption="Same line number, new message — line 10 replaced. That is the first rule of the line number: it is the line's *address*. Write to an address that already holds a line, and you replace what was there.

## Milestone 3 — slip a line in between

Here is why the numbers jump in tens. Suppose you want a line of dashes *between* the welcome and the next line. You don't retype everything — you give the new line a number that falls in the gap: 25, sitting between 20 and 30.

Type line 25 on its own and press Enter. You don't move anything else; the Spectrum files it in numerical order. Run it, and the dashes appear between the first message and the second — line 25 runs after 20 and before 30.

<Figure src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/story-builder/unit-01/step-03-dashes.png" alt="The Spectrum screen with a line of dashes between the welcome message and the next line." caption="Line 25 runs between 20 and 30 — the dashes land in the middle, without touching the lines around them." />

This is why programs number their lines in tens, not ones: it leaves room to slip a line in between. We lean on that habit all through Story Builder — every gap in the numbering is space held open for something a later unit adds.

## When it doesn't work

The first program fails in small, recognisable ways:

- **`Nonsense in BASIC` or the line won't accept.** A typo in the keyword or a missing quotation mark. `PRINT` needs both quotes around its text — `PRINT "Hello"`, not `PRINT "Hello`. On the Spectrum, `PRINT` comes from a single keypress (`P`) in keyword mode, not typed letter by letter.
- **The lines print in the wrong order.** The line numbers, not the typing order, decide the order. Whatever you want first needs the lowest number. List the program (`LIST`) and read the numbers top to bottom — that is the order it runs.
- **Old text is still on screen.** No `CLS`. Without a clear, each `RUN` prints on top of whatever was already there. Line 10's `CLS` wipes the slate first.
- **Your new line replaced one you wanted to keep.** You re-used a number that was already taken. Every line you want to keep needs its own number; pick a free one in a gap (like 25) to slot between, not on top of, existing lines.

## Before and after

You started with a blank machine and finished with a three-line program you grew a line at a time — adding lines, replacing a line, and slipping one into a gap. The whole unit rests on one idea: **the line number is an address, and the program runs them in order.** Everything Story Builder does later is more lines, filed into the gaps you're leaving now.

## Try this

- **A longer welcome.** Add line 35 — another message, between the dashes-and-line-30 and line 40. Predict where it lands before you run it.
- **Reuse the gap.** Change line 25 from dashes to your name. Same number, new content — the replacement rule from Milestone 1, used to edit a line in the middle of a program.
- **Break it on purpose.** Delete the `CLS` (type `10` and Enter to remove line 10), run it twice, and watch the screen fill up. Then put it back. Seeing the failure is how you'll recognise it later.

## What you've learnt

- A program is a list of **numbered lines**, run from lowest number to highest — not in the order you typed them.
- A line number is the line's **address**: re-use it to replace the line, pick a free one to add a line.
- `PRINT` shows text; `CLS` clears the screen.
- **Number in tens** so there's always a gap to slip a line into later — the habit the whole game depends on.
