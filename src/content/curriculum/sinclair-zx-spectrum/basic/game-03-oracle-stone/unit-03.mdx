The Oracle answers instantly. That is wrong — an Oracle should think. The pause before the answer is where the magic lives.

Lines 70–90 create the thinking sequence:

- `PRINT "The Oracle ponders..."` — tells the player something is happening.
- `BEEP 0.3, 20: BEEP 0.3, 15: BEEP 0.3, 10: BEEP 0.3, 5` — four descending tones, about 1.2 seconds total. The sound of deep thought.
- `PAUSE 25` — half a second of silence after the tones. The breath before the answer.

Line 100: `CLS` clears everything — the question, the thinking message, all of it. The answer gets the screen to itself.

Line 120: `BEEP 0.1, 24` — a single clear tone. "Here it comes."

## Anticipation

The embedded concept for Oracle Stone is **Anticipation** — the feeling of waiting for something you know is coming but cannot control. The thinking sequence creates anticipation deliberately:

1. The player asks a question.
2. "The Oracle ponders..." — the player knows an answer is coming.
3. Descending tones — something is happening, but what?
4. Silence — the pause holds the tension.
5. The screen clears — old context disappears.
6. A clear tone — now.
7. The answer appears.

Steps 2–6 take about two seconds. Without them, the answer would appear in a tenth of a second. Those two seconds are the difference between "the computer picked a random string" and "the Oracle considered my question."

## Try it

Make the thinking longer: `PAUSE 100` instead of 25. Does it feel more dramatic or just slow? Make it shorter: `PAUSE 5`. Does the answer feel rushed?

The sweet spot is personal — but there is always a sweet spot. Too short feels mechanical. Too long feels broken. Find the length where you lean forward slightly.
