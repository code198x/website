## What changed

**Line 10:** `BORDER 1: PAPER 1: INK 7: CLS` — dark blue background, white text, blue border. The screen goes from the Spectrum's default grey-and-black to a midnight palette. The mood shifts before a word appears.

**Lines 30–40:** The title — `*** THE ORACLE STONE ***` — centred with leading spaces, framed by blank lines. The asterisks are decoration, but they make the title feel like a nameplate rather than a heading.

**Lines 180, 350:** Decorative lines of equals signs above and below the answer. A simple frame — but the answer now lives inside a visual container rather than floating alone on the screen.

**Line 220:** `INK 5` — cyan. The answer prints in a different colour from the surrounding text. The colour shift says "this is the Oracle's voice" without using words. Line 330 switches back to `INK 7` (white) for the closing frame.

## Colour as atmosphere

The Spectrum has 8 ink colours and 8 paper colours. That is a narrow palette — but narrowness forces choices, and choices create identity.

Dark blue paper with white and cyan ink feels cold, distant, mystical. Swap to `PAPER 0` (black) with `INK 4` (green) and the Oracle feels like a computer terminal. Swap to `PAPER 2` (red) with `INK 6` (yellow) and it feels like a warning.

The same program, the same answers, the same thinking sequence — but the colours change the mood entirely. Colour is not decoration. It is the cheapest, most powerful tool for establishing atmosphere on the Spectrum.

## What you have built

A complete fortune-telling program: dark chamber, mystical title, dramatic thinking sequence, randomly chosen answer in a distinctive colour, framed with decorative lines. Twenty lines of BASIC that feel like a place you visit to ask a question.

No new commands in this game — everything was available after Lucky Number. What changed is how you used them. `IF/THEN` became a selection pattern. `PAUSE` and `BEEP` became dramatic tools. `INK` and `PAPER` became atmosphere. The same vocabulary, applied differently, produces a completely different experience.

| What you practised | Where |
|-------------------|-------|
| Chained `IF/THEN` selection | Units 2, 4 |
| `PAUSE` + `BEEP` for timing | Unit 3 |
| `INK` / `PAPER` / `BORDER` for mood | Unit 5 |
| `CLS` as a scene transition | Unit 3 |
| PRINT-character decoration | Unit 5 |

And two design concepts:

- **Chance** — the program selects from authored content. The player experiences unpredictability; the programmer wrote every possibility.
- **Anticipation** — the pause before the answer matters more than the answer itself.
