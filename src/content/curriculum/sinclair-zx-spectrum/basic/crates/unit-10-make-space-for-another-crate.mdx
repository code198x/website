Room three begins at 8200. Line 4003 selects it, while line 810 now wraps room **4** back to 1. The message conditions change so rooms one and two offer the next room and room three offers a new game.

There are no new collision cases. A crate still needs floor or a target beyond it. Another crate is neither, so an adjacent pair cannot move together. Both targets contribute to the same counter; filling one leaves one uncovered.

## Make room to work

Reach room three and try I from the starting position. The player is at `(6,4)`, behind crates at `(5,4)` and `(4,4)`. Nothing should move and STEPS should remain zero. The failed push has preserved both crates.

Try to solve it before opening the route. Think about the square you will need to **stand on** for a later push, as well as the destination of the crate you are moving now. A crate can block either one.

<Question prompt="After the first delivery, GOALS reads 1/2. Should N advance to a new game?">

No. One uncovered target remains, so left is 1 and won stays zero. Progression depends on completing the room, not on the latest push having delivered a crate.

</Question>

<details>
<summary>One route through the two-crate room</summary>

From the fresh room, press **J, I, L**. This parks the lower crate to the right at `(5,5)`, giving access to the upper crate. Then **I, I** pushes that upper crate into the deep target at `(2,4)`. GOALS is 1/2; the room remains active.

Continue **K, L, L, K, J**. You get round to the right of the parked crate and push it left into column 4. Finish with **K, J, I, I** to get underneath and push it onto the remaining target at `(3,4)`.

The complete route is fourteen successful steps and six pushes. The first crate passes across the nearer target before reaching the deeper one. Leaving one target and entering another in that push keeps the uncovered count unchanged.

</details>

Check R before completion: both crates and the player must return to their initial positions and GOALS to 0/2. Finish, try a movement key, replay with R, then finish again and press N. Room one should return with one crate, one target and zero steps. Q still leaves from either active play or success.

The extra difficulty lives in the map. The same movement rule can ask a new question when its objects obstruct each other's access. If you design another layout, try explaining the decisions it asks for before counting how many crates it contains.
