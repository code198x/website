## Two-cell lookahead

Most movement checks look one cell ahead: "is the destination walkable?" Pushing requires looking two cells ahead: "is the destination a crate, and is the cell beyond the crate empty?" The direction offsets `dy` and `dx` serve double duty — they locate both the crate and the space behind it. The same pair of values that moved the player now also moves the crate.

This is the core mechanic of every Sokoban-style puzzle. One rule: you can push a crate one cell, but only if the cell beyond is empty. You cannot pull. You cannot push two crates at once (because the beyond cell would contain a crate, not floor). You cannot push into a wall. From this single constraint, every puzzle in the game emerges.

## Array first, screen second

The update order matters. The array changes at lines 550 and 193 — the crate's new and old positions. The screen follows with PRINT AT statements. If the program drew first and updated the array later, a crash between the two steps would leave the display and the data out of sync. Array first, screen second. The authority leads; the display follows.
