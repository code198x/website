This is the rule that makes Crates a game. Walking into a crate pushes it one cell — but only if
there is room on the far side. From that single constraint every puzzle in the game grows, and in code
it is a *two-cell* lookahead: check the crate, then check the space beyond it.

## Looking two cells ahead

When the destination holds a crate (`v = 3`), line 440 branches to the push routine. Lines 520–530
find the cell *beyond* the crate by stepping the same direction again — `br = nr + dy`, `bc = nc + dx`
— and line 540 is the rule: `IF bv <> 0 THEN GO TO 320`, the push fails unless that far cell is empty
floor. The offsets `dy, dx` do double duty, locating both the crate and the space behind it. Walls
need a one-cell lookahead; pushing needs two, and that second look is the entire mechanic.

## One rule, every puzzle

Everything Sokoban is famous for falls out of this one check. You cannot pull — the routine only ever
moves a crate ahead of you. You cannot push two crates at once — the cell beyond the first would hold
the second, not floor, so the push fails. You cannot shove a crate into a wall. And a crate pushed into
a corner is stuck forever, because neither of its remaining exits is clear. No special cases encode
those rules; they are all consequences of "the far cell must be empty".

## Array first, screen second

On a successful push the order is deliberate: line 550 writes the crate into its new cell in `g`, line
590 clears its old cell, and the player advances into the gap — each change made to the array first,
then mirrored to the screen with `PRINT AT`. The array is the truth; the display follows it. Keep that
order and the picture can never disagree with the state.
