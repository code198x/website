Lines 10–70 add the title screen. `BORDER 0: PAPER 0: INK 7` sets a dark palette — black border, black paper, white text. Three lines explain the rules: push crates, use I/J/K/L, you can push but never pull. PAUSE 0 waits for a key press before starting.

Line 80 initialises the move counter. Line 140 resets it to 0 at the start of each level. Line 500 increments `moves` after a plain step, and lines 630–640 do the same after a push — every successful move counts, and each path updates the total in the top-right corner. Line 340 also resets the counter on restart. Line 190 prints the initial count.

Line 540 adds a low BEEP (`0.05, -5`) when a push is blocked — the crate cannot move. Lines 730 play a rising three-note jingle (`BEEP 0.1, 10: BEEP 0.1, 15: BEEP 0.1, 20`) when a level is completed. The blocked-push sound is short and flat. The completion sound rises. The contrast makes success feel earned.

## The move counter as a second challenge

The move counter does not affect whether the player wins. It adds a second, self-imposed challenge: can you solve the level in fewer moves? A player who completes level 1 in 15 moves might restart and try for 10. The counter turns a single puzzle into a repeatable optimisation problem without adding any mechanical complexity — just one variable, bumped wherever the player successfully moves.

## Sound as feedback, not decoration

The blocked-push BEEP at line 540 serves a functional purpose: it tells the player their input was received but the action was impossible. Without it, a blocked push looks identical to no input at all — nothing happens. The sound distinguishes "you pressed a key and the push failed" from "the program did not register your key press." Feedback sounds are information. The completion jingle is celebration, but the blocked-push buzz is communication.
