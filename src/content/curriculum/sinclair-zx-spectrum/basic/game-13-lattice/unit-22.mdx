When a connection is rejected because it crosses an existing line, the player needs clear feedback. A low beep alone is not enough — the player might not notice it, or might not understand why the connection failed. A red flash along the proposed line shows exactly where the crossing would occur.

## How It Works

**Lines 130-150** handle a rejected crossing. Instead of just beeping, the program now draws the proposed line in red (INK 2), pauses briefly, then erases it by redrawing in black (INK 0). After the flash, the program does a full redraw to restore any pixels that were affected.

**Lines 700-740** are the rejection animation subroutine. It PLOTs to the start node, DRAWs to the end node in red, pauses for 10 frames (about 0.2 seconds), then redraws the same line in black. The pause gives the player enough time to see where the line would go and why it was rejected.

**Lines 750-780** redraw the puzzle. Because the red line and its black erasure may have overwritten node circles or existing connections, a full redraw restores everything to the correct state.

## Why Flash in Red?

Red universally signals rejection or error. The player sees a red line appear briefly along the path they attempted, which does two things:

1. **Confirms the action was received.** The player knows they pressed the right keys and selected valid nodes — the rejection is about the line path, not the input.
2. **Shows where the conflict is.** The red line visually overlaps with the existing line that blocks it. The player can see the exact point of crossing.

This is better than a text message like "Lines cross!" because it uses the same visual language as the rest of the game — lines on screen.

## Animation Timing

The flash must be long enough to see but short enough not to feel sluggish. PAUSE 10 gives about 0.2 seconds at the Spectrum's 50fps timing. PAUSE 5 is too quick to register. PAUSE 20 feels like the program has stalled. The sweet spot is 8-12 frames.

## Try This

**Trigger a rejection.** Draw one connection, then try to draw another that crosses it. Does the red flash clearly show the conflict?

**Try different pause values.** Change PAUSE 10 to PAUSE 5 and PAUSE 20. Which feels best? The right timing is subjective — pick what feels responsive without being too brief to notice.
