## How It Works

**Lines 10-30** introduce a difficulty band system. Puzzles are grouped into three bands:

- **Band 1** (puzzles 1-5): 4-5 nodes, 4-6 connections. Simple layouts, obvious solutions.
- **Band 2** (puzzles 6-15): 5-7 nodes, 6-9 connections. Grid and irregular layouts.
- **Band 3** (puzzles 16-25): 7-10 nodes, 9-14 connections. Circular and complex layouts.

**Lines 40-50** determine the band from the puzzle number and store it in variable `bn`. The band affects the difficulty label shown on the HUD.

**Lines 1200-1400** contain the new Band 3 puzzle DATA. These puzzles use combinations of the layout techniques from previous units — irregular positions, circular arrangements, and mixed grids. Each puzzle has been tested to ensure at least one valid solution exists.

## Difficulty Scaling

Difficulty in Lattice comes from three factors:

**Node count.** More nodes mean more potential crossings to track. A five-node puzzle has few spatial conflicts. A ten-node puzzle has many.

**Connection density.** The ratio of connections to nodes determines how crowded the puzzle becomes. Higher density means more lines competing for space.

**Layout geometry.** Grid layouts offer clear spatial reasoning. Circular layouts make crossings harder to judge. Irregular layouts combine both challenges.

Band 3 puzzles increase all three factors. The player must develop strategies for managing complexity — perhaps starting with the longest connections first, or working from the outside inward.

## Testing Puzzles

Every puzzle must be solvable. Before adding a puzzle to the DATA, solve it on paper. Draw the nodes, then try to draw all connections without crossings. If you cannot find a valid order, the puzzle is either unsolvable (a design error) or extremely hard (which might be intentional for a final challenge).
