The player needs to see their progress at a glance — which puzzle they are on, how many connections remain, and their current score. A heads-up display along the bottom two rows of the screen provides this information without overlapping the puzzle area.

## How It Works

**Lines 10-20** reserve the bottom two character rows for the HUD by keeping all node positions above pixel y = 20. This ensures circles and lines do not overlap the status text.

**Lines 500-540** are the HUD subroutine. It uses PRINT AT to display information on rows 21 and 22:

- **Row 21** shows the puzzle number and total score
- **Row 22** shows connections drawn out of the total required

**Lines 30-40** call the HUD subroutine after initial setup and after every successful connection or undo. The display updates in real time as the player makes progress.

## Mixing Graphics and Text

Lattice uses pixel graphics (PLOT, DRAW, CIRCLE) for the puzzle area and character graphics (PRINT AT) for the HUD. These two systems coexist on the same screen. The key is to keep them in separate regions — pixel graphics stay in the upper portion, text stays in the bottom rows.

The division is based on the character grid. Each character cell is 8x8 pixels. Row 21 starts at pixel y = 8 (from the bottom), and row 22 at pixel y = 0. Keeping node positions above y = 20 leaves a comfortable margin.

## Updating the HUD

The HUD must update whenever the game state changes:

- A connection is drawn — increment the count
- An undo is performed — decrement the count
- A puzzle is completed — show the result
- A new puzzle loads — reset the count

Calling the HUD subroutine after each of these events keeps the display consistent. The subroutine always prints the complete HUD, overwriting the previous values. This is simpler than tracking which specific number changed.
