Four coloured panels fill the screen. The computer plays a sequence — each panel flashes bright with a distinct tone. Watch carefully. Now repeat it. Get it right and the sequence grows longer. Get it wrong and it's over.

![Wrong — the sequence was 2 long](/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-03-bright-spark/overview/wrong.png)

## How It Works

Bright Spark teaches **pattern recognition** — how sequences, memory, and increasing difficulty create engagement. The game is simple: watch and repeat. But the simplicity is the point. Each round adds one element to the sequence. Your memory is the limit. The moment you fail, you know exactly what went wrong — you can almost see the sequence you missed. That makes you want to try again.

This is also where `FOR/NEXT` loops stop being abstract. Every panel is drawn with a loop. Every sequence is played with a loop. Every flash is a loop. Loops are the tool that makes the Spectrum paint.

## What You'll Learn

- **FOR/NEXT** — counted loops
- **STEP** — counting backwards, counting by twos
- **INK/PAPER/BORDER/BRIGHT** — the full colour model
- **Nested loops** — a loop inside a loop
- **The screen as a grid** — 24 rows, 32 columns
