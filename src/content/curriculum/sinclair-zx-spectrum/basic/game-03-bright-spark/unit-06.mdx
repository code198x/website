The computer played a sequence. The player watched. Now the player repeats it — pressing keys one at a time, each one checked against the stored string.

## Matching Step by Step

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-03-bright-spark/unit-06/match-game.bas" />

The sequence is `"1324"`. The FOR loop goes from step 1 to step 4. At each step, INKEY$ waits for a keypress. If the key matches `s$(i)`, the game continues. If not, it stops with "Wrong at step N".

Three concepts working together:
- **FOR** counts through the sequence positions
- **INKEY$** captures what the player presses
- **String comparison** (`k$ <> s$(i)`) checks if it's correct

This is the exact logic of Bright Spark's player turn (lines 420-540). The only additions in the full game are the panel flash (GO SUB when a key is pressed) and the key drain (waiting for the key to be released before accepting the next press).

## Immediate Feedback

Notice line 90: `PRINT "Step "; i; ": correct"`. The player gets **feedback** after every single keypress — not just at the end. They know immediately whether they're right or wrong. This matters because:

- **Right** reinforces the mental model — "yes, that was the pattern"
- **Wrong** pinpoints the failure — "step 3 was where I lost it"

If the game only said "wrong" at the end without telling you which step failed, it would be much less satisfying. The step-by-step feedback is what makes the player feel they *almost* had it — and that "almost" is what drives replay.

## What You've Learnt

- **FOR + INKEY$ + comparison** creates a step-by-step matching game
- Check each keypress against `s$(i)` — the expected character at that position
- **Immediate feedback** on each step makes failure informative, not frustrating
- The **key drain** (`IF INKEY$<>"" THEN GO TO`) prevents a held key registering twice
