Lucky Number talked to you. It asked a question, waited for your answer, and responded politely. There was no rush. You could think as long as you wanted.

Wire Panic does not wait.

A bomb sits on screen. A countdown ticks. A timer bar fills with red. The border shifts colour. Four wires dangle from the device. One is safe. The others are not. You have twenty seconds.

## Run It

Type `RUN` and press ENTER.

The device appears — a box with cogs, LEDs, wires, and a countdown. The digits tick down. The timer bar fills. The border shifts from green to yellow to red. A tick sound marks each second.

![The device — countdown running, timer bar filling, wires waiting](/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-02-wire-panic/unit-01-type-it-in/device.png)

Press 1, 2, 3, or 4 to cut a wire. Choose right and the device is defused — green digits, green LEDs, the border flashes, a rising tone plays.

![Defused — green digits, time to spare](/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-02-wire-panic/unit-01-type-it-in/defused.png)

Choose wrong — or wait too long — and it explodes. Red digits, red border, random border strobing, chaotic sound, BOOM.

![Explosion — red digits, the correct wire revealed](/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-02-wire-panic/unit-01-type-it-in/explosion.png)

## What Makes It Tense

Play it again. Notice how you feel during the countdown. Your pulse quickens. You want to press a key — any key — just to make it stop. That's **tension**, and the game creates it through four channels at once:

- **The countdown digits** tell you how much time is left — the number getting smaller is pressure
- **The timer bar** shows the same information visually — red filling the space is a threat you can see
- **The border colour** shifts the entire screen surround from safe green through warning yellow to danger red — the *world* is telling you to hurry
- **The tick sound** gives the countdown a heartbeat — each tick is a second you can't get back

Remove any one of these and the game still works. Remove all four and it's just "press a number" — no tension, no excitement, no fun. Tension isn't one thing. It's layers of feedback working together to create urgency.

## What Just Happened

You typed in a game that uses PLOT, DRAW, and CIRCLE to build a visual device on screen. Those commands were black boxes in Lucky Number — you typed them without understanding. Over the next seven units, you'll learn what they do and how to use them yourself.

You also experienced tension as a design concept. Lucky Number taught feedback — the game responding to your actions. Wire Panic teaches the next step: feedback that *escalates*. The game doesn't just respond — it pressures you. That pressure is what makes twenty seconds feel like five.
