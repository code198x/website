A bomb sits on screen. Cogs turn. LEDs blink. A countdown ticks down in seven-segment digits. A timer bar fills with red. The border shifts from green through yellow to danger red. Four coloured wires lead from the device. Cut the right one and you're a hero. Cut the wrong one — or run out of time — and it's over.

## How It Works

Wire Panic teaches **tension** — how time pressure creates engagement. The countdown, the filling bar, the shifting border, the ticking sound — they all exist to make you feel the urgency. Remove any one of them and the game still works, but it stops being exciting.

This is also where PLOT and DRAW stop being black boxes. In Lucky Number, the digit renderer used PLOT and DRAW but you typed them in without understanding. In Wire Panic, you learn what they do — and use them to draw the bomb device yourself.

## What You'll Learn

- **CLS** — clearing the screen
- **PRINT AT** — placing text at specific positions
- **PLOT** — drawing a pixel
- **DRAW** — drawing a line
- **BEEP** — making sound
- **PAUSE** — waiting
- **INKEY$** — checking the keyboard without stopping
- **String variables** — storing and comparing text
