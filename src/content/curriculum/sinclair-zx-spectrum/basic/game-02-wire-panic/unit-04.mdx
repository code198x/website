Play Wire Panic with your eyes closed. You can still tell what's happening. The steady tick marks the countdown. The pitch drops as time runs out. Random noise means explosion. An ascending melody means defusal. Sound tells the story without a single pixel.

That's not an accident. Every sound in the game carries meaning.

## BEEP — Pitch and Duration

`BEEP duration, pitch` plays a tone. Two numbers:

- **duration** — how long, in seconds. `0.5` is half a second. `0.05` is a quick blip.
- **pitch** — how high or low, in semitones from middle C. `0` is middle C. `12` is one octave up. `-12` is one octave down.

Three notes: middle C for one second, one octave up for one second, one octave down for one second. The pitch number controls the tone. Higher numbers = higher notes. Lower numbers = lower notes.

You don't need to memorise this. What matters is the principle: **higher pitch = higher number**. The rest is experimentation.

## Sound Effects

Games don't play scales — they play *effects*. A sound effect is just BEEP with deliberate choices about pitch, duration, and repetition:

Three effects in one program:

**The tick** (lines 20-60) — short, sharp, repeated at intervals. The same pitch each time. Steady, mechanical, relentless. This is the heartbeat of a countdown.

**The explosion** (lines 90-110) — random pitches, rapid fire. No pattern, no melody, just noise. Chaos sounds like chaos.

**The victory** (lines 140-170) — ascending pitches in a loop, finishing with a long high note. Rising tones feel like triumph. The long final note signals "it's over, you won."

Each effect uses the same command — BEEP. The difference is how it's used. Pitch, duration, repetition, and randomness combine to create sounds that *mean* something.

The pitch is `t` — the countdown value. When t=20, the pitch is 20 (high). When t=5, the pitch is 5 (low). When t=1, the pitch is 1 (very low). The tick sound *descends* as time runs out.

You hear the countdown getting deeper, heavier, more urgent. The information is the same as the number on screen — how much time is left — but sound delivers it differently. You feel it rather than read it. A dropping pitch is a warning your ears understand before your eyes do.

## Sound Design

Game designers call this **sound design** — choosing sounds that communicate meaning. It's not about making things sound realistic. It's about making things sound *right*:

- A tick should feel relentless — same rhythm, changing pitch
- An explosion should feel chaotic — random everything
- A victory should feel earned — rising tones, a final sustained note
- Silence has meaning too — the moment between cutting the wire and hearing the result is the most tense part of the game, and it's silent

Sound is feedback you can't ignore. You can look away from the screen. You can't unhear a descending tick.

## What You've Learnt

- **BEEP duration, pitch** plays a tone
- **Duration** controls length — short blips for effects, long notes for emphasis
- **Pitch** controls tone — higher numbers = higher notes
- **Repetition** creates rhythm — ticks, melodies, chaos
- **Random pitch** creates noise — explosion effects
- **Ascending pitch** signals triumph, **descending pitch** signals danger
- **Sound design** — choosing sounds that carry meaning, not just noise
