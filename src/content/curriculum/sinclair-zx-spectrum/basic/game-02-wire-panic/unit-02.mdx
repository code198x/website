In Lucky Number, PRINT put text on the next available line. Each PRINT appeared below the last, scrolling down the screen. You couldn't control where things went — the Spectrum decided.

Wire Panic places things exactly where it wants them. The wire labels sit at row 19. The title sits at row 0. The countdown digits appear in the centre of the device. Nothing scrolls. Everything is positioned.

Two commands make this possible: `CLS` and `PRINT AT`.

## CLS — Start Fresh

`CLS` clears the entire screen. Every character cell becomes blank. Every colour resets. The cursor returns to the top-left corner.

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-02-wire-panic/unit-02/cls-demo.bas" />

Lines 10-30 print three lines of text. Line 40 pauses for two seconds (you'll learn PAUSE properly in Unit 7). Line 50 clears everything. Line 60 prints on the now-empty screen.

Wire Panic uses CLS at line 30 — right at the start, before drawing anything. Every game does this: clear the screen, then build the display from scratch.

## PRINT AT — Put Things Where You Want Them

`PRINT AT row, column` places text at an exact position on screen. The Spectrum's screen is a grid: 22 rows (0 at the top, 21 at the bottom) and 32 columns (0 at the left, 31 at the right).

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-02-wire-panic/unit-02/print-at.bas" />

![Text placed at five specific positions — corners and centre](/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-02-wire-panic/unit-02-clearing-the-screen/print-at.png)

Five labels, five positions. No scrolling. Each one placed exactly where the code says. Row 0 is the top of the screen. Row 20 is near the bottom. Column 0 is the left edge. Column 23 is near the right.

The CLS on line 10 is important — without it, the text from a previous RUN would still be on screen. CLS gives you a clean canvas. PRINT AT lets you paint on it.

## Building a Layout

PRINT AT turns the screen into a designed space. Instead of text flowing down the page, you place elements where they belong:

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-02-wire-panic/unit-02/layout.bas" />

![A game layout — title, score, lives, and footer all positioned](/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-02-wire-panic/unit-02-clearing-the-screen/layout.png)

A title at the top. Score on the left, lives on the right. A message in the middle. A footer at the bottom. Eight lines of code, and the screen looks like a game — not because of what it says, but because of *where things are*.

This is **screen layout** — designing the arrangement of information. Where you put things tells the player what matters. The title is at the top because it's the first thing you see. The score is near the top because it updates often. The footer is at the bottom because it's secondary. Every game makes these choices. PRINT AT is how you implement them.

## How Wire Panic Uses PRINT AT

Look at these lines from the game:

Four wire labels, each at row 19, at different columns (8, 12, 16, 20). Each in a different INK colour matching its wire. PRINT AT places them precisely below their wires. The position *is* the information — the player can see which number matches which colour without reading instructions.

## What You've Learnt

- **CLS** clears the screen — a clean canvas before drawing
- **PRINT AT row, column** places text at an exact position
- **Rows** go from 0 (top) to 21 (bottom)
- **Columns** go from 0 (left) to 31 (right)
- **Screen layout** — where you place things tells the player what matters
- CLS + PRINT AT together let you design the screen rather than just filling it
