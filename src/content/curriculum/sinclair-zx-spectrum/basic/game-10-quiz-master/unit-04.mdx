In unit 3, the correct answer was bundled into each question's DATA block. That meant reading six fields per question — five for display, one to check. If we separate the answers from the questions, the DATA becomes cleaner and the answers become reusable.

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/game-10-quiz-master/unit-04/arrays.bas" />

Line 10: `DIM k(4)` creates an array — four numbered slots, `k(1)` through `k(4)`. DIM stands for *dimension*: you're telling the Spectrum how many values to reserve space for.

Lines 20–40 load the correct answers from DATA into the array. The answers live at line 500: `DATA 2,2,3,2`. After the loop, `k(1)` is 2, `k(2)` is 2, `k(3)` is 3, `k(4)` is 2.

The questions at lines 510–540 no longer carry their answer numbers — just the question and four options. During the quiz, line 200 checks `g = k(n)` to look up the correct answer by round number.

## Why this matters

The answers are loaded once and stored by position. `k(3)` means "the correct answer for question 3" — you can access any answer instantly, without reading through DATA again. This is **indexed access**: storage you reach by number, not by sequence.

In Locksmith (the next game), you'll use arrays to track which digits are in the right position. In Sonar, you'll use a two-dimensional array for a grid. DIM is the foundation for all of them.
