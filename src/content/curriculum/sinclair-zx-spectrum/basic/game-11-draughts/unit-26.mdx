When a player selects a piece, seeing which squares it can reach helps them plan their move. This unit briefly flashes dots on the legal destination squares, then clears them before the player enters the destination.

## How It Works

**Line 125** calls `GO SUB 1800` after the source is validated. The player has selected a piece and we know it is theirs. Now we show where it can go.

**Lines 1800-1860** scan the four diagonal directions from the selected piece. For each direction:

- **Simple moves** (line 1830): If the adjacent diagonal square is empty, print a flashing dot using `FLASH 1`.
- **Captures** (line 1840): If a jump is possible (enemy piece in between, empty landing), print a flashing dot at the landing square.

**Lines 1825-1827** respect the forward-only rule. Non-kings skip backward directions.

**Line 1860** pauses for 30 frames (about 0.6 seconds) to let the player see the highlights.

**Lines 1870-1898** clear the highlights by printing spaces over every position that was marked. This restores the board to its normal appearance before the move is validated.

## FLASH as a Highlight

The Spectrum's FLASH attribute alternates between the foreground and background colours, creating a blinking effect. A flashing white dot on a black square stands out clearly without overwriting the board permanently. After the pause, we clean up by printing a plain space.

## User Experience

Highlighting is a user experience feature, not a game logic feature. It does not change the rules or the validation. It helps the player see their options at a glance, which is particularly valuable late in the game when positions are complex and captures may be mandatory.

The highlight appears for about half a second - long enough to scan visually, short enough not to feel slow. The timing is a design choice, not a technical constraint.

## Try This

**Change the highlight duration.** Try `PAUSE 50` (one second) or `PAUSE 15` (0.3 seconds). Which feels best? Too short and the player misses it. Too long and the game feels sluggish.

**Change the highlight character.** Try "x" or "*" instead of ".". Different characters have different visual weights. A dot is subtle; an asterisk is more noticeable.
