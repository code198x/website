The captured count tells players how many pieces they have taken. But it is equally useful to know how many pieces remain on the board. This unit enhances the status display to show remaining piece counts alongside the captured totals, formatted with player colours.

## How It Works

**Line 835** displays the captured count in a formatted layout: "Captured: " followed by the red count `g`, a dash, and the green count `h`. The INK colours match each player, so the numbers are instantly identifiable.

The remaining piece count is derived from the captured count. Each player starts with 12 pieces. Player 1 has `12 - h` pieces remaining (since `h` tracks pieces captured by Player 2). Player 2 has `12 - g` pieces remaining.

## Formatted Status Display

A well-formatted status area communicates game state at a glance. The key information players need:

- **Whose turn is it?** Shown in the player's colour on line 12
- **How many pieces has each side captured?** Shown on line 13
- **Who is winning?** The capture counts answer this implicitly

The colours carry meaning. Red numbers relate to Player 1. Green numbers relate to Player 2. The player does not need to read labels; the colour tells them.

## Running Tallies

The capture counters `g` and `h` are running tallies. They increment after each capture and never decrease. This makes them reliable indicators of game progress. A game where `g = 7, h = 3` tells you Player 1 is dominating. A game where `g = 6, h = 6` is closely fought.

In games with more complex scoring, running tallies are a standard pattern. They are cheap to update (one increment per event) and always current.

## Try This

**Add remaining counts.** After the captured line, add a second line showing "Remaining: 8 - 5" (calculated as 12 minus the opponent's captures). This gives players both views of the same data.

**Show kings separately.** Count kings and ordinary pieces separately. Display "Pieces: 5 Kings: 2" for each player. This gives more strategic information.
