## The Principle

**Content vs logic** is the most powerful idea in game development. Separate what *varies* (the words, the levels, the enemies, the music) from what's *fixed* (the scramble algorithm, the game loop, the scoring). Build the fixed part once. Change the variable part forever.

Every game engine works this way. Unity doesn't know what your game is about — it provides rendering, physics, and input. You provide the content: models, textures, scripts, levels. The engine is the logic. Your assets are the content. They're independent.

Letter Soup is the simplest version of this principle: a generic word game engine with a DATA-driven word list. But the pattern scales to any size. Bright Spark could store its panel colours in DATA. Hot and Cold could store treasure positions. Wire Panic could store bomb designs. Any game where the content can vary independently of the mechanics benefits from this separation.
