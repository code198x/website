## Permanent and temporary colours

The Spectrum keeps two sets of colours. The **permanent** colours are what every PRINT, PLOT, DRAW and CLS uses unless told otherwise. `INK 6` as a statement on its own, or after a colon, changes the permanent ink, and it stays changed until another statement changes it again. CLS also clears the whole screen in the permanent colours, so line 1000 starts each new round in whatever ink the last round left behind.

An `INK 6;` written as an **item** straight after PRINT, PLOT or DRAW is temporary. It colours only what that one statement prints or draws, and lasts until the end of that statement. Our PRINT lines already work this way: line 1060 is `PRINT AT 18,2; INK 7;"Your move: choose 1-9."`. The drawing lines are the ones that do not.

Every pixel PLOT or DRAW sets also gives its whole character square the ink in use. That is how the yellow line turns short pieces of the grid yellow where it crosses them. That clash is part of how the Spectrum stores colour, and it stays; what we are fixing is the colour that carries on after the drawing has finished.

Lines 1030 and 1040 draw the grid in green, INK 4. Bright green stands out clearly against black and is still different from the cyan X, the white O and the yellow winning line. Line 1040 needs the change too: it used to rely on the blue that line 1030 had left behind.

Line 2050 draws X in cyan, and line 6040 draws the winning line in yellow, each with temporary items. O is white, which is already the permanent ink from line 10, so line 2100 simply drops its `INK 7:` and draws in the permanent colours. The permanent ink now stays white for the whole session.

<Question prompt="Line 2050 puts INK 5; after every PLOT and every DRAW. Why is one INK 5; after the first PLOT not enough?">

A temporary colour lasts only until the end of its own statement, and each PLOT or DRAW is a separate statement. A DRAW without an item draws in the permanent white and gives every character square it passes through white ink, including the square the PLOT has just made cyan. The cross would come out white.

</Question>

Typing INK inside a line works as it does at the start of a statement: enter extended mode, then Symbol Shift with X. The semicolon after the colour number is Symbol Shift with O.

## Check the colours

RUN the changed program and play 1, 8, 7, 4 again.

<Figure src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/three-in-a-row/teaching/colours-win.png" width={352} alt="The same winning position with a bright green grid. The crosses are cyan, the noughts white and the winning line yellow; the footer “R: next round Q: quit” is white." caption="The grid is easy to see, and the footer stays white after the yellow winning line." />

The footer is white. Press R and play a round to a draw: the footer stays white whichever mark was drawn last. If a mark comes out in the wrong colour, check that every PLOT and DRAW in its line carries the item and ends it with a semicolon. If the footer still changes colour, look for a statement INK still left in lines 1030 to 6040.

Try other grid colours by changing the 4 in lines 1030 and 1040. Before each RUN, predict whether the grid will still be easy to tell apart from the cyan crosses, the white noughts and the yellow winning line, then compare.
