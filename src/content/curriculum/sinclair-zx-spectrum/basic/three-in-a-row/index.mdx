Take a square, watch O's reply and see why it chose that move. Three matching marks in a row, column or diagonal win. A full board without a winner draws.

Play with **1–9**, numbered across each row. You are X; the computer is O. **S** starts, **R** restarts or begins the next round, and **Q** quits. Completed rounds alternate who starts and add to a session tally. The final board stays visible so you can inspect the result.

The game runs in **Sinclair BASIC on a stock 48K ZX Spectrum**. It is silent and has no time limit. Its opponent takes an immediate win, blocks an immediate threat, then prefers the centre, corners and edges. It can be beaten: understanding that limitation is part of the game.

## Build the decision

Ten lessons use eight runnable programs. Begin with a numbered board, place crosses and add alternating players. A table of eight winning lines supplies the result checker. Then give O to the computer, improve its choices and expose the rule behind each one.

A worked fork shows why looking one move ahead can fail. Finish with opening instructions, alternating starters, a tally and a self-starting tape. Arrays, loops and subroutines are explained where they matter; no assembly or earlier game is required.
