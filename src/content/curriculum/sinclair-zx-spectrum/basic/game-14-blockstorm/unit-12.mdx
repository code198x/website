The formation moves left and right, but never gets closer. Without descent, the player can sit still and wait for targets to drift into their line of fire. This unit adds the classic Space Invaders mechanic: when the formation reaches a screen edge, it drops one row before reversing direction. Each bounce brings the enemies closer. If they reach the bottom, the game is over.

## How It Works

**Line 424** adds the descent trigger. When the formation hits either edge and `fd` flips, the base row `fy` also increases by 1. The formation drops one row every time it reverses direction.

**Line 426** checks for invasion. If `fy + nr` (the base row plus the number of enemy rows) exceeds row 19, the enemies have reached the play area bottom. This triggers an immediate game over regardless of how many lives remain. The aliens have invaded.

## The Invasion Rule

The invasion fail state creates tension that scoring alone cannot provide. No matter how many enemies the player has destroyed, no matter how high the score, if the formation reaches the bottom, the game ends. This forces aggressive play — the player must shoot quickly to thin the formation before it descends too far.

In Space Invaders, this mechanic creates an elegant feedback loop: as the player destroys enemies, fewer remain, but the fewer enemies there are, the faster the game runs (fewer objects to process). In Blockstorm, the speed is set by the wave data, not the enemy count. But the descent pressure is the same.

## Descent Speed

The formation descends one row every time it reverses direction. How quickly it descends depends on how wide the play area is and how far the formation travels before hitting an edge. A narrow formation reaches the edges more often and descends faster. A wide formation has less room to move and also descends quickly. The sweet spot is a formation width that gives the player enough time to clear a few enemies before the formation drops close.

## Try This

**Count the bounces.** Add a variable that increments every time the formation reverses direction. Display it in the HUD. How many bounces does it take for the formation to reach the bottom from its starting position?

**Start higher.** Change the initial `fy` to 1 instead of 2. The formation starts one row higher, giving the player slightly more time. Does one extra row make a noticeable difference?
