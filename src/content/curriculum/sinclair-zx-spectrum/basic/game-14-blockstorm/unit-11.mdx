Static enemies are easy targets. Moving enemies are a game. This unit makes the row of drones shift left and right across the screen as a unit. All living drones move together — their individual offsets stay the same, but the formation base position changes. When the formation hits the screen edge, it reverses direction.

## How It Works

**Variables `fx`, `fy` and `fd`** track the formation. `fx` is the base column (the left edge of the formation), `fy` is the base row (the top edge), and `fd` is the direction: 1 for rightward, -1 for leftward.

**Lines 400-447** are the formation movement routine. First, the routine erases every living enemy at its current position by printing spaces. Then it updates the base position: `fx = fx + fd`. If the formation has reached the screen edge, `fd` flips sign: `fd = -fd`. Finally, every living enemy is redrawn at its new position.

Each enemy is drawn at screen position `(fx + e(i), fy + r(i))` — the formation base plus the individual offset. When `fx` changes, every enemy moves. When an enemy is destroyed (hit points at 0), it is skipped during both erasing and drawing.

**A tick counter** controls how often the formation moves. The formation does not move every game loop iteration — that would be too fast. Instead, a counter increments each loop, and the formation only moves when the counter reaches the wave speed value. Faster waves have lower speed values, meaning the formation moves more often.

## Shared Offset Movement

Moving a group of objects by changing a single offset variable is efficient and clean. Each enemy stores its position relative to the formation, not its absolute screen position. To move the entire formation, change one variable. To know where any enemy is on screen, add the formation base to the individual offset.

This pattern has a name in game development: relative positioning. It makes group operations trivial. Move the group? Change the base. Check boundaries? Check the base plus the maximum offset. Rotate the group? Change the offsets (though in Blockstorm, formations only shift horizontally).

## Direction Reversal

When `fx + rightmost_offset` exceeds column 30, or `fx` drops below column 1, the direction flips. The same logic as a bouncing ball: hit the wall, reverse. The formation ping-pongs left and right across the screen, giving the player a moving target to track.

## Try This

**Speed it up.** Reduce the tick threshold so the formation moves more frequently. At what speed does it become too fast to hit reliably? At what speed does it become unfair?

**Uneven movement.** Instead of moving one column at a time, make the formation move two columns per step. The drones now jump across the screen rather than gliding. How does this change the feel of the game?
