Lines 290–330 replace the old grid drawing with colour-coded output. Distance 1–2 prints in red (`INK 2`) — very close. Distance 3–4 prints in yellow (`INK 6`) — getting warm. Distance 5 or more prints in cyan (`INK 5`) — still far away. Found targets print in green (`INK 4`). Unexplored cells stay white. Line 350 resets `INK 7` after each row.

Line 290 also changes: hidden targets (`v = 9`) now show as dots, same as empty cells. The X debug display is gone. Targets are truly hidden from this point on.

## The heat map

The grid is now a heat map. Reds cluster near targets. Blues spread across empty space. The player does not need to read individual numbers — the colour pattern tells the story at a glance. A band of red cells narrows the search. A field of blue says "nothing here."

This is the same colour-as-information principle from Hi-Lo (green/red feedback) and Touchdown (border colour for altitude), but applied to spatial data. Each cell encodes its distance in both a number and a colour. The number is precise; the colour is immediate. Together they give the player two channels of the same information — one for analysis, one for intuition.
