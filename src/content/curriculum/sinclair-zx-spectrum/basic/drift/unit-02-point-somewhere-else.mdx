Turn the ship without moving it. O turns anticlockwise, P turns clockwise, and eight turns bring the nose back to its starting direction.

We will calculate eight versions of the triangle once, store their offsets, and choose one each time we draw. There are several arrays in this change, but they all describe the same eight headings.

## A direction has two parts

A pair of numbers can describe a direction: how far right and how far up. We call that pair a **vector**. East is (1,0); north is (0,1); west is (−1,0).

SIN and COS give the vertical and horizontal parts of a direction from an angle. Spectrum BASIC measures these angles in **radians**: a full turn is 2*PI. Dividing that turn into eight gives PI/4 per step.

| Heading h | Angle | Direction |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | East |
| 2 | PI/4 | Northeast |
| 3 | PI/2 | North |
| 4 | 3*PI/4 | Northwest |
| 5 | PI | West |
| 6 | 5*PI/4 | Southwest |
| 7 | 3*PI/2 | South |
| 8 | 7*PI/4 | Southeast |

The calculation `(i-1)*PI/4` makes the first entry start at zero. a(i) holds COS angle; b(i) holds SIN angle. Together they give a vector of length one, apart from the small approximation in the computer's arithmetic.

## Build the corners from that direction

Imagine an arrow **forward** along the nose and another arrow **left** at a right angle to it:

```text
             left = (-b,a)
                 ^
                 |
              centre ----> forward = (a,b)
```

This diagram shows the arrows when the ship faces east. They rotate together.

The nose sits five forward. Each rear corner sits three backwards, then three to one side:

| Corner | Construction | Horizontal | Vertical | Stored in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nose | 5 forward | 5*a | 5*b | c,d |
| Left rear | −3 forward + 3 left | −3*a−3*b | −3*b+3*a | e,f |
| Right rear | −3 forward − 3 left | −3*a+3*b | −3*b−3*a | g,j |

Facing east, a=1 and b=0. These produce (5,0), (−3,3) and (−3,−3), our original triangle.

Facing northeast, a and b are both about .7071. Before rounding, the nose is about (3.5355,3.5355), the left rear (−4.2426,0), and the right rear (0,−4.2426). Rounded for drawing, those become (4,4), (−4,0) and (0,−4). The shape now points diagonally.

## Store eight answers

DIM a(8) reserves eight numeric entries, addressed as a(1) through a(8). We use eight arrays as columns of one table: a/b for direction, c/d for the nose, e/f for one rear corner and g/j for the other. At heading h, every lookup uses that same h.

FOR i=1 TO 8 and NEXT i fill the entries in order. We round the corner offsets but keep a/b fractional, ready to use them for thrust later. [A Box That Holds Many](/foundations/structure/unit-01/) explores indexed collections further; here our fixed-size BASIC arrays use parentheses.

## Follow the loop

k$ is a string variable: the dollar sign marks it as text. INKEY$ supplies a pressed character, or an empty string when no key is held. IF tests a condition; when it is true, the statements after THEN on that line run. OR lets either of two comparisons match, so both lower- and upper-case letters work. GO TO 200 returns to the next key read.

The steps variable increases on each pass. It is an update counter we will retain during flight, even though this checkpoint does not display it.

## Erase before turning

Line 230 draws the old triangle again to erase it. Lines 240–270 change h and wrap it: 9 becomes 1, and 0 becomes 8. Line 350 draws with the new offsets. The ship's x/y never changes.

The drawing routine still joins three corners, but now subtracts the coordinates of one corner from the next to obtain each DRAW movement. If turning leaves fragments, check those subtractions and make sure erasure happens before h changes.

<Question prompt="Why is table entry 4 northwest rather than west?">

The first entry is already east at angle zero. Entry 4 has made three eighth-turns: 3*PI/4, or 135 degrees. West is the next entry, at PI.
