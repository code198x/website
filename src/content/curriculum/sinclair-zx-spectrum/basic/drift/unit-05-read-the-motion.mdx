After RUN, both components are zero. Burn, coast and turn. The readings change when thrust changes velocity; turning alone leaves them alone. SLOW means the ship is within the speed band we will use for docking. There is no dock yet.

## One speed, two directions

Routine 6000 calculates speed from vx/vy. The top number is the total distance travelled per update. The lower DRIFT line gives each component separately, using E or W for horizontal motion and N or S for vertical motion.

ABS gives the magnitude without the sign. A vx of −.6 is therefore displayed as W .6. The direction letter keeps the information that ABS removes. Near zero, the display uses a dash instead of a compass letter, so a tiny arithmetic remainder does not imply a meaningful drift direction.

Lines 6050–6080 use .001 for that near-zero decision. They only choose letters: they do not set vx or vy to zero. A value can also round to 0 on the display while still being large enough to retain its direction letter.

## Round the picture, keep the value

The expression `INT (10*speed+.5)/10` rounds to tenths. For speed .56, multiplying by ten gives 5.6, adding .5 gives 6.1, INT gives 6, and division by ten gives .6.

The display does not replace the stored velocity with its rounded result. Its number is a convenient view of the state, not the state itself.

SLOW uses the full values: vx*vx+vy*vy must be at most .16. That is the square of .4, so it tests for speed at most .4 without another square root. Above it, the message is TOO FAST. These words refer to our future docking limit; they do not mean the ship has reached its maximum speed of 3.

<Question prompt="Both components read .3. Is that necessarily slow enough for a .4 speed limit?">

No. With exact components .3 and .3, squared speed is .09+.09=.18, which exceeds .16. The total speed is about .4243. Each component can be below .4 while the combined motion exceeds it. Rounded display values add another reason to use the cue rather than judge from the digits alone.

</Question>

## Overwrite the whole field

PRINT AT puts each field in a known character position. The spaces after a number clear old digits when a new value is shorter. The two speed messages also occupy the same width. Without that padding, fragments of the previous reading could survive.

We call the display after drawing the starting ship and after each thrust update. Coasting and turning cannot change velocity, so neither needs another calculation and set of PRINTs. Drawing the ship first also avoids leaving it erased while the readout is updated.

This work still costs time in interpreted BASIC. A thrust update now does more than a coasting update. The units remain pixels per update, and steps remains an update count; neither has become a clock.

Try braking again. Watch one component fall towards zero, then change direction if you keep burning. If old digits remain, check the trailing spaces and AT positions. If the display changes on a turn without thrust, inspect the velocity assignments: h and vx/vy have different jobs.
