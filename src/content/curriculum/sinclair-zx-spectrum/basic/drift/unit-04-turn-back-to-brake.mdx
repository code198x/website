## Push against the motion

Start a fresh attempt with R. Give the northeast-facing ship a short burn, release SPACE and let it coast. Turn through four headings until the nose points southwest. Then give another short burn.

Watch the travel, not just the nose. The second burn should slow the northeast motion. Keep burning for too long and the ship begins moving southwest instead. The same thrust can accelerate, brake or reverse motion, depending on the velocity already present.

Turning takes updates too. The ship carries on travelling while you rotate, so begin well before the wall. There is no automatic drag and no separate brake key.

## Count burns on paper

Here is a trace with exactly four northeast burn updates, followed later by exactly four southwest burn updates. Each component changes by about .1414 per burn; the values below are rounded for reading.

| Event | vx | vy |
|---|---:|---:|
| Start | 0 | 0 |
| Four northeast burns | .5657 | .5657 |
| Coast and turn to southwest | .5657 | .5657 |
| One southwest burn | .4243 | .4243 |
| Two southwest burns | .2828 | .2828 |
| Three southwest burns | .1414 | .1414 |
| Four southwest burns | approximately 0 | approximately 0 |

The equal and opposite changes cancel velocity. They do not cancel the distance already travelled. The ship stops somewhere ahead of its starting point.

This trace counts updates, not how many seconds a key is held. In manual play, two holds that feel equally long can be read a different number of times. Use the movement to judge the burn rather than expecting a stopwatch to guarantee a stop.

## Inspect what remains

Press Q to stop, then enter this unnumbered command at the BASIC prompt:

```basic
PRINT vx,vy
```

STOP leaves the variables available to inspect. If holding Q left repeated q characters in the editor, remove those before entering PRINT. Positive vx means east; negative vx means west. Positive vy means north; negative vy means south.

A small residual value can remain after apparently equal opposite burns because the calculations are approximate. Distinguish that tiny remainder from an extra whole burn's worth of velocity. RUN gives you a fresh attempt.

<Question prompt="The ship is moving east, but its nose points north. Will turning the nose south and burning bring it to rest?">

No. Southward thrust subtracts from vy, while the eastward vx remains. To oppose a purely eastward velocity, point west. The direction opposite the nose is useful for braking only when the nose was pointing along the current motion.

</Question>

Try a second flight with burns in two different directions. Now the motion contains both contributions. You can remove them separately, or steer a burn against their combined direction. With only eight available headings, small corrections can take patience.
