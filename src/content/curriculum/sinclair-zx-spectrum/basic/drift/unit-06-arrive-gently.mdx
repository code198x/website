## The box and the centre

The drawn box spans x=184–216 and y=88–120. Our success test uses a smaller rectangle for the ship's centre: x=190–210 and y=94–114, including the edges.

That six-pixel margin leaves room for the ship. At any heading, no triangle offset extends more than five pixels along either axis. Testing the centre against the inner rectangle therefore keeps the complete outline within the drawn box, with some room to spare.

These are geometric tests. The white border is a guide, not a solid wall: you can pass through it too quickly without winning or crashing. The outer arena still ends a flight.

## Position and speed together

Line 360 joins five conditions with AND: a lower and upper bound for x, a lower and upper bound for y, and the squared-speed limit. All must be true in the same update.

The cue now reads DOCK OK instead of SLOW. It still describes **speed only**. You can see DOCK OK at the starting position because the ship is stationary, but that does not mean it has docked. Conversely, being in the box while TOO FAST is showing does not finish the flight.

The test uses the unrounded velocity, just like the cue. A displayed speed of .4 is not enough to decide: values slightly above .4 can round to the same number.

## Finish at a valid position

The loop checks the candidate position against the arena first. If it is safe, the program accepts the position, draws the ship and refreshes the readout when necessary. Only then does it test docking.

Success puts a message into e$ and jumps to the same result routine used by wall failure. There is no further position update there. The ship freezes, and the routine waits until the current key is released before listening for R or Q. That prevents the key held at arrival from immediately choosing another action.

Try entering the box too quickly, then returning slowly. Also stop outside it and inspect the cue. If the game announces success outside the dock, check all four position comparisons and their ANDs. If a slow approach fails, look at both components and ensure the centre, rather than just the nose, has entered the inner region.
