RUN, hold SPACE briefly, then release it. The ship begins moving northeast and continues after release. R starts a fresh attempt; reaching a wall ends the current one. Leave yourself space for the turn rather than holding thrust until the wall arrives.

## A burn adds velocity

The new reset gives vx and vy zero. SPACE reaches line 290, which adds .2*a(h) horizontally and .2*b(h) vertically. That change in velocity is **acceleration**. Without SPACE, line 280 skips the burn and leaves both velocity components alone.

At the initial northeast heading, a and b are about .7071. One burn adds about .1414 to each velocity component. Two burns from rest give about .2828,.2828. A later update without thrust keeps those values.

The pair's length matters. Adding .2 to both components would make diagonal thrust stronger than an eastward burn. Using our unit direction vector gives a total velocity change of .2 whichever heading we choose.

Every update, including those spent turning, calculates `nx=x+vx` and `ny=y+vy`. Those are candidate coordinates: where the ship would go next. An eastward vx of 1.2 adds 1.2 pixels each update, even if rounding means the picture sometimes moves one pixel and sometimes two.

## Limit speed without bending the path

SQR calculates a square root. `SQR (vx*vx+vy*vy)` gives the vector's length: the ship's speed in pixels per update. Squaring removes the signs; travelling west is not a negative speed.

Above speed 3, line 310 multiplies both components by the same fraction, 3/speed. For a hypothetical velocity (3,4), speed is 5 and that fraction is .6. The result (1.8,2.4) has length 3 and the same direction. Limiting each component separately would distort that direction.

## Test before committing

The centre must stay within x=22–233 and y=30–145. These limits leave space for the triangle inside the arena. The test is numerical; it never asks what colour a pixel is.

At this point in the loop the old ship has been erased. If nx or ny is outside the limits, line 330 draws it back at its last valid position and selects the failure message. The result routine waits for key release, then accepts R or Q.

Otherwise line 340 accepts nx/ny and increases steps. Line 350 draws the ship at its new centre. steps counts updates, not seconds: BASIC takes different amounts of time through different paths. This is also why short taps may be missed.

<Question prompt="After a northeast burn, you turn the nose south without pressing SPACE. What changes in the motion?">

Only the heading changes. vx and vy retain their northeast components, and every update continues adding them to x/y. A southward burn would begin reducing vy, but turning alone cannot do that.

</Question>

If releasing SPACE stops the ship, check that vx/vy are reset only at line 100. If turning redirects it immediately, check that position is updated from vx/vy, not directly from a(h)/b(h).
