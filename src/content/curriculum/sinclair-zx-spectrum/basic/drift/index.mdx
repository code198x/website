The nose points one way. The ship travels another. Your job is to make those two facts work together long enough to reach the dock gently.

Use **O/P** to turn anticlockwise or clockwise and **SPACE** to thrust. Release thrust and the ship keeps drifting. To slow down, turn against the motion and burn again. **DOCK OK** means the speed is low enough; the ship must also be inside the docking region. **R** retries, **Q** quits and **S** starts from the title.

Drift runs in Sinclair BASIC on a stock **48K ZX Spectrum**. It is silent, with eight headings and a single docking challenge. Turning and thrust are separate actions. The speed and directional readouts help you judge motion that can be hard to see in a small triangle.

## A nose is not a velocity

We begin a new program with three lines around a centre. A table of eight directions lets the shape turn; thrust then changes its velocity. Braking follows from those same rules. Later we expose the motion in a readout and combine position with speed to decide whether an arrival counts.

Each lesson explains the BASIC it uses. The finished source grows through six runnable checkpoints across eight lessons: the braking experiment and the final saving lesson reuse programs that already work. The last two lessons add opening instructions and save a tape that starts in a fresh machine.
