Lucky Number is the first real game you build. The computer picks a secret number between 1 and 100. You guess. The computer tells you higher or lower — but it also tells you with colour and sound, not just words.

<Figure src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/lucky-number/unit-10/screenshot.png" alt="ZX Spectrum win screen with a green border, a guess count, and the rating Not bad!" caption="Where you are headed: a win, the guess count, and a verdict — framed in green." />

You start with a welcome message (one you already know how to write) and build up, one concept per unit, until you have a complete game with random numbers, a colour-coded border, directional sound, and a rating at the end.

The playable game arrives at Unit 5 — about halfway through. Everything after that is making it feel like a game, not just a working program.

**What you will learn:**

- `LET` — storing values in variables
- `INPUT` — asking the player for a number
- `IF`/`THEN` — making decisions
- `GO TO` — looping
- `RND`, `INT`, `RANDOMIZE` — random numbers
- `ABS` — distance calculation
- `BORDER` — colour as feedback
- `BEEP` — sound as feedback
- `PAUSE` — dramatic timing

**10 units. About 10–15 hours.**
