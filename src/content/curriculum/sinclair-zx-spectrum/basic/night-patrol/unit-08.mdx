## Change the facts, then their appearance

At 3005, READ gets one signed change into ad, and ABS produces its cell index. Line 3010 stores either a space or `!` in v$. Sinclair BASIC comparisons yield zero for false and one for true, so `32+(ad>0)` is 32 or 33; CHR$ turns those into the two characters.

The next line updates that square's attribute byte. Attributes hold colour and brightness separately from the character's dot pattern. Their memory starts at 22528, with 32 entries per screen row. Our map row appears one screen row lower than its number, giving:

`22528 + 32*(row+1) + column = 22560 + cell`

For cell 169, that is address 22729. POKE changes its colours without printing another character.

## Read the colour arithmetic

An attribute combines INK, eight times PAPER, 64 for BRIGHT and 128 for FLASH. We leave FLASH off. Ordinary floor uses bright white ink on black: 64 + 7 = 71. A visible square adds yellow paper, 8 × 6 = 48, making 119.

The expression at 3020 starts with 71, subtracts two at file cell 123 for cyan ink, subtracts three at entrance cell 515 for green ink, and adds 48 for positive changes. Negative changes remove the yellow paper. These fixed cells come from `32*3+27` and `32*16+3`.

After updating the mask, the routine redraws the player; line 455 redraws the guard. The player drawing rule can use red paper under a visible symbol. This is why the general attribute update and entity drawing have a deliberate order. Neither POKE nor the drawing colours decide detection: v$ and the coordinates do.

## Check danger twice

Trace one update in the listing:

1. Read a key, propose a player move and reject walls.
2. Check the player's position against the **current** mask and guard.
3. Pick up the file, or finish a safe return to the entrance.
4. When beat reaches two, advance the phase and restore the old guard square.
5. Apply the sight changes, draw the guard and check the **new** danger.
6. Reset the wait clock and repeat.

<Question prompt="Why not wait until the guard has moved before testing the player's square?">

That would let a player enter a square the guard currently sees and escape detection if the fan moved away in the same update. The first check catches entering danger; the second catches danger arriving.

</Question>
