RUN and stay at the entrance. Watch the guard make a full circuit without pressing anything. It walks between (7,5), (24,5), (24,14) and (7,14). Try meeting it in a corridor; the capture result should freeze play.

## Direction is a pair of changes

gd is the guard's direction, numbered clockwise. The arrays a() and b() hold its horizontal and vertical changes. DIM initially fills their entries with zero; line 7010 sets the four nonzero values.

| gd | Facing | a(gd) | b(gd) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | East | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | South | 0 | 1 |
| 3 | West | −1 | 0 |
| 4 | North | 0 | −1 |

The matching character in g$ draws an arrow. CHR$ 94 supplies the upward caret. At a corner, line 430 advances the direction and wraps five back to one. Line 440 then adds the chosen offsets to gx and gy. The route's coordinates are part of this map's design; this guard does not search for paths.

## Give both actors a turn

The main loop reads input even while waiting. When an update is due, it tries the player's move, checks contact, then handles the file and entrance. beat counts those updates. Every second update resets beat and advances the guard. A second contact check catches the guard arriving on the player.

No key is required to increase beat. A blocked move also consumes an update. At a result, execution leaves this loop, freezing both actors. Reaching home safely with the file ends the game before another guard move.

## Read a clock byte

PEEK reads a byte from memory. Address 23672 is the low byte of the ROM's frame counter; on this PAL target it advances fifty times a second and wraps from 255 to zero. tick remembers its value after an update. Subtracting tick gives elapsed frames; if the result is negative, adding 256 handles a wrap. For example, 3 − 252 + 256 is seven frames.

Line 240 waits until at least eight frames have elapsed. This is a minimum wait after the preceding work, not a fixed frame rate: drawing and BASIC calculations take time too. Reading only one byte is sufficient for this short wait, not for measuring arbitrary long pauses.

<Question prompt="If the player presses into a wall, should the guard stop?">

No. The wall rejects the proposed position, but execution continues through the contact and objective checks to beat. The world keeps moving while the player makes no progress.
