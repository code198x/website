Leave the ship still in [unit 26](../unit-26/)'s program and let a meteor hit it. You hear the falling impact tone, and the moment it ends the screen clears to SHIP DESTROYED. You never see what hit you, or what happened to the ship. A crash ends much like reaching clear space: a sound, then the result screen.

In this lesson the program stops for one second at contact and shows it. The border flashes red, the storm freezes where it is, and the ship breaks into eight pieces that are thrown up and outwards, fall back and land. Then the result appears.

<MeteorExperiment checkpoint="debris" bytes={["phase","hull","border","debris_time"]} words={["elapsed"]} controls="finished" />

Space launches; O/P steer; hold Space for 2X. To see the crash, launch and leave the ship alone: the first meteor hits it in the middle about 2.2 seconds later. Hold O and Space from launch to be hit at the left edge, or P and Space for the right. R and Q do nothing until the result appears; then R retries and Q returns to the title.

Tick **Sound** beside the buttons under the editor to hear the crash. If you ticked it in an earlier lesson, it is already on.

## What you should see

<Figure src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/assembly/meteor-storm/unit-27/debris-impact.png" alt="Meteor Storm with a red border. At the bottom centre, where the ship was, a meteor overlaps a cluster of ship fragments. Other meteors and a star are frozen in place, and the HUD still reads TIME 002.18." caption="Two frames after contact, from a run where the ship was left in the middle. The border is red and the pieces are starting to leave the meteor that hit." />

At contact the whole border turns red. The meteor that hit sits where the ship was, and the pieces burst out of it. Everything else stops but stays drawn: the other meteors, the stars and the HUD. The centre piece rises highest; the nose halves and wings arc out to each side; the tail pieces skid out low, and one drops straight down. After six frames the border is black again.

<Figure src="/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/assembly/meteor-storm/unit-27/debris-scatter.png" alt="The same frozen scene with a black border. Small ship fragments are spread in an arch above and beside the place where the ship was." caption="Twenty-four frames after contact, halfway through the phase. The pieces are spread across their arcs, and the flash is over." />

The pieces come down and land along a line just above the controls text. In the middle of the screen all eight have landed by the end of the second. Then the screen clears to the same result as before, with a black border and no piece left.

The inspector shows the phase from the program's side. `hull` drops to 0 and `phase` becomes 2 at contact. `border` reads 2 during the flash and 0 afterwards. `debris_time` counts down from 25 to 0, one step per update. The readings are samples, so you may not catch every value.

## Leave flight at contact

Unit 11 gave the game its phases: 0 for the title, 1 for flight, 2 for a lost result and 3 for a won one. Until now, contact went straight from flight to the result. The main loop still notices contact the same way, by testing `hull` after moving the meteors, but now jumps somewhere new:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="debris" from="race_step:" to=" call waves" />

`jp z,destroyed` was `jp z,lost`. `destroyed` is a short loop of its own that shows the crash, then falls into `lost`, so the result that follows is unchanged.

The contact itself changes in two ways:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="debris" from="meteor_contact:" to="advance_meteors_remove:" />

It used to fall through to `advance_meteors_remove`, which frees the meteor's slot. Now it jumps to `advance_meteors_skip`, the path that leaves a slot alone, so the meteor keeps its record and stays on screen. It sits exactly where it hit, which is what the player needs to see. The run is over, so the extra active slot costs nothing: `new_game` clears it with the rest of the run's state.

The other change is `start_sound` in place of `play_sound`. We come back to that when we listen.

## Flash the border

Here is the start of `destroyed`:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="debris" from="; The storm stops where it is." to=" ld hl,debris_start" />

`phase` is set to 2 straight away, because the run is lost from this moment. Then the flash. [Unit 24](../unit-24/) gave the border colour its own byte, `border`, and made every speaker write carry that byte's colour bits. Here we store `FLASH_BORDER` (2, red) in it and write it to port $FE, so the border turns red at once. The impact sound writes to port $FE 240 times while it plays, twice for each of its 120 cycles, and each write carries `border`, so the border stays red instead of flickering back to black. `erase_ship` then removes the ship.

## Cut the ship into pieces

The pieces are not new drawings. The asset script cuts them from the ship's own artwork, in three bands of five rows each, starting at ship rows 1, 6 and 11. The nose band is cut into two halves, left and right. The middle band is cut into three: left wing, centre and right wing. The tail band is cut into three too. That makes eight pieces, each with 8 to 20 lit pixels.

Each piece keeps its pixels in the same columns of the ship's 24-pixel-wide frame. Draw every piece at the ship's X, each at the row where its band starts, and the eight together are the ship, pixel for pixel. That is how the break-up can begin without a jump: the first picture of the pieces is exactly the picture of the ship.

Each piece gets its own shift table, like the ship's from [unit 2](../unit-02/): eight copies of the artwork, one for each pixel offset within a byte, so any X can be drawn without shifting at run time. The tables follow each other in `assets.inc` from the label `debris_sprites`. Each is 512 bytes, eight shifts of 64 bytes, laid out like the ship's table so `draw_sprite` finds the right shift in the same way. A piece is only five rows tall, so `draw_sprite` reads just the first 20 bytes of each shift.

## Give every piece a record

Each piece needs a position and a speed, and eight pieces need eight of them. We already have a way to hold that: [unit 12](../unit-12/) gave every meteor a record with fixed field offsets, read through IX. A piece's record is four bytes:

| Offset | Field |
|---|---|
| 0 | X |
| 1 | Y |
| 2 | dX, the sideways speed, signed |
| 3 | dY, the vertical speed, signed |

"Signed" works as it did for drift in [unit 15](../unit-15/): -3 is stored as 253, and adding 253 to a byte has the same low-byte result as subtracting 3. A negative dY moves a piece up the screen, because Y counts downwards.

The starting records sit in the source as data:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="debris" from="; Debris records:" to="frames: defb 0" />

The first two are the nose halves, starting one row into the ship. The next three are the middle band and the last three the tail. The dX values push pieces outwards, and the upward dY values give each band a different throw: the centre piece gets the hardest push, -12, and the tail pieces the gentlest.

`destroyed` copies those 32 bytes into the run's state, then fills in X:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="debris" from=" ld hl,debris_start" to="destroyed_wait:" />

`ldir` copies BC bytes from the address in HL to the address in DE. `new_game` has used it since [unit 11](../unit-11/) to clear the run's state; here it copies the 32 bytes of records into `debris`. The copy leaves every X at 0, so the `destroyed_place` loop stores `ship_x` into each record. It is [unit 13](../unit-13/)'s pool walk: IX points at a record, DE holds the stride of 4, and B counts the pieces down with `djnz`. Then `draw_debris` draws all eight, which puts the ship straight back where it was, now in pieces.

`draw_debris` walks the same eight records:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="debris" from="; XOR every piece" to="; Add each piece's dX" />

For each record it loads X into E and Y into D, sets A to the five rows and calls `draw_sprite`, just as `draw_ship` does with sixteen rows. The difference is HL. It starts at `debris_sprites` and moves on by 512 after each piece, so record 0 draws with piece 0's table, record 1 with piece 1's, and so on.

<Question prompt="`destroyed_wait` calls `draw_debris` twice in a row with only `move_debris` between them. How can one routine both erase and draw?">

`draw_sprite` XORs the artwork onto the screen. XOR undoes itself: XOR a byte with the same pattern twice and you get the original back. The first call runs at the positions the records held when the pieces were last drawn, so it removes them. `move_debris` changes the records, and the second call draws at the new positions. It is the same erase-before-draw we have used since [unit 5](../unit-05/), with the records deciding where.

</Question>

XOR also means a piece can pass over a frozen meteor or the star without damaging it. Where they overlap, pixels flip off, and the erase flips them back.

## Run the phase

Now the loop that runs the phase:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="debris" from="destroyed_wait:" to="won:" />

It is the main loop in miniature. `wait_frame` from [unit 26](../unit-26/) waits for the next frame and plays any sound that is due. The next three lines skip odd frames, so the phase updates on every second frame like flight: 25 times a second. Each update erases the pieces, moves them and draws them again.

`debris_time` starts at `DEBRIS_UPDATES`, 25, and counts down once per update. When it reaches 22, three updates in, `border` is set back to 0 and written: the flash is over after six frames. That restore matters for more than looks. `border` sits outside the run's state, so `new_game` does not reset it. If `destroyed` left it at 2, the result and every later run would have a red border.

When `debris_time` reaches 0 the loop ends and the program falls through into `lost`, which saves the score and shows the result as before. `result` clears the screen, so no piece survives it. Twenty-five updates at 25 a second take one second: the result now appears 50 frames after contact.

Nothing in this loop reads the keyboard. Q, R, steering and boost are all ignored until the result, so nothing can cut the crash short. The result still waits for any key held from flight to be released before it reads R or Q, as it always has.

## Add gravity

Moving the pieces is where they start to look like pieces:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="debris" from="; Add each piece's dX" to="release_keys:" />

The first part adds dX to X. We come back to the `cp 8` and `cp 225` tests below. After that, one instruction does all the physics: `inc (ix+3)` adds 1 to dY. Then `move_debris` adds dY to Y.

That is gravity. A piece thrown upwards starts with a negative dY. Every update makes dY one larger, so the piece rises by less each time, stops, and then falls faster and faster. Nothing in the program knows about arcs or physics formulas. A steady change to the speed produces the curve.

<Question prompt="The centre piece starts at Y 166 with dY -12. Gravity adds 1 before each move. How far does it rise, and after how many updates is it back at 166?">

The first update makes dY -11, so the piece moves up 11 pixels. Then 10, then 9, down to 1:

| Update | dY | Y |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | -11 | 155 |
| 2 | -10 | 145 |
| 3 | -9 | 136 |
| ... | ... | ... |
| 11 | -1 | 100 |
| 12 | 0 | 100 |
| 13 | +1 | 101 |
| ... | ... | ... |
| 23 | +11 | 166 |

It rises 11 + 10 + ... + 1 = 66 pixels, to Y 100, the highest any piece goes. On update 12 dY is 0, so it hangs for one update. On the way down it falls 1, 2, ... 11 pixels, the same distances in reverse, so after 23 updates it is back at 166. A tempting answer is 24, twelve updates up and twelve down, but the first move is already -11, not -12, because gravity comes before the move.

The same sums give the others. The nose halves start at -11 and rise 10 + 9 + ... + 1 = 55 pixels. The wings start at -8 and rise 28.

</Question>

The centre piece still has two updates to go after it is back at 166. Update 24 moves it 12 pixels to 178. Update 25 would move it 13, to 191, which is where the floor comes in.

## Land, and stay on the screen

`DEBRIS_FLOOR` is 179, the lowest Y a piece may have. A piece's five rows then end at Y 183, just above the controls line at 184. After adding dY, `move_debris` compares the new Y with `DEBRIS_FLOOR+1`. Below that, the carry is set and the new Y is stored. Otherwise the piece has landed: Y becomes 179, and dX becomes 0 so it stops sliding. Its dY keeps growing, but every later update lands it on 179 again, so it stays put.

The centre piece lands on update 25, the last one. The wings, which rise only 28 pixels, are back at their start height after 15 updates and land two updates later.

Could a piece's Y wrap round, below 0 or past 255? The highest point is 100, and the fastest fall is 26 pixels, starting from no lower than 179. 179 + 26 is 205, which the floor test catches. So with these starting speeds Y stays between 100 and 179.

X needs the same care. `draw_sprite` expects X from 8 to 224, and the screen edge is close when the crash happens at the side. The tests are the same `cp 8` and `cp 225` pair that bounds drift in unit 15. A piece whose new X would fall outside that range keeps its old X, so it stops moving sideways short of the edge. Crash at the left edge, where the ship's X is 8, and the pieces thrown left cannot move left at all: 8 plus 253, keeping the low byte, is 5, which is below 8. They rise and fall in a straight line at X 8.

## Hear the crash

In unit 26, the impact still blocked. By the time it played, there was no update left to delay, and a 193 ms sound spread across frame waits would only flutter. Now there is work to do while it plays, so the impact goes through `start_sound`, like the star and boost. It starts at contact, and the waits in `destroyed_wait` play it while the pieces fly.

If it still blocked, the pieces would sit still for about 193 ms, ten frames, before they started moving. The break-up and the sound would not happen together.

There is a cost, and you can hear it. With **Sound** ticked, crash and listen: the three falling notes (measured at 882, 668 and 525 Hz in an Emu198x capture) rattle. Every update, the speaker stops while the program erases and redraws sixteen sprites and moves eight records, about 8 ms of silence. Six of those gaps fall inside the sound, one every 40 ms, so 193 ms of tone is spread over 241 ms. It is the 25 Hz flutter from unit 26's long star. A rattle does not sound out of place in a crash, but nothing in the sound table asks for it: it is the price of drawing while the sound plays.

Now reach clear space and compare. The arrival sound still blocks through `play_sound` and cuts straight to the result, so arriving and being destroyed now look and sound different.

## Experiments

Keep a working copy before each change, then press **Revert** when you have finished.

<Question prompt="Predict: delete the first `call draw_debris` after `jr nz,destroyed_wait`, so the pieces are drawn but never erased. What will the second after contact look like?">

Every position a piece is drawn at stays on screen, so each piece leaves a trail, and the trails show the shape gravity gives each path: arcs that rise, flatten and fall. The trails are drawn with XOR, so where two overlap they cut holes in each other. The centre piece has no sideways speed, so it comes down through the same positions it went up through, and drawing it there a second time removes its own trail. The result still clears everything.

</Question>

<Question prompt="Predict: delete `inc (ix+3)`, turning gravity off. What happens to the centre piece?">

Without gravity dY stays -12, so every piece flies in a straight line. The centre piece rises 12 pixels an update, from 166, and reaches the HUD after 12 updates, at Y 22, where it XORs through the text. One more update takes it to 10. The next would take it to -2, but a byte cannot hold -2: 10 + 244 is 254 once the carry is dropped. 254 is above `DEBRIS_FLOOR`, so the floor test treats the piece as landed and puts it on 179. Its dY is still -12, so it climbs again. The floor test compares unsigned bytes and cannot tell "fell too far" from "went above the top". It is only safe because gravity keeps Y between 100 and 179.

</Question>

<Question prompt="Change `FLASH_BORDER` to 7. Which parts of the flash change?">

The border flashes white instead of red, for the same six frames. The impact still carries it, because every speaker write ORs in `border`. Only the colour bits come from `border`: unit 24's `and 7` keeps any value from touching the speaker or MIC bits.

</Question>

Two more to try, with your own prediction first:

- Double the gravity by writing `inc (ix+3)` twice. The centre piece's moves become -10, -8, -6, -4 and -2: it rises 30 pixels, hangs for one update, and is back at its start height after 11 updates. Everything lands sooner and the second ends with the pieces lying still.
- Set `DEBRIS_UPDATES` to 15. The flash is unchanged, but the result arrives after 30 frames, while the centre piece is still near the top of its arc. Too short a phase cuts off the thing it exists to show.
