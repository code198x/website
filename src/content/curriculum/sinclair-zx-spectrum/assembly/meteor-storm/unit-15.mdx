A safe route keeps the ship alive. A star gives the player a reason to leave it. We will let an object's kind choose its picture and contact consequence.

The byte at record offset 2 now means **kind**: 0 is free, 1 is meteor, 2 is star. Nonzero still means occupied, so the existing pool search and occupied-object count keep their meaning. We have extended a concrete representation rather than adding a second pool.

The event table regains all 120 accepted entries: one hundred meteors and twenty stars. Events now contain X, speed, next delay, drift and kind. Their stride is five; the object stride stays five because kind replaces active rather than adding another field.

## One contact test, two consequences

Both kinds move down at their stored speed and use the same contact region. A meteor contact clears the ship's one-life state. A star contact increments score and retires the star without damaging the ship.

Stars bypass horizontal drift before the drift gate. Their event record can contain a drift byte, but their kind determines that it will not be used. Watch a star's X in the active-record table: it should stay fixed as Y increases.

`draw_meteor` now selects `meteor_sprites` or `star_sprites` from kind. Its name has survived the first implementation, but it draws either falling object. The caller must not clear kind before erasing: the old kind selects the image that must be XORed away.

## Award a pickup only once

After adding a point unit, the star takes the same removal path used when an object leaves the play area. That clears kind to zero. On the next visit, the slot is free and its old coordinates cannot award more points.

<Question prompt="What if we incremented score but left the star active until it passed the ship?">

The same contact could be found again on later updates, awarding points repeatedly. Collection is a state transition, not just a sound or a displayed number. Retiring the object makes the one-pickup rule explicit.

</Question>

The `score` byte stores **tens of points**: 1 means 10 points, 2 means 20. At normal speed each star adds one unit. The next lesson follows that byte into its printed decimal representation.

Try changing one star's event kind from 2 to 1 in the companion file, then reassemble. It should use meteor artwork, take the drift path and become harmful. Change all three expectations before running; a data edit can affect several branches that agree on the meaning of kind. Revert restores the companion file as well as the main source.
