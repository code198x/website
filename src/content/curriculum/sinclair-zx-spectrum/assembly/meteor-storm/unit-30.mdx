[Unit 29](../unit-29/) ended with a score that could not keep up. On a full voyage it climbed to 1940, fell to 0500, climbed again and finished on 0020, although the run had earned 5,140 points. The score was one byte, and a byte stops at 255.

In this lesson the score gets a second byte. That means adding, comparing and printing numbers that do not fit in A, using the 16-bit instructions the Z80 has and one it does not.

The score line has an extra digit: `SCORE 00000`. On the route the checks fly, the score at the start of each storm now reads 0, 960, 1940, 3060 and 4050, and the voyage ends at 05140. Those are unit 29's numbers with the lost 2560s put back.

## Two bytes, low first

`score` changes from `defb 0` to `defw 0`, and so do `best_score` and `hud_last_score`, the HUD's copy of the score it last printed. A word is two bytes, low byte first, the order [unit 18](../unit-18/)'s `elapsed` and [unit 29](../unit-29/)'s `course` use. Together they count to 65,535, and since the score stores tens of points, that is 655,350 points. A five-storm voyage comes nowhere near.

The low byte does what the one-byte score did. The high byte counts how many times the low byte has gone round: a score of 5,140 points is 514 tens, which is 2 × 256 + 2, so the high byte holds 2 and the low byte 2. Unit 29's program kept only the low byte, which is why it showed 0020.

## Adding

A star used to add with `add a,c`. Now:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="two-byte-score" from="advance_meteors_absolute:" to="meteor_contact:" />

The points, 1 at 1X or 2 at 2X, go into E with D set to 0, so DE holds the same number as a word. `ld hl,(score)` loads both bytes of the score into HL, `add hl,de` adds the two words, and `ld (score),hl` stores both bytes back.

`add hl,de` does the carry for us. When the low bytes add up to more than 255, the extra goes into the high byte, exactly as a carry in column addition goes into the next column. 255 tens plus 2 is 257: low byte 1, high byte 1.

The finish bonus is added the same way:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="two-byte-score" from="finish_bonus:" to=" ld de,(best_time)" />

`finish_points` stays one byte. One storm's bonus is never more than 100, and adding it to the score goes through DE like a star's points.

<Question prompt="The score is 255 tens and you collect a star at 2X. What does the HUD show next?">

`SCORE 02570`. DE is 2, so HL goes from 255 to 257. In bytes, the low byte goes from 255 to 1 with a carry, and the high byte from 0 to 1. `decimal4` prints 257 as 0257, and the score line's fixed final 0 makes it 2570.

</Question>

## Comparing

A one-byte comparison was `cp`. The Z80 has no 16-bit compare, so the program subtracts and reads the flags. The HUD only reprints the score when it has changed:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="two-byte-score" from="hud_choose_score:" to="bonus_line:" />

`sbc hl,de` subtracts DE from HL, and the carry flag as well. That is what the C in the name means, and it is why `or a` comes first: OR-ing A with itself leaves A unchanged and clears the carry flag, so the subtraction is HL minus DE and nothing more. If the score equals the last one printed, the result is zero, the zero flag is set and `ret z` returns.

The subtraction destroys HL, so the score is loaded again before it is copied into `hud_last_score`.

`save_score` uses the other flag:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="two-byte-score" from="; Keep the score if it is at least" to="; HL = sound:" />

When the score is lower than the best, the subtraction has to borrow, and the carry flag is set. `ret c` keeps the best. Otherwise the score is at least the best, and it is stored as the new best score.

<Question prompt="Why does save_score need `or a` before `sbc hl,de`, when it has just loaded HL and DE and done nothing else?">

The carry flag is whatever the last instruction that changed it left behind. `ld` instructions do not change the flags, so after the two loads the carry is still whatever it was before `save_score` was called. If it happened to be set, `sbc` would take away one more than DE, and a score equal to the best would look lower than it and not be saved. `or a` makes the carry 0 every time.

</Question>

## Printing four digits

`decimal3` turned a byte into three digits: it subtracted 100 as many times as it could, then 10, and the units were what was left. The new routine, `decimal4`, does the same with 1,000, 100 and 10 for a word in HL:

<AssemblyExcerpt checkpoint="two-byte-score" from="; HL (0-9999) to four decimal" to="; HL holds elapsed PAL frames" />

Each digit comes from `decimal_digit`. It is handed a negative power of ten in BC, and adds it to HL again and again. Adding a negative number is subtracting, but it gives a useful flag. In 16 bits, -100 is 65,436, and adding 65,436 to HL carries out of the top bit exactly when HL was at least 100. So the carry flag means "there was another hundred to take away".

A counts the adds. It starts at one below the character `'0'`, and each pass adds 1 to it before adding BC. Here is the hundreds digit of 514:

| Pass | A | HL after `add hl,bc` | Carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | '0' | 414 | yes |
| 2 | '1' | 314 | yes |
| 3 | '2' | 214 | yes |
| 4 | '3' | 114 | yes |
| 5 | '4' | 14 | yes |
| 6 | '5' | 14 - 100, wrapped | no |

The sixth add found no hundred left: HL has gone below zero, wrapping round to 65,450. The carry flag is clear, so `sbc hl,bc` subtracts BC with nothing extra, and subtracting -100 adds the 100 back: HL is 14 again. A holds `'5'`, and `ld (de),a` stores it. The tens digit then comes out as `'1'`, and the 4 left over is the units, made into a character with `add a,'0'`.

Four digits cover 0 to 9,999, or 99,990 points. `score_line` and `records` call `decimal4`, and both digit buffers have a fifth character, the fixed `0`.

## Experiments

Keep a working copy before each change, then press **Revert** when you have finished.

<Question prompt="In decimal4, change the third `ld bc,-10` to `ld bc,-100`. What does the score line show for a score of 514 tens?">

`SCORE 050>0`. The first two steps take out no thousands and five hundreds, leaving 14. The third step now tries to take out 100, finds none and stores `'0'`. The units step is handed 14, and `'0'` plus 14 is character 62, `>`. Each step must take out ten times less than the one before it, or the last step is left with more than one digit's worth.

</Question>

<Question prompt="Put it back. Then, in the star code, replace `ld d,0` with `ld d,1`. What does each star add?">

256 more than before. DE is now 256 plus the star's points, so a star adds 257 or 258 tens, over 2,500 points. `add hl,de` adds all 16 bits of DE, so the high byte has to be set deliberately, which is why the original sets D to 0.

</Question>

The score can now count the whole voyage. Every storm still pays the same bonus, though, and the fifth is the hardest to reach. [The next lesson](../unit-31/) packages the game as a tape; a later one will make the fifth storm's bonus worth more.
