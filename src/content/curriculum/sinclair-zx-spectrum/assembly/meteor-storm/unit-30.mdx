The browser experiment proves that our code can run when placed directly into a prepared machine. A distributable tape needs a different check: loading through the Spectrum's ROM from a fresh start, then playing with the sound on.

Use [unit 29](../unit-29/)'s program: four sounds, with the star, boost and impact playing while the loop waits for the frame, a ship that breaks apart when it is hit, a storm coloured in bands, and a voyage of five storms. This lesson adds no new game code. First play it, then save the files you intend to share.

## Keep source and data together

Download **source** and **assets.inc**. The source contains the program, its sound tables, the starting records for the pieces and the table of row colours; the companion contains the pre-shifted artwork, including the eight pieces cut from the ship, and the five prepared courses. Keep both in one directory. Saving only the assembly file leaves its include dependency missing.

You can also download a tape assembled from the current contents of both editors. Edits that have not been run yet are still included in that download, so test the version you intend to distribute. Revert restores both editors.

For an independent local build:

```sh
asm198x --dialect pasmo --cpu z80 --tapbas meteor-storm.asm -o meteor-storm.tap
```

Or use upstream Pasmo:

```sh
pasmo --tapbas meteor-storm.asm meteor-storm.tap
```

The tape contains a small BASIC loader and the machine-code block. You do not need to author a BASIC game to use that loading convention. The code begins at 32768 and establishes its own stack, interrupt route and border colour when entered.

## Load what you will share

The experiment above plays sound when you tick **Sound**, but it starts the code directly rather than loading a tape. The site's Spectrum player does what this check needs: it loads a tape through the ROM on a fresh 48K machine, with sound. Open the [Sinclair ZX Spectrum page](/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum/), press the play button under its screen and tick **Sound**, which starts unticked. Press **Choose program or disk**, pick `meteor-storm.tap`, then press **Start**. The ROM loads the tape on its own; you will hear the loading tones. Click the screen before using the keys.

On the desktop, start a fresh 48K Spectrum emulator, mount `meteor-storm.tap`, enter `LOAD ""` and start tape playback. In Emu198x the equivalent command is:

```sh
emu198x-spectrum --machine spectrum_48k --tape meteor-storm.tap --autoload-tape --scale 3
```

Confirm that the title appears in its bands of cyan, green, yellow, red and white, Space launches after release, O/P steer and held Space boosts. In flight the time and speed are cyan over a white score, and each meteor changes colour as it falls: cyan, green, yellow, then red just above the white ship. Listen for each event: a chirp for a star, a lower one when you press Space in flight (and none while you keep holding it) and a rising fanfare in clear space. Let a meteor hit the ship and watch the crash: the border flashes red, the storm freezes, and the ship breaks into eight pieces that fly up and land during a rattling, falling tone. About a second later the result appears with a black border and no pieces left. Cross a storm and check the voyage carries on: the fanfare, two seconds of CLEAR SPACE with the finish bonus, then `STORM 2/5` with the clock back at 00.00 and the ship in the centre. If the storm is all white, the border stays red, the result cuts in at once or clear space ends the game after one storm, you are not running this lesson's program. Retry after the crash; then return to the title. Cross a storm when you can and check that the BEST STORM time and the best score survive another retry within that session. After a long voyage the score itself is wrong, wrapped round past 2550, as [unit 29](../unit-29/) explains.

A snapshot saves a machine's state and can be useful while investigating a fault. It is not the same evidence as a fresh ROM load of the tape. A snapshot can preserve an already configured stack, interrupt mode or record that a fresh launch has not established yet.

<Question prompt="You set a best time, download the tape, reset, and load it again. Should that record still be there?">

No. Tape download assembles the source and initial data, not a recording of the running machine's RAM. Session records begin unset on a fresh program launch. Saving records across sessions would require a separate persistence feature.

</Question>

## Choose a next experiment

Keep a working copy before changing the game. Try one design change with a prediction: a different drift cadence, an altered event spacing, revised star artwork or a new sound table. State which behaviour should change and which should remain intact, then retest collision, retry and completion. For a sound, work out its length in T-states first. A star or boost sound plays in the time an update leaves over; one longer than that is split across several waits and flutters.

We have built a game whose positions and rules live in state, whose picture is drawn from that state, and whose progress is measured separately from time. Its sounds are data too, and the ones heard in flight play in time the game would otherwise spend waiting. Even its crash is state: eight small records, moved by one rule and drawn from the ship's own artwork. Its colours belong to places, not objects: 24 bytes written once per screen, so nothing ever clashes. And its storms are data: five tables, chosen by one address. Those distinctions will still matter when another game needs a different renderer, more objects or a different machine.
