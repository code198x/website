Unit 5 moved the lamplighter by blanking the cell he left — and we said plainly why that was a trap: it only works while the floor is empty. The first thing with real pixels he steps onto, the blank-erase will scrub away.

This unit fixes it for good, and it completes the cell-sprite technique. The idea is simple: instead of blanking, **remember**. Before he stands in a cell, save the nine bytes already there; when he leaves, put them back. From now on he can walk over anything without harming it.

## What you'll see by the end

![The walled square with the lamplighter left of centre, having walked there — one clean figure, the floor undamaged.](/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/assembly/gloaming/unit-06/screenshot.png)

He's walked left this time — but otherwise it looks exactly like Unit 5: one figure, clean floor, no trail. On bare cobbles, save-and-restore and blank-erase are indistinguishable. The difference is invisible *today* and essential the moment there's something on the floor worth keeping. (The last "Try this" makes it visible — it's the mirror of Unit 5's bug.)

## What's under him

A cell is nine bytes: the **eight bitmap rows** that hold its pixels, plus the **one attribute** that holds its colour. To preserve a cell while the lamplighter stands on it, we copy all nine into a small buffer in memory:

```
under_lamp:
    defb 0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0   ; 8 pixel rows + 1 attribute
```

`under_lamp` always holds "whatever is underneath him right now". Save fills it as he arrives; restore empties it back as he leaves.

## The dance: restore, step, save, draw

Every move is now four steps in a strict order:

1. **Restore** — write the buffer back over his current cell. The background he was covering reappears, exactly as it was.
2. **Step** — change `lamp_col`.
3. **Save** — copy the new cell's nine bytes into the buffer, *before* we draw over them.
4. **Draw** — stamp him into the new cell.

Order is everything. Restore before you step (or you'll restore the wrong cell); save before you draw (or you'll save his own figure as the "background" and carry it around like a stain). Get it right and he glides across the floor leaving it untouched.

## The same address, reused four ways

Saving, restoring and drawing all need the address of his cell — and it's the same simple sum from Unit 5: `ROW_SCR + col` for the pixels, `ROW_ATTR + col` for the colour. Rather than write it out four times, two tiny helpers (`scr_addr`, `attr_addr`) hand back the address in `HL`, and save / restore / draw each lean on them. Same arithmetic, three jobs.

## Try this: watch it survive

This is the exact mirror of Unit 5's "watch the assumption bite". Poke a mark into a floor cell in his path, in setup:

```
ld   hl, ROW_SCR + 9
ld   (hl), %11111111
```

In Unit 5, walking over column 9 wiped that bar away. Now walk over it and **it's still there** when he leaves — saved on the way in, restored on the way out. Same scene, opposite outcome: that's the whole unit, made visible.

## Try this: forget to restore

Comment out one `call restore_under`. The trail of lamplighters comes straight back — because the old cell never gets rebuilt, so his figure stays stamped in it. It's a neat way to see that restore is doing real work every single step, even when the floor looks empty.

## Try this: the square hole (a look ahead)

Fill a cell in his path with a stipple instead of a solid bar (`%10101010`), and walk onto it. While he's standing there, notice his cell is a solid block — his white figure on black — and the stipple is completely hidden *under* him, not showing through the gaps in his shape. Save-and-restore protects the background, but he still covers it with an opaque square while he's on it. Drawing only his *lit* pixels, so scenery shows through the gaps, is **masking** — the upgrade a later game makes to this very technique.

## If it doesn't work

- **A trail of figures again.** `restore_under` isn't being called, or runs after the step. Restore the **old** cell first, then change `lamp_col`.
- **He drags a smear of background around with him.** You saved *after* drawing — so the buffer holds his own figure, and you stamp it into the next cell. Save the new cell **before** `draw_lamp`.
- **The cell he started on is wrong after he first moves.** Setup must `save_under` *before* the first `draw_lamp`, so the buffer holds real floor, not his figure.
- **It looks exactly like Unit 5.** Correct, on a blank floor. Use the first "Try this" to see the difference.

## What you've learnt

- Preserve a sprite's background by **saving** the cell's bytes before drawing and **restoring** them when it leaves.
- A cell is **nine bytes**: eight bitmap rows plus one attribute.
- The order is **restore (old) → step → save (new) → draw** — and save must come before draw.
- This solid save/restore is the honest **"before"**; drawing only lit pixels — **masking** — is the upgrade a later game makes.
