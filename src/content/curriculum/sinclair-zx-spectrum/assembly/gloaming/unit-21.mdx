Unit 20 put Gloaming on real iron, and playing it there — with real hands, on a real evening — teaches you something no checklist does: you *hear* the gaps. The title appears in silence. THE NIGHT IS HELD prints in silence. Night falls in silence. The two little blips of Unit 17 carry the whole game's voice, and the three moments with the most feeling in them say nothing at all. This unit gives each one a phrase: a chime for the dusk, a fanfare for the held night, a sting for the fallen one.

## Where we start

Unit 19's finished, looping game (the same code Unit 20 put on tape). It already knows how to beep — `beep` takes a length in `B` and a pitch in `C` and that is the whole engine. What it has never done is say two notes in a row.

## A phrase is beeps in a row

There is no new machinery in this unit, and that is the point. A musical phrase, on the beeper, is just `beep` called a few times with different numbers:

```
ld  b, $83          ; length — how many cycles
ld  c, $CF          ; pitch  — the flip delay (smaller is higher)
call beep           ; one note
...                 ; a breath
ld  b, $A5
ld  c, $A4
call beep           ; the next note, a third higher
```

Between the notes sits a **rest** — and a rest is not nothing, it is *part of the phrase*. Run the notes together and they smear into one another; give them a breath and each one lands. Our `rest` is the smallest possible: `B` frames of `halt`. Which hides one trap — `halt` waits for an interrupt, so a phrase can only play **after `ei`**. That is why the title chime is called *below* the `im 1` / `ei` pair in setup, not beside `draw_title_screen` where you might first reach to put it.

## The shape carries the meaning

Three phrases, three shapes — and the shape *is* the message:

- **`chime_dusk`** — two bell notes, a bright strike and a lower answer, like a clock sounding over the square. It plays when the title appears, and again every time you come back to it.
- **`fanfare_held`** — a rising run: C, E, G, and a held top C. Up means triumph; it has meant triumph since long before computers. One Z80 detail: `B` only counts to 255, so the held note is *two* beeps back to back — listen for the join and you won't find it.
- **`sting_nightfall`** — two steps down and then a long, low note the phrase settles on. Down means loss. The night arrives on the A below the fanfare's first note.

Nobody reads the screen text first. The phrase tells you which ending you got before a single word lands — exactly the job the lamp blips do in miniature, scaled up to the moments that decide the game.

If you tried Unit 17's last experiment — "a win fanfare" — you have already written the middle of this unit. Here it is grown up, with rests and a held note, and joined by its two siblings.

## Milestone — the endings sing

We add a `rest`, the three phrases, and four calls: the chime after `ei` in setup and on every return to the title, the fanfare after `draw_win_screen`, the sting after `draw_lose_screen`. The phrases are blocking, like every beeper sound — and here that is a *feature*: the game holds still while the moment sounds, and the input lock picks up where the phrase ends.

## When it's wrong, see why

- **The machine hangs at the title, silent.** The chime ran before `ei` — `rest` uses `halt`, and `halt` with interrupts off waits forever. The phrase calls live *after* `im 1` / `ei`.
- **The notes smear into one buzz.** No rests. Even `B` = 3 or 4 frames of silence between notes is the difference between a phrase and a noise.
- **The held note has a hiccup in the middle.** That is the join between the two back-to-back beeps — a few microseconds of cone-settle. If yours is audible, the second beep's `B`/`C` don't match the first.
- **The fanfare plays but the win screen is blank behind it.** Call order: draw first, *then* sing. The phrase blocks for over a second — the player stares at whatever was on screen when it started.
- **A warble on every note.** Same as Unit 17: the frame interrupt is cutting in. `beep` already wraps itself in `di` … `ei`; if you wrote a new phrase helper, it must run its notes through `beep`, not toggle the port itself.

## Before and after

You started with a game whose endings happened and finished with a game whose endings *land*. The machinery was already there — one `beep` routine, four years old in game-time — and the whole unit is just calling it in patterns: up for triumph, down for loss, two bells for the dusk. That is the deepest lesson Gloaming's sound has to offer: feeling, on a one-bit speaker, lives in the *sequence*, not the synth.

## Try this: re-pitch the dusk

The chime is two numbers twice. Make both notes lower and the title turns solemn; make the second note *higher* than the first and the square sounds like it is asking a question. The game's whole opening mood is four bytes.

## Try this: a phrase for the eighth lamp

The last lamp before the win is a moment the game knows about — `lit_count` reaches `NUM_LAMPS - 1`. Give it a two-note "almost there" flourish, a little brighter than the ordinary blip. One comparison, one new phrase: the game starts building tension on its own.

## Try this: feel the itch

Look at the three phrases side by side. The same five lines repeat — load `B`, load `C`, call `beep`, load a rest, call `rest` — with only the numbers changing. Code where only the *data* changes is begging to become a table with a loop reading it. Don't fix it here; just notice it. Shadowkeep's theme music is exactly that fix, and you will recognise it the moment you meet it.

## What you've learnt

- A **phrase is sequenced beeps** — the same `beep`, called in a row with rests between; no new engine needed.
- **Rests are notes** — silence between tones is part of the music, and `halt` only works after `ei`.
- **Shape carries meaning** — rising for triumph, falling for loss, paired bells for arrival; the pattern speaks before any text does.
- **`B` is 8-bit** — a held note longer than 255 cycles is two beeps back to back.
- **Polish is hearing-driven** — this unit exists because playing the finished game on real hardware exposed silences no checklist listed.
