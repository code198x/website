The game plays and sounds right. This unit adds the last layer to the play itself — **atmosphere**. As you light lamps, the square warms: the cold blue stone of the walls catches the spreading light and glows, brightening through to a dusk-magenta as the night is held back. Snuff a lamp and the stone cools again. The *look* of the place starts telling you how you're doing, before you ever glance at the tally.

The same square — but the walls, blue and cold since Unit 1, have warmed to a glowing magenta now that five lamps are lit. Light more and they brighten further; let the draught snuff one and they cool. The whole frame becomes a mood ring for your progress.

## Mood is just colour, driven by state

There's no new technique here — only the oldest one, used for feeling. We keep a small **table** mapping "lamps lit" to a wall colour, and whenever the count changes we repaint the frame in that colour:

```
wall_ramp:  blue → bright blue → magenta → bright magenta   (by lamps lit)
```

`warm_walls` reads `lit_count`, looks up the colour, and redraws the four edges of the frame in it — the same attribute writes you've made since the very first unit, now reaching for atmosphere instead of just structure. It's called wherever the count changes: after a lamp lights (the stone catches the light) and after one is snuffed (the stone cools).

## One bit you must not break

There's a constraint, and it's a good lesson in respecting your own systems. The walls have to stay **solid** — the collision test from Unit 7 reads the bottom bit of PAPER (bit 3) to decide what's a wall. So every colour in the ramp must keep that bit set. That limits the warm palette to PAPER colours whose low bit is 1: **blue (1)** and **magenta (3)**, brightened for extra warmth. Warm the walls all you like — but never touch the bit that makes them walls. (Reach for red or yellow PAPER and the draught would start drifting straight through the frame.)

## Try this: a different mood

Edit `wall_ramp`. You're free to use any PAPER whose bottom bit is set — blue (1), magenta (3), cyan (5), white (7) — plus the BRIGHT bit. Try a cold-to-icy ramp (blue → bright cyan → white) for a frostier night, or hold the cold longer and only warm in the final few lamps for a sudden dawn. The mood is yours; just keep every PAPER odd.

## Try this: warm the sky too

The border has no collision to respect, so it's a free second canvas. Add a `border_ramp` table and `out ($FE), a` the border colour alongside the walls — the night sky lifting as the square fills. (Keep it off blue so it doesn't blur into the walls.) Two surfaces warming together reads as a real change of hour.

## Try this: break it on purpose

Put a red PAPER (`%00010111`) in the ramp and light enough lamps to reach it. The walls turn red — and the draught sails straight through the frame and off the screen, because red's PAPER bit 0 is clear, so the collision no longer sees a wall. It's a vivid lesson: a cosmetic change quietly violated an invariant. Then put it back.

## If it doesn't work

- **The walls stop blocking — sprites pass through them.** A ramp colour has a PAPER whose bottom bit is clear. Keep every PAPER odd (1, 3, 5, 7).
- **The walls never change colour.** `warm_walls` isn't called where the count changes, or it's indexing the wrong table. Call it after `light_pip` and `unlight_pip`.
- **The wrong colour, or a crash, at full lamps.** The ramp must have an entry for every count `0`–`8` (nine entries). Indexing past the end reads junk.
- **A visible flicker as the frame repaints.** Repainting ~110 cells is well within a frame; if you see tearing, you're repainting every frame rather than only when the count changes.

## What you've learnt

- **Mood is just colour driven by state** — the attribute writes from Unit 1, reaching for atmosphere.
- A **ramp table** maps progress to appearance; repaint only when the state changes.
- **Respect your invariants:** the walls must stay solid, so the ramp keeps the collision bit set — a cosmetic change must not break a mechanic.
- **Atmosphere is feedback you feel** before you read it.
