Lamps light, but the game keeps no score and tells you nothing. A game should answer "how am I doing?" at a glance. So we add a **tally**: a row of pips along the top, one for each lamp, cold at the start and warming as you light them. Fill the row and you've filled the square.

We build it the simplest way that works — and the simplest way turns out to be the very first thing we ever drew.

## What you'll see by the end

![The square with a row of pips along the top; two pips are bright yellow and the rest cyan, matching two lit lamps below and the lamplighter beside them.](/images/sinclair-zx-spectrum/assembly/gloaming/unit-11/screenshot.png)

A strip across the top holds eight pips — and here two of them have warmed to yellow, because two lamps are lit below. The tally and the lamps tell the same story two ways: glance at the top and you know how close you are without counting a thing.

## Pips are just coloured cells

A pip doesn't need a picture. It's a single cell showing a colour — **cyan** for a lamp not yet lit, **bright yellow** for one that is. No glyph, no pixels: pure attribute, exactly like the cobbles and walls back in Unit 1. The whole tally is eight coloured blocks in a row.

```
PIP_UNLIT  equ  %00101000   ; PAPER cyan  — cold
PIP_LIT    equ  %01110000   ; BRIGHT PAPER yellow — warm
```

Drawing actual *numbers* — a "3 / 8" readout — is a genuine technique: you need a font and a way to render digits, and a bigger game later builds exactly that. This coloured-pip bar is the honest, smaller version, and for a game with a handful of lamps it's arguably *better*: you read it instantly, with no arithmetic.

## A place to put it

The pips need to live somewhere the lamplighter can't trample. So we give the game a **HUD strip**: the top wall moves down from row 0 to row 1, and row 0 becomes a black band above the playfield where the pips sit. Because the wall is now at row 1, he's stopped at row 2 — he can never reach the HUD. The playfield loses one row; the score gains a home. (Every later game in the course will carve out a HUD the same way.)

## Counting, and warming the next pip

We keep one byte, `lit_count`. The lighting code from Unit 10 already knows the exact moment a *cold* lamp becomes lit — so that's where we bump the count and warm a pip:

```
call light_pip      ; in the "this lamp was cold" branch only
```

`light_pip` warms the pip at the current count, then increments it — so pips fill left to right in the order you light lamps. And because Unit 10's lighting is **idempotent** — re-crossing a lit lamp does nothing — the count can never run ahead of reality. Walk back and forth over a lit lamp all you like; the tally doesn't budge.

## Try this: change the lamp count

Add or remove lamps in `lamp_data`, and set `NUM_LAMPS` to match. The pip bar grows or shrinks with it — the tally is sized by one constant. (Try it with the count *wrong* on purpose: too few pips and the ninth lamp warms nothing; too many and the bar can never fill. A score and the thing it scores must agree.)

## Try this: move the HUD

Put the pips along the **bottom** instead. They're just cells: change `PIP_BASE` to a row-23 address (`$5800 + 23*32 + 12`), and they'll sit in the bottom band. Or recolour them — make unlit pips dim blue and lit ones flashing — to taste. The readout's look is entirely yours.

## Try this: count down instead

Flip the meaning: start all eight pips **lit** and turn one **cold** for each lamp you light — "lamps remaining". Same mechanism, opposite reading. Which feels better — filling up, or running down? That's a real design choice, and you can try both in a minute.

## If it doesn't work

- **Pips never warm.** `light_pip` is in the wrong place — it must be called only inside the "this lamp was cold" branch, right where you set the lit attribute.
- **The wrong pip warms, or it spills past the bar.** `PIP_BASE` is off, or `lit_count` isn't being used as the index. The first pip is at `PIP_BASE`, the *n*-th at `PIP_BASE + n`.
- **The lamplighter can walk into the HUD.** The top wall isn't at row 1, so row 0 is reachable. Draw the top wall at `$5820`, not `$5800`.
- **The bar can't fill, or fills too early.** `NUM_LAMPS` doesn't match the number of lamps in `lamp_data`. Keep them in step.

## What you've learnt

- A **HUD is just more cells** — dedicate a strip outside the playfield and the sprite can't disturb it.
- A **coloured-cell readout** (pure attribute) is a simple, instantly-readable score — no digits required.
- Track game state in a byte (`lit_count`) and **reflect it in the HUD** as it changes.
- **Idempotent lighting keeps the count honest** — the score can't drift ahead of the game.
