The Z80 runs instructions, reads and writes memory, and communicates with the Spectrum's hardware. We will give it small jobs whose results we can predict and inspect.

Begin with a stock **48K ZX Spectrum**, either emulated or real. You do not need to have studied BASIC or completed another programming course. Each lesson provides a complete source file and an editable browser experiment. Asm198x is the recommended assembler for local work; the same source also builds with Pasmo.

## Eight experiments

1. [Change the border](./unit-01/): assemble a program and send a value to the hardware.
2. [A byte becomes pixels](./unit-02/): follow eight bits from a labelled address to the screen.
3. [Build an eight-row shape](./unit-03/): choose the patterns and addresses that form a small picture.
4. [Draw with a counted loop](./unit-04/): keep the picture, store its patterns as data, and replay the loop’s actual writes.
5. [Call a drawing routine](./unit-05/): draw in two places, follow each return, and check a routine’s contract.
6. [Move your character](./unit-06/): read keys, keep a position and inspect the erase and redraw.
7. [Give movement a clock](./unit-07/): hold a direction and change how many frames pass between updates.
8. [Find the first wrong step](./unit-08/): stop at a decision and compare a prediction with registers, flags and RAM.

Make a prediction before each run. When the result differs, follow the value and its destination: understanding the first unexpected change is more useful than guessing at several edits at once.

## After the introduction

You can now follow instructions through registers and RAM, call a drawing routine, read input, pace movement and investigate a fault. **Meteor Storm**, the first game using this foundation, is in development. Its lessons are not yet available.
