The finish phase opens at the front door. Since Gloaming unit 19, the title has played its dusk chime — four notes, once, then silence and a prompt. It was right for a tiny game; the five-watch night deserves **bells**: an eight-step tune that tolls, lifts into the lamp motif, kindles, and leans back into the dark on a hanging D — then round again, for as long as the title stands. The notes were composed on the same one-bit beeper you built in Gloaming unit 18; nothing about *making* sound changes tonight. What changes — and what this unit is really about — is a contract. A one-shot chime borrows the machine for two seconds at boot and hands it back. A looping tune **owns the title screen**, and every screen that returns to the title now returns *into a performance*. That has consequences the chime never had.

## Milestone 1 — the bells toll (and something goes missing)

The tune is a step table: eight fragments, each one beep plus a rest, with `title_music_step` remembering where in the round we are. `title_step` plays **one step per pass** — that's the first half of the contract honoured: between any two cells of music, the keyboard gets polled, so a press waits at most one bell. The chime and its calls retire; the boot no longer blocks; `end_step`'s return-to-title resets the tune to the toll, so the bells always start from the beginning of their story.

Now play the build and *use* it. Tap SPACE — briskly, the way a player who's seen the title five times taps it. About half your taps do nothing. Listen to where the tune is when they die: inside the **rests**. A note is a few tenths of a second, but the rests between phrases run to a quarter of a second and more, and `title_rest` — for now just `rest` with its own name — counts those frames stone deaf. The poll sits *between* steps; a step is note *plus* rest; and a tap that lands mid-rest is simply gone. The title has started swallowing input, which is the one thing a title must never do — the screen whose entire job is "press a key" now ignores keys, rhythmically, to its own music.

## Milestone 2 — the rests learn to listen

You can't poll during a *note* — the beeper owns the CPU with interrupts off, and that's physics. But the rests are HALT loops riding the heartbeat, and every one of those frames can afford a glance at the keyboard. So: `title_rest` polls SPACE each frame, and on a press it **latches** — sets `title_key_seen` and cuts the rest short. It can't *act* on the press; acting means starting a game, and a rest twelve calls deep in a tune step is no place to make that decision. It records, returns, and next pass `title_step` finds the latch and starts the game before playing another bell. A tap can land inside a note now — where it waits a few tenths at most — but never inside a rest.

The latch needs hygiene, and the diff's last lines are it: when *any* screen returns to the title, the stale latch is cleared along with the tune position — otherwise a press that ended the previous game starts the next one. You met this bug's cousin in Gloaming as the input lock; the latch is its inverse (a press remembered rather than a press ignored), and both exist for the same reason: **input outlives the moment it was pressed in**, and every screen transition has to decide what to do about that.

## When it's wrong, see why

- **The title ignores taps.** Step 1's disease. If it persists past step 2: the latch is set but never *read* — `title_step` must check `title_key_seen` before polling, and clear it when it acts.
- **A game starts itself the moment you return to the title.** The stale latch — `end_step`'s title path must zero `title_key_seen` along with `title_music_step`.
- **The tune stutters or drones.** Each step must play exactly one beep and one rest, then return — a `jp title_rest` that became a `call` plays the rest and then falls into the next step's notes.
- **The bells resume mid-phrase after a game.** The tune position isn't reset on the way back to the title; the toll is the tune's front door.
- **The snuff blip plays when *you* light a lamp.** The call is on the wrong side of the mirror — `blip_lit` belongs in the player's commit, `blip_snuff` in the wisp's, and each reads its own buffer.

## Before and after

The game sounds finished now — bells at the door, a voice for every verb — but the durable lesson is the contract. Music on an interactive screen is a *negotiation with the input system*: play in steps, poll between them, make the silences listen, latch what you can't act on, and clear the latch at every door. That shape — blocking work sliced small, interleaved with polling, with a byte carrying intent across the slices — is how every beeper game of the era kept its title responsive, and it's cooperative multitasking built by hand. You'll meet it again the first time you put music *under* gameplay.

And one thing on this title is now quietly wrong. Look at the pip row under GLOAMING: three cells, counting the *lives you kept* on your best win. On a five-watch night, "most lives held at any single dusk" is a strange thing to treasure — you can hold three lives through watch one on a run that dies at watch two, and the title will remember it as your finest hour. The night got longer than a life. Something about what deserves to survive a run has changed, and the scoreboard hasn't noticed yet.

## Try this: play the contract breaking

Rebuild step 1 (or just re-read its `title_rest`) and count your dead taps per minute. Then step 2, same test. Nothing about the *tune* changed — every note, every rest identical — and the screen went from infuriating to instant. Input contracts are invisible when honoured and unbearable when not; this pair of builds is the cleanest demonstration you'll ever own.

## Try this: recompose the bells

The tune is eight steps of three bytes' interest each: pitch, length, rest. Retune it — a major lift instead of the cold toll, shorter rests, a resolving final note instead of the hanging D. Notice the hanging D was doing *design* work: an unresolved phrase makes silence feel pending, which keeps the title alive. (If notation-first composing appeals more than poking hex, unit 10 shows the tool this game's phrases were actually written with.)

## Try this: the joystick on every 1984 inlay

Gloaming unit 5 taught you to read a half-row from port `$FE`; here's that lesson generalised to a second device. The **Kempston interface** — on virtually every commercial inlay of 1984 — is one port and no rows: `IN A, ($1F)` returns `000FUDLR`, *active high* (a 1 is a press — the opposite convention to the keyboard). Two edits wire it in. In the held-key gate, after the third keyboard read merges into `E`:

```text
            ld      e, a
            in      a, ($1F)
            and     %00001111
            or      e
```

…and at the end of the direction decode, before the final `ret`, the stick's own branches — `bit 1` to `.pleft`, `bit 0` to `.pright`, `bit 3` to `.pup`, `bit 2` to `.pdown`, each on `jr nz` because pressed reads high.

Build it, don't touch anything, and watch: **the lamplighter walks himself to the west wall.** Nothing is broken — there's no interface attached, and an unmapped port doesn't politely read zero; it reads whatever noise the bus is carrying (on this emulator it pins one direction; on real hardware it flickers with the screen contents). Active-high input plus floating noise equals phantom presses, and now you know why those 1984 inlays listed *"Keyboard, Kempston, Sinclair"* as a **menu** — joystick reads were gated behind the player choosing them, because always-on Kempston steered by static. Gate yours the same way (a flag set from a title-screen choice, checked before the two new reads) and you've shipped period-correct joystick support — and learnt the deeper rule: never trust an input source you haven't confirmed exists.

## What you've learnt

- **A tune changes the input contract** of every screen that returns to it — the unit's law, learnt by feeling it broken.
- **Slice blocking work and poll between slices** — one tune step per pass is cooperative multitasking, hand-rolled.
- **Latch what you can't act on**: the listening rest records a press; the decision-maker honours it next pass — and stale latches are cleared at every screen door.
- Notes are deaf (interrupts off, physics); **rests can listen** (heartbeat frames, one IN each).
- Voices complete the vocabulary: the snuff's falling third is audio-as-information for events outside your gaze.
