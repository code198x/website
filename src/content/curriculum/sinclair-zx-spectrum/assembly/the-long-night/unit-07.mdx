Hold all five watches of the night you built last unit, and your reward is… watch five again. And again. The tables clamp, the difficulty plateaus, and the run only ever ends one way: NIGHT FALLS, eventually, for everyone. That's the *endless arcade* model, and it has an honourable history — but the playtest killed it here without sentiment, and the reasoning deserves to be quoted into your design vocabulary: a run this demanding needs an ending you can **reach**, not just a wall you eventually hit. Endless reframes every run as a defeat deferred. A reachable dawn reframes the same five watches as a story with a last page — and the difference in how a loss *feels* afterwards is enormous, because now you lost three watches short of morning, not three watches into infinity.

## Milestone 1 — an ending that exists

The machinery is everything you already know how to build, which is itself the lesson — endings are cheap once state machines are in your hands. A new state, `STATE_DAWN`. One check at the only beat the game can be won: the eighth lamp lights, and before the ordinary held-screen path runs, ask *which watch is this?* On the last one, take the other exit: the morning's own screen — same lift-and-say shape as the win screen, with DAWN BREAKS in the morning's words — the fanfare, and a state whose only future is the title. The run is over, and it was won.

Two shapes worth noticing. The check is `cp NUM_DUSKS - 1` on the *current* watch — dawn is deciding at the moment of the win, not after the interstitial, so the fifth held dusk never shows the ordinary held screen at all. And `end_step` needed **no change whatsoever**: STATE_DAWN isn't STATE_WIN, so the existing "anything that isn't a win waits for SPACE, then title" branch already does the right thing. A well-shaped default absorbs new states for free — that's why the win check was written as the special case back when there were only two endings.

## Milestone 2 — the ceremony

Play step 1's ending and feel what's missing. The words are right; the *world* hasn't heard the news. Gloaming's endings already knew this lesson in miniature — the fanfare blocks gladly, because the game it would pause is already over — and the dawn deserves the full version: **morning enters from the sky**. Row by row, top to bottom, the ground warms to gold: cobble and pool alike to `GLOW`, and the night's mist burns off entirely — dark cells get their stipple back and then their gold, the one place the tendril's claims are erased by something other than the ring. Walls, lamps, text and the two figures keep their own palettes; morning arrives around them.

The sweep *blocks* — two HALTs per row, twenty-three rows, most of a second of nothing but sunrise — and that's a decision, not an accident. Blocking is this course's cardinal sin during play, and endings are the one place the rule inverts: there is no game left to starve. Pace ceremony in heartbeats, same as the rests in the music.

## When it's wrong, see why

- **Dawn fires at watch 4.** The check compares against `NUM_DUSKS - 1` because `dusk` counts from zero — watch five *is* dusk 4. Off-by-one here ends the night a watch early, and the run feels inexplicably short.
- **Dawn never fires.** The check must run *before* the ordinary win path, in the same beat — after `jr z, .thedawn` falls through to the held screen, there is no second chance until the next watch.
- **The sweep gilds the walls or the text.** The row loop covers rows 1–23, and the cell test converts only `COBBLE`, `GLOW` and `DARK`. Anything else it touches means a missing compare — the sweep's restraint is what keeps the figures and the words legible through it.
- **Mist survives into the morning.** The `DARK` branch must re-blit the stipple *and* set the gold — mist pixels under a gold attribute read as gilded fog, which is atmospheric and wrong.
- **SPACE during the sweep is lost.** It isn't — it's blocked, like everything else, and that's correct. The input lock after the sweep is what eats the *stale* press, exactly as on every other end screen.

## Before and after

The night has a last page. Losing still costs the same, but it costs *against morning* now — the run has a destination, the fifth watch is a climax instead of a treadmill, and the game's full shape is finally on the board: five watches, one dawn, or the fall of night. The engineering was almost embarrassingly cheap — one state, one check at the one beat wins happen, one screen, one sweep — which is the unit's quiet point: by this stage of the course, *meaning* costs less than mechanics. The expensive part was knowing an ending was needed; the playtest paid that for you.

## Try this: morning from the east

Make the sweep enter column by column instead — the loops swap roles, the HALTs move to the column loop. Watch it and notice it reads as *wind*, not sunrise. Vertical light is a deep human prior; the direction of a ceremony is doing more work than you'd guess.

## Try this: the long night indeed

Set `NUM_DUSKS` to 7 and pad both tables (clamped entries for the tail). The dawn moves two watches further away — feel how the *fourth* watch changes emotionally when it's no longer the penultimate one, even though its numbers are identical. Endings radiate backwards through everything that leads to them.

## What you've learnt

- **Reachable endings beat endless walls** — the same difficulty reads as story instead of attrition when there's a last page.
- New endings are **cheap state machinery**: a state, a check at the win beat, a screen — and a well-shaped default (`end_step`'s else-branch) absorbs the new state without edits.
- **Ceremony may block** — endings are the one place frame discipline inverts, and heartbeats make good ceremony clocks.
- Sweeps are recompute-with-manners: the dawn is `recompute_pools`' vocabulary (read cell, convert by kind) with time as a paintbrush.
- Watch your zero-based counts at the boundaries — the last watch is `NUM_DUSKS - 1`, and endings are where off-by-ones get seen by everyone.

## What's next

The night is complete — and almost silent. One chime at boot, a blip per lamp, and nothing else in a game that now runs ten minutes. **Unit 8** opens the finish phase: the title learns the dusk bells — a real tune — and with it the hardest small lesson in game audio: a tune on the title *changes the input contract of every screen that returns there*. Plus, at last, the snuff finds its voice.
