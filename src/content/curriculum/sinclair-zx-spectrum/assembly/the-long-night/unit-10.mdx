Win the night you've built and listen to what happens: DAWN BREAKS, the sweep pours gold — and the music that plays is `fanfare_held`, the *win jingle*, promoted to the biggest moment in the game because nothing better exists. It works. It's also borrowed, and you can hear the borrowing: the run's crowning moment sounds exactly like every held dusk on the way to it. ## The audition sheet

Here's how the real phrase was chosen, and the method matters more than the winner. Not "compose the right thing" — **write three candidates, one musical idea each, and choose by ear**. The candidates were sketched in a small notation this course's tooling understands (notes, durations, rests — nothing you can't read cold), and each got a WAV to audition. This is the sheet, verbatim from the prototype's files:

```text
phrase dawn_answer       ; the bookend — dusk's falling bell, reversed
  C5 400ms               ; where dusk ended, the ground note
  rest 8
  E5 300ms               ; the answer rises the motif
  rest 4
  G5 550ms               ; and opens into morning

phrase dawn_bloom        ; stepwise sunrise — climbs by step, not arpeggio
  C5 170ms
  rest 2
  D5 170ms
  rest 2
  E5 170ms
  rest 2
  G5 220ms
  rest 3
  C6 700ms               ; the sun clears the roofline

phrase dawn_amen         ; the plagal cadence — the night lets go
  F5 260ms               ; the held breath
  rest 3
  E5 260ms               ; released
  rest 6
  C5 320ms               ; ground regained
  rest 4
  C6 650ms               ; morning, wide open
```

The vote went to `dawn_answer`, and the reason is the module's last musical idea: **motif discipline**. Listen to the dusk bells again — they end on the motif falling to C5, the day giving up. `dawn_answer` starts on that same C5 and walks the motif *back up*, ending on a G5 that doesn't resolve… until the fanfare's C answers it, which is exactly what plays next in the dawn sequence. It isn't the prettiest candidate in isolation (`dawn_bloom` probably is). It's the one that makes the *game's whole soundtrack* one story: dusk asks, dawn answers, the fanfare completes. Themes beat tunes.

## Milestone 1 — the answer, in its place

The phrase compiles to the same straight-line shape as every voice in the game — beeps in a row, rests riding the heartbeat — with one idiom at the end you've seen once before: the held G5 wants more than 255 cycles, so it's sounded twice, seam hidden in the wave. It slots into the dawn sequence *first*: the motif rises, the G5 hangs… the sweep holds the breath… and the fanfare's C answers it. Ceremony, phrased.

## When it's wrong, see why

- **The dawn hangs forever on one note.** The held G5 is two beeps back to back with `B` at maximum — a loop that reloads `B` without the second explicit beep never terminates the note.
- **The phrase is out of tune with the bells.** Motif discipline is arithmetic: the chime's C5/E5/G5 must use the same half-period constants as the tune's motif steps. If you transcribe pitches from a different octave table, the conversation becomes two strangers.
- **The chime plays but the sweep doesn't follow.** Order in `.thedawn` is the phrase, the sweep, the fanfare — a `ret` slipped between calls ends the ceremony early.
- **Silence at the dawn.** The chime runs before the state flips, inside the win beat — if you've moved it after the `game_state` write, it still plays; if you've moved it into `end_step`, it plays when the player *leaves* the dawn, which is a different (and worse) piece of theatre.

## Try this: audition your own dawn

Write a fourth candidate — in the notation above, or straight into `chime_dawn`'s shape. One musical idea, stated in the comment before a note is placed. Render or hand-assemble it, play the full dawn sequence, and judge it the way the sheet judged: does it *answer the bells*, or is it just nice? Keep whichever wins honestly. It's your game — that's the point of the byte gate: from here, any change you make is a fork of the real thing.

## Try this: the 16K question

This game fits a 48K Spectrum with room to spare — and nearly half the machines this course's era sold were 16K. Look at your symbol file: where does the build end? The answer (and the two-line change it invites) is a story for another day, but the habit is the try-this: **know your byte budget**. Commercial reality on this platform was a number, and the number was smaller than you think.

## What you've learnt

- **Motif discipline**: phrases that share material make a soundtrack one story — dusk asks, dawn answers, the fanfare completes.
- **Audition, don't oracle**: three candidates, one idea each, chosen by ear — the working method for any creative decision a program has to embody.
- Notation-first tooling: `build198x beeper` turns readable phrases into audition WAVs and phrase assembly — tools emit *content*, engines stay yours.
- The held-note idiom, second sighting: one byte of duration means long notes are two beeps with a hidden seam.
- **The byte gate**: your build equals the shipped game exactly — convergence held across a full redesign, and provable with one compare.
