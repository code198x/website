## What you'll build

A keep on a dark hill. Magic has gone wrong; the shadows walk its halls. You enter alone to recover the Heart of the Keep — the relic that can drive the dark back. Find it, escape, the keep stands. Fail, and the shadows take another.

A multi-room top-down adventure in the lineage of *Atic Atac* and *Sabre Wulf* — the 1984 Ultimate bar for an ambitious bedroom-coded game on the 48K Spectrum. Nine connected rooms, a hooded thief in red, walls and doors and locked doors and gold and keys and shadow hazards. A beeper title theme. Title screen, play, win-or-die, restart. Cassette quality, period-correct, real.

## The flagship insight

The Spectrum's most famous limitation — one foreground colour and one background colour per 8×8 cell — is Shadowkeep's whole rulebook.

Walls are cells whose attribute starts with INK 1 (blue). Doors are PAPER 5 (cyan). Treasure is BRIGHT yellow. Keys are BRIGHT *and* FLASH yellow. Hazards are FLASH red. The exit is green. **Eight cell types, every one decoded from a single attribute byte.** Collision is `LD A,(HL)` and `BIT n,A`. There are no sprites for walls; no tile engine; no map editor. The screen is the map. The attribute byte is the rule.

This is the design's flagship insight, and it's what makes Shadowkeep a Spectrum game rather than "a maze game on a Spectrum".

## How the build works

Shadowkeep is taught using the **incremental scaffold** pattern. There's no pre-typed engine to type in and chip at. Unit 1 starts from nothing — one byte at one address. Unit 16 is the keep, complete. Each unit between adds exactly one mechanic.

Phase 1 is sixteen units:

| # | Title | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A Coloured Block | One attribute write, one cell on screen |
| 2 | Reading the Walls | Read attributes back; AND/OR for bit fields |
| 3 | Loops Make Rooms | DJNZ fills a row; nested loops fill regions |
| 4 | The Keyboard | Read keys via port `$FE`, half-row scanning |
| 5 | Our Hero | Bitmap-drawn hooded thief in the centre of the screen |
| 6 | Stride | Erase-and-redraw movement, hero responds to keys |
| 7 | The Walls Hold | Attribute-read collision; you can't walk through walls |
| 8 | The Room Has Bones | Room described in a data table; same code, any layout |
| 9 | Two Rooms | A door takes you to a second room |
| 10 | The Whole Keep | 9 rooms (3×3), full N/S/E/W navigation |
| 11 | Treasure | Pick up gold cells; score increments |
| 12 | Keys and Locks | Keys unlock locked doors |
| 13 | Things That Hurt | Static shadow cells; lives; brief invulnerability |
| 14 | The Beeper Sings | Title-screen melody from a data table |
| 15 | Escape | Exit cell ends the game in victory |
| 16 | The Keep Stands | Title → play → win/die → title, the whole loop |

A learner who finishes Phase 1 has typed sixteen units of Z80 and ended up holding a thing they could give a friend on a real cassette.

## Beyond Phase 1

Shadowkeep is planned to four phases, with capacity for up to eight if the game justifies it. Later phases bring wandering shadow enemies, a wider keep (up to 5×5 rooms), a 2-frame walking cycle, in-game beeper SFX, save/load, and a lore-driven ending. Each phase ships a complete, playable game in its own right — a Phase 1 Shadowkeep is a finished game; a Phase 4 Shadowkeep is a richer one.

## What you'll learn

By the end of Phase 1:

- **Attribute memory** — the 768-byte grid that defines every colour on screen, and the address formula that finds any cell.
- **Bitmap memory** — including the Spectrum's notorious three-thirds quirk, and how to draw an 8×8 hero on top of an attribute-driven world.
- **Z80 fundamentals** — `LD`, `DJNZ`, `JP` / `JR` / `CALL`, register pairs as pointers, indexed iteration, conditional jumps, bit-test instructions.
- **Hardware I/O** — the keyboard via port `$FE`, the beeper via port `$FE` bit 4.
- **Game state** — lives, score, inventory, current room, game-state byte; a state machine in fewer instructions than you'd think.
- **Music driver** — a tiny one-voice beeper player driven by a tick counter, fed by a byte-pair pattern.
- **Real-hardware testing** — Fuse for fast iteration, Spectrum Next or 48K real iron for ground truth.

## Prerequisites

This is Game 1 of the Spectrum assembly track. No prerequisites beyond willingness — every Z80 concept and every Spectrum hardware quirk is introduced in the unit that needs it.

## Toolchain

- **Assembler**: `pasmonext` (a `pasmo` derivative; Apple Silicon native)
- **Emulator**: Fuse (recommended) or any 48K-accurate Spectrum emulator
- **Real hardware** (optional but recommended): a Spectrum Next, +2/+3, or original 48K with a tape interface
