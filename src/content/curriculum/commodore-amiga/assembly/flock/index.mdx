## What You'll Build

A farmyard arcade game in the lane-crossing tradition of *Frogger* and *Freeway*. One dim sheep at a time, you steer up from the field, across a lane of tractors and hay carts, over the stream by footbridge or hay bale, and into a pen in the fold. Fill every pen and the level is won. Lose a sheep — squashed, drowned, or carried off by the current — and the flock thins.

The road and the river play by opposite rules, and that opposition is the game: on the lane, touching a sprite kills you; on the stream, *not* touching one drowns you. Avoid, then ride. Same hardware, two meanings.

## Why This Game?

Flock is built on the Amiga's **hardware sprites** — the chip draws and moves your characters for free, no bitplane redrawing, no erasing. The sheep is a sprite. The tractor is a sprite. The hay bale you ride is a sprite. And the question every crossing turns on — *did the sheep touch that?* — is answered by **hardware collision detection**, the custom chipset feature this game teaches from both sides:

- **Avoid-mode** — `CLXDAT` says sheep met tractor: she's lost.
- **Ride-mode** — `CLXDAT` says sheep stands on bale: she's safe, and the bale carries her.

Around that core the game layers everything a real arcade game needs, one unit at a time: joystick steering, sprite animation, deterministic traffic you learn to read, lives, score, a fold to fill, Paula sound, a title screen — and a rare **black sheep** worth risking everything for.

## Skills You'll Master

- **Hardware sprites** — data, position and control words, sprite DMA
- **Sprite collision** — `CLXCON`/`CLXDAT`, in avoid and ride modes
- **Copper playfield composition** — bands and per-band recolouring
- **Joystick input** — grid-step movement
- **Deterministic hazards** — lane choreography you read and time
- **Game structure** — lives, score, levels, state machine, Paula SFX

## Prerequisites

The [Meet the Machine](/commodore-amiga/assembly/meet-the-machine) primer — the 68000, the Copper, and the bitplane display. Flock picks up exactly where it left off.

You'll need an emulator (FS-UAE, WinUAE or Emu198x), an assembler (vasm), and disk tools (amitools).
