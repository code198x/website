Most arcade games bolt a countdown to the top of the screen and call it tension. Flock's clock is in the sheep. Stand mid-crossing weighing your options for too long and she starts to *tremble*; a moment more and she frets out loud; keep dithering and the wind gets up and she **bolts** — three hops, forward, and the stick has nothing to say about it. Time pressure, with no timer drawn anywhere: the pressure is a character losing her nerve, and it only ever punishes standing still.

## The Clock on Her Courage

<CodeFromFile src="commodore-amiga/assembly/flock/unit-16/snippets/01-fret.asm" />

`nerve` counts the frames since her last hop, and the whole mechanic is two thresholds on that one counter. The shape matters more than the numbers:

- **Hopping clears it.** Any hop, any direction. A player crossing at a thinking pace never meets this system at all — which is the design's spine. Pressure that punishes *playing* is noise; pressure that punishes *freezing* teaches.
- **The home field doesn't count.** South of the crossing she can graze all day. The clock starts where the danger starts.
- **The beats don't count either.** The post-squash calm and the pen celebration freeze the world; charging her nerve through them would be a fine print nobody agreed to.

At the first threshold she's *worried* — one thin falling baa (latched by `fretted`, so it plays once, not fifty times) and the trembling starts. At the second, `panic` loads three hops and `fret`'s job is done; the bolt itself lives in `steer`.

## Walking on the Spot

<CodeFromFile src="commodore-amiga/assembly/flock/unit-16/snippets/02-trembling.asm" />

The trembling costs no art at all. Her walk has been two step images since Unit 4, swapped on every hop. A frightened sheep walks on the spot: when `nerve` is past the edge, `showframe` ignores which step she's *on* and alternates the pictures off the frame counter — feet going, sheep going nowhere. It reads instantly from across the room, and it's nothing but a different *index* into pictures that already existed. A surprising amount of animation is exactly this: not new frames, new *schedules* for old ones.

## The Bolt

In `steer`, panic is checked before the stick is read — that ordering *is* the mechanic. While `panic` counts down, her hops are `up` whether or not anyone's holding `up`; the player is a passenger for three beats. Two details earn their keep:

- **She bolts forward.** Toward the fold, into whatever the road is doing. Forward is fair — it's where she was going — and it's frightening, because the road doesn't pause for panic. A random direction would be crueller and less readable; backwards would be a free escape. Forward is the only direction that's both kind and dangerous.
- **A bolt at the fence slams her into it.** The panic hop calls `trypen` exactly as a real `up` would — so a sheep who panics at the gate might pen herself in a blind rush. That's not a bug to fence off; it's the system's rules composing into farmyard truth.

This is the first time the game overrides the player. Used carelessly that's poison — input is a promise. It works here because the player was *warned twice* (tremble, baa), the override is short, and every frame of it traces back to the player's own choice to stand still.

## Experiment: Tune the Fear

- `NERVE_EDGE equ 50` — she's a wreck the moment she steps out. `equ 400` — she's stoic and the system disappears. The personality of the whole game lives between those two numbers.
- `PANIC_HOPS equ 8` — a real stampede. She'll cross half the farmyard on her own. Find the count where the bolt is a punishment but not an execution.
- Make the *black* sheep fret sooner — she's already skittish; `isblack` is sitting right there. One compare turns two systems into one character.
- Bolt *backwards* instead. Feel how the threat evaporates when panic is an escape hatch. Direction is design.

## If It Doesn't Work

- **She trembles at the spawn point?** The home-field gate is missing — `nerve` should only tick when she's north of the field row. Grazing isn't dithering.
- **The worried baa machine-guns?** That's the `fretted` latch gone. Threshold sounds need a played-once flag that hops reset.
- **She bolts mid-squash-beat, or while the fold celebrates?** Every world-freezing state must also freeze the clock on her courage. List your freezes; gate `fret` on all of them.
- **The bolt fights the player?** Check the order in `steer`: panic first, stick second. If the stick is read first, a held direction wrestles the bolt and she staggers diagonally through traffic.

## Try This

1. **A visible fuse.** The trembling is the warning — but could her *colour* drain as nerve builds? Unit 15 taught you to poke her palette mid-game. Three pokes from wool toward grey, scheduled off `nerve`. Decide whether the extra telegraph helps or whether the tremble was already enough.
2. **Panic spreads.** If she bolts within a sheep's width of the cow, the cow should startle — a one-off speed burst, `vehtab` is data and Unit 14 made speeds pokeable. Comedy, and a systems lesson: reactions are just writes.
3. **Count the bolts.** A run where no sheep ever bolted deserves recognition at the fold. One flag, cleared on bolt, paid at the pen — design the bonus so the player discovers it instead of reading about it.

## What You've Learnt

- **Time pressure without a timer** — the clock is a character's patience; it punishes freezing, never playing.
- **Threshold design** — one counter, two thresholds, three behaviours (calm, warned, gone). Most escalation systems are exactly this shape.
- **Animation is scheduling** — trembling is the old step images on a new clock. New feel, zero new frames.
- **Latched one-shots** — threshold events need a fired-once flag with a reset; otherwise every threshold sound is a machine gun.
- **Overriding input is a loan, not a theft** — warn first, keep it short, make it traceable to the player's own choice. Then the game taking the stick reads as drama instead of betrayal.
