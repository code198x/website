A ship can respond immediately, slide past its target or take time to gather speed.
The controls may be identical, but the experience changes with the rules for movement.

[Try the movement playground →](/experiments/game-feel/index.html)

Hold a direction, release it and reverse. Compare three ships receiving the same
input, then inspect their acceleration and braking. This browser experiment is a
starting point; the lesson sequences below are planned.

## Play, compare, explain

Start with an experience you want to create. Change one rule, play again, and
explain what changed. Responsive does not always mean instant: momentum can make
a vehicle satisfying to steer, while the same delay may frustrate someone trying
to place a cursor precisely.

Feedback should help the player understand an action. More flashing, shaking or
sound is not automatically better. Provide ways to reduce disruptive effects and
alternatives when an important cue relies on colour or sound alone.

A grace period can help a player escape after respawning. A new-button-press check
can prevent accidental restarts. Neither demands a universal invulnerability timer
or a mandatory pause: choose and test the rule for the game in front of you.

The Craft explains those choices. The system tracks show how to implement and
measure them on a particular machine.
