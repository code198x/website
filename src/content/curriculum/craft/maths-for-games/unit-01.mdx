Twelve rolls will not settle whether a die is fair. They are enough to make a small experiment you can inspect: one numbered result at a time.

Use **Sinclair BASIC on a stock 48K ZX Spectrum**. Begin with a new program and enter this listing. BASIC runs numbered lines in numerical order; the gaps leave room for later additions. [Meet BASIC](/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/meet-basic/) offers a slower introduction to entering and editing programs.

<CodeFromFile src="sinclair-zx-spectrum/basic/dice-roller/teaching/roll/dice-roller.bas" />

Enter `RUN` without a line number. You should see twelve numbered rolls, each from 1 to 6, followed by a `STOP statement` report. STOP is deliberate: this first version has finished its work. The actual rolls will vary; they do not need to include every face.

## Turn a fraction into a face

RND produces a pseudorandom number from zero up to, but not including, one. It follows a calculated sequence. `RANDOMIZE` without a number chooses a starting point using the machine's clock; it does not turn the computer into a source of physical chance.

Multiplying RND by six gives a number from zero up to, but not including, six. INT rounds down to a whole number. The result is therefore 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5. Adding one makes the six face numbers.

LET stores that result in d. Each visit to line 450 calls RND once and stores one roll; printing d does not roll again.

<Question prompt="Could this expression produce a seven? Could it produce a zero?">

Neither. Before adding one, INT can produce only 0 through 5. Afterwards the range is 1 through 6. The order matters: the six positions are the result we want, not a limit we check after rolling.

</Question>

## Repeat a small experiment

Line 30 stores twelve in b, the batch size. FOR starts i at one. NEXT increases i and returns to the loop until all twelve iterations have run. In `PRINT i;": ";d`, semicolons keep the number, separator and die face together. The end of the PRINT statement moves to a new row.

Line 10 chooses a black border and paper, white ink and normal brightness, then clears the screen with CLS. Colons separate statements on the same numbered line. This is presentation setup; it does not change the roll.

RUN again. Repeats and clusters are allowed. A row of repeated faces is not evidence of a broken loop: the numbered labels show that the loop is advancing. If there are fewer than twelve labels, inspect line 30 and the FOR/NEXT pair.

For now, keep the batch at twelve so every numbered result fits on screen. Later, a fixed display will let you compare much larger batches without scrolling.

<details>
<summary>Keyboard notes</summary>

The Spectrum enters keywords using their keys rather than spelling them out. RANDOMIZE uses T in keyword mode; RND uses T in extended mode, reached with Caps Shift and Symbol Shift together. INT uses R in extended mode. FOR uses F, NEXT uses N, and TO is Symbol Shift with F. The ROM supplies keyword spacing; preserve the spaces inside quoted text.

</details>
