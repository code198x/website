A straight route and an across-then-up route reach the same point. Their lengths are different. The right triangle lets us measure the straight route.

<AimingExperiment step={2} title="Measure the triangle" />

## Three sides, one relationship

Start at (120, 90). Going across and then up travels 120 + 90 = 210 units. The straight line is shorter. It forms the **hypotenuse**: the side opposite the right angle.

For a right triangle, Pythagoras gives r² = x² + y². Squaring means multiplying a number by itself. Here 120² + 90² = 14,400 + 8,100 = 22,500. Its positive square root is 150, so the straight distance is 150 units.

The experiment calculates that distance as you change the point. Change X to −120. The coordinate is negative, but the side length is 120. Squaring removes the sign: (−120) × (−120) = 14,400. Distance stays non-negative.

<Question prompt="Double both coordinates to (240, 180). Does the distance double or quadruple?">

It doubles to 300. Each squared term quadruples, giving 90,000, but the square root brings the factor back to two. The triangle keeps its shape.

</Question>

## Separate direction from size

At (120, 90), the horizontal share of the sloping side is 120 / 150 = 0.8 and the vertical share is 90 / 150 = 0.6. At (240, 180), those ratios are still 0.8 and 0.6. They describe the same direction at a different distance.

Try (0, 0). Distance is zero and there is no direction to describe; dividing by zero cannot produce these shares. The experiment marks them as undefined.
