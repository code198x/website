# SOL-20

*Historical Reference*

The SOL-20 was the first true personal computer - a complete system with built-in keyboard, video display, and cassette storage. Unlike the Altair's switches and lights, the SOL-20 established the template that all future personal computers would follow.

## Historical Significance

The SOL-20 bridged the gap between hobbyist computers and practical machines. It proved that personal computers could be complete, usable systems rather than bare circuit boards requiring extensive assembly.

## Revolutionary Design

- **All-in-One**: Keyboard, display, and computer integrated
- **Built-in Video**: 64×16 character display
- **Cassette Interface**: Reliable program storage
- **Attractive Case**: Professional walnut cabinet
- **Complete System**: Ready to use out of the box

## Technical Excellence

Built around the Intel 8080A, the SOL-20 featured:
- Up to 65KB RAM (massive for 1976)
- High-quality keyboard
- Composite video output
- Multiple expansion slots

## Market Impact

At $995 assembled, the SOL-20 was expensive but represented incredible value as a complete system. It influenced every personal computer that followed, establishing expectations for integrated design.

## Legacy

The SOL-20's greatest contribution was proving that personal computers should be complete, integrated systems. Every modern computer - from the Apple II to today's laptops - follows the template established by the SOL-20.
