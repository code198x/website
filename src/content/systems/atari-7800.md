# Atari 7800

*Historical Reference*

The Atari 7800 ProSystem was Atari's attempt to reclaim the home console market from Nintendo. With superior graphics and backward compatibility with the 2600, it should have succeeded but arrived too late with too little software support.

## Historical Significance

The 7800 represents Atari's last serious console effort before the Jaguar. Despite technical advantages over the NES, Nintendo's third-party licensing agreements limited software availability, relegating it to a footnote in console history.

## Technical Features

- **Backward Compatible**: Played all Atari 2600 games
- **Better Graphics**: 320×200 resolution, 256 colors
- **Advanced Sprites**: Better than NES capabilities
- **Sound Limitation**: Had to use 2600's TIA for compatibility

## Too Little, Too Late

The 7800 was actually completed in 1984 but shelved during Atari's sale to Jack Tramiel. By its 1986 release, Nintendo had already locked up the market with exclusive licensing deals.

## Legacy

The 7800 proved that technical superiority means nothing without software. It remains a "what if" console - had it launched in 1984 with proper third-party support, gaming history might have been different.
