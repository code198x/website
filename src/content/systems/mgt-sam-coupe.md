# SAM Coupé

*Historical Reference*

The SAM Coupé was designed as the spiritual successor to the ZX Spectrum, offering Spectrum compatibility with vastly superior graphics and sound. Created by Miles Gordon Technology (makers of Spectrum peripherals), it arrived in 1989 just as the world was moving to 16-bit machines.

## Historical Significance

The SAM represented the ultimate evolution of 8-bit Z80 computing. With a 6MHz Z80B, up to 512KB RAM, and impressive graphics modes, it showed what might have been if the 8-bit era had continued.

## Technical Features

- **Z80B at 6MHz**: Fastest Z80 in a home computer
- **Spectrum Compatible**: Ran most Spectrum software
- **128 Colors**: From a palette of 128
- **4-channel Sound**: Built-in SAA1099 chip
- **512×192 Mode**: Highest resolution of any 8-bit machine

## Too Little, Too Late

Despite its impressive specifications, the SAM arrived just as the Amiga and ST dominated and consoles were taking over gaming. MGT went bankrupt in 1990, though the Czech company continued support until 1992.

## Legacy

The SAM Coupé proved that 8-bit machines still had untapped potential. Its small but devoted community continues to develop for it, creating demos and games that showcase what this remarkable machine could do. It remains the ultimate "what if" of 8-bit computing.
