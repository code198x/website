# MOS Technology KIM-1

_Historical Reference Only_

The **KIM-1** (Keyboard Input Monitor) was released by MOS Technology in 1976 as a low-cost development system for the then-new 6502 microprocessor. It was intended for engineers and hobbyists and quickly became one of the most popular training and prototyping systems of its era.

## Historical Significance

With just a hexadecimal keypad, six 7-segment LED displays, and 1 KB of RAM, the KIM-1 allowed direct memory manipulation and program entry. Despite its minimalism, it was immensely influential:

- **6502 Introduction**: One of the first systems available to hobbyists using the revolutionary MOS 6502 chip
- **Educational Platform**: Taught countless engineers and hobbyists about microprocessor programming
- **Foundation Technology**: The 6502 processor later powered the Apple I, Commodore PET, Atari 2600, and Nintendo Entertainment System
- **Design Influence**: Its architecture was later used as the foundation for the Commodore PET

## Technical Innovation

- **MOS 6502 Processor**: 1MHz 8-bit processor that would go on to power numerous iconic systems
- **Direct Programming**: Hexadecimal keypad allowed direct memory manipulation and program entry
- **LED Display**: Six 7-segment displays for address and data visualization
- **Expansion Ready**: Application connector allowed for extensive hardware expansion
- **Built-in Monitor**: KIM Monitor provided basic system functions and debugging capabilities

## Legacy Impact

The KIM-1's influence extends far beyond its modest specifications:

- **Processor Legacy**: The 6502 became one of the most important processors in computing history
- **Educational Value**: Thousands learned assembly programming on this system
- **Commercial Foundation**: Many successful computer companies started with KIM-1 development
- **Design Philosophy**: Demonstrated that powerful computing could be accessible and affordable

## Did You Know?

- The KIM-1 was one of the first systems available to hobbyists that used the MOS 6502 chip, which also powered the Apple I, Commodore PET, Atari 2600, and Nintendo Entertainment System
- Its design was later used as the foundation for the Commodore PET
- Expansion boards added serial ports, displays, keyboards, and more, transforming the basic system into a full computer
- Many early video game developers learned assembly programming on the KIM-1

## Development Context

While the KIM-1 is too primitive for practical modern game development education, understanding its role in computing history provides valuable context for the evolution of the platforms that followed. The 6502 processor it introduced became the foundation for an entire generation of gaming systems.
