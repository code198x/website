# VIC-20

*Historical Reference*

The VIC-20 was Commodore's first mass-market home computer and the first computer of any kind to sell over one million units. With only 3.5KB of usable RAM, it taught a generation of programmers to work within extreme constraints.

## Historical Significance

Launched by William Shatner asking "Why buy just a video game?", the VIC-20 proved that home computers could be affordable and accessible. At $299, it undercut all competitors and found its way into homes that had never considered a computer before.

## Technical Innovation

- **Color and Sound**: Built-in color graphics and sound in 1980
- **Expandability**: Cartridge port and expansion slots
- **User Port**: Allowed hardware hacking and peripherals
- **Built-in BASIC**: Made programming accessible to everyone

## Legacy

The VIC-20's success funded Commodore's development of the C64, which would become the best-selling computer model of all time. Many legendary programmers, including Jeff Minter, got their start on the humble VIC.
