# Commodore PET 2001

_Historical Reference_

The Commodore PET 2001 was the first true all-in-one personal computer. Announced at CES in January 1977 and shipping that October, it beat the Apple II and TRS-80 to market as a complete system with built-in keyboard, monitor, and cassette drive.

## Historical Significance

The PET represented Commodore's entry into personal computing and established them as a major player. While it found more success in education and business than homes, it proved that computers could be appliances rather than kits.

## Technical Innovation

- **All-in-One Design**: Complete computer in one unit
- **Built-in Monitor**: 9" CRT display included
- **PETSCII Characters**: Unique character set with graphics symbols
- **IEEE-488 Port**: Professional-grade peripheral interface
- **Chicklet Keyboard**: Infamous calculator-style keys (later improved)

## Chuck Peddle's Vision

Designed by Chuck Peddle (creator of the 6502), the PET embodied his vision of computers as tools for everyone, not just hobbyists. Its professional appearance and integrated design influenced all future business computers.

## Legacy

The PET's success in schools introduced a generation to computing. Commodore's experience with the PET directly led to the VIC-20 and ultimately the C64. The IEEE-488 port became a standard in laboratory equipment that persists today.
