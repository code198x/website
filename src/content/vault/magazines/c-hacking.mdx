**C= Hacking** was a Usenet net-magazine about making the Commodore 64 and 128 do
things they were not documented to do. Craig Taylor announced it on `comp.sys.cbm` on
**8 February 1992**:

> Due to the recent influx of "Tech-Know" (People who actually understand / hack te
> C=64 into doing stuff that was previously unknown) I am going to tentativly start a
> C= Hackers Net-Mag (Hacking in the 90's since of the word, not the 80's).

The parenthesis is the editorial line. Issue 1 spends its opening column separating
the two meanings of the word, and rules the other one out of scope: the magazine
"will not tell people how to 'phreak', how to hack voice mailboxes and other illegal
activities."

## The premise

What it would do instead was explain the gap between the C64's specification and what
people had got out of it:

> The Commodore 64 came out in late 1982 (I believe) and was known to only support 16
> colors, 320 x 200 resolution graphics of 2 colors... Since then people have pushed
> the Commodore 64 to its limits with apparent resolution of 320 x 200 with a
> resolution of 4 colors and even higher... more than 8 sprites on the screen... fast
> high-quality digitized sounds.

Every clause in that list is a technique the magazine went on to document.

## The Demo Corner

Pasi 'Albert' Ojala's column is the reason a large part of C64 practice exists in
writing rather than only in demos:

| Issue | Article |
|---|---|
| 3 | "Missing Cycles" — raster timing, bad lines, sprites |
| 4 | "FLI — More Color To The Screen"; "Introduction to the VIC-II" |
| 5 | "Stretching Sprites" — "how to trick VIC into making some very large sprites" |
| 6 | "[DYCP](/vault/techniques/dycp) — Horizontal Scrolling"; "Opening the Borders" |
| 7 | Tech-tech, or "wave" |

The DYCP article was written in Finnish in May 1991 and translated for the magazine a
year later, which is how a Finnish demo scene's working knowledge reached an
English-speaking readership.

## Not only demos

The table of contents is broader than the column suggests. Craig Taylor serialised a
machine-language course and a C128 multitasking kernal; Craig Bruce covered dynamic
memory allocation on the 128, burst-mode fastloading on the 1571 and 1581, and an
MS-DOS file reader; Robert Knop wrote on GEOS 128; Bruce Vrieling on rasters and the
GeoPaint file format; Anthony McSweeny disassembled and annotated Rob Hubbard's music
driver.

## Distribution

Free, with authors keeping their rights: redistribution "in whole, freely for
non-profit use", with individual articles requiring the author's permission, and a
cap of five US dollars for disk and library costs.

## The run

Issue 21 appeared on **5 February 2002**, almost exactly ten years after the founding
post it reprints, still asking readers to write up whatever they were working on.
