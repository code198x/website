**Pixel** (ΡΙΧΕL) was a Greek computer magazine published in Athens by
[Compupress](/vault/companies/compupress) from **October 1983** to **June
1996**. This library holds **133 issues and 17,310 pages** of it — more than it
holds of any title from any territory except Britain, Italy, the United States
and Germany.

## It was commissioned by a questionnaire

Most magazine launches are explained afterwards, by people remembering. *Pixel*
explained itself on page three of issue one, and named its evidence.

The magazine was a spin-off. Its publisher's letter describes it as an effort by
an existing title, *Computer για Όλους* (*Computer for Everyone*), to cover home
and personal computers "από πλευράς προγραμμάτων" — from the programs side. The
reason it existed at all was a reader survey:

> "Η ανάγκη για τη δημιουργία ενός τέτοιου εντύπου εντοπίσθηκε στο
> ερωτηματολόγιο του 5ου τεύχους του *Computer για Όλους*, όπου διαπιστώθηκε ότι
> ένα μεγάλο ποσοστό αναγνωστών ζητούσαν περισσότερα έτοιμα προγράμματα."

The need was identified in the questionnaire in the parent magazine's **fifth
issue**, where a large proportion of readers asked for more ready-made programs.
A publisher counted the replies and started a magazine.

It opens by claiming to be "**το πρώτο ελληνικό περιοδικό**" — the first Greek
magazine — dealing exclusively with program listings for the most popular micros
of the Greek market.

## The promise, and what happened to it

The same letter states the frequency and the ambition: the new publication would
be **τριμηνιαίο**, quarterly, "με προοπτική να πυκνώσουν στο μέλλον οι εκδόσεις
του" — with the prospect of the editions becoming more frequent in future.

The holdings record whether that came off. One issue in 1983, four in 1984, then
twelve in 1985 and eleven a year, every year, until 1995. Quarterly for fifteen
months and monthly for the next eleven years.

## A price list nobody meant to leave

Issue one lists the machines it carries programs for, and adds a parenthesis that
turns a contents page into a market record: *η σειρά παρουσίασης των μοντέλων
έγινε με βάση την τιμή* — the models are presented **in order of price**.

1. [Sinclair ZX81](/vault/systems/zx81)
2. [Jupiter Ace](/vault/systems/jupiter-ace)
3. [Sinclair ZX Spectrum](/vault/systems/zx-spectrum)
4. Oric 1
5. Texas Instruments TI-99/4A
6. TRS-80 Color
7. [Commodore VIC-20](/vault/systems/vic-20)
8. NewBrain
9. [BBC Model B](/vault/systems/bbc-micro)
10. Epson HX-20
11. Apple IIe
12. Osborne 1

This is the Greek market ranked by what the machines cost there in late 1983,
stated by someone who had no reason to misreport it — a listings magazine
ordering its own contents. It is not a British price list with the names
translated: the Jupiter Ace sits second, above the Spectrum, and the imported
American machines occupy the top of the range.

Every listing, the letter adds, had been checked by the magazine's contributors
so readers could type them in "άφοβα" — without fear.
