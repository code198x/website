**PAGE 6** began as a **computer club's magazine**. Issue 1 is cover-dated December 1981 / January 1982, edited by **Les Ellingham**, published bi-monthly by **B.U.G.** — the **Birmingham User Group**, described in its own editorial as "the largest independent Atari Computer Club" in the country. Six issues cost £3.75; a single copy was 65p.

Its reason for existing is stated plainly on page 4: none of the general UK computing magazines were "of much help" to [Atari](/vault/companies/atari) owners. So the club made its own.

## It outlasted almost everything

87 issues are held here, running from **December 1981 to July 1998**, the later ones under the title **New Atari User** after a rename around issue 38 in mid-1989.

That is a **seventeen-year** run, and it is worth setting against the professional titles this Vault spends most of its time on:

A magazine run by an enthusiast club from Stafford lasted longer than [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield)'s entire output, and is the **earliest-dated British computer magazine** in this library apart from *C&VG*, which precedes it by one month.

## Why that is not a curiosity

The story this Vault usually tells about the British computer press is a professional one: [EMAP](/vault/companies/emap), [Future](/vault/companies/future-publishing), Newsfield, [Europress](/vault/companies/europress), and the commercial logic of [launching in October to catch Christmas](/vault/culture/magazine-distribution).

*PAGE 6* ran on none of that. It was bi-monthly, sold by subscription from a club, and it began in December. It survived the collapse of the 8-bit market, the arrival and departure of the 16-bit machines, and every publisher in the table above except one.

⚠ Longevity is not circulation, and this entry claims nothing about how many people read it. What the holdings establish is that it kept coming out.
