**Amiga Computing** was a British [Amiga](/vault/systems/commodore-amiga) monthly published from **June 1988** to **October 1997**, 154 issues of which are held here. It is the second-largest body of Amiga writing in this library after *[CU Amiga](/vault/magazines/cu-amiga)*, and like that title it had no entry in this Vault until the holdings were counted rather than read.

## The colophon is the interesting part

Issue 1 names the publisher as **Database Publications Ltd**, of Europa House, Adlington Park, Macclesfield. Seven issues later the same small print adds a clause:

> "Database Publications is a division of **Europress Ltd**."

That is a line worth following, because Europress is the company that took *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* and *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* out of the wreckage of [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield) in 1991. This Vault dates that handover to CRASH issue 94, and issue 94 — December 1991 — happens to contain a staff writer's aside that settles it from the inside: "as CRASH work isn't all I do **at Europress**".

So the Macclesfield house that had been quietly publishing an Amiga monthly since 1988 was, three years later, the one holding the two most-loved 8-bit magazines in Britain. The Ludlow titles did not go to a stranger; they went to an established competitor that had been in the market the whole time.

## What it was for

Where *[Amiga Power](/vault/magazines/amiga-power)* was a games magazine with opinions and *[Amiga Format](/vault/magazines/amiga-format)* was a general monthly with cover disks, *Amiga Computing* carried the word **computing** in its title and meant it — the Amiga as a machine to work on as well as play on. Its issue 1 contents run to adventure tutorials alongside hardware news, and the masthead sat beside Database's other computing titles rather than beside the games press.

⚠ That characterisation is drawn from the issues held here rather than from any statement of editorial policy, and the balance shifts across nine years.
