**Club Commodore** was a bulletin for **VIC-20** and CBM users, published in Spain
from 1982 by **Microelectrónica y Control, S.A.** of Barcelona and Madrid. Six
issues are held here, from **December 1982** to July 1983, of sixteen to twenty
pages each.

It is the earliest club magazine in this library — two months ahead of
*[Atari Club Magazin](/vault/magazines/atari-club-magazin)* and seven years ahead
of *[Club Nintendo](/vault/magazines/club-nintendo)*.

## A third kind of house organ

The publisher is named in the bulletin's own advertising: **"Distribuidor
exclusivo para España: Microelectrónica y Control, S.A."** — Commodore's
exclusive Spanish distributor, not Commodore.

That makes a third arrangement inside the category this Vault calls a
[manufacturer's house organ](/vault/culture/magazines-across-borders), and the
three are worth keeping apart:

| | Published by | Made |
|---|---|---|
| *Club Nintendo* | Nintendo's own European subsidiary | centrally, localised outward |
| *Atari Club Magazin* | Atari's German operation | locally |
| *Club Commodore* | an independent national **distributor** | locally |

The third is the loosest tie to the manufacturer, and it shows in what the
bulletin contains.

## Technical, not promotional

The contents of the December 1982 issue are not what a marketing department
produces:

- the VIC-20 **memory map**
- a character editor for the VIC
- a program to encipher and decipher messages
- home automation with the VIC-20
- common-emitter stage calculations
- getting the VIC-20 started on **RTTY** for radio amateurs

The cipher article introduces itself as "algo exótico y poco habitual en las
revistas serias de informática" — something exotic and unusual in serious
computing magazines — and the listing's own REM header credits **Microelectrónica
y Control, Barcelona, 14 September 1982**. The distributor's staff were writing
the programs.

The editorial voice is engineers'. Announcing an increase to sixteen pages, it
describes the jump as "**exactamente el doble (+3 dB)**" — a decibel joke about
page count, three decibels being a doubling.

## Four languages

The December issue closes its editorial with new year wishes, and does it four
times:

> ¡FELICES FIESTAS Y PRÓSPERO AÑO NUEVO! · BON NADAL I FELIÇ 1983! ·
> ZORIONAK ETA URTE BERRI ON · ¡BOAS FESTAS!

Spanish, **Catalan**, **Basque** and **Galician** — Spain's four language
communities, addressed in a computer bulletin in December 1982, seven years after
the end of a regime under which the other three had been suppressed in public
life. The same editorial quotes a Catalan saying, introduced as "como decimos en
Cataluña".

⚠ Nothing in the bulletin comments on any of that, and this entry does not
either beyond noting the date. It is recorded because it is on the page and
because no British or American publication in this library does anything
comparable.
