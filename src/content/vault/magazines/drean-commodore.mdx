**Drean Commodore** was published in Argentina from **December 1985**. This
library holds 27 issues, running to 1988.

## The machine has the licensee's name in it

Argentina did not import the Commodore 64; it built one. **Drean** manufactured
Commodore machines locally under licence, and the magazine names the product the
way its readers actually bought it:

> "…que Peek presentó en el mercado argentino para **la Drean Commodore 64**."

Not *the Commodore 64 sold by Drean* — the Drean Commodore 64. Across the run
the word **Drean** appears more often than any machine name: 1,095 times against
the C-64's 837.

That single naming detail is the difference between Argentina's route to the
machine and Britain's, and it is a third route again from
[Brazil's](/vault/culture/brazilian-market-reserve), where imports were
restricted and the domestic industry built MSX machines and cloned the ZX81 and
Apple II instead of licensing Commodore at all.

## What it covered

Issue one leads on the **C-16**, presented as an advanced computer with features
"que sólo se encuentran en equipos de precios muy superiores" — found only in far
more expensive equipment. Its reviews are of games a British reader would
recognise, under names a British reader would not: *Cabecera de Playa* is *Beach
Head*, alongside *Blue Max*, *Donkey Kong* and *Archon*. It promises to cover
"los programas que se encuentran en el mercado argentino" — the programs found on
the Argentine market, which is not the same set as anyone else's.

⚠ The C-16 lead is issue one's, not the run's. Measured across all 27 issues the
C-64 dominates at 66% of machine mentions and the C-16 takes 7%. A cover is not a
corpus, and this entry originally drew the wrong conclusion from that first page
before the run was counted.

The other Argentine machines appear as competitors: **Talent**, the local MSX,
and **Czerweny**, the firm behind the country's ZX81-family clones.

⚠ This entry is built from issues 1 and 2 and from counts across the run. This
library holds no account of Drean the company beyond what its magazine says, and
nothing here establishes the terms of the licence, the volumes built, or when
manufacture stopped.
