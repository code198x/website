**Commodore Computer Club** was published from **Piazza Arduino, Milan**, from
**September 1982** to December 1991 — ninety issues over nine years, under
*direttore responsabile* **Michele Di Pisa**.

It is the earliest club magazine in this library. *[Club
Commodore](/vault/magazines/club-commodore)* in Spain follows it by about a
month, *[Atari Club Magazin](/vault/magazines/atari-club-magazin)* by four, and
*[Club Nintendo](/vault/magazines/club-nintendo)* by seven years.

## A Commodore 64 preview in September 1982

Issue one leads with **"Anteprima: Commodore 64"** — a preview of a machine
announced in January 1982 and only then reaching buyers. Around it sit seven
programs for the VIC and the PET, a part-work course (*un corso a dispense*)
called *Impara a programmare col VIC*, an article on PET machine language, and
one on what is in the VIC's memory.

The interview is with **Dario Baldan Bembo**, introduced as a
*"compucantautore"* — a computer singer-songwriter, a coinage the magazine
appears to have made for him. The cover addresses its readers as
*"Commodoristi"*.

## Paid in cassettes

The contributor terms on page 51 are worth recording because they are not money:

> "ciascun articolo e/o programma pubblicato… verrà compensato con **l'invio
> d'una intera scatola di cassette e/o dischetti**."

Each article or program published will be compensated with a whole box of
cassettes or diskettes. The rights position is broad in the other direction —
reproduction rights are *"ceduti illimitatamente all'editore"*, ceded without
limit, explicitly including use in *"banche per lo scambio di programmi"*,
program-exchange banks.

Compare *[MICRO-80](/vault/magazines/micro-80)* in Australia three years earlier,
which bought reader programs and paid royalties in cash. Two magazines with the
same reader-supplied model and quite different terms.

## Whose club?

Nothing in the first issue claims a relationship with Commodore. There is no
manufacturer's address, no distributor's imprint, no licence line — the
publisher is a Milanese editorial office, and the magazine is registered as a
supplement under a **Tribunale di Milano** number issued on 3 December 1979,
nearly three years before it launched.

⚠ So this belongs with *[Nintendo Magazine
System](/vault/magazines/nintendo-magazine-system)*'s Australian edition and
*[Australian Personal Computer](/vault/magazines/australian-personal-computer)*
in the group this Vault cannot classify: a magazine carrying a manufacturer's
name whose relationship to that manufacturer is not stated anywhere found here.
Unlike Spain's *Club Commodore*, which prints its publisher's status as
Commodore's exclusive Spanish distributor, this one prints nothing.
