**ST Magazine** was a French monthly for the **Atari ST**, published by
**Pressimage S.A.R.L.** from **September 1985**. This library holds 128 issues,
running to September 1998.

It is the earliest ST-dedicated magazine held from anywhere — six months before
*[Atari ST User](/vault/magazines/atari-st-user)* (March 1986) and nearly four
years before *[ST Format](/vault/magazines/st-format)* (July 1989). The first
magazine about the Atari ST in this collection is French.

## Launched on a price argument

Issue one's cover asks a single question — **"POURQUOI ACHETER UN 520 ST"**, why
buy a 520 ST — and the editorial answers it for readers who do not yet own the
machine. Its case is not about capability but about cost, made with a comparison
table: what you would have to spend on a **Macintosh** or an **IBM PC** equipped
with the same options and the same software already included with a 520 ST at
**8,432 francs**.

The magazine is priced for three countries on its cover — 25 F, 200 Belgian
francs, 10 Swiss francs. Like *[Amiga Joker](/vault/magazines/amiga-joker)* in
German, its market is a language rather than a border.

## "We are not journalists"

The December 1986 editorial opens with a line that turns a trade complaint into
something more interesting:

> "**Non ! Non ! Nous ne sommes pas des journalistes !** C'est en tout cas ce que
> décident les membres de la **Commission paritaire** en privant *ST Magazine*,
> sous sa forme actuelle, de leur **agrément**."

No, no, we are not journalists — that at any rate is what the members of the
Commission paritaire decide, in depriving *ST Magazine*, in its present form, of
their approval. The reasoning is given plainly:

> "Les revues dédiées (à un standard informatique) sont assimilées, grosso modo,
> à des **catalogues de publicité pour la marque**."

Magazines devoted to a single computing standard are treated, roughly, as
advertising catalogues for the brand.

France ran a state accreditation regime for the press, and it had reached a view
on what a single-platform computer magazine was. The British press had no
equivalent body, which is why nothing like this appears anywhere else in this
Vault: a British magazine could be as devoted to one machine as it liked and no
one adjudicated whether it counted as journalism.

## The line the Commission drew

The judgement is worth sitting with rather than dismissing, because this Vault
draws a version of the same line. It separates a
[manufacturer's house organ](/vault/culture/magazines-across-borders) —
*[Club Nintendo](/vault/magazines/club-nintendo)*,
*[Atari Club Magazin](/vault/magazines/atari-club-magazin)* — from the
independent press, on the grounds that one is published by the industry and the
other about it.

The Commission paritaire put the line somewhere else: not at who publishes, but
at how narrow the subject is. On that test a magazine can be editorially
independent, staffed by people with no connection to the manufacturer, and still
function as promotion, because covering one machine and nothing else means never
telling a reader to buy something different.

⚠ And *ST Magazine* is an awkward case for its own defence. It was published by
Pressimage, its subscription cheques were payable to **Micro-Application**, and
the page carrying this editorial is itself largely an advertisement for
Micro-Application's Atari ST books. That does not make the Commission right, and
this entry takes no view on whether it was — but the magazine's commercial
entanglement is visible in the same column as its protest.

⚠ What happened next is not recorded here. The magazine continued for another
twelve years, so the loss of *agrément* was evidently not fatal, but this library
holds no account of whether the decision was reversed, appealed, or worked
around by changing the magazine's form.
