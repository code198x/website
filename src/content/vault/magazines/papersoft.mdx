**PAPERsoft** was an Italian weekly published in Milan by **J-soft editrice**
from **15 June 1984**. Ninety issues are held here, thirty-two pages each,
running to 27 December 1985.

Its cover states what it is: **"il primo settimanale di software su carta"** —
the first weekly of software on paper.

## A weekly because monthlies are too slow

The opening editorial makes the case for the cadence, and the argument is about
readers rather than about software:

> "Un settimanale di programmi per il personal computer? Chi l'avrebbe mai detto,
> fino a pochi mesi fa? … **Lasciamo dunque ai mensili il compito di approfondire
> certi argomenti, di pubblicare programmi complessi.**"

A weekly of programs for the personal computer — who would have said so, a few
months ago? The monthlies, it says, are all lovely, but "l'impazienza degli
appassionati possessori di un personal computer è senza limiti" — the impatience
of enthusiastic computer owners is limitless. So leave the monthlies to go deep
and publish complex programs; this will do the other thing.

What the other thing is, it also states: *PAPERsoft* publishes **only** software.
No reviews, no news, no hardware. A computer without software, the editorial
says, is just a nice electronic box, and what it offers is programs "pronto per
l'uso" — ready to use — of a kind it cheerfully calls **"usa e getta"**, use and
throw away, or else worth keeping in an inexhaustible library.

## Every program crossed a border

The contents page is the valuable part, because it credits each listing twice.

| Program | Author | Translated and adapted by |
|---|---|---|
| Meteor Storm | R. Harper | M. Cerofolini |
| Atterraggio lunare | H. Sanday | M. Cerofolini |
| La disfida di Camelot | G. Garrett | E. Re Garbagnati |
| Aereoporto | D. Courtier-Dutton | C. Panzalis |
| Gotcha! | D. Smoak | U. Barzaghi |
| Vic-Tastiera | D. Bingamon | U. Barzaghi |

English-speaking authors throughout, and a named Italian **traduttore e
adattatore** for each. *Trad. e adatt.* on a program listing is more than a title
change: prompts and messages become Italian, and the code is adapted to the
machines actually on sale.

This Vault has documented [three ways a magazine crossed a
border](/vault/culture/magazines-across-borders). This is a different crossing
and a smaller unit — the **software itself**, moving one listing at a time, with
the adapter credited by name beside the author. It is what a non-English market
looked like before commercial localisation reached it: not translated boxes in
shops, but translated listings on newsprint, weekly, for anyone willing to type.

## It split by machine

Volume 1, through 1984, is multi-format — the first issue alone carries programs
for the TI-99/4A, the Spectrum, the C64 and the VIC-20. Volume 2, in 1985, breaks
into machine-specific series: a **Commodore** run of thirty-one issues held, a
**Sinclair** one, and two further numbered series.

That is the same move this Vault records among British publishers, who launched
one title per machine as each market grew large enough to carry it — compressed
here into eighteen months and happening inside a single weekly rather than across
a catalogue.

⚠ This entry is built from the first issue and the shape of the run. This library
holds *PAPERsoft* in quantity and no account of it from elsewhere, so nothing
here says where J-soft obtained the programs, whether the original authors were
paid, or on what terms the adaptations were made. The credits establish that
adaptation was done and named, not that it was licensed.
