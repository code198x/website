**Micro Sistemas** launched in **Rio de Janeiro** in **October 1981**, priced at
Cr$ 200,00, describing itself as *"a primeira publicação dedicada exclusivamente
aos microcomputadores e calculadoras programáveis"* — the first publication
devoted exclusively to microcomputers and programmable calculators. This library
holds 65 issues, running to **July 1995**.

Its first issue is earlier than all but one British title held here. *Your
Computer* precedes it by four months; *Computer & Video Games* follows it by one.

## "The dream is over"

The editorial on page four is titled **O Sonho Começou** — the dream has begun —
and it opens somewhere no computer magazine opens:

> "Há poucos meses a bala de um assassino encerrou, inapelavelmente, uma época
> que já estava virtualmente terminada. Chorando John Lennon, milhões de jovens
> e pais de jovens, no mundo inteiro, tristemente murmuravam: **o sonho
> acabou**. Assim é a vida: a cada instante há sonhos acabando, e sonhos
> começando."

A few months earlier an assassin's bullet had closed an era that was already
virtually over; mourning John Lennon, millions murmured *the dream is over*.
Such is life — at every moment there are dreams ending and dreams beginning. And
one of the beginning ones, the sentence continues, is this magazine.

The same editorial states the expectation plainly: *"tal como aconteceu nos
países que nos antecederam neste caminho, as fábricas e marcas vão se
multiplicar"* — just as happened in the countries that came before us on this
road, the factories and brands will multiply. **Factories**, not importers. That
is the assumption the rest of the issue runs on.

## The issue is a snapshot of a market being built

Around the editorial sits a first issue unlike a British one of the same year.

- The cover story is a **Brazilian machine**: "O D-8000 é o primeiro lance da
  [DISMAC](/vault/companies/dismac) na disputa do mercado brasileiro de
  computadores pessoais."
- A feature visits a primary school in Tijuca where five hundred children aged
  two to ten are taught with a computer, and the head teacher explains that
  manufacturers' leaflets had "muitas fotos bonitas e poucas informações" —
  lots of pretty photographs and little information.
- A chess feature asks whether man or machine will be *Grande Mestre*, with the
  president of the Brazilian Chess Confederation.
- A report covers a visit by **Wayne Green**, editor of the American *Kilobaud*
  and *80 Microcomputing*.
- On page two, a shop advertisement: *Computique, a boutique dos computadores*,
  in the Cassino Atlântico shopping centre on Avenida Nossa Senhora de
  Copacabana.

## The interview that explains the machines

The issue's long interview is with **Oscar Alegre**, commercial director of
DISMAC, and it is the most valuable thing in it. He begins by welcoming the
arrival of the first Brazilian magazine devoted to microcomputing, and then sets
out the reasoning behind [Brazil's market
reserve](/vault/culture/brazilian-market-reserve) — including why a Brazilian
computer cost more than an imported one, and why that was accepted.
