**Amiga Action** was a games magazine for the [Commodore Amiga](/vault/systems/commodore-amiga) that began in Chichester, West Sussex, and ended up in a business park in Cheshire. Gollner Publishing launched it from Chichester in October 1989. Within a year it was being made at Adlington Park, near Macclesfield, alongside *[Amiga Computing](/vault/magazines/amiga-computing)*, and the company name in its credits changed three times in five years. After 1996 it lived on only as the games section of that same stablemate.

## A launch from Chichester

The first issue's colophon gave the publisher as Gollner Publishing Ltd, Latham House, Quarry Lane, Chichester, with Hugh Gollner as publisher. Its own news pages announced the launch: Gollner, "one of the leading publishers of specialist computing magazines", was "the company behind the stunning new magazine", which promised "100% full colour" and coverage of arcade games, adventures and role-playing games. The first cover lines included "Psygnosis' Beast reviewed", "9 pages of news & previews" and "12 joysticks megatested".

Amiga Action was not Gollner's first games magazine. Its first editor, Steve Merrett, was introduced as "a long-term reviewer in our sister mag, ST Action", who would go on writing for that magazine while editing the new one. He was twenty.

It arrived in a crowded month. *[Amiga Format](/vault/magazines/amiga-format)* from Future Publishing had started in August 1989, EMAP's *Commodore User* was on its way to becoming *[CU Amiga](/vault/magazines/cu-amiga)*, and Database Publications had been publishing *Amiga Computing* since June 1988.

## Score sheets and a computer

Issue 1 explained how the magazine reviewed a game, and printed the form to prove it. One reviewer played the game and wrote the main review and captions; then the others played it "for hours" and added their own comments. Each reviewer also filled in a score sheet that broke the game into parts, such as smoothness, quality of animation, speed of movement and music, and marked each from 1 to 5, with a separate "overall gut feeling" score. "The two results are combined using a special piece of computer software, which calculates the final scores."

## From Gollner to Europress

In April 1990 readers were told that "the name Gollner Publishing has been replaced by the new-fangled sounding Interactive Publishing", after what the magazine called "a mind-boggling merger". The deal was with Database Publications, the publisher of *Amiga Computing*, which later recalled that Interactive Publishing had managed *Amiga Computing* too, for fourteen months. By the July 1990 issue Interactive Publishing's address was Europa House, Adlington Park, Macclesfield, Database's own building. Hugh Gollner stayed on as publisher.

The editors changed as well. By December 1990 Steve White was the magazine's "team leader", and by June 1991 he was named as editor.

In June 1991 Derek Meakin put all of his group's companies under one name, and Interactive Publishing became Europress Interactive, the publisher of *Amiga Action*, *ST Action* and *Games-X*. *Amiga Action*'s June 1991 credits already read "Europress Interactive Ltd"; for a few months after, some issues still printed the old name. So Europress did not buy the magazine in 1991: it had been inside Meakin's group since the spring of 1990, and in 1991 the group gave it a new company name.

## IDG and the end

At the end of 1994 Europress sold eleven of its magazines to the American publisher [IDG](/vault/companies/idg). *Amiga Action*'s March 1995 credits read "CREATED BY... IDG Media Ltd, Adlington Park, Macclesfield", with Denise Wright as publisher; by June 1995 Paul Roundell was editor. The team stayed where it was.

In December 1996 *Amiga Computing* told its readers that "from our Christmas issue onwards we will incorporate our sister magazine, Amiga Action", which it called "Britain's most popular Amiga entertainment title". Its old games section, System, was replaced by pages headed *Amiga Action*. According to a later secondary account, the separate magazine had run to 89 issues, the last dated December 1996.
