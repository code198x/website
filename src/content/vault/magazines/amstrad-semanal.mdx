**Amstrad Semanal** — *Amstrad Weekly* — was published in Spain by **Hobby
Press, S.A.**, beginning on **3 September 1985**. This library holds a hundred
regular issues, running to 1987, plus a run of *Amstrad Especial* specials.

## Weekly, and demonstrably so

Most magazine entries take a publishing frequency from the masthead. Here the
filenames carry it: the first five issues are dated **3, 10, 17 and 24 September
and 1 October 1985** — seven days apart, four times over.

The weekly computer magazine was rare. Counting titles in this library whose
issues are dated to the day rather than the month, *Amstrad Semanal*'s hundred
issues is the largest such run held of anything, ahead of Britain's *Personal
Computer News* at twenty-three. The longest weekly run in this collection is
Spanish, and it is about a single machine.

## What a weekly puts on its first cover

Issue one leads on three things: machine code taught *bit a bit*, an assessment
of the newly announced **CPC 664** — "¿Qué aporta el nuevo Amstrad?", what does
the new Amstrad bring — and a review headlined:

> "**KNIGHT LORE** — Analizamos el programa n.º 1 en el mundo"

We analyse the number one program in the world. This Vault dates
*[Knight Lore](/vault/games/knight-lore)*'s British release from simultaneous
multi-title advertising; here it is nine months later on the cover of a Spanish
weekly, described without qualification as the best game there is.

The advertising is the other thing worth reading. Games are sold on being
"**íntegramente en castellano**" — entirely in Castilian — and on having already
succeeded elsewhere: *Beach Head* is pitched as "por fin para tu Amstrad el juego
que mayor popularidad alcanzará en Commodore y Spectrum", at last for your
Amstrad the game that reached greatest popularity on the Commodore and Spectrum.
A market being supplied second, and told so plainly in the copy.
