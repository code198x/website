The first issue of **ZX Computing**, dated Summer 1982, called itself "Britain's Biggest Magazine For The Sinclair User" and promised over 100 pages of "information and programs". There was a chess game that fitted in 1K, business routines, a survey of memory expansions, a do-it-yourself memory upgrade and "secrets revealed at last" about machine code. Its editor, Tim Hartnell, timed eight benchmark programs on a ZX81 with Henry Budgett, the editor of Argus's *Computing Today*, and interviewed Nick Lambert of [Quicksilva](/vault/companies/quicksilva) about the memory packs he had started making for the ZX80. The same issue carried [Toni Baker](/vault/people/toni-baker)'s "First steps in Machine Code".

That was the magazine's character for most of its five years: it treated the Sinclair machines as computers to program and expand, and reviewed games alongside the listings and the hardware. It changed as its readers did, from the ZX81 to the [ZX Spectrum](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum) and the [QL](/vault/systems/sinclair-ql), and in March 1986 it went monthly, promising more for games players.

## Fast facts

- **Published:** Summer 1982 to June 1987; quarterly at first, bi-monthly from late 1982, monthly as *ZX Computing Monthly* from 28 March 1986.
- **Publisher:** Argus Specialist Publications, of 145 Charing Cross Road and later 1 Golden Square, London, which also published *Computing Today*.
- **Editors:** Tim Hartnell (1982–83); Roger Munford (by late 1983); Ray Elder (from early 1984); Bryan Ralph (by 1986).
- **Machines:** the [ZX80](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx80) and [ZX81](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx81), then the ZX Spectrum and the QL.
- **Price as a monthly:** £1.50.

## Quarterly, then bi-monthly

Argus planned ZX Computing as a quarterly, "published quarterly in April, July, October and January", according to the first issue's masthead. It did not stay one for long. By the third issue the subscription pages carried the line "Now bi-monthly!", and later issues carried double dates such as June/July and Aug/Sept. It was one of a family of Argus computer titles: in 1983 *Personal Computing Today*, *ZX Computing*, *Computing Today*, *Home Computing Weekly* and *Personal Software* jointly sponsored a series of Home Computer Shows around Britain.

Hartnell was a prolific author of program books, and the magazine advertised them. In 1982 it described him as "the editor of ZX Computing" in an offer for his *Getting Acquainted with your ZX81*, "128 pages" with over 80 programs. His own articles taught structured programming, how to write a board game that could think, and later, in the December 1984 issue, how to build an expert system on the QL.

By the Christmas issue of 1983 the editor was Roger Munford. Ray Elder took over at the start of 1984, and in the December 1984/January 1985 issue he wrote that it marked "the end of a full year sitting in the editor's chair". Elder ran hardware projects of his own and stayed on after the editorship changed hands, answering readers' technical problems in issues that called him "our technical wizard".

## Programs, projects and machine code

Listings were the core of every issue, and the magazine paid for them. Baker kept writing about machine code, and in early 1984 the magazine regretted that it had reached "the last part of Toni Baker's superb series that takes the mystery out of Machine Code". In 1986 [Steve Turner](/vault/people/steve-turner), the author of *Quazatron*, wrote a series of advice for would-be games writers, and the magazine printed a dictionary look-up routine from the game. Even in 1987 the monthly was still sending readers who wanted to learn assembly language to *[Machine Code for Beginners](/vault/books/machine-code-for-beginners)*.

## How it scored games

ZX Computing gave marks, but not percentages. In the June/July 1983 issue a "Summary Chart" rated each tape out of five for qualities including addictiveness, use of graphics and value. By 1985 each review ended with stars for graphics, addictiveness and overall, up to five of each. In the August/September 1985 issue those stars were given to Beyond's *[Shadowfire](/vault/games/shadowfire)*, an icon-driven game whose reviewer warned that "some time has to be spent learning to use the system".

The verdicts in words could be blunt. Reviewing the *Hotshots* compilation in June 1986, the reviewer admitted finding *Gyron*, "a variation on the old 3D maze games", "a bit dull", while praising *Shadowfire* as "an exciting and challenging game". In June 1987 a round-up of budget software judged that [*Thrust*](/vault/games/thrust) was "perhaps their best release for the Spectrum" from [Firebird](/vault/companies/firebird).

## ZX Computing Monthly

In early 1986 the magazine announced: "On March 28 ZX Computing goes monthly", at £1.50 an issue. From then on the running heads read *ZX Computing Monthly*, and the November 1986 cover described it as "for all Sinclair users". Bryan Ralph was the editor; in the October 1986 issue he signed a readership survey asking which features readers enjoyed and which they would like "buried forever".

The monthly lasted just over a year. In May 1987 the *C16/Plus 4 Handbook* reported that "Computer Gamer, and ZX Computing" would produce their last issues in June, "both in fact owned by Argus Press". The June 1987 issue is the last.
