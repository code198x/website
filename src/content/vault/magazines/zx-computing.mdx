**ZX Computing** covered the ZX81 and Spectrum from **1982**, and its character
comes from where it came from rather than what it covered. It emerged from the
same publishing stable as *Computing Today* — a general electronics and
computing title — and it reads like it: listings, hardware projects, technique
articles, with games reviewed alongside rather than as the point.

That makes it a useful corrective. Read only the games press and the Spectrum
looks like a games machine. ZX Computing treated it as a computer that also
played games, which is closer to how it was sold.

## Fast facts
- **First issue:** 1982 — by August that year it was already referring back to "the first issue of ZX Computing".
- **Publisher:** Argus Specialist Publications — the *Computing Today* stable.
- **Covered:** ZX81 and ZX Spectrum.
- **In this library:** 37 issues, June 1982 to June 1987 — roughly bi-monthly.
- **Spectrum reviews:** 345 scored reviews recorded in ZXDB — more than [*ACE*](/vault/magazines/ace-magazine) or [*The Games Machine*](/vault/magazines/the-games-machine) managed.

## Its scoring
ZX Computing frequently declined to give a number. Where the games press ran
percentages, ZX Computing often filed a verdict in words — *Rebelstar* is rated
just "**Good**"; [*Thrust*](/vault/games/thrust) likewise. Elsewhere it used
five-point ratings, giving *Shadowfire* 5/5 in August 1985 and
[*Heavy on the Magick*](/vault/games/heavy-on-the-magick)'s stablemates similar
marks.

A magazine that will print "Good" as a review score is one that does not think
the score is the review, which is worth remembering when this Vault tabulates
its marks alongside percentages.
