**Nintendo Magazine System** was launched in Britain by
[EMAP](/vault/companies/emap) in **October 1992**. This library holds 21 British
issues and 10 Australian ones.

## The licence runs the other way

The small print in issue one is the interesting part, because it inverts the
arrangement this Vault records for
*[Club Nintendo](/vault/magazines/club-nintendo)*:

> "**Nintendo of America Inc.** and its associated companies are the owners of
> the copyright in certain of the characters, artwork, logos, scripts,
> trademarks and representations used in this publication. **These are under
> licence to EMAP.**"

*Club Nintendo* is Nintendo publishing a magazine. *Nintendo Magazine System* is
a commercial publisher issuing its own magazine and licensing Nintendo's
intellectual property to do it. The copyright line is EMAP's — "© EMAP IMAGES
1992" — and the address is Farringdon Lane, not Großostheim.

So "official Nintendo magazine" describes two quite different things in this
Vault, and which one a given title is can only be settled by reading its
colophon:

| | Publisher | What Nintendo supplies |
|---|---|---|
| *Club Nintendo* | Nintendo of Europe GmbH | the magazine |
| *Nintendo Magazine System* | EMAP Images | a trademark and artwork licence |

An arrangement of the second kind lets a magazine carry the marks and the
blessing while remaining editorially a publisher's product — which is a
different proposition for a reader than a house organ, and looks identical from
the newsagent's shelf.

## The Australian edition, and what cannot be said about it

An *Nintendo Magazine System* also appeared in Australia. Its colophon names a
different company entirely:

> "*Nintendo Magazine System* is published by **Trille Corporation**, 363A Pitt
> Street, Sydney, NSW 2000, Australia."

It has its own staff — a publisher, editorial director and managing editor, all
Australian, all given joke middle names on the masthead — its own numbering, and
about **66 pages** against the British edition's 120. Its letters come from
Adelaide, and its news is local: one reply explains that Australia will not be
getting *Tuff E Nuff* at all, because **Mattel**, handling Nintendo in Australia,
would wait for *Street Fighter II Turbo* before launching another high-profile
beat 'em up.

⚠ **What connects the two is not stated anywhere found here.** The Australian
issues name no licence, no relationship to EMAP, and no relationship to Nintendo
beyond the title. A shared name, a local publisher, local staff and diverging
content is exactly the profile of the licensed editions this Vault documents in
Italy — but there the colophon said *"Su licenza di NEWSFIELD PUBLICATION"*, and
here nothing says anything.

Recorded as two magazines of the same name with an unestablished relationship.

## The shapes are only visible when the colophon says so

That limit is worth stating as a general one, because this is now the second time
it has come up. *[Australian Personal Computer](/vault/magazines/australian-personal-computer)*
carries British by-lines and British column furniture and cannot be connected to
its apparent parent either.

The [three border-crossing arrangements](/vault/culture/magazines-across-borders)
this Vault distinguishes were identifiable because the magazines said which they
were. Where a title crosses a border and prints nothing about why, the same
shapes may well be present and simply cannot be told apart — which means the
taxonomy describes what the record documents, not what happened.
