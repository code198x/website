**Computer & Video Games** — universally *C&VG* — arrived in **November 1981**,
which is the fact that matters most about it. The Spectrum did not exist. The
Commodore 64 did not exist. C&VG was founded on the proposition that games
across all machines were a subject worth a monthly magazine, and it turned out
to be right for twenty-three years.

Its own launch copy is characteristically pleased with itself: the magazine is
"packed with new levels of stimulation for people who get fun out of computers",
and readers were promised "some free brain torture" on the cover.

## Fast facts
- **First issue:** November 1981.
- **Publisher:** EMAP.
- **Scope:** multi-format from the start — arcade, console and home computer.
- **Ran until:** 2004 in print.
- **In this library:** **226 issues, 21,959 pages** — the largest magazine holding here.
- **Spectrum reviews:** 347 scored reviews recorded in ZXDB.

## Why it matters to this Vault
Two reasons, and the second is the more useful one.

It is a **cross-check**. Most of the Spectrum press reviewed the Spectrum, so
when [*CRASH*](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine),
[*Sinclair User*](/vault/magazines/sinclair-user) and
[*Your Sinclair*](/vault/magazines/your-sinclair) disagree, they are three
specialists arguing inside one world. C&VG reviewed the Spectrum version
alongside everything else, which makes its score the one that reflects how a
game looked against the whole market rather than against its own platform.

That shows up repeatedly in this pass. C&VG gave *Chase H.Q.* **97%** and
*R-Type* **93%** — higher than the Spectrum press did — and gave *Out Run* 87%
while *CRASH* landed on 72%.

It is also the **competitive backdrop**. Newsfield launched
[*The Games Machine*](/vault/magazines/the-games-machine) in 1987 specifically
in response to a revamped C&VG, so the multi-format magazine C&VG had invented
in 1981 was still setting the agenda six years later.
