**Bajtek** was a Polish computer monthly, first published in **September 1985**
at 60 złotych. This library holds 115 issues to December 1995, fifty of them
from before 1990.

Its masthead slogan is *Z mikrokomputerem na ty* — roughly, on first-name terms
with the microcomputer. Its title is explained on the first page: *BAJT — to 8
bitów, podstawowych jednostek informacji.*

## Published by the state, to a purpose

The editorial states what the magazine is, and it is not a commercial venture:

> "**BAJTEK jest wspólnym, miesięcznym dodatkiem do «Sztandaru Młodych» i
> «Odrodzenia».**"

A joint monthly supplement to *Sztandar Młodych* — the newspaper of the official
socialist youth organisation — and to the weekly *Odrodzenie*. The same page
places it beside a national campaign, the PRON *Narodowy Czyn Pomocy Szkole*, a
drive to equip schools.

And it states an aim:

> "Ambicją zespołu redakcyjnego jest… **zwalczanie analfabetyzmu
> mikrokomputerowego w Polsce.**"

The editorial team's ambition is to combat **microcomputer illiteracy in
Poland**.

Nothing else in this Vault reads like that. British magazines of 1985 sold
themselves on reviews, cover tapes and being first with the news;
*[Pixel](/vault/magazines/pixel)* in Greece was created because a reader survey
asked for listings; *[MC-microcomputer](/vault/magazines/mc-microcomputer)* was a
commercial monthly from a Rome publisher. *Bajtek* is a literacy programme
attached to a youth newspaper.

## What a state literacy programme printed

Issue one is not propaganda. Its cover promises Professor **Władysław M.
Turski**, the **ZX Spectrum**, **LOGO**, software protection, a piece on the
joystick, a survey of microcomputers around the world — and
*[Atic Atac](/vault/games/atic-atac)*, a British game from
[Ultimate](/vault/companies/ultimate).

A magazine published under the auspices of the official youth press, in 1985,
reviewing a Spectrum game from Ashby-de-la-Zouch. The [survey of what
Polish readers were reading about](/vault/culture/the-c64-across-borders) bears
it out: across *Bajtek*'s pages the Spectrum is the most-named machine by a wide
margin, ahead of the Amstrad, the PC and the C64.

## Three prices for the same machine

The most useful thing *Bajtek* printed was a price column, and its column
headings are the whole argument of this Vault's
[geographic comparison](/vault/culture/the-c64-across-borders) in one line. The
February 1989 table is headed **CENY NA DZIEŃ 15.02.1989** — prices as at 15
February 1989 — over three columns:

| GIEŁDA „BAJTKA" | PEWEX / BALTONA | RFN |
|---|---|---|
| tys. zł | USD | DM |

The magazine's own survey of the **giełda**, the open market, in thousands of
złoty. The **Pewex** and **Baltona** hard-currency state shops, in dollars. And
**RFN** — the Federal Republic of Germany — in Deutschmarks.

A British magazine printed one price because there was one way to buy a
computer. *Bajtek* printed three because there were three, in three currencies,
and which one applied to you depended on what money you had access to.

The figures for that week run roughly: a ZX Spectrum 48K at **390** on the
market against **90–190 DM** in West Germany; a Spectrum +3 at **850** against
280–400 DM; a Timex 2048 at 370; a **C-64C at 650**.

⚠ The page is column-interleaved in this scan — the price table sits beside a
readers' contact column — so the alignment of figures to headings is legible but
not certain, and the ranges are recorded here as read rather than as verified.

⚠ Readers' names, ages and postal addresses appear in the adjacent column and
are not reproduced. One correspondent writes from Prague, which is worth noting
only because it shows the readership crossed borders the machines did.

## Where machines actually came from

The vocabulary bears the column headings out across the run. *Giełda* appears in
100 of the 115 issues held, *dolar* in 105, *komis* — the commission shop — in
60, *Pewex* in 56 and *Baltona* in 34. *Przemyt*, smuggling, appears in four.

An October 1985 piece asks how long the **Jarmark** will remain "Mekką amatorów
małej informatyki", the Mecca of small-computing enthusiasts, and whether it can
withstand competition from Pewex. A 1986 interviewee describes buying
English-language books "na giełdach, od różnych osób" — at the markets, from
various people — and paying five thousand złoty for a photocopied 250-page
manual.

That is the acquisition machinery of a market with no retail channel, described
by the magazine that served it.

⚠ This entry is built from issue 1, from the price column, and from measurements across the run. This
library holds *Bajtek* in quantity and no secondary account of it, so nothing
here describes how it changed across eleven years, who wrote it, or how it fared
after 1989 — the run held continues to 1995, well past the state that launched
it.
