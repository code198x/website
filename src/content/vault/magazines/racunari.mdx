**Računari** was published in **Belgrade** from **January 1984**. This library
holds 140 issues to December 1998.

It opens with a special edition, *Računari u vašoj kući* — Computers in Your
House — priced at 200 dinars, written by **Dejan Ristanović** and issued under
the imprint of *Galaksija*. Its cover offers a survey of how to choose and use a
computer, a 32-page beginners' programming insert, and one thing no other launch
issue in this Vault offers:

> "**kompletno uputstvo za samogradnju kućnog kompjutera**"

Complete instructions for building a home computer yourself.

## The magazine printed the machine

The computer was the **Galaksija**, and the issue says who made it:

> "Računar «Galaksija» je projektovao **jedan jedini čovek, Voja Antonić**, za
> nepunih mesec dana."

The Galaksija was designed by a single man, Voja Antonić, in under a month —
and, the same passage says, barely a few months passed from the first idea to
the first examples appearing.

The stated purpose is that enthusiasts should "finally come to their first
machine", and that the project should break the myth surrounding computers.

This is the sharpest illustration in this library of what
[geography did to computing](/vault/culture/the-c64-across-borders). Elsewhere a
magazine told you which computer to buy.
[In Brazil](/vault/culture/brazilian-market-reserve), where imports were
restricted, a domestic industry built licensed and cloned machines instead. In
Yugoslavia a magazine printed the circuit and the listing and let readers build
one.

## Afterwards

The run held continues monthly to **December 1998** — through the end of the
state it began in. Across its pages the most-named machine is the PC, at roughly
40% of machine mentions in 1984–87, ahead of the Amstrad; the C64 is named in
about one mention in twenty.

⚠ This entry is built from the January 1984 issue and from measurements across
the run, and the Serbo-Croatian OCR is rough. This library holds no secondary
account of *Računari*, so nothing here describes its later editorial history,
its circulation, or how it handled the wars that ended Yugoslavia.
