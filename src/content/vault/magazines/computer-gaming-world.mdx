**Computer Gaming World** began as a magazine for people who played wargames on their computers. Its first issue, dated November-December 1981 and priced $2.75, led with "The Future of Computer Wargaming", reviewed SSI's submarine game *Torpedo Fire* and ran a tournament for *Robotwar*, in which players programmed battling robots. It was put together in Anaheim, California, by Russell Sipe, who wrote in his first editorial that people had told him a magazine on computer gaming was "OVERDUE". Twenty-five years later it was the leading American magazine about PC games, owned by the publisher of *PC Magazine*, and it ended by becoming Microsoft's official Windows games magazine.

## Fast facts

- **Founder:** Russell Sipe, editor from 1981, then editor-in-chief and publisher.
- **Publisher:** Golden Empire Publications, Anaheim, 1981 to 1993; Ziff-Davis from 1993.
- **First issue:** Volume 1, Number 1, November-December 1981, $2.75, bi-monthly.
- **Frequency:** six issues a year at first, nine a year by 1986, monthly from 1988.
- **Editors-in-chief:** Russell Sipe; Johnny Wilson by 1994; George Jones by 1999; Jeff Green by late 2001.
- **Circulation:** "25,000 readers", by the magazine's own count, in January 1984.
- **Renamed:** *Games for Windows: The Official Magazine*, announced in August 2006; the last issues under the old name appeared in late 2006.

## A wargamer's magazine

Sipe wanted CGW to be "a forum for an intelligent dialog between gamers, designers, manufacturers and retailers". The first issue promised that each issue would "evaluate computer games, give advice on strategy and tactics, announce new products", and it paid contributors 2 cents a word. Its guidance for reviewers set the tone for what followed: "Try not to spend too much time in describing the fiction or setting of the game (readers can read ad descriptions as well as you)", and spend "the bulk of your time evaluating the strong and weak points of the game". The issue opened with an advertisement from SSI for two new games "for the Apple and the TRS-80".

It was a small operation. Suzanne Sipe ran operations, Kirk Robinson was assistant editor, and a subscription cost $11 for six issues. In September 1982 an editor's note compared CGW's economics with those of the big computer magazines: "Our circulation is about 100 times smaller", so its "cost per 'book' at 40+ pages is about the same as their cost per 'book' at 200+ pages." By January 1984 it counted 25,000 readers. The magazine was published nine times a year by 1986 and monthly from 1988.

## Scorpia and the Hall of Fame

In July 1983 CGW introduced "A New Column...": a walkthrough of the adventure *The Serpent's Star*, "as told by Scorpia". Scorpia was already known on CompuServe's games forum, where an article two months earlier had printed her online replies to stuck players. She wrote about adventures and role-playing games for CGW for the next sixteen years, into 1999, in walkthroughs and in reviews under the heading "Scorpia's View"; in January 1994, for example, she was not persuaded by [Activision](/vault/companies/activision)'s *Return to Zork*.

In March 1988 the magazine opened the CGW Hall of Fame, "formed to honor those games that over the course of time have proven themselves to be long term top rated games by the readers of CGW". Readers' ratings put a game on the list; the staff chose which to induct, and an inducted game was "retired from the current Game Ratings List". The charter inductees included [*Ultima*](/vault/games/ultima) III and IV, *[Wizardry](/vault/games/wizardry)*, *The Bard's Tale*, *Kampfgruppe*, *M.U.L.E.* and *Chessmaster 2000*. The magazine looked beyond computers too: in November 1988 its video game awards named Nintendo's *[The Legend of Zelda](/vault/games/legend-of-zelda)* the best adventure.

## Johnny Wilson and Ziff-Davis

Johnny Wilson joined as assistant editor in 1987, was associate editor by January 1989 and editor from 1990, with Sipe as editor-in-chief. In October 1993 the magazine announced that "Ziff-Davis Publishing Company has acquired Computer Gaming World, the number one computer game magazine". Ziff-Davis already published *PC Magazine*, *PC Week*, *MacUser* and *Computer Shopper*. The announcement promised that CGW would "maintain its editorial direction, location and staff", and quoted Ziff-Davis's president, J. Scott Briggs, calling "publisher Russell Sipe and editor Johnny Wilson" "among the most respected voices within the computer game industry". By mid-1994 Wilson was editor-in-chief and Sipe publisher. Sipe handed the publisher's job to Jonathan Lane in 1995, and by 1996 the masthead listed him as the magazine's founder.

## The Ziff Davis years

George Jones was editor-in-chief by May 1999, and Jeff Green by November 2001. Green had come to CGW about 1996 from a job editing modem reviews for a Macintosh magazine. Writing in the 200th issue, in March 2001, he remembered reading "Johnny Wilson. Charles Ardai. Martin Cirulis. Scorpia. Month after month, I read their articles in Computer Gaming World and marveled at their writing", and thanked the writers and editors "who, over the years, helped give CGW its heart and soul", beginning with Russell Sipe.

On 2 August 2006 Ziff Davis and Microsoft announced that CGW would be replaced by *Games for Windows: The Official Magazine*, which would "carry on the editorial, production and art staff of Computer Gaming World". Wikipedia, a secondary source, counts 268 issues of CGW, the last dated November 2006. The new magazine lasted until April 2008, when Ziff Davis ended the print edition with its April/May issue and moved Jeff Green and three other editors to its website, 1UP.com. The company's chief executive, Jason Young, said that "PC gamers and marketers have made the online space their home".
