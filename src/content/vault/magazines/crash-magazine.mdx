*CRASH* was a British monthly magazine about [ZX Spectrum](/vault/systems/zx-spectrum) games. Its first issue, dated February 1984, cost 75p and came from a small company in Ludlow, Shropshire: "Crash Micro is published monthly by Newsfield Ltd, PO Box 10, Ludlow". The editor was [Roger Kean](/vault/people/roger-kean); [Oliver Frey](/vault/people/oliver-frey) was credited as designer and painted the cover.

The first editorial set out what would make the magazine different. Every major review would be "undertaken by at least three reviewers" so that personal taste would even out, and the reviewers would be "games players, not professional reviewers in the usual sense". *CRASH* ran for 98 issues, until April 1992, and its reviews, maps, tips and letters became a large part of how Spectrum owners talked about their games.

## Fast facts

- **Publisher:** [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield), Ludlow, Shropshire, from issue 1 to issue 93; then [Europress](/vault/companies/europress) from issue 94.
- **First issue:** February 1984. **Final issue:** April 1992 (issue 98).
- **Founding editor:** [Roger Kean](/vault/people/roger-kean), with [Oliver Frey](/vault/people/oliver-frey) as designer and cover artist.
- **Cover price:** 75p at launch; £1.25 in May 1989; £1.50 with the first monthly cover tape in June 1989; £1.95 by December 1989; back to £1.70 in April 1990; £2.20 by the last issue.
- **Cover tape:** the Mega Power Tape, later the Powertape, a monthly cassette of games, demos and POKEs from issue 65 (June 1989).

## The CRASH Smash and the score

A *CRASH* review was a serious thing, and the shape of it changed as the magazine aged. Issue 1 in 1984 scored seven axes:

| 1984 | by 1989 |
|---|---|
| Use of computer | Presentation |
| Graphics | Graphics |
| Playability | Sound |
| Getting started | Playability |
| Addictive qualities | **Overall** |
| Value for money | |
| **Overall** | |

"Use of computer" and "Getting started" date the magazine to 1984, when whether a program used the machine well, and whether you could work out how to start it, were open questions. Both were gone by the end of the decade, replaced by Presentation and Sound. By August 1985 the magazine explained that the Overall percentage was "a separate and distinct rating", and that "ALL ratings are an average of three reviewers' opinions".

**Lasting Interest** was never one of the axes, though it is often remembered as one. The words turn up in *CRASH* reviews as ordinary prose, "little lasting interest", but never as a line in a score box. As a scoring category it belongs to magazines such as *Amiga Format*. **Lastability**, likewise, was a score line in *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)*; in *CRASH* it was only ever a word in a review. The difference matters because this Vault quotes *CRASH* percentages throughout, and a score attributed to an axis the magazine never had is a score nobody can check.

A score above 90% earned the **CRASH Smash**, the magazine's top award; a game rated at exactly 90% in 1984 was also called a Smash. Publishers quoted Smashes in their advertising: "Crash Smash 91%" was still appearing in mail-order advertising in 1990.

The writers who filled those pages over the years included Robin Candy, Ben Stone, Mark Caswell, Nick Roberts and Richard Eddy.

## Oliver Frey covers

Much of *CRASH*'s look came from Oliver Frey's painted covers, which often illustrated one of the month's games. The covers could be controversial. In October 1987 the letters page was still sorting through the "Barbarian backlash" over a recent cover, while a reader complained that *CRASH* had started putting games that were not Smashes, such as *Renegade* and *Barbarian*, on the front because their images "were better for selling magazines". The same page answered a rumour about the *Renegade* cover's model: it was Roger Kean, "well his arms anyway", with Robin Candy supplying the stomach.

## Multi-page maps

A big game in *CRASH* often came with a map of every screen. The back-issue lists advertise a *Jet Set Willy* map in issue 5 (June 1984), a *Lords of Midnight* map in issue 11 (December 1984) and a *Knight Lore* map in issue 17 (June 1985). Readers drew them too: in 1984 the magazine ran a competition with Ultimate for the best-looking *Sabre Wulf* map and printed the honourable mentions in December.

## Lloyd Mangram and the Forum

The letters page, "Lloyd Mangram's Forum", mixed genuine reader questions and complaints with running jokes that recurred for years. Lloyd's byline appears from the first issue. He did not exist. In 2004 former Newsfield employee Matthew Uffindell wrote in *Retro Gamer* that "Lloyd's input was the joint combination of text and opinion from both Roger and myself", meaning Roger Kean. The name also signed letters pages in Newsfield's Commodore magazine *ZZAP!64*.

## The end

*CRASH* ended in April 1992 with issue 98, but it changed hands first, and the join is visible in the run itself. Issue 93 is dated **October 1991**; issue 94 is dated **December 1991**. There is no November. [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield) had gone under, and **Europress** appears in the magazine for the first time in issue 94. That issue told the story in its own style: the staff arrived at "CRASH Towers" on 17 September 1991 to find "a spaceship that looked an awful lot like a redundancy cheque", and noted that [Future Publishing](/vault/companies/future-publishing), publisher of rival *Your Sinclair*, had tried to buy *CRASH* and *ZZAP!64* when Newsfield went bust.

So the last five issues are not Newsfield's, and issue 98 announced that "From next month CRASH is joining forces with SINCLAIR USER".

The name came back in 2017, when Fusion Retro Books crowdfunded a hardback *CRASH Annual 2018*, edited by Roger Kean with a cover by Oliver Frey.
