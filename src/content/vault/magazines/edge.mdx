**Edge** told its first readers what they were getting before they reached the contents page. "Yes, Edge is expensive," the opening page of issue 1 admitted in October 1993, but it promised the whole story, "not just a handful of intro screenshots and a mouthful of garbled rumour", and added: "above all, we take videogames seriously." [Future Publishing](/vault/companies/future-publishing) had built its business on magazines for one machine each; *Edge* covered every console, computer and arcade board, and cared as much about how games were made and sold as about how they played. It has been published every month since.

## Fast facts

- **Publisher:** Future Publishing, Bath (now Future plc).
- **First issue:** October 1993, "Issue one", with the cover line "The future of videogaming" and the price printed as "£three".
- **Founding editor:** Steve Jarratt, issues 1 to 9 (October 1993 to June 1994).
- **Later editors:** Jason Brookes (from July 1994), Tony Mott (from 1998, and for most of the next two decades), João Diniz Sanches (to late 2003), Margaret Robertson (2006 to 2007), Alex Wiltshire (2012 to 2013), Nathan Brown (about 2015 to 2020), Jen Simpkins (2020).
- **Reviews:** *Testscreen*, marked out of ten, with five as the average.
- **First ten out of ten:** *Super Mario 64*, in issue 35 (August 1996).
- **Issue 400:** 2024.

## Issue one

The first cover listed the formats *Edge* would follow: Mega Drive, Super Nintendo, PC, Amiga, PC Engine, Neo Geo and 3DO. Its big story was the 3DO Interactive Multiplayer: "Only one magazine in the UK has been permitted inside the 3DO company", the editorial boasted, and the report began on page 48. Another feature explained how [Psygnosis](/vault/companies/psygnosis) had made its CD-ROM shoot 'em up *Microcosm*, and *Testscreen* reviewed games including a Japanese import of *Street Fighter II Turbo* at £90. The editorial page gave the magazine's reason for existing in money: "The UK market for games is now worth over £600 million".

The staff list, headed "People on Edge", named Steve Jarratt as editor, Jason Brookes as production editor and [Chris Anderson](/vault/people/chris-anderson), Future's founder, as managing director. Jarratt had joined *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* as a reviewer in April 1987, introduced as a 24-year-old who "used to be an industrial chemist", had edited *[Commodore Format](/vault/magazines/commodore-format)* in 1990 and 1991, and had started the Nintendo magazine *Total!* with Andy Dyer in 1992.

## One voice

*Edge* spoke as *Edge*. Its first contents pages said "Edge rates the current batch of new releases" and "Edge spoke to the visionaries": the magazine, not a named writer, was the one with the opinion. It got close to the people making games. In June 1994 "Edge was allowed inside Sega's headquarters" to interview [Yu Suzuki](/vault/people/yu-suzuki), head of [AM2](/vault/companies/sega-am2), about his arcade work. Over the following decade its interviews and letters pages became a record of the industry thinking aloud: in October 1995 a reader wrote that "the days of the bedroom coder are as good as over", and in April 1997 Infogrames' president Bruno Bonnell told *Edge* that [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software), which Infogrames had bought, would become its "action-based label, directed primarily at teenagers and arcade fans". It reported on its own owners too: in January 1995 it told readers that Anderson had sold Future to Pearson.

Jarratt left the editor's chair after nine issues. From July 1994 Jason Brookes was editor, with Jarratt listed as consultant editor; by then he had become editor of *[Amiga Format](/vault/magazines/amiga-format)*, which announced him in June 1994. The masthead's tagline had changed too, from "The future of videogaming" to "The future of interactive entertainment".

## Five is average

*Edge*'s marks became the thing it was best known for. Jarratt later explained that "after several years struggling with review scores, I knew that I really wanted to try and have 'five' as a proper benchmark average, with higher scores reflecting the quality titles." He did not set out to make ten "some sort of Holy Grail"; he wanted *Edge* to be "fair", and "was sick of seeing the 'seven as average' thing". A chart on the office wall reminded reviewers what the numbers meant: six equalled competent, eight excellent. Ten, the magazine admitted in 2003, originally "did not equal revolutionary — it simply said 'Miyamoto'."

It was a long wait. "It took 35 issues and three full years before *Edge* awarded its first ten", to [Shigeru Miyamoto](/vault/people/shigeru-miyamoto)'s *[Super Mario 64](/vault/games/super-mario-64)* in August 1996. The second, *[Gran Turismo](/vault/games/gran-turismo)*, "caused the most outrage" and made the magazine "question its own criteria". By its tenth birthday, in October 2003, only two more games had joined them: *[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time](/vault/games/legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time)* and *[Halo](/vault/games/halo)*. "One racing game, one platformer, one adventure game and one shooter." The same article described how a ten was reached, after "days of playtesting, more debate and then a heated argument", with the reviewer holding the casting vote.

## Editors and editions

Brookes edited *Edge* until 1998, and by the August 1998 issue Tony Mott was editor. Mott would run the magazine, as editor or editor-in-chief, for most of the next two decades. João Diniz Sanches was editor until the end of 2003; Margaret Robertson, games editor by 2005, became editor in 2006; Alex Wiltshire was editor by early 2013, and Nathan Brown was games editor by the end of that year. Brown edited the magazine for five years until April 2020, when his deputy Jen Simpkins took over; she left at the end of that year, and public sources since name Mott as editor again.

*Edge* also travelled. From 1995 to 2002 some of its material appeared in the United States in *Next Generation*, published in San Francisco by Imagine Publishing, a company owned by Chris Anderson. An Australian edition began in October 2004 with the cover line "The future of electronic entertainment".

## Still published

*Edge* is still a monthly print magazine from Future plc in Bath, publishing thirteen issues a year. In January 2015 Future announced that the *Edge* website would be folded into GamesRadar. It reached issue 400 in September 2024, thirty-one years after the first.
