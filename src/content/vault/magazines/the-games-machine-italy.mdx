**The Games Machine** was published in Italy under licence from
[Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield) from **September 1988**. This library
holds **112 Italian issues** against **34 British ones**. The magazine Britain
remembers as a short-lived three-year experiment ran in Italy for close to
eleven years.

## The licence

Issue 1's colophon states it plainly: **"Su licenza di NEWSFIELD PUBLICATION"** —
under licence from Newsfield Publication. The publisher was
[Edizioni Hobby](/vault/companies/edizioni-hobby) of Milan, and the arrangement
was not new. The editorial on the same page, signed **Roberto Ferri**, credits
"gli amici della Newsfield che **ancora una volta** hanno creduto nelle nostre
capacità" — the friends at Newsfield who *once again* believed in our abilities.
Once again, because Edizioni Hobby had been publishing
[*Zzap!*](/vault/magazines/zzap-64) under the same licence since May 1986.

The Italian edition was not a translation of the British one in the way the
phrase usually implies. It took Newsfield material — Andrew Braybrook's
development diaries, reportage by Julian Rignall, the Gary Penn and Gary Liddon
double act — and set it alongside Italian editorial with its own staff and
letters page.

It also had a different pitch. The British TGM was multi-format across 8-bit and
16-bit. Its Italian counterpart opened by claiming to be "**prima in Italia**,
una rivista interamente dedicata al divertimento elettronico ed al sedici bit" —
the first magazine in Italy given over entirely to electronic entertainment and
to 16-bit. Where the British title had been Newsfield's answer to a rival, the
Italian one was staking a claim on a machine generation.

## What happened when the licensor died

Newsfield went into liquidation in 1991. The Italian colophons record what that
looked like from Milan, and the sequence is unusually legible because the
magazine printed the same block every month:

| Issues to July 1991 | September 1991 | From October 1991 |
|---|---|---|
| Edizioni Hobby | **Xenia Edizioni** | Xenia Edizioni |
| "Su licenza di NEWSFIELD PUBLICATION" | licence line still present | **licence line gone** |

The publisher changed first; the Newsfield credit was dropped the month after.
Everything else in the colophon carried on unaltered — the same tribunal
registration (Milan no. 587 of 19 September 1988), the same printer at Cernusco
sul Naviglio, the same distributor on Via Famagosta, the same advertising agency
at Piazzale Archinto. Roberto Ferri, who signed the first editorial in 1988, is
*direttore responsabile* under [Xenia](/vault/companies/xenia-edizioni) in 1992.

*Zzap!* moved on exactly the same two months, which is why this reads as one
transfer of a Milanese operation rather than two separate deals.

The licence ended with the licensor. The magazine did not. It ran another seven
and a half years and closed in **May 1999**, by which point nothing about it was
British except the name on the cover.

## Why this entry exists

A Vault written only from British holdings gets *The Games Machine* materially
wrong. From Ludlow it is a three-year magazine that closed in 1990. Counted
across everything this library holds, it is an eleven-year Italian institution
with three times the page count, and its longest and least British phase is the
part no British source covers.

⚠ This entry is built from colophons and from two editorials in issue 1. The
library holds the Italian run in quantity and holds no secondary account of it,
so nothing here describes how the magazine changed across eleven years, who read
it, or what it meant to Italian gaming. All three would be worth knowing and
none is claimed.
