**The Micro User** was a [BBC Micro](/vault/systems/bbc-micro) monthly launched in **March 1983** by **Database Publications Ltd** of Europa House, Macclesfield, under managing editor **Derek Meakin**.

## Why it matters beyond the BBC

Database Publications is the company that became part of **[Europress](/vault/companies/europress)** — the publisher of *[Amiga Computing](/vault/magazines/amiga-computing)* from 1988, and the buyer of *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* and *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* when [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield) collapsed in 1991.

That matters because it dates the group far earlier than its games titles suggest. Reading only *Amiga Computing*, Europress looks like a 1988 arrival. *The Micro User* shows Database publishing computer magazines from **March 1983** — before *CRASH* existed — and naming Europress in its pages from 1985.

So when the Ludlow magazines changed hands in 1991, they went to a house that had been in British computer publishing for eight years, and had started on Acorn machines.

⚠ This library holds *The Micro User* thinly — fifteen issues across a run that reached at least 1991 — so the dates here bracket the run rather than define it.

## Against Acorn User

*[Acorn User](/vault/magazines/acorn-user)* was Acorn's **official** magazine, published for the manufacturer. *The Micro User* was not: it was an independent title covering the same machine from a rival house, launched seven months later. Readers of the BBC Micro had both, and only one of them could afford to be rude about Acorn.
