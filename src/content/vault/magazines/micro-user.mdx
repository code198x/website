**The Micro User** was the BBC Micro magazine that took a Stockport publisher into home computing. In March 1983 Database Publications of Hazel Grove, Stockport, until then the publisher of *Windfall*, a magazine for Apple computers, and the trade paper *Computer Dealer*, put out the first issue of *BBC Micro User*, priced £1, for owners of the [BBC Micro](/vault/systems/bbc-micro). Within a year the same house was running a second magazine for Acorn's Electron and a trade show that drew thousands of visitors to Manchester. By the end of the decade it was publishing magazines for the Atari ST and the Amiga as part of the [Europress](/vault/companies/europress) group.

## An independent magazine for one machine

The first cover called it "The independent magazine that helps you to make the most of your micro", and the small print spelled out what that meant: "neither the BBC nor Acorn Computers Ltd are responsible for any of the articles in this issue or for any of the opinions expressed". The BBC Micro already had an official magazine, *[Acorn User](/vault/magazines/acorn-user)*, which Addison-Wesley had been publishing for Acorn since its first issue, dated July/August 1982. *The Micro User* was a rival house's view of the same machine.

Issue 1 offered a game to type in, *Death Watch*, a way to "iron out those cassette bugs", instructions for building a games paddle, a review of the Alphabeta word processor ("useful but over-priced") and a look at "What's good — and bad — in BBC software". Derek Meakin was managing editor, Mike Bibby features editor and Mike Cook technical editor. The welcome page promised that "one thing we are not going to do is to waste space on long editorials — we've got better uses for it!"

For its first three issues the magazine ran "BBC Micro User" across the top of every page. From June 1983 the page headers read simply "Micro User". The colophon had called it *The Micro User* from the start.

## A show and a second magazine

Database also ran events. The first BBC Micro User Show was held over three days in the summer of 1983 in UMIST's Renold building in Manchester, and the magazine reported that more than 12,500 visitors came through the doors, with over 50 companies exhibiting. Acorn had planned to use the show to launch its second processors for the BBC Micro.

That autumn the house added a second title. The October 1983 issue of *The Micro User* carried "the first edition of ELECTRON USER — the exciting new magazine for users of Acorn's latest micro", bound in as a section, and the November issue carried the second. *Electron User* soon became a magazine in its own right.

## Into the Europress group

Database went on to publish magazines for other home computers: *[Atari ST User](/vault/magazines/atari-st-user)* and, from June 1988, *[Amiga Computing](/vault/magazines/amiga-computing)*. By June 1988 it had moved from Hazel Grove to a new Europa House at Adlington Park, near Macclesfield, and by November 1988 its magazines described Database Publications as "a division of Europress Ltd".

The name Europress appears in *The Micro User* much earlier, but in a different role. In 1985 the magazine's news-trade distributor was "Europress Sales and Distribution Limited" of Crawley, West Sussex, while the colophon still gave the publisher as Database Publications at Hazel Grove. That credit shows who got the magazine into the shops, not who owned it.

In June 1991 Meakin brought all of the group's companies under the Europress name, and *The Micro User* was listed among the titles of Europress Publications. By then it covered Acorn's newer machines too: the September 1991 issue, published from Europa House, Adlington Park, was full of software and hardware for the Archimedes and A3000 alongside the BBC Micro and Master.

In October 1992 the magazine was renamed *Acorn Computing*, and according to a later secondary summary it ran until 1995.
