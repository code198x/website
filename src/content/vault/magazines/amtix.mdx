**Amtix!** was [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield)'s magazine for the
[Amstrad CPC](/vault/systems/amstrad-cpc), and the shortest-lived of its titles. It ran from
an **issue 0** in **October 1985** to **issue 18** in **April 1987** — nineteen issues in
eighteen months, against the 117 that [Amstrad Action](/vault/magazines/amstrad-action)
managed on the same machine over the same decade.

Issue 0 announces itself plainly: "Newsfield Publications have launched a new monthly
magazine for Amstrad owners." It was published from **1-2 King Street, Ludlow, Shropshire**
— the same address as [CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine) and
[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64), by the same people.

## Fast facts

- **Publisher:** Newsfield Ltd, 1-2 King Street, Ludlow, Shropshire.
- **Run:** issue 0 (October 1985) to issue 18 (April 1987).
- **Award:** the **Accolade**, Newsfield's third house award after *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)*'s Smash and *ZZAP!*'s Sizzler.
- **Named on the masthead:** [Roger Kean](/vault/people/roger-kean), **Franco Frey** as technical editor, **Robin Candy**.
- **Machine:** the [Amstrad CPC](/vault/systems/amstrad-cpc).

## Launched into a fight it lost

*Amtix!* and *Amstrad Action* both carry **October 1985** on their first issues. Two
magazines for one machine, launched the same month, by the two publishers who between them
defined the British 8-bit press — Newsfield from Ludlow, Future from Somerton.

Future's title ran for ten years. Newsfield's stopped after eighteen months, and its final
issue carries farewell features.

⚠ Why it closed is not established from the sources held here. The CPC had a smaller
installed base than the Spectrum or C64, and Newsfield was already publishing two
magazines; neither observation is the same as evidence.

## Why it matters

The Accolade appears throughout this Vault as the third of the three Newsfield awards. When
an entry says a game "took a *CRASH* Smash, a *ZZAP!* Sizzler and an *Amtix!* Accolade", it
is describing a clean sweep of one publisher's judgement across three machines — and the
shortest-lived of the three magazines is the one that makes the sweep possible.
