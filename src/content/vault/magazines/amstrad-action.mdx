**Amstrad Action** was the magazine that started Future Publishing. Chris Anderson, who had edited *Personal Computer Games* and launched *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* for [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield) earlier in 1985, published its first issue in October 1985 from an office in Somerton, Somerset. At the time it was his company's only title. Three years later Future's order forms listed *ACE*, *PC Plus*, *8000 Plus*, *ST Amiga Format* and *New Computer Express* beside it. *Amstrad Action* itself ran for nearly ten years and 117 issues, covering the [Amstrad CPC](/vault/systems/amstrad-cpc) long after the big software houses had left it.

## Somerset, 1985

Issue 1 came from Future Publishing's address at Somerton, with Peter Connor, "another veteran of PCG", as editor and Bob Wade among the staff. From issue 2 Chris Anderson appeared on the masthead, and by the spring of 1986 he was listed as Publisher. He also wrote for the early issues: in December 1985 he drove to the Midlands to find out how the three men of Gargoyle Games produced *Marsport*, and he reviewed business software as "small businessman Chris Anderson".

Games were only part of it. The second issue reviewed book-keeping software, and the third compared word processors.

## The Mastergame

The first issue's review of Melbourne House's *Way of the Exploding Fist* made it the magazine's first "Mastergame", the award *Amstrad Action* gave to the best games it reviewed. By the December 1985 issue, *Marsport* had "arrived and become the AA Mastergame".

## Editors

The mastheads show how often the chair changed:

- **Peter Connor**, from the first issue.
- **Matt Nicholson**, announced as "the new Editor" in July 1986.
- **Bob Wade**, by August 1987 into 1988.
- **Steve Carey**, in 1988–89.
- **Rod Lawton**, by January 1990 to February 1993.
- **Linda Barker**, in March 1993.
- **Tim Norris**, as acting editor from April 1993, then editor to August 1993.
- **Dave Golder**, from September 1993 to October 1994.
- **Karen Levell**, from January 1995.

By 1991 the magazine had moved with Future to 30 Monmouth Street, Bath.

## Covertapes

*Amstrad Action* had given away tapes before, but from April 1991 (issue 67) it put a covertape on every issue. That issue announced "new, regular covertapes" and started with a playable level of Ocean's *Total Recall* and a new "AA Toolbox" of routines and utilities. The tape was still there in 1994, when readers argued on the letters page about whether it should go.

## The last issue

In its last year *Amstrad Action*'s mastheads printed an audited circulation of 15,168. The June 1995 issue, number 117, reminded readers that they were holding "a magazine of over ten years' standing", with writers who had been with the machine from the start, and said that the big companies had pulled out and left the market to small, independent software houses run by CPC fans. Its lead feature was on desktop publishing with a CPC.

Nothing in that issue says it is the last. It trails the next month's columns, and its contents page carries the on-sale date for the next issue, Thursday 22 June 1995, in the same place as issue 116 had announced issue 117. No issue 118 appeared. Neither the magazine nor the public accounts of it explain the decision to stop.

*Amstrad Action* outlived Newsfield's *[Amtix!](/vault/magazines/amtix)*, launched a month after it, by eight years. *Amtix!* ran for eighteen numbered issues and ended in April 1987, when Newsfield passed the title to Database Publications, part of [Europress](/vault/companies/europress), to fold into *Computing with the Amstrad*.
