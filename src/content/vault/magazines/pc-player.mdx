**PC Player** was a German PC games monthly, published from **January 1993**.
At 112 issues and **16,983 pages** it is the largest German title in this
library, and the fourth largest from any territory — behind only Britain, Italy
and the United States.

Its *Chefredakteur* was **Heinrich Lenhardt**, who appears in its own pages
arguing that CD-ROM games should be cheaper, not dearer, than floppy ones: *"CDs
sind billig in der Herstellung und nahezu unkopierbar — also runter mit den
Preisen!"* — CDs are cheap to make and near-impossible to copy, so bring the
prices down.

## It reported its own sale, at the last possible moment

The September 1999 issue carries a note that had plainly gone in late:

> "**Praktisch direkt vor dem Belichten unserer Seitenfilme** kam die Nachricht:
> Ab dieser Ausgabe gehört die *PC Player* nicht mehr zur WEKA-Verlagsgruppe,
> sondern zum **Future Verlag GmbH**."

Practically right before the page films went to exposure, the news came: from
this issue *PC Player* no longer belongs to the WEKA publishing group but to
Future Verlag GmbH. The editorial then reassures readers that *alles wird wie
gewohnt weiterlaufen* — everything will carry on as usual.

Two facts fall out of that sentence beyond the sale itself. It names **DMV
Verlag's parent as the WEKA group**, which the monthly colophon does not. And it
dates the change precisely: DMV is named in every issue held from January 1993
to June 1999, Future Verlag from September 1999 on.

## What Future looked like from Munich

The note goes on to explain the buyer, on the assumption German readers had not
heard of it — *"Wem dieser Name nichts sagen sollte"*, for anyone to whom the
name means nothing:

> "Future ist eines der führenden Unternehmen in der Spielezeitschriftenbranche
> und veröffentlicht alles in allem **108 Magazine**, unter anderem die
> Marktführer in **Großbritannien, Italien, Frankreich und USA** wie
> beispielsweise die amerikanische *PC Gamer*."

This is a useful thing to have. [Future Publishing](/vault/companies/future-publishing)
appears elsewhere in this Vault as the Somerton startup that launched *Amstrad
Action* in 1985 and grew into the British Amiga press. Here it is described from
outside, by a magazine it had just bought, at the moment of buying: 108 titles
and the market leader in four countries, Italy among them.

It is also the second time this Vault has watched a national games-magazine
publisher sold to a foreign multinational and read about it in the acquired
magazine's own news pages. [IDG](/vault/companies/idg) bought eleven Europress
titles at the end of 1994 and *Amiga Computing* ran the story under "AC joins
IDG", quoting Europress's founder on Britain being the one gap in a network of
220 publications across 64 countries. Five years later the direction is
reversed — a British company buying into Germany — and the note strikes the same
two beats: here is how large we are, and nothing will change for you.

about when the magazine closed.
