**ACE**, short for *Advanced Computer Entertainment*, set out to be the games magazine for grown-ups. Its first issue, dated October 1987 and priced £1.50, promised no "kiddy comic-style reviews that treat you like a five year old", no "incomprehensible programmer-speak" and no "endless little competitions with pathetic prizes". It covered every popular machine, from the Spectrum and Commodore 64 to the Atari ST, Amiga and the new consoles, and rated each game with a graph of how long it would hold your interest. It ran for 55 issues under two publishers, [Future Publishing](/vault/companies/future-publishing) in Bath and then [EMAP](/vault/companies/emap) in London.

## Launched by Future

Future had started in 1985 with *[Amstrad Action](/vault/magazines/amstrad-action)*, a magazine for one machine. ACE was its attempt at a magazine for all of them. The welcome page in issue 1 said it came "courtesy of the team who produced the legendary *Personal Computer Games*", people who believed "that computer entertainment is at last coming of age". The office was at 4 Queen Street, Bath. Peter Connor and Steve Cooke were co-editors, Andy Wilton was reviews editor, and [Chris Anderson](/vault/people/chris-anderson), Future's founder, was named as publisher by mid-1988. The first issue's news pages announced that issue 2 would give Commodore and Spectrum owners the first five levels of Firebird's *Bubble Bobble* on a free cover cassette.

The magazine gave its readers space for more than games. Issue 1 had sections on computer graphics and on making music with MIDI, and in September 1988 two consultant editors joined, Brian Larkman for graphics and Mark Jenkins for music. The same issue welcomed Bob Wade, who had worked on *Personal Computer Games* and edited *Amstrad Action*, and said goodbye to "launch editor Pete Connor". Cooke edited alone until December 1988, when Graeme Kidd, who had first written for ACE in issue 4, joined him as editor. By January 1989 Kidd was sole editor and Cooke wrote the adventure pages.

## The interest curve

ACE's reviews were built around its **Predicted Interest Curve**. Each review drew a graph of how interested the team expected a player to be over time, from the first hour to a year later. As the magazine explained, "brilliant coin-op conversions will start high and then steadily tail off; games demanding some thought and planning will stay higher longer." The **ACE Rating** was "calculated to show the area underneath the curve" and "marked out of 1000". A game that scored over 900 was "ACE Rated".

Each version of a game also got its own box with scores for graphics and sound and two more unusual ones: an **IQ Factor** for how much thought the game demanded, and a **Fun Factor** for whether it was instantly enjoyable. A high score on one did not mean a low score on the other. *Bonecruncher*, the magazine pointed out, scored 9 for IQ and 7 for fun.

## The EMAP years

In the summer of 1989 EMAP bought the magazine. The June 1989 issue still came from Future in Bath. By July the competition entries were going to Priory Court, 30-32 Farringdon Lane, the London building where EMAP made *[Computer and Video Games](/vault/magazines/computer-and-video-games)* and *[Commodore User](/vault/magazines/cu-amiga)*. The September 1989 masthead read "EMAP B&CP" and "© EMAP B&CP 1989". Cooke and Connor were back as co-editors, Terry Pratt was publisher, and the contributors included [Julian Rignall](/vault/people/julian-rignall). That November a reader asking about missing competition results wrote that they were "aware of the recent take-over by EMAP".

Cooke was editor through 1990, with Rik Haynes as his deputy. In February 1990 ACE told its readers that it had been celebrating "the award of Magazine of the Year". The February 1991 issue announced that Jim Douglas would take over "the reins from Steve Cooke", who had been with the magazine "(with one short break) ever since it started". Douglas edited ACE for the rest of its life, with Cooke as consultant editor and Gary Whitta on the staff.

## The last issue

The April 1992 issue, number 55, still carried a coverdisk and a full set of reviews. Its games tips section apologised that the second half of a *Monkey Island 2* solution promised for the next month could not appear, because "this will be the last, if you will, 'ish' of dear old ACE Magazine", a "rather terminal fact" that had not been known when the previous issue went to press. So it printed the whole of the remaining solution instead.

Page 82 said goodbye: "you have now arrived at the last page of the last issue of ACE Magazine. After this, there will be no more." ACE had run for "just over four and a half years". The page pointed its readers to EMAP's other magazines, among them *PC Review*, a new *ST Review* and *Mean Machines*, and told Amiga owners that *[The One](/vault/magazines/the-one)* for Amiga Games was "our new home!" It was signed "Jim & Dave & Gary & Jim".
