**Australian Personal Computer** first appeared in **May 1980**, a monthly from Carlton, Victoria, priced at $2.20. Its cover called it "Australia's first micro magazine". It was not quite the first, and it was not wholly Australian either: within months its masthead said it was "produced in Australia under licence" from an English company, with a London editorial office at the publisher of the British monthly *Personal Computer World*. Under the shorter name **APC** it was still being published monthly more than forty years later.

## Issue one

The first issue was published by Australian Microcomputer Journal from a post office box in Carlton, with a second address in North Sydney. Its editor was **Sean Howard**. His first editorial looked past the hardware to software, which he thought had lagged behind: "Australians should recognize the potential for innovative software and strive to establish an international reputation in this field." An invitation on the same page asked readers to write for "Australia's first microcomputer journal". A year's subscription cost $24, or $30 to New Zealand.

The contents list included:

- **Benchtest**, "the first of our structured investigations into micro hardware", in which Sue Eisenbach tested the Compucolor II
- **Computer Answers**, in which Sheridan Williams took readers' software, hardware and systems problems
- a series by **David Levy** on the theory behind computer game-playing
- a specially commissioned ten-part series on Pascal by Chris Sadler and Sue Eisenbach
- **Leisure Lines**, puzzles and prizes from J.J. Clessa
- a cover illustration by **Ingram Pinn**

There was local material too. Miriam Cosic reported on the Victorian Education Department's attitude to computers in schools, and the advertising came from Melbourne and Sydney dealers. The masthead hinted at where much of the rest came from. Under Howard it listed an "Editor (U.K.)", **Bruce Sawford**, and a technical editor, **David Tebbutt**.

## Made under licence

By November 1980 the masthead set the arrangement out in full. Howard was now "Australian Editor"; Tebbutt was editor, Peter Rodwell deputy editor and Sawford executive editor. The magazine had a "London Editorial Office" at **Sportscene Publishers (PCW) Ltd., 14 Rathbone Place**, and a line at the foot of the column read: "Produced in Australia under licence from Felden Productions Ltd., England." The American magazine *MICRO* listed *Personal Computer World* at that same publisher and address in 1981 and 1982, and *MICRO-80*'s lists of British user groups gave Tebbutt's contact address as c/o 14 Rathbone Place.

The licence and the London office explain the British by-lines in issue one. The magazine was printed in Victoria and distributed by Gordon & Gotch, and the local news, local dealers and Australian writers sat alongside material edited in London. The same November issue reported that sales had taken "a hefty leap" and that the distributors had asked for the print run to be doubled, "and immediately".

## The cover claim

The "first" on the cover does not survive comparison with [*MICRO-80*](/vault/magazines/micro-80), a monthly published from Goodwood, South Australia, since **December 1979**. *APC*'s own first issue carried a Sydney dealer's advertisement for "the full range of MICRO-80 newsletters and recorded software". *MICRO-80* described itself more narrowly, as "the first and only Australian monthly magazine devoted entirely to the TRS-80 microcomputer". A general magazine might not have counted a single-machine one as a rival, but *MICRO-80* was Australian, monthly, about microcomputers, and earlier.

## Australia's top seller

By January 1985 *APC* was published by Computer Publications in Melbourne, with Howard as managing editor, at $2.95. The hundredth issue, dated December 1988, cost $7.95 and called itself "Australia's top selling computer magazine" on the cover. Its letters went to 124 Castlereagh Street, Sydney. The British link was still visible in the contents. David Tebbutt opened a four-part series on the user-interface research at Rank Xerox EuroPARC in Cambridge, alongside an interview with Bill Gates and an essay by Isaac Asimov. A reader's classified advertisement in the same issue offered up to $50 for a copy of the May 1980 first issue.

In 1990 a subscription advertisement in *Australian PC User* called *APC* "Australia's top selling magazine", at $4.95 an issue on the newsstand. That is the publisher's own claim, not an audited figure.

## ACP and Future

By the end of the 1990s *APC* was a Sydney title of ACP. The December 1998 issue lists Jeremy White as editor-in-chief, with staff on acp.com.au addresses, and in 2004 the magazine stated that it was "published by ACP Publishing Pty Ltd", edited by Tony Sarno, with its own website at apcmag.com. On 16 August 2013 the British publisher Future bought *APC* and its sister title *TechLife* from Bauer Media Group. Announcing the deal, Future described *APC* as Australia's "longest-published" computer brand. Future still lists *APC* as a monthly magazine.
