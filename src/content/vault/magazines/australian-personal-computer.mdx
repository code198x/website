**Australian Personal Computer** launched in **May 1980**, published by
*Australian Microcomputer Journal* of **Carlton, Victoria**, at $2.20. Its run in
this library reaches into the 2000s.

## The cover claim, and what this library holds

Issue one carries the line **"AUSTRALIA'S FIRST MICRO MAGAZINE"** across the top
of the cover.

This library holds [*MICRO-80*](/vault/magazines/micro-80), published monthly from
Goodwood, South Australia, from **December 1979** — five months earlier.

The two claims are not quite in direct collision. *MICRO-80* describes itself
more narrowly, as "the first and only Australian monthly magazine devoted
entirely to the TRS-80 microcomputer", and a general-interest magazine might
reasonably not count a single-machine one as its predecessor. But *MICRO-80* was
an Australian magazine about microcomputers, monthly, and it came first.

Recorded as a cover line tested against the holdings, not as a resolved dispute.

## An unmistakably British issue one

The first issue is assembled from British contributors and British column
furniture:

- **Benchtest** — "the first of our structured investigations into micro
  hardware", by **Sue Eisenbach**, testing the Compucolor II
- **Newsprint** — "APC inaugurates a column of micro happenings"
- **Computer Answers** — **Sheridan Williams** on readers' software problems
- **Artificial Intelligence Without Tears** — a **David Levy** series on game
  theory
- Cover illustration by **Ingram Pinn**

The content is genuinely local — the Benchtest subject is an Australian-market
machine, the advertising is from Melbourne dealers — but the format and the
by-lines are not.

⚠ **An open question this library cannot settle.** Those names and column titles
belong to the British magazine *Personal Computer World*, and the pattern
resembles the licensed editions this Vault has documented
[elsewhere](/vault/culture/magazines-across-borders). But nothing in *APC*'s own
pages states a relationship, and the only issues of *Personal Computer World*
held here are from **2005** — twenty-five years too late to compare by-lines,
columns or artwork. The resemblance is recorded; the relationship is not
established, and would need contemporary British issues to test.
