**Mikroszámítógép Magazin** was published in Budapest from **1984** — *a Neumann
János Számítógéptudományi Társaság kiadványa*, a publication of the **John von
Neumann Computer Science Society**, produced with the state computing
co-ordination institute. Sixty-two issues are held here, running to May 1990.

It is the first pre-transition Hungarian title in this library. Until it arrived,
Hungary was represented only by *[576 KByte](/vault/magazines/576-kbyte)*, which
begins in January 1990 — so the Vault could describe a market already opening and
not what it opened from.

## A machine landscape that then disappears

| | 1984–89, *Mikroszámítógép* | 1990–93, *576 KByte* |
|---|---|---|
| PC | 25% | ~0% |
| C64 | 23% | **41%** |
| **C16 / Plus/4** | **13%** | ~0% |
| Spectrum | 11% | ~0% |
| **Enterprise** | **11%** | ~0% |
| **Primo** | **5%** | ~0% |
| Amiga | — | **56%** |

Three things in the earlier column appear nowhere else in this Vault at these
shares. The **Enterprise** — designed in Britain, sold largely into Hungary — at
11%. The **Primo**, a Hungarian domestic machine, at 5%. And the **C16 and
Plus/4** at 13%, a pair that failed commercially in Britain and that
[Argentina's Commodore magazine](/vault/magazines/drean-commodore) also gave more
room than the British press ever did.

By 1990–93 the Hungarian press is an Amiga and C64 duopoly and the rest is gone.

⚠ **These are two different magazines, not one magazine changing.**
*Mikroszámítógép* was a computer-society publication with an education and
professional bent; *576 KByte* is a games magazine. Some of that collapse is
certainly editorial rather than economic, and this library cannot separate the
two. What can be said is that a diverse landscape including domestic machines is
visible in the Hungarian press before 1990 and absent after it.

## Who published it

The masthead is the point of comparison with the rest of the bloc. This is not a
commercial magazine: it is the publication of a **learned society**, made with a
state institute, its editorial board led by **Kovács Győző**, a founder of that
society. Its first issue runs features on computing in schools and on educational
software in England.

Set beside *[Bajtek](/vault/magazines/bajtek)*, a supplement to Poland's official
youth newspaper, and
*[Mikroprozessortechnik](/vault/magazines/mikroprozessortechnik)*, published for
the GDR's engineering chamber under a state press licence, a pattern appears —
see [who published the bloc's computer press](/vault/culture/institutional-computer-press).

⚠ This entry is built from issue 1 and from counts across the run. This library
holds no secondary account of the magazine, and nothing here establishes its
circulation or why the run held stops in May 1990.
