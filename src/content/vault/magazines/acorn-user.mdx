**Acorn User** began in **August 1982**, and its first page says exactly what it was:

> "The **official magazine** for users of the Acorn Atom, the BBC microcomputer system, and the Econet system, **published by Addison-Wesley for Acorn Computers Limited**."

That sentence separates it from nearly every other title in this Vault. *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)*, *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)*, *[Your Sinclair](/vault/magazines/your-sinclair)* and the rest were independent magazines that covered a manufacturer's machines and could be rude about them. *Acorn User* was [Acorn](/vault/companies/acorn-computers)'s.

## Why that matters for Elite

The consequence is visible in the single most celebrated launch in British home computing. *[Elite](/vault/games/elite)* was written for the [BBC Micro](/vault/systems/bbc-micro), published by *[Acornsoft](/vault/companies/acornsoft)* — Acorn's software arm — and launched in *Acorn User*, which carried both Acornsoft's advertisement and Tony Quinn's review in the same October 1984 issue.

Every part of that was in-house. The machine, the publisher, the magazine that announced it and the magazine that reviewed it were one company. It is worth holding that in mind beside the review, and beside the fact that the advertisement and the review in that issue [do not agree on the price](/vault/games/elite).

⚠ Being the manufacturer's magazine is not the same as being uncritical, and nothing here establishes that *Acorn User*'s reviews were softened. The point is structural, not an accusation.

## The publisher

Addison-Wesley — an academic and technical book publisher rather than a magazine house — produced it for Acorn at the start. **Redwood Publishing** appears in its pages from 1984 onwards.

⚠ This library cannot date a clean handover between them. Addison-Wesley keeps appearing after 1984, but so do advertisements for Addison-Wesley's own computing books, and the two are not separable from the page text alone.
