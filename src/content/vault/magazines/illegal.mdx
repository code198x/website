A crack intro could announce a group, but it couldn't hold a conversation. *Illegal* did. Produced for the Commodore 64 by Jeff Smart in association with [Triad](/vault/groups/triad), it was a **diskmag** — a magazine that booted on the machine itself — and one of the cracking scene's most important. Where the intro was a one-way broadcast, *Illegal* was the scene's debating chamber: news, rumours, interviews, charts, and the running argument over who was the best.

That argument had teeth. The magazine's final issue, #38, famously voted [Ikari](/vault/groups/ikari) the world's best cracking group — the kind of verdict that carried real weight in a community where reputation was the whole currency.

## Fast facts

- **Platform:** [Commodore 64](/vault/systems/commodore-64) (diskmag).
- **By:** [Jeff Smart](/vault/people/jeff-smart), in association with [Triad](/vault/groups/triad).
- **Role:** The cracking scene's news and charts.
- **Famous for:** Its final issue (#38) crowning [Ikari](/vault/groups/ikari) the world's best.

## Why it mattered

Diskmags like *Illegal* (alongside *Sex'n'Crime* and *Zine*) are how the cracking scene argued, organised, and remembered itself. They preserve the texture of a community that otherwise communicated in scrolltexts — and for historians, they are some of the richest surviving records of what the scene thought at the time.
