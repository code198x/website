Most of what the Commodore 64 cracking scene said about itself went out on disk, in scrolling messages and crack intros. *Illegal* went out on paper. Jeff Smart, a teenager in West Germany, started it in January 1986 with two pages. By March 1988 his photocopied fanzine was on its 26th issue, selling about 400 copies at 1.60 Deutschmarks each, with news of the latest cracks, gossip, interviews and monthly charts ranking the cracking groups against each other.

That was enough to reach a British newsstand magazine. In June 1988 *Commodore User* described "It's Illegal (But Who Cares?)" to its readers, and noted that it "revels in its downright naughtiness".

## Fast facts

- **Format:** Photocopied paper fanzine; some issues were later typed up and passed round on disk.
- **Editor:** [Jeff Smart](/vault/people/jeff-smart), in West Germany.
- **Run:** January 1986 to December 1991, numbered up to issue 38.
- **Languages:** English and German.
- **Group links:** Published with [Triad](/vault/groups/triad) during 1988, when Smart was a member; the final issue was produced with the cracking group Elite.

## What was in it

*Commodore User*'s account is the fullest description in the period press. Mike Pattenden's piracy feature listed the April 1988 issue's contents: "news, interviews with hackers, a report on the Venlo copy party, a review of *Rolling Thunder*, a debate on the relative merits of 64 and Amiga, classified ads, news of the latest cracked games and charts." In his view the fanzine "holds the software industry in complete contempt and almost goes as far as to rationalise a kind of Robin Hood image for itself". He quoted the March issue's interview with Ian of the British group Fusion, who said he cracked games "to give people who can't afford to buy a game the chance to have it."

The charts were the part the scene cared most about. Issue 26, dated 7 March 1988, ranked the "Cracker of the Month" for February by readers' points: Fusion first, [Fairlight](/vault/groups/fairlight) second and Triad third, with [Ikari](/vault/groups/ikari) fifth and [Eagle Soft Incorporated](/vault/groups/eagle-soft-incorporated) eighth. A later scene history calls *Illegal* one of the first magazines to rank groups this way. Triad's profile of Smart singles out one column, "The Story of System 4", as "a causerie of the Scene": a chatty running story that added to the legend of the groups it described.

What survives today mostly survives because scene members copied it. Issue 26 exists as a text file "rewritten from paper" by a Triad member, who left out the German-language parts because he could not read them.

## Triad and after

[Triad](/vault/groups/triad) was a Swedish group, but *Commodore User* called *Illegal* "the magazine published by Germany's TRIAD group". The mistake followed from its editor. The scene database CSDb lists Smart as a Triad member from January to October 1988, and credits issue 28, dated 21 May 1988, to "Jeff Smart and Triad", with contributions from members of Fusion, Fairlight, Orion and other groups. In October 1988 *Commodore User* printed the opening of a letter he signed "Jeff Smart, TRIAD, West Germany", and cut it off there. According to Triad's own history, Smart moved to the Scouse Cracking Group in late September 1988.

In May 1989, according to scene accounts, German police raided Smart over the swapping of pirated software and seized his computers, disks and magazines. The case was dismissed in 1990 for lack of evidence, but the two accounts on Triad's website disagree about what happened to his equipment: one says it was eventually returned, though a disk drive never worked properly again; the other says it was destroyed, and that he was told never to publish a cracker fanzine again.

*Illegal* went quiet. In December 1991, after 18 months without an issue, Smart published number 38, subtitled "Judgement Day", with the cracking group Elite, and called it "the final and last issue of ILLEGAL". Its charts named [Ikari](/vault/groups/ikari) the world's best cracking group.

## Why it matters

*Illegal* is one of the few written records of the Commodore 64 cracking scene made at the time. Its charts show which groups the scene rated, month by month. Its interviews show how crackers explained themselves. *Commodore User*'s report shows it reaching well beyond the scene in 1988. The [scene diskmags](/vault/magazines/scene-diskmags) that followed took over the same job of news, interviews and charts, on disk instead of paper.
