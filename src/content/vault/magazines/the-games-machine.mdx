**The Games Machine** was [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield)'s multi-format
magazine, launched in **October 1987**. Its own first issue frames it as "a
brand new magazine… that represents something of a departure for Newsfield the
publishers" of [*CRASH*](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine) and
[*Zzap!64*](/vault/magazines/zzap-64).

Departure is the right word. Newsfield had built its reputation on single-format
magazines that knew one machine intimately. TGM covered everything.

## Why it launched
A rival publication is more candid about the reason than TGM was. Writing in
August 1987, the *C16/Plus 4 Handbook* records:

> September 19th has seen the launch of yet another computer magazine, from the
> Newsfield stable. Angered by the success of a re-vamped **Computer and Video
> Games**, along comes Newsfield's answer with the Games Machine.

⚠ That is a competitor's characterisation of a motive, not Newsfield's own
account, and the launch date it gives differs from the October cover date on
issue one. It is worth recording because it places TGM in a specific
competitive moment: [C&VG](/vault/magazines/computer-and-video-games) had
invented the multi-format magazine in 1981 and, six years on, had revamped
successfully enough to provoke a response.

## Fast facts
- **First issue:** October 1987.
- **Publisher:** Newsfield, of Ludlow.
- **Scope:** multi-format, review-led — issue two describes "the next 38 pages" as "the core of" the magazine.
- **In this library:** 34 UK issues, 3,651 pages — against 112 issues of the [Italian edition](/vault/magazines/the-games-machine-italy).
- **Spectrum reviews:** 303 scored reviews recorded in ZXDB.

## How it scored
TGM appears throughout this Vault as the sceptical voice. Its marks tend to sit
below the Spectrum specialists on the same games — *Chase H.Q.* 91% against
*CRASH*'s 95%, *R-Type* 90% against 92%, *Out Run* **61%** against 72% — which
is what a multi-format reviewer does to a conversion. Judged against the arcade
original and the 16-bit versions on the next page, an 8-bit port has less room.

Ex-TGM writers turn up later in the record: *Amstrad Action* in 1990 describes a
magazine produced by "a group of ex-Newsfield writers (mostly from The Games
Machine)".

## The edition that outlived it

Newsfield licensed TGM to [Edizioni Hobby](/vault/companies/edizioni-hobby) of
Milan, whose first Italian issue appeared in **September 1988** carrying the
line "Su licenza di NEWSFIELD PUBLICATION". The British magazine closed after
issue 34 in September 1990 and Newsfield collapsed the year after. The Italian
edition changed publisher, dropped the licence credit, and ran until **May
1999**.

Counted across this library's whole holdings, then, TGM is not a three-year
magazine. It is an eleven-year one, and its longest phase is Italian — 112
issues and 11,563 pages against Britain's 34 and 3,651. See
[The Games Machine (Italy)](/vault/magazines/the-games-machine-italy).
