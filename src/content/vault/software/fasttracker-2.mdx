In **FastTracker II**, an instrument can choose different samples for different notes and carry its own volume and panning envelopes. A musician does not need to repeat every small change in loudness in the pattern. The instrument can describe part of the sound’s behaviour itself.

**Fredrik Huss** and [Magnus Högdahl](/vault/people/vogue), of [Triton](/vault/groups/triton), explain that aim in the introduction to the version 2.08 manual. They wanted instruments to make composition easier, rather than compete only by adding more effect columns.

## Notes, samples and instruments

A sample holds a waveform. An instrument adds a keyboard map and settings that can apply to several samples. A low note can select one recording and a high note another, while an envelope shapes their loudness over time.

The **XM** format stores that organisation: pattern order, note events, instruments, sample maps and envelope points. Its sample headers also describe loops and sample properties. A module therefore contains instructions for constructing a performance, rather than a finished recording of one. (Mr. H, *XM format description*, instrument and sample sections.)

## Patterns remain reusable

The pattern editor retains the [tracker](/vault/culture/tracker-music) idea of rows and channels. XM permits varying pattern lengths, and the order table decides which pattern comes next. Repeating a musical section need not mean storing another identical pattern.

Playback still depends on the interpretation of effects, envelopes and pitch calculations. Loading another tracker’s file is not a promise that every detail survives conversion to XM.

## The DOS program and its later clone

The modern **ft2-clone** runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. Its maintainer documents a player ported from the original source alongside additional sample tools and mixing changes. It carries the older working style forward, but its extra facilities should not be assigned retrospectively to the DOS release.
