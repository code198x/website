## Overview

A wargame simulates military conflict at the operational level: the player commands formations across terrain, manages supply and morale, and wins through position, not reflex. It arrived on home computers as a direct descendant of the tabletop hex-and-counter hobby, and for years it kept that hobby's conventions — dense manuals, numbered scenarios, and an assumption that the player already knew what a zone of control was.

It was a substantial category in the British press, appearing across roughly 9,600 pages in more than 3,600 issues, though usually in its own reviewing ghetto: many magazines gave wargames a separate column, on the reasonable grounds that their readership overlapped little with the arcade audience.

Distinguish it from later real-time strategy. The wargame is turn-based, historically specific, and interested in accuracy; the RTS that displaced it in the 1990s is real-time, usually fictional, and interested in tempo.

## Fast facts

- **Core loop:** assess, order, resolve, repeat by turn.
- **Map:** hexes or squares, with terrain modifying movement and combat.
- **Subject:** usually a specific historical campaign.
- **Defining titles:** the SSI catalogue, *Laser Squad*, *Chaos*, later *UFO: Enemy Unknown*.
- **Audience:** older, overlapping with tabletop wargaming.

## Genre conventions

- **Turn structure:** orders issued, then resolved simultaneously or in sequence.
- **Combat resolution:** odds ratios and a random factor, inherited from tabletop tables.
- **Supply and morale:** units degraded by attrition, not destroyed outright.
- **Fog of war:** limited intelligence about enemy positions.
- **Scenario sets:** one engine, many historical situations.

## Evolution

- **1980-84:** direct translations of tabletop systems, often by their designers.
- **1985-89:** the computer starts doing what paper could not — hidden movement, automatic resolution, an opponent.
- **1990s:** the squad-level branch (*Laser Squad*, *UFO: Enemy Unknown*) reaches a wider audience than the operational one.
- **Mid-1990s:** real-time strategy takes the mainstream, and the wargame returns to its specialist base.
