The shoot 'em up is the defining action form of the home-computer era. The player pilots a ship or figure, enemies arrive in patterns, and survival depends on reading those patterns while returning fire. Everything else — scrolling direction, weapon systems, power-up economies — is variation on that core.

Its grip on the period is measurable. Across the magazine archive it is the single most-used genre term in the British computer press, appearing on some 16,000 pages in over 4,600 issues. Reviewers assumed it as the default: a game was a shoot 'em up unless it was something else.

The spelling was never settled. *Shoot 'em up*, *shoot-em-up*, *shootem-up* and *shoot 'em-up* all appear, often within one magazine and occasionally within one review.

## Fast facts

- **Core loop:** move, fire, avoid, survive the wave.
- **Perspective:** vertical or horizontal scrolling, or fixed-screen in the earliest form.
- **Skill model:** pattern memorisation over reflex alone.
- **Defining titles:** *Space Invaders*, *Defender*, *Gradius*, *R-Type*, *Uridium*.
- **Period status:** the assumed default genre in British reviews.

## Genre conventions

- **Waves and patterns:** enemies arrive in choreographed formations, not randomly.
- **Power-up economy:** collected upgrades that are lost on death, making recovery hard.
- **The end-of-level guardian:** a large, multi-part enemy with a weak point.
- **One-hit death:** common on home machines, softened by shields on later titles.
- **Scroll lock:** the playfield moves whether the player does or not.

## Sub-forms the period distinguished

- **Vertical scroller:** *Xevious*, *Raiden*.
- **Horizontal scroller:** *R-Type*, *Gradius*, *Uridium*.
- **Fixed screen:** *Space Invaders*, *Galaxian*.
- **Multi-directional:** *Robotron 2084*, *Defender*.
- **Isometric and 3D variants:** presentation changes, genre does not.

## Evolution

- **1978-82:** fixed-screen origins in the arcade, ported everywhere.
- **1983-87:** scrolling becomes standard; the home conversion is a critical battleground.
- **1988-92:** 16-bit machines deliver arcade parity; *R-Type* on the Amiga is a landmark.
- **1990s onward:** the form narrows into the enthusiast "bullet hell" tradition.
