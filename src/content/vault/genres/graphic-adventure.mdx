The graphic adventure is the adventure game with pictures. In its first form that is exactly what it was — a text adventure with an illustration above the parser, as in *The Hobbit* — and the term meant only that the game was not purely textual.

What followed was a decade-long migration. The picture grew, the text shrank, the parser became a verb list, and the verb list became a set of icons. By the early 1990s the graphic adventure had become the point-and-click adventure, with no typing at all, and the genre's centre of gravity had moved from Britain to LucasArts and Sierra in the United States.

The archive records roughly 3,600 pages across more than 2,100 issues, distinct from the *text adventure* the British scene had championed first.

## Fast facts

- **Core loop:** examine, collect, combine, unlock the next scene.
- **Input:** parser, then verb menu, then mouse.
- **Failure model:** early titles could be made unwinnable; later ones mostly could not.
- **Defining titles:** *[The Hobbit](/vault/games/the-hobbit)*, *[King's Quest](/vault/games/kings-quest)*, *[Maniac Mansion](/vault/games/maniac-mansion)*, *Monkey Island*, *[Beneath a Steel Sky](/vault/games/beneath-a-steel-sky)*.
- **Distinguishing feature:** the image carries information, not just decoration.

## Genre conventions

- **The inventory puzzle:** progress gated by finding the right object for the right obstacle.
- **Scene as screen:** discrete locations, not continuous space.
- **Dialogue trees:** conversation as a puzzle surface, from the late 1980s.
- **Pixel hunting:** the genre's characteristic vice — a critical object rendered three pixels wide.
- **Death, then no death:** LucasArts' decision to make its games unlosable was a genuine design argument of the era.

## The British line

Britain's adventure tradition ran through Level 9, Magnetic Scrolls and Melbourne House, and it kept the parser longer than the American studios did — partly conviction, partly memory constraints that made a text engine cheaper than an animation engine. Magnetic Scrolls' games paired sophisticated parsing with genuinely accomplished artwork, arriving at the same destination from the opposite direction.

## Evolution

- **1982-85:** illustrated text adventures; the picture is an addition.
- **1986-88:** verb menus and mouse pointers begin replacing typing.
- **1989-93:** the point-and-click era; the parser disappears entirely.
- **Mid-1990s:** the genre stalls commercially, and survives through revival and independents.
