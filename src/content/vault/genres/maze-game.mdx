The maze game confines play to a fixed labyrinth. The player clears it of something — dots, diamonds, boulders, keys — while pursuers move through the same corridors under their own logic. Everything hangs on route planning under pressure: the maze is known, the threat is mobile, and the interest lies in the geometry between them.

*Pac-Man* fixed the template in 1980 so firmly that the genre spent the decade in its shadow. On British 8-bit machines the maze game was a staple of budget publishing, in part because a single-screen playfield was cheap to build and cheap to hold in memory — an entire game could fit where a scrolling level would not.

## Fast facts

- **Core loop:** clear the maze without being caught.
- **Playfield:** fixed and fully visible, or a small scrolling window.
- **Threat model:** pursuers with distinct movement behaviours.
- **Defining titles:** *[Pac-Man](/vault/games/pac-man)*, *[Dig Dug](/vault/games/dig-dug)*, *[Boulder Dash](/vault/games/boulder-dash)*, *[Repton](/vault/games/repton)*, *[Bomberman](/vault/games/bomberman)*.
- **Economics:** cheap to produce, a mainstay of budget labels.

## Genre conventions

- **The power reversal:** a temporary state in which the hunted becomes the hunter.
- **Distinct pursuer behaviours:** each enemy given its own rule, so the pattern reads as intent.
- **Collect-to-clear:** the level ends when the maze is emptied, not when a timer expires.
- **Warp tunnels:** edges that wrap, turning a bounded space into a loop.
- **Escalation by speed:** later levels rarely change layout, only pace.

## The digging branch

A significant strand let the player reshape the maze instead of crossing it. *Dig Dug* tunnelled through soil; *Boulder Dash* and *Repton* added gravity, so excavation became a physics puzzle with lethal consequences. These are maze games in structure but puzzle games in feel, and the British press moved between both labels depending on which aspect a review wanted to emphasise.

## Evolution

- **1980-82:** *Pac-Man* and immediate imitators establish the form.
- **1983-85:** the digging and gravity variants add puzzle depth.
- **1986-89:** the genre settles into budget publishing on 8-bit machines.
- **1990s:** survives mainly through *Bomberman*'s multiplayer reinvention.
