By the mid-1980s "arcade game" had stopped meaning a game found in an arcade. In the British computer press it named a manner of play: instant to start, reflex-driven, scored, and structured in lives, not chapters. A game was an arcade game because of how it behaved, not where it came from — plenty of titles never saw a coin-op cabinet.

It is the broadest genre term in the archive, appearing on roughly 19,000 pages across more than 5,500 issues, and the most frequently used of any category word measured. It functioned as the opposite pole to "adventure": one demanded reflexes, the other patience.

The term invites misreading today. Only about seven per cent of its uses in the archive sit near "conversion" or "coin-op" — it was not primarily shorthand for a port. That sense exists, but it is a separate and much smaller category.

## Fast facts

- **Played in public:** a gathered audience, and the design assumed one.
- **Core loop:** immediate action, escalating difficulty, high score.
- **Session length:** minutes, by design.
- **Failure model:** finite lives, no save, back to the start.
- **Opposite of:** adventures, strategy, anything requiring reading.
- **Period status:** the broadest genre word in British reviews.

## Genre conventions

- **No preamble:** play begins within seconds of loading.
- **Score as the point:** the high-score table is the progression system.
- **Lives, not health:** a fixed budget of failures.
- **Difficulty ramp:** the same systems accelerated, not replaced.
- **Attract mode:** inherited from cabinets even where there was no cabinet.

## Relationship to other genres

Arcade game is an umbrella. Shoot 'em ups, maze games, platform games and most driving games all sit inside it, and reviewers used the broad and narrow terms interchangeably depending on how much precision a sentence needed. Where a review says "arcade game" and nothing more, it usually means the game resisted narrower classification.

## Evolution

- **1978-82:** the coin-op boom defines the vocabulary.
- **1983-87:** home machines adopt the style wholesale; the term detaches from the venue.
- **1988-92:** 16-bit conversions narrow the gap between home and cabinet.
- **1990s:** as home games grow longer and story-driven, "arcade" becomes a term of contrast — and eventually of nostalgia.
