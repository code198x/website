**Tracker music** is a composition method using pattern-based sequencing and sampled sounds, originating with Ultimate Soundtracker on the Amiga in 1987. The MOD format and its successors became the standard for game audio, and tracker composers formed the pipeline to professional game music.

## The tools were free, which is the point

The format spread because nothing stood between a fourteen-year-old and a
composition tool. By 1993 an Amiga book could tell beginners:

> Amazingly enough, the Soundtracker, Noisetracker and Protracker programs are now
> available in the public domain. So you can treat yourself to an immediate copy
> from your favourite PD library for around £3!

Three pounds bought the disk and the postage. The software cost nothing. Protracker
was also cover-mounted — "given away free with a recent issue of *CU Amiga* (Disk
14)".

**And they were hard, on purpose.** The same book is candid about why: "All the PD
music editors have been designed by and for hackers, who are dedicated to getting
the absolute maximum out of the Amiga's sound system. Inevitably, this adds a great
deal of complication to the resulting programs." Its own answer to that complaint:
"I'm not complaining of course! After all, we're not actually paying for their
software, are we?"

⚠ **The tracker line runs through the Atari ST as well.** The table below goes
Amiga then PC. The French ST press was listing and reviewing **Protracker**,
**Noisetracker** and **Equinox Soundtracker** as ST software from 1991 to 1993. And
the Amiga field was wider than two programs: an Italian five-way comparison in 1991
put **MED**, **StarTrekker** and **Jam Cracker** alongside ProTracker and
NoiseTracker.

## Fast facts

- **Origin:** Ultimate Soundtracker (Amiga, 1987)
- **Format:** MOD, S3M, XM, IT
- **Method:** Pattern-based sequencing
- **Impact:** Game audio standard
- **Legacy:** Professional composers

## How Trackers Work

| Element | Function |
|---------|----------|
| **Samples** | Recorded sounds |
| **Patterns** | Sequences of notes |
| **Channels** | 4 (Amiga) to 32+ (PC) |
| **Effects** | Volume, pitch, panning |

## Cultural impact

Trackers democratised music:

| Impact | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Free tools** | Anyone could compose |
| **Sharing** | MOD files traded freely |
| **Learning** | Study others' techniques |
| **Community** | Scene parties, competitions |

## Legacy

Tracker music trained a generation of game composers while establishing file formats and tools that games used throughout the 1990s. Modern DAWs owe conceptual debt to tracker interfaces.
