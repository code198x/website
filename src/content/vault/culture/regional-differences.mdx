Localisation went beyond translation. Japanese games received American makeovers: changed sprites, removed religious imagery, added difficulty. European versions often ran slower on PAL systems. Some changes improved; others baffled. Understanding regional differences became essential knowledge for serious players and collectors.

## Two axes, not one

⚠ **"Regional difference" collapses two distinct kinds of change**, and the
documentation projects that catalogue them keep the two apart. Every Sega Retro
comparison page carries both headings:

- **Localisation comparisons** — Japanese release against Western release.
- **Version comparisons** — one platform or re-release against another.

Getting them the wrong way round produces false claims about censorship. The
red-cross removals in *Phantasy Star II* are a standard example of 1980s
regional editing, and they are not: Sega Retro files them under *version*, between
the Mega Drive original and the **Virtual Console re-release** — "the hospital icon
has been changed from a cross into an 'H'", "the red cross has been removed from the
doctor's suit". That is trademark compliance at re-release, decades after the fact.

**Sound was localised too**, which the list below misses. Of the same game: "the
original Japanese version features different, louder drum samples for the game's
soundtrack, which were replaced for the Western versions." Not translation, not
censorship — a mix decision taken per territory.

**And some changes are neither regional nor cosmetic but structural.** *DJ Boy* on
Mega Drive: "in the Arcade version, the player loses a life after taking 2 hits. In
the Mega Drive one, it was changed to a health bar with only one life." A coin-op's
life economy does not survive the removal of the coin slot.

## Fast facts

- **Causes:** censorship, cultural adaptation, technical requirements.
- **Common changes:** difficulty, [sprites](/vault/techniques/sprites), text, content removal, audio mixes.
- **PAL issues:** 50Hz versus 60Hz speed differences.
- **Box art:** frequently different between regions.
- **Modern persistence:** some changes continue today.

## Types of changes

Regional variations included:
- **Censorship:** violence, religious imagery, substance references.
- **Difficulty:** often made harder for Western release.
- **Sprites:** character designs changed.
- **Content:** scenes or features removed/added.
- **Technical:** PAL optimisation (or lack thereof).

## Notable examples

Famous regional differences:
- **Contra (NES):** renamed *Probotector* in Europe with robot sprites.
- **Final Fantasy:** spell names, monster designs changed.
- **Sonic CD:** different soundtracks by region.
