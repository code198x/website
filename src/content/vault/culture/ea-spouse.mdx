**"EA Spouse"** was the pseudonymous 2004 blog post by Erin Hoffman describing her fiancé's brutal working conditions at Electronic Arts. The post detailed 85+ hour weeks, cancelled time off, and management indifference. It went viral, sparked class action lawsuits, and forced industry-wide discussion of crunch culture.

## The blog did not act alone

⚠ **A class action was filed at the same time**, and the entry tells the story as a
blog post. *EGM*, February 2005: "around the same time the lawsuit was filed against
Electronic Arts, the anonymous 'EA Spouse' wrote a blog entry about the challenging
work schedules at the company" — the suit being "a class action... in which an
animator is suing for unpaid overtime."

A post is an argument. A class action is a cost. They arrived together.

**The economic case had a number attached.** Per a 2003 Gamasutra survey cited in
the same feature, "a programmer with two to five years' experience makes an average
of $70,000 a year — the same amount programmers in other industries can earn
straight out of college." Employers "can squeeze more and more work out of people
without having to pay massive salaries."

⚠ **And the causes were not only exploitative.** The same feature lists them
honestly: they "range from potentially exploitative (the 'shark pool' mentality) to,
in Schafer's case, a timeline that won't give, not enough preplanning, and simple
passion for making the product the best it can be." Tim Schafer:

> Yes, I want to go home. I don't want to work this hard. But am I willing to hurt
> my baby for it? That's a tough choice.

## What it displaced

*Edge* caught the shift within weeks, and its phrasing marks what the post broke:
"**Crunch time has long been accepted as a necessary evil of the games industry**,
but many are being forced to question that status quo by the webjournal of a
self-described 'disgruntled spouse' of an Electronic Arts employee."

## Fast facts

- **Published:** November 2004
- **Author:** Erin Hoffman (revealed 2005)
- **Subject:** EA's working conditions
- **Impact:** Lawsuits, settlements, industry discussion
- **Platform:** LiveJournal

## The Revelations

| Condition | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Hours** | 85+ per week |
| **Duration** | Months without end |
| **Comp time** | Promised but never given |
| **Overtime pay** | None (salaried exempt) |
| **Time off** | Cancelled repeatedly |

## The Fallout

| Event | Result |
|-------|--------|
| **Viral spread** | Picked up by mainstream media |
| **Class actions** | Artists and programmers sued |
| **Settlements** | $15.6M to artists, $14.9M to programmers |
| **Policy changes** | EA improved conditions (temporarily) |

## Legacy

The EA Spouse post remains the most significant single document in game industry labour history. It proved that public exposure could force change and inspired subsequent revelations at other companies. The issues it raised—crunch, overtime classification, work-life balance—remain unresolved.
