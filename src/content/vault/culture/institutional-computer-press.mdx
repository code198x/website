Across the socialist bloc, computer magazines were not commercial ventures that
happened to be published in planned economies. They came out of **institutions**,
and this library now holds four cases where the institution is named on the first
page.

| Magazine | Country | Published by |
|---|---|---|
| *[Bajtek](/vault/magazines/bajtek)* | Poland | supplement to *Sztandar Młodych*, the official **youth newspaper** |
| *Mikroklan* | Poland | **Wydawnictwo NOT-SIGMA**, the technical publishing house of the engineers' federation |
| *[Mikroszámítógép Magazin](/vault/magazines/mikroszamitogep-magazin)* | Hungary | the **John von Neumann Computer Science Society** |
| *[Mikroprozessortechnik](/vault/magazines/mikroprozessortechnik)* | East Germany | the **Kammer der Technik**, the engineering chamber, under state licence 1710 |

## The parent predicts the pages

Poland is the test, because it has two titles from the same country and the same
years with different parents.

| | *Bajtek* (youth newspaper) | *Mikroklan* (professional journal) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Spectrum | **34%** | 15% |
| PC | 15% | **58%** |
| Amstrad | 19% | 13% |
| C64 | 13% | 7% |

The youth-organisation magazine is a Spectrum magazine. The engineers'
federation magazine is a PC magazine. Same country, same decade, and a spread wider than
most of the differences between countries in this Vault's
[C64 comparison](/vault/culture/the-c64-across-borders).

This is the second country to show it. Yugoslavia's *Računari* and *Svet
Kompjutera* differ by a factor of nearly four on the C64. Poland's two differ by
almost four on the PC. In both cases the divergence tracks the magazine's
audience, not its nation.

## Why it matters for reading any of this

A magazine born inside a youth newspaper is trying to reach schoolchildren. One
born inside a professional journal is trying to reach people who use computers at
work. Neither is a survey of what a country owned, and the difference between
them is not evidence about the country at all.

That has a direct consequence for every national figure in this Vault: **the
institution that produced a magazine is a better predictor of its contents than
the country it was produced in.** Where only one title is held for a territory —
Argentina, Czechoslovakia, East Germany, Russia — nothing here can separate the
two.

⚠ Four cases, three countries, and Yugoslavia is deliberately excluded: its
*Računari* came from a commercial Belgrade publisher, not an institution, which
is consistent with its economy differing from the Warsaw Pact states and is a
reminder that "the bloc" was not one thing.

*Mikroklan*'s imprint states it directly: **"Wydawnictwo Czasopism i Książek
Technicznych NOT-SIGMA, Warszawa 1987"**, ISSN 0860-1941 — the technical
periodicals house of the **Naczelna Organizacja Techniczna**, Poland's federation
of engineering associations. That makes the Polish pair a near-exact parallel of
the German one: a youth body and an engineers' body, each with a magazine.

⚠ External metadata also describes early *Mikroklan* as an inset in
*Informatyka*, the professional computing journal. That is not confirmed from
any page held here and is not relied on; the NOT-SIGMA imprint is.
