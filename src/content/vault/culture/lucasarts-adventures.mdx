LucasArts (originally Lucasfilm Games) created adventure games with distinctive philosophy: players couldn't die or reach unwinnable states. This removed Sierra's frustrations while keeping clever puzzles. The SCUMM engine powered classics from *Maniac Mansion* through *The Curse of Monkey Island*. Sharp writing, memorable characters, and logical (usually) puzzle design made these games beloved.

## Gilbert's Rules of Thumb

The philosophy was not a house style inferred afterwards. Ron Gilbert published it
as twelve numbered rules in the *Journal of Computer Game Design*, under the title
**"Why Adventure Games Suck"**, while he was making these games — *Computer Gaming
World* abstracted it in April 1990.

1. The final objective needs to be clear
2. Even sub-goals need to be obvious
3. Live and learn — "danger should be survivable if the player is clever enough"
4. Watch out for "backwards puzzles"
5. Avoid the "I forgot to pick it up" syndrome
6. Make the puzzles advance the story
7. Real time gaming offers poor quality drama
8. There must be incremental rewards
9. Avoid arbitrary puzzles
10. Reward the player's intent
11. Make the events and puzzles consistent
12. Give the player lots of options

Three are worth pulling out.

**Rule 6** is the test that separates these games from their imitators: "Unless
solving a puzzle brings the player closer to understanding both the story and the
game, it is simply busy work."

**Rule 7** is a position this entry omits entirely — Gilbert was against real-time
pressure in adventures, reasoning "that 'timing' is a vital ingredient to effective
drama" and objecting "to time-based puzzles, especially if the player must fail on
several occasions before getting his timing down."

**Rule 12** carries the image the whole school turns on. Gilbert "pictures games
which are too linear as being analogous to a cage with the player locked into the
cage until he or she finds a certain key to get out. He suggests, instead, that
designers think of the player as being outside the cage and the puzzles being
locked within."

## Design philosophy

What made them special:
- **No deaths:** explore without fear.
- **No dead ends:** always solvable.
- **Humour:** wit throughout.
- **Polish:** voice acting, animation, music.
