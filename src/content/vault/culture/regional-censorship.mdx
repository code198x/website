**Regional censorship** refers to the modification of game content for different markets based on cultural sensitivities, legal requirements, or corporate policies. From religious symbol removal to violence reduction, these changes meant players in different regions often experienced fundamentally different games.

## Fast facts

- **Era:** 1980s-present
- **Types:** Violence, religion, sexuality, politics
- **Methods:** Removal, replacement, recolouring
- **Major markets:** US, Japan, Germany, Australia

## Nintendo's Content Policies (1980s-90s)

| Prohibited | Example |
|------------|---------|
| **Religious symbols** | Crosses removed from Castlevania |
| **Blood** | Made green or removed |
| **Tobacco/alcohol** | Bars became cafes |
| **Death references** | "Defeated" not "killed" |

## Germany (BPjS Indexing)

⚠ **The body was the BPjS in this period.** Through the 1980s and 1990s the German
authority was the *Bundesprüfstelle für jugendgefährdende Schriften* — the federal
review office for youth-endangering **writings**. It was renamed BPjM, für
jugendgefährdende **Medien**, in 2003. Anything indexed in the era this entry
covers was indexed by the BPjS.

**And the changes were often pre-emptive.** *PC Player*, September 1997, on the
German *Carmageddon*: the pedestrians "turned into rickety robots in the German
version. Because better to give offence with a lifeless tin pal than with the BPjS,
thought the publisher SCI." The publisher changed the game to avoid a ruling rather
than in response to one.

The index itself was a published document a reader could follow — *PC Player* told
one correspondent to "best subscribe to the review office's information bulletin"
— which is what made that calculation possible to make in advance.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **System** | BPjS index (BPjM only from 2003) |
| **Effect** | Indexed games can't be advertised |
| **Solutions** | Robot replacements, green blood |
| **Notable:** | Carmageddon required robots |

## Legacy

Regional censorship created the concept of "import gaming"—players seeking uncensored versions. Digital distribution has complicated regional content control, though region-specific ratings and laws continue to affect releases.
