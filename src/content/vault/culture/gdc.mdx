The Game Developers Conference (GDC) is the world's largest annual gathering of professional game developers. Running since 1988, it combines educational sessions, networking, and industry awards, serving as a crucial venue for knowledge sharing.

⚠ **Not always in San Francisco.** GDC 2002 was held in San Jose; San Francisco
became "its new, permanent" home after that, per *Edge* in 2007. The venue moved and
came back.

## What it is for

*Edge*'s 2002 report describes a working conference rather than an announcement
platform: "academia and game design issues at the fore", with two-day tutorials
"devoted to game tuning and the other to the role of academia", and a strong game
design track. Warren Spector's verdict on the mood that year: "I think next year's
show should be called 'Pose and Discuss.'"

The magazine's explanation for a flat year is a useful caution against reading
conference buzz as an industry indicator: "GDC 2002 was less sexy than previous
years, but this was probably because developers were spending less time talking
about what they hoped to do and more time actually doing it."

⚠ **There was a European conference too.** The 2002 San Jose show had "a smaller
than usual UK and European contingent speaking this year, no doubt reflecting the
organiser's keenness to focus on the European version of the conference to be held
in **London, during August**." British speakers who did travel included Peter
Molyneux on "Evolution of the Role-Playing Genre" and Dene and Simon Carter on "The
Pros and Cons of Organic Design".

## Key Components

- **Talks:** Developers share techniques and postmortems
- **GDC Vault:** Archive of past presentations
- **IGF:** Independent Games Festival showcase
- **GDCA:** Game Developers Choice Awards
- **Expo floor:** Tools and middleware demonstration

## Educational value

GDC talks cover:
- Programming techniques
- Art and animation
- Game design theory
- Production methodologies
- Business and marketing

Many talks are later published to the GDC Vault, creating an invaluable educational resource for developers worldwide.
