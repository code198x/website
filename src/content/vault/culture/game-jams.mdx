Game jams compress game development into intense bursts of creativity, typically 24 to 72 hours. Participants work alone or in small teams to create playable games around announced themes. The format strips away perfectionism and overthinking, producing raw, experimental work that often influences mainstream design.

## What a jam is for

The output is not the point. Asked what jams do, participants in *Edge*'s 2013
feature answer with completion:

> One of the strengths of game jams is that they motivate developers to actually
> finish a project. That helps a lot of people, since they fall into a trap of not
> ever finishing a thing, or even starting a thing.

And with volume. "I have dozens of game prototypes that I've produced from jams,
and I think that body of work is necessary for finding a style that suits you" —
because at 48 hours, "you don't give a shit about polish". The pull-quote in the
feature: "Jams are about volume, about making a volume of work to hone your skillset
and craft."

⚠ **And it is not only an indie practice.** "It's not just the indies: game jams

projects."

One detail about how the institution came to exist: Ludum Dare got "a proper home on
the web" because Phil Hassey decided it should. In a colleague's account: "Phil was
like, 'Dude, I want to do this. I want this to keep going, because this is
important.'"

## Fast facts

- **First major jam:** Indie Game Jam (2002).

- **Longest-running:** [Ludum Dare](/vault/communities/ludum-dare) (since 2002).
- **Typical duration:** 48-72 hours.

## Ludum Dare

The defining online jam:
- **Founded:** 2002 by Geoff Howland.
- **Format:** 48-hour Compo (solo) or 72-hour Jam (teams).
- **Voting:** Community rates games in categories.
- **History:** Many commercial games started here.

Notable Ludum Dare alumni:
- Minecraft (Notch was active participant)
- Evoland
- Titan Souls

## Global Game Jam

In-person worldwide event:
- Sites in 100+ countries.
- Same theme announced simultaneously.
- Local community building.
- 2020: 48,700 participants, 9,600 games.

## Why jams matter

Benefits for developers:
- **Finishing:** Complete projects rather than endless iteration.
- **Experimentation:** Low stakes encourage risk-taking.
- **Networking:** Meet collaborators.
- **Learning:** Rapid skill development under pressure.

Benefits for gaming:
- **Innovation:** Unconventional ideas surface.
- **Prototypes:** Commercial games test concepts.
- **Diversity:** Lower barrier to entry.

## Game jam culture

Unwritten rules:
- Theme is binding (interpret creatively).
- Sleeping is optional (but recommended).
- Share your process (dev logs, streams).
- Vote honestly, comment constructively.

## Commercial successes

Games that started as jam entries:

|------|-----|--------|
| Superhot | 7DFPS | Commercial hit |
| Surgeon Simulator | Global Game Jam | Franchise |

## Running a jam

Key elements:
- Clear theme announcement.
- Submission is a form and a link.
- Reasonable time window.
- Community voting system.
- Celebration of participation.

## Modern evolution

Recent developments:
- Virtual jams (pandemic acceleration).
- Streaming culture (live development).
- Corporate sponsorship (mixed reception).
- Educational integration (teaching tool).

## Legacy

Game jams show that constraints breed creativity. The format has become essential infrastructure for indie development, talent discovery, and gameplay innovation.
