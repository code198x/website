Mortal Kombat changed everything. After congressional hearings about video game violence—featuring Mortal Kombat's fatalities and Night Trap's controversy—the industry created self-regulatory ratings systems. The ESRB in North America, PEGI in Europe, and similar bodies worldwide now classify games by age-appropriateness, transforming how games are sold and marketed.

## Britain had its own system, and two of them at once

⚠ **This entry runs ESRB 1994 → PEGI 2003 and skips what happened in between in
the country this Vault is written from.**

**ELSPA, April 1994.** European games became "subject to a classification system,
organised by the European Leisure Software Publisher's Association (ELSPA). The
system, which applies to all ELSPA members, is based on self-regulation, where each
firm has the chance to provide specific age groups on the cover of their games."
Sixty companies were in. Geoff Brown of US Gold called the voluntary guidelines "a
major step forward in assisting parents to choose appropriate games for their
children."

**Two systems ran in parallel, and only one had teeth.** *PC Zone*, correcting a
reader in 2001:

> There are two separate systems in use on games. An ELSPA rating and, on some
> games a BBFC rating. The ELSPA rating is advisory and voluntary. The BBFC is
> legally binding.

The consequence, in the same letter: a game ELSPA had given "a restricted rating"
but which was "not BBFC-rated" was "therefore legal to distribute to any age."

**PEGI took over in April 2003.** It replaced ELSPA, "covering sixteen European
countries and administered by the Netherlands Institute For The Classification Of
Audiovisual Media". Under the old system, anything seeking 15+ or 18+ "were examined
by the VSC" — the Video Standards Council — "as an independent third party".

⚠ **And PEGI is advisory too.** *Edge* set this in capitals:

> IT IS NEITHER COMPULSORY FOR A PUBLISHER TO APPLY FOR A PEGI RATING NOR CARRY THE
> RATING ON PACKAGING. THE RATING IS ADVISORY ONLY.

In Britain the legal force sat elsewhere — the Video Recordings Act, the Protection
of Children Act, the Obscene Publications Act. A ratings body is not a regulator.

## Fast facts

- **ESRB founded:** 1994.
- **ELSPA (UK/Europe):** 1994-2003, voluntary.
- **PEGI founded:** April 2003, replacing ELSPA; advisory, not compulsory.
- **BBFC:** the legally binding British classification, running alongside.
- **Trigger:** Congressional hearings (1993).
- **Purpose:** Self-regulation, parental guidance.
