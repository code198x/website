**Brazilian gaming** developed distinctly due to the "Reserva de Mercado" (1977-1992), an import ban that protected domestic computer production. This created a unique ecosystem of domestic clones, MSX dominance, and later, the world's largest Master System market through Tectoy's local manufacturing.

## Fast facts

- **Import ban:** 1977-1992
- **Result:** Domestic clone industry
- **Console winner:** [Sega Master System](/vault/systems/sega-master-system)
- **Key company:** [Tectoy](/vault/companies/tectoy)
- **Legacy:** Distinct gaming culture

## The clone ecosystem, counted

The Brazilian edition of *INPUT* printed a compatibility table in every issue,
listing domestic machines against the originals they followed. It is a much fuller
picture than the four rows below — dozens of models from more than twenty
manufacturers: Apply, Codimex, Digitus, Dismac, Dynacom, Engebras, Filcres,
Gradiente, Kemitron, LZ, Magnex, Maxitronica, Microcraft, Microdigital, Milmar,
Multix, Omega, Polymax, Prológica, Ritas, Sharp, Spectrum, Suporte, Sycomig,
Sysdata, Unitron, Victor do Brasil.

⚠ **And it complicates the "MSX dominance" framing.** By number of distinct models
in 1986, the American lineages are far the largest:

| Original | Brazilian models in the table |
|---|---|
| Apple II+ / IIe | ~15, including Microengenho I and II, Elppa II Plus and Jr, Maxitronica MX-2001/48/64, Milmar Apple Master, Unitron AP II |
| TRS-80 / TRS-Color | ~12, including Prológica CP-300, CP-400 and CP-500, Sysdata II–IV, Dismac D-8000, Kemitron Naja 800 |
| Sinclair ZX-81 | 7 — Microdigital TK-82C, TK-83, TK-85; Prológica CP-200; Ritas Ringo R-470; Engebras AS-1000; Filcres NEZ-8000 |
| MSX | 2 — Gradiente Expert GPC4, Sharp Hotbit HB-8000 |
| Sinclair Spectrum | 1 — Microdigital TK-90X |

Model count is not market share, and the MSX machines may well have outsold the
rest. But the ecosystem the reserve market produced was broader than this entry
describes, and its centre of gravity was Apple and Tandy rather than MSX.

Note also that the **ZX81**, not the Spectrum, is the deeper Sinclair line in
Brazil — seven models against one.

## The teaching literature was localised too

The reserve market produced more than hardware. The British partwork
[*INPUT*](/vault/culture/type-in-programs) was translated into Portuguese and
rewritten around Brazilian machines: its machine-code introduction discusses
assemblers for the "linha TRS-80, TRS-Color, MSX, Sinclair Spectrum e ZX-81", and
singles out "o TK-2000, da Microdigital" as the local machine that shipped with a
built-in monitor.

| Effect | Result |
|--------|--------|

| **Innovation** | Local adaptation required |
| **End (1992)** | Market opened, clones declined |

## Legacy

The import ban period created a distinct Brazilian gaming culture with different platform preferences and extended hardware lifecycles. This experience shaped how gaming developed in Brazil even after market liberalisation.
