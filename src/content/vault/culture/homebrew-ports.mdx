Homebrew ports fill gaps publishers left. Fans convert games to platforms that never received official versions: bringing *Doom* to calculators, *Super Mario Bros.* to the Commodore 64, modern indie games to vintage hardware. These projects show technical mastery and community devotion to keeping old platforms alive with new software.

## The scene can outlive the platform

⚠ **This entry frames homebrew as filling gaps publishers left.** The Dreamcast case
is stronger. "Despite its apparent death 17 years ago, the Sega Dreamcast still has
a hardcore group of developers behind it", and "even years later, the Dreamcast
remains a thriving venue for indie developers." The scene outlasted the console's
commercial life by nearly two decades — not filling a gap, but continuing after the
shop closed.

⚠ **And the ports run outward as well as backward.** Senile Team's *Intrepid Izzy*
is "a puzzle platformer coming soon to the PC, PS4 and Dreamcast", and its coder
says "I started this project with only the PC in mind." That is a new game acquiring
a Dreamcast release, not a fan reconstructing an old one for a machine that missed
it.

It was also not confined to dead hardware: *Retro Gamer* in 2005 describes "the
vibrant GBA home-brew scene" on a console still in production.
