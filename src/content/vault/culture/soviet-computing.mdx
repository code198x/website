**Soviet computing** developed as a parallel tradition to Western computing, combining reverse-engineered Western designs with original architectures. Despite resource constraints and official ideology, engineers created viable computers and a unique software culture that produced Tetris and other innovations.

## Fast facts

- **Era:** 1960s-1991
- **Approach:** Mix of clones and originals
- **Famous software:** [Tetris](/vault/games/tetris) (1984)
- **Major platforms:** [Spectrum clones](/vault/culture/spectrum-clones), BK-0010
- **Legacy:** Active nostalgia scene

## How the designs arrived

The clone programme did not start from nothing. Western machines came in first,
informally: Spectrums were "often smuggled from the West" and spread "due to their
low cost and ease of use", and it was that demand which led "local microelectronics
companies [to make] 'their' versions of the machine". Imports created the market
the clones then served.

The result is legible in software. Six bloc machines — Pentagon 128, Didaktik M,
Didaktik Gama, Bulgaria's Byte, Poland's Elwro and the ZS Scorpion — carry their
own hardware IDs in the Spectrum community's [TZX tape
standard](/vault/culture/spectrum-clones), because enough software was written for
them specifically to be worth declaring.
