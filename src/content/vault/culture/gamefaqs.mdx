**GameFAQs** is a website founded in 1995 by Jeff "CJayC" Veasey that revolutionised how players got help with games. By hosting free user-submitted guides, it replaced expensive telephone hint lines and strategy guides with comprehensive community-written walkthroughs.

## Who wrote them, and why

⚠ **This entry credits an anonymous community. The writers were individuals with
names and counts.**

*Edge* went looking for them in 2006. The site began as an archive in 1995 and was
"later renamed GameFAQs.com. It wasn't the first site of its kind, but it was the
first to go mainstream. Founded by Jeff 'CjayC' Veasey, and originally hosting 100
articles, the site now holds information on over 50,000 titles" — and "all of them
written by devoted gamers for **little or no monetary reward**."

That last clause is the whole economic story. It is the point at which
[hint lines](/vault/culture/hint-lines) charging 36p to 48p a minute stop working.

**Alex Eagleson**, "a university student in his 20s", had "written a total of 53
unique walkthroughs, ranging from RPGs like *Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion* to
platformers such as *New Super Mario Bros*." His reasons: "There's a definite appeal
to both helping frustrated gamers, and exposing your work to such a wide audience."

**Andrew Schultz** wrote only about older games, and described the work as
preservation:

> Technology may be obsolete, but the good games that ran on obsolete technology
> never will be. I enjoy being able to write for a game and know I'm the first
> person to [document it].

He also gives the craft answer: "I like puzzling things out. The people that do this
work the best are able to pick apart whole worlds."

**And the payment was reputation.** One writer's own summary of what covering
Metroid, Zelda and "minor games like *Startropics* and *Mario's Picross*" had earned
him: it made him "the most famous Daniel Engel on Google".

## Legacy

GameFAQs proved that community knowledge sharing could outcompete commercial alternatives. Its model influenced wikis, forums, and modern game help resources.
