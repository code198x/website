In July 1992 a few members of the Finnish Amiga group Rebels hired a school in Kauniainen, a small town just west of Helsinki, for a weekend. With Complex and [Future Crew](/vault/groups/future-crew) they ran demo competitions for the Amiga and the PC, and when it was over their results file boasted "over 700 people showed up... biggest party ever held in Finland". Future Crew's *Unreal* won the PC demo competition. The next summer *[Second Reality](/vault/demos/second-reality)* won it too. Assembly has been held every summer since, and by the end of the decade it was filling a Helsinki sports arena with thousands of computers.

## Fast facts

- **Held:** every summer since 1992, usually in late July or early August; a winter event since 2007.
- **Where:** Kauniainen (1992), Kerava (1993), then Helsinki: an ice hall in 1994, the exhibition centre from 1995, the Hartwall Areena from 1999 and the exhibition centre again from 2014.
- **Run by:** demo groups at first, among them Rebels, Complex and Future Crew; from 1995 the company Assembly Organizing, majority-owned by the telecoms company Telia since 2018.
- **Size:** over 700 visitors in 1992, by the organisers' count; more than 1,300 in 1993, according to *MCmicrocomputer*; about 3,000 in 1994, according to Future Crew.

## A school in Kauniainen

Assembly's organisers describe its start as "a rebel dream": a party "dreamed up a couple of guys from Rebels", run by its first main organiser, Meegosh, together with Rebels, Complex and Future Crew. Future Crew's own history tells the story from its side. Meegosh telephoned the group about a month before a Swedish party, the Mega-Leif Convention, and asked for an invitation intro for the PC scene. Future Crew put *Unreal* aside for two weeks to write it, then finished the demo in time for Assembly.

The party ran from 24 to 26 July 1992 at the Kasavuoren koulukeskus, a school campus in Kauniainen. The copy of the results kept in the scene.org archive carries a heading that places it in Kerava, but the organisers' own list of past events and Demozoo both give Kauniainen, and Kerava was the venue of the following year. The results show an Amiga side and a PC side. Reflect won the Amiga demo competition. In the PC demo competition *Unreal* came first, Triton's *[Crystal Dream](/vault/demos/crystal-dream)* second and EMF's *Internal Damage* third. That December the Greek magazine *PC Master* reviewed *Unreal* as the best demo yet released for the PC, and perhaps one of the best on any computer.

## Kerava, 1993

Assembly '93 moved to the Nikkari secondary school in Kerava, north of Helsinki, from 30 July to 1 August. The Italian magazine *MCmicrocomputer* reported more than 1,300 visitors, each paying 100 markka for a weekend in which the school stayed open from 10 on Friday morning until 2 on Sunday afternoon, with a canteen open around the clock, beds and showers. The shareware publisher [Epic MegaGames](/vault/companies/epic-games) put up $1,500 in cash for the PC demo competition. Future Crew ran the PC competitions and, in its own words, made the party "a big advancement on the PC side", with PC intro, music and graphics competitions for the first time.

The PC demos all ran on the organisers' 486 PCs. Voters named up to five entries, with five points for a first choice, four for a second and so on. *Second Reality* won with 381 points to 334 for [The Silents](/vault/groups/the-silents)' *Optic Nerve* in the organisers' results file, from 105 ballots. Future Crew's own count, published that October as "the final and true results", gave 472 to 403 from 130 votes. On the Amiga side Pygmy's *Extension* won the demo competition with 196 points, ahead of Virtual Dreams' *242* and [Melon Dezign](/vault/groups/melon-dezign)'s *Romantic Demo*.

## Helsinki

In 1994 Assembly moved into Helsinki, to the city's ice hall. Future Crew, which organised it with Accession, Sonic PC, Virtual Dreams and The Movement, put the crowd at about 3,000 for the party from 5 to 7 August.

From 1995 the party used the Helsinki exhibition centre in Pasila, and its organisers set up a non-profit company, Assembly Organizing, with a team of 60 to 80 people. The Polish magazine *Bajtek* sent a reporter to Assembly '95, held from 10 to 13 August. A poster at the entrance promised "The Greatest Scene Event in the World". Inside he found fast-food stalls every few metres, a sleeping room and showers, security staff who threw out anyone drunk ("no alcohol allowed"), and a network with a server connected to the internet, which he used to send his report home. PCs outnumbered Amigas, and *[Doom](/vault/games/doom)* was everywhere. On the second day a 3.5-inch disk-throwing contest was won with a throw of 110 metres. *MCmicrocomputer* reported that the prizes ran from $600 for the winner of the four-channel music competition to $5,000 for the winner of the PC demo competition, which went to the French group Nooon with *Stars*.

Assembly also became a place to show products that were not demos. At Assembly '97 [Remedy Entertainment](/vault/companies/remedy-entertainment), founded by members of Future Crew, premiered *Final Reality*, a 3D benchmark program, and the Pyramid3D graphics card that Future Crew's coders had been working on was shown there too.

## Gamers and sceners

By the late 1990s many visitors came to play, not to make demos. In March 1997 Thies Edeling of the online magazine *RAW* listed the big parties for *Amiga Computing*: [The Gathering](/vault/events/the-gathering) in Norway, where he had heard the visitors were mostly PC gamers, then "of course there is Assembly in Finland, every year at the beginning of August", and [The Party](/vault/events/the-party) in Denmark. In October 1998 the Danish magazine *Amiga Advis* counted Assembly among "the big three", with The Party and The Gathering, each with more than 3,000 visitors, and complained that all three now drew every nerd who could play *[Quake](/vault/games/quake)*. In 2000 the same magazine's reporter at [Mekka & Symposium](/vault/events/mekka-symposium) in Germany contrasted that sceners' party with The Party, The Gathering and Assembly, which he found overrun with *Quake* players.

From 1999 to 2013 the summer party was held in the Hartwall Areena, a sports arena in Helsinki. The demo competitions carried on alongside the gaming. At Assembly 2000, from 3 to 6 August, Exceed's *Spot* won the demo competition with 8,154 points to 3,621 for Haujobb's *Art*. At Assembly 2002 the composer [Rob Hubbard](/vault/people/rob-hubbard) spoke at a seminar about his years writing Commodore 64 music. At Assembly 2006 an Amiga demo, *[Starstruck](/vault/demos/starstruck)* by [The Black Lotus](/vault/groups/the-black-lotus), won the main demo competition against the PC entries with 10,891 points to 7,330 for [Fairlight](/vault/groups/fairlight)'s *track one*; its makers later wrote that it was shown in the [WinUAE](/vault/emulators/winuae) emulator. In 2007 [RGBA](/vault/groups/rgba)'s *Stiletto* came second in the 4K intro competition.

## Winter events, Telia and the present

In 2007 Assembly added a winter event, first held at Pirkkahalli in Tampere, and in 2014 the summer party moved back to the Helsinki exhibition centre. The organisers write that gaming became so central that it grew into this second event. In 2018 Telia became the majority shareholder of Assembly Organizing, with an eye on esports and gaming, and the company went on to produce esports leagues and gaming programmes for online video and television.

During the COVID-19 pandemic the party went online: Assembly Online 2020 ran from 14 to 18 October 2020. In 2020 Finland also added the demoscene to its national inventory of living heritage; see [demo scene](/vault/communities/demo-scene#heritage). The summer party returned to Helsinki afterwards. At Assembly Summer 2024 the Future Crew name reappeared on *Superselection*, made with Byterapers and Doomsday, which came second in the demo competition. Assembly Summer 2026 ran from 30 July to 2 August 2026, and its results list competitions from four-kilobyte intros and oldskool demos to game development and "Coolest Computer Place". The demo competition went to *Before Zero* by Byterapers and friends.
