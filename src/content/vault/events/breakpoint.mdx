Breakpoint brought unfinished programs, musicians, artists and their audience to the same place for an Easter weekend. A demo could be completed at a table in the hall and shown later on the big screen. Its official first-time visitor guide described a gathering built around making and sharing computer art, with competitions at the centre of the weekend.

The first event’s results identify Bingen, Germany, and 18–21 April 2003. The last ran on 2–5 April 2010. The organisers called that eighth meeting the final Breakpoint, explaining that the same team had spent years preparing, running and clearing up after the party. ([2003 results](https://files.scene.org/get/parties/2003/breakpoint03/results.txt); [2010 announcement](https://breakpoint.untergrund.net/).)

## Different machines, different competitions

A [demoparty](/vault/communities/demo-parties) supplies an audience and a deadline, but its results need to be read by category. Breakpoint’s 2003 file separates Amiga, C64 and PC work, and distinguishes full demos from small executable intros, graphics and music. A size-limited intro has to fit its program and data into a stated allowance; a full demo answers different constraints.

Three later winners show the range:

- **[Ocean Machine](/vault/demos/ocean-machine)**, by [The Black Lotus](/vault/groups/the-black-lotus), won the **Amiga demo** competition in 2005 with 1,021 points.
- **[Debris](/vault/demos/debris)**, by [Farbrausch](/vault/groups/farbrausch), won the **PC demo** competition in 2007 with 1,474 points.
- **[Elevated](/vault/demos/elevated)**, by [RGBA](/vault/groups/rgba) and TBC, won the **PC 4K intro** competition in 2009 with 1,137 points.

Those are category results, not three examples of a single overall championship. The original results files preserve the distinction. In 2010, even [Crest](/vault/groups/crest)’s C64 production *BluREU* appears under **Console/Real Wild**, explicitly labelled C64+REU: its RAM expansion mattered to its placement.

## The people around the screen

The organisers’ visitor guide explains how entrants handed in work before a deadline so it could be checked and prepared for presentation. Visitors voted using keys issued at the party. The result was a public showing with an audience that could respond to the work, followed by a recorded ranking. ([Original guide](https://breakpoint.untergrund.net/newvisitors.php).)

The social part needed space too. In his contemporary *Hugi* report, KeyJ of Kakiarts described the final event’s crowded hall, outdoor tents and conversations with other visitors. His report is a participant’s account, distinct from an organiser’s invitation or a results table. It records what the weekend felt like from one place within it.

## Ending the series

The final invitation explained that the organisers wanted a chance to attend a party as visitors. They offered to help a new team, recalling the assistance Breakpoint itself had received after [Mekka & Symposium](/vault/events/mekka-symposium) ended. [Revision](/vault/events/revision-party) subsequently became another German Easter meeting; it has its own history and identity.
