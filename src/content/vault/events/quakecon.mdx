QuakeCon began in August 1996 as a weekend of *[Quake](/vault/games/quake)* in a Texas hotel, organised by players who had met online, two months after the game came out. It happened to be near [id Software](/vault/companies/id-software)'s offices in Mesquite, just outside Dallas, so they asked id to help. As id's chief executive Todd Hollenshead told the story in 2002, "John [Carmack] felt sorry for these guys, who gathered here on their own and didn't quite have enough to cover the hotel, so he gave them a few bucks and took the time to speak. From then on, we took on the responsibility of putting it on."

What id then built was unusual: a free event, paid for by the developer and its sponsors, where more than a thousand people carried their own PCs into a hall and plugged them into one network. By 2002 *Computer Gaming World* was calling it "easily the largest LAN party in the world", and John Carmack's annual talk about the technology behind id's games had become as much a draw as the tournaments.

## Fast facts

- **First held:** August 1996, at a hotel in Garland, Texas.
- **Run by:** volunteers, with [id Software](/vault/companies/id-software) paying the bills and taking on more of the organising in the 2000s; now by id's owner, ZeniMax Media.
- **Where:** the Dallas area every year: hotels in and around Dallas in the early years, the Mesquite Convention Center from 1999 to 2002, and since then hotels and convention centres in Dallas and nearby Grapevine.
- **Admission:** free in the 1990s and 2000s; the bring-your-own-computer (BYOC) hall is now a paid, ticketed event.
- **Size:** about 3,000 registrants and 1,250 BYOC seats in 2002; 5,570 attendees in 2004.
- **Held:** every August; online only from 2020 to 2022, during the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Fans in a hotel

The first gatherings were small and informal. *Retro Gamer* later wrote that QuakeCon "came from gamers and modders joining forces" out of the Quake and Doom fan sites, and that it is hard to pinpoint exactly where it started. *Computer Gaming World* described a small group of players meeting for "a little weekend fragging" at a Best Western in Garland in August 1996; members of id and the add-on developer Hipnotic turned up to play deathmatch alongside the fans.

In July 1997 the magazine announced "the second annual Quake Convention" in Dallas and expected players from as far away as Australia. John Romero, who had left id the year before, and his new company Ion Storm were among the sponsors. That winter Micron advertised that its PCs had run the QuakeCon '97 tournament, quoting the chief organiser, Jim "H2H" Elson, and naming the winner, Dan "RIX" Hammans.

## id takes over

From 1999 to 2002 the event filled the Mesquite Convention Center, a short drive from id's offices. It was now a showcase as well as a party. *Quake III Arena* was the first game previewed at QuakeCon for the fans, in 1998, and in 1999 *Computer Gaming World* reported that QuakeCon 99, "sponsored by id", would be showing the game from 6 to 8 August.

Prize money arrived with the sponsors. The 2001 *Quake III* tournament had a $50,000 purse, the first cash prize at the show, according to *IGN Unplugged*, and its winner, John "zeRoq" Hill, took home thirty thousand dollars from NVIDIA. That year 3,000 gamers came, and *Computer Gaming World* described QuakeCon as having become "a mini-E3" for the Dallas shooter community. The first day brought a power cut when the hired generator failed, and id had to bring in a larger one.

*Computer Gaming World* went behind the scenes in 2002, and its figures show the scale of the job:

- around 3,000 registrants, mostly in their late teens to mid-twenties;
- 1,250 confirmed seats in the BYOC hall, and 1,700 people on the waiting list;
- 150 volunteers, 8 servers and four miles of cable;
- a cost to id of more than $100,000, with admission, parking and a barbecue lunch all free.

Marty Stratton, id's director of business development, started lining up sponsors in March and finalised the details the day after E3 ended. ATI, whose Radeon 9700 had run the *Doom 3* demonstration at E3 that year, put up $100,000 for the tournaments and took preorders for the card on the show floor. Stratton told *IGN Unplugged* why it was worth it: "It's only 3,000 people, but it's also the most vocal and most active users out there." The hall, he admitted, was "maxed-out and busting at the seams", and it was the first year every BYOC place had been taken.

## Carmack's keynote

For many attendees the event's highlight was hearing [John Carmack](/vault/people/john-carmack) speak. *IGN Unplugged* reported in 2002 that he had made the talk an annual event, and that it drew nearly everybody away from their computers: "his speech spirals into levels of technology that I'm almost positive that most people in the room don't completely understand but love to listen to nonetheless." A question-and-answer session followed, in which fans pressed him on unannounced games while Activision's PR staff winced. In 2002 the keynote dwelt less on technology and more on *Doom 3* itself, and attendees were given tickets to a theatre showing the demo from E3. Carmack's talks continued every year until 2013, the year he left id.

## Growth and new owners

QuakeCon moved to larger venues as it grew. In 2004, *Computer Gaming World* reported, 5,570 PC gamers came to Dallas, some from Europe, to compete for a $25,000 first prize or just to bring their computers to the BYOC hall. The tenth anniversary event, in August 2005, was held at the Gaylord Texan Resort and Convention Center in Grapevine, and id was describing it as "four days of peace, love, and rockets". Hardware companies used it to launch products: Dell unveiled its XPS 600 gaming PCs there in 2005, and NVIDIA its GeForce 7800 GT graphics card. In 2006 the NVIDIA-sponsored tournaments paid out $100,000 in total, and *Edge* described an event "long organised by its people, for its people".

In June 2009 ZeniMax Media, the owner of Bethesda Softworks, bought id Software, and QuakeCon became a showcase for ZeniMax's other studios as well as id's. In July 2019 QuakeCon went abroad for the first time, to London. In September 2020 Microsoft agreed to buy ZeniMax for $7.5 billion.

The 25th QuakeCon, planned for August 2020, was cancelled in March because of the COVID-19 pandemic. It ran online that year and in 2021 and 2022, and returned to Texas in 2023. QuakeCon 2026 took place from 6 to 9 August at the Gaylord Texan in Grapevine, thirty years after the first one.
