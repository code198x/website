In April 1999 the German magazine *Amiga Fever* looked back ten years, to when scene meetings in Venlo had been for insiders only. Now, it complained, a big party such as The Party in Denmark was open to anyone, and so full of kids who came only to play 3D shooters on the network that the real coders, artists and musicians were almost lost in the flood. Mekka & Symposium promised something else. For six Easters, from 1997 to 2002, it filled the Heidmarkhalle in Fallingbostel, a small town between Hamburg and Hanover, with Amiga, PC and Commodore 64 sceners, and its organisers went as far as banning *[Quake](/vault/games/quake)* to keep it that way. A Danish reporter called the 1997 party the best-organised demo party ever.

## Fast facts

- **Held:** every Easter from 1997 to 2002, from Good Friday to Easter Monday.
- **Where:** the Heidmarkhalle, Fallingbostel, Lower Saxony, Germany.
- **Before it:** two separate parties at Easter 1996, the PC party Mekka and the all-format Symposium, both at Burg Seevetal near Hamburg.
- **Size:** almost 1,000 visitors in 1998, according to *Amiga Fever*; about 1,200 in 2000, according to *Amiga Advis*.
- **After it:** Breakpoint (2003–2010) held the German Easter party next, then [Revision](/vault/events/revision-party) from 2011.

## Two parties in 1996

The name joins two parties. Mekka '96, "organized by amable", was a PC party, held from 29 to 31 March 1996 at Burg Seevetal in Hittfeld, south of Hamburg; Demozoo records about 300 visitors, and its results were signed by Ambient Move and Landru. Fatal Justice's *Blasphemy* won the demo competition.

A week later, over Easter weekend from 5 to 8 April, the same venue hosted Symposium '96. Its invitation explains that three German organising teams had joined forces for it: the people behind the 680XX-Convention of 1993, the Black Box Symposium of 1995 and the Fried Bits parties. They promised a party for "all the platforms which are important in the guild of the demomakers", the Amiga, the PC, the C64, the Acorn Archimedes and the Atari ST and Falcon, for 35 marks at the door, and they were clear about what they did not want: "we don't want to rent huge congress centres... we don't want to make money." Among the groups involved were Polka Brothers, Lego and Phantasm on the Amiga, Cubic Team on the PC, Avena and Absence on the Atari and [Oxyron](/vault/groups/oxyron) on the C64. Artwork's *The Gate* won the Amiga demo competition.

In 1997 the two became one party, Mekka & Symposium, and moved to Fallingbostel.

## The first Mekka & Symposium

Mekka & Symposium '97 ran from 28 to 31 March 1997. Martin Halberg of the Danish magazine *Amiga Advis* wrote that many people had been badly disappointed by [The Party](/vault/events/the-party) in Denmark the previous December: it had grown big and commercial, and hundreds of non-sceners with their network games spoiled the atmosphere. Mekka & Symposium was different: "you quickly noticed that the organisers were active in the scene themselves". It was, he thought, the first party that could boast of having no delays at all, because the organisers had recorded every entry on VHS tape in advance and only had to press play. The price was the picture and sound quality. His verdict: "without doubt the best-organised demo party ever", with the demo scene at the top of the list of priorities.

The results file lists competitions for the Amiga, the PC and the C64, plus graphics, music, ANSI art, a 32K game competition and a fast intro competition. Artwork's *Megademo IV* won the Amiga demo competition by six points, 258 to 252 for Arsenic's *Fear Factory*. Deathstar's *Claudia* won the PC demo competition and Smash Designs' *Project Pitchfork* the C64 one. Sanction's *Omniscent* won the PC 4K intro competition with 592 points, more than twice the runner-up.

## Fallingbostel at Easter

The party kept its shape for five more years, each with its own motto: "we are family" in 1998, "MS2k-1" in 1999, "expect nothing" in 2000, "demo scene olympics" in 2001 and "defenders of the scene" in 2002.

*Amiga Fever*'s preview of the 1999 party gives the practical details. Entry cost 60 marks. The hall at An der Heidmarkhalle 1 had room for 1,200 visitors and their equipment, with a separate sleeping hall, and the competitions were shown on a 6-metre by 4-metre screen with a proper sound system. The magazine estimated that perhaps 40 per cent of visitors would bring a PC and the rest mostly Amigas, which made it "an absolute Amiga event". There was a network with an internet connection, and voting could be done online. The 1999 party also held a competition for demos on the Sony [PlayStation](/vault/systems/sony-playstation), won by Haujobb's *Orgonakkumulator*.

In 2000 *Amiga Advis* sent its reporter Dreyer on one of two coaches from Copenhagen and Aalborg; nearly 80 Danes came, about 60 of them by coach. He put the crowd at about 1,200 sceners and found that, unlike The Party, [The Gathering](/vault/events/the-gathering) and [Assembly](/vault/events/assembly-party), Symposium was attended mainly by people who made demos. One way the organisers kept the network-game players away was to ban *Quake*. Another was to offer a network that worked but was nothing special. The German Commodore 64 magazine *GO64!* reported the same party: a SID-Station synthesiser demonstration, a concert by the band Welle:Erdball, who played two pieces entirely on a Commodore SX-64, and prizes that included flight tickets. The Danish group Loonies won the Amiga demo competition with *Cybercinematastic*, and [Crest](/vault/groups/crest) and Oxyron's *[Deus Ex Machina](/vault/demos/deus-ex-machina)* won the C64 demo competition with 362 points to 284 for Plush's *+H2K*.

Not everyone thought the big parties were getting better. In September 1999 *GO64!* wrote that the bigger parties, The Party and Mekka & Symposium among them, were losing popularity because of rising entrance fees and commercialism, and that many sceners now preferred small meetings.

## Breakpoint and Revision

The 2002 party was the last. At Easter 2003 a new party, Breakpoint, was held in Bingen am Rhein, and The Black Lotus won its Amiga demo competition with *Magia*. In November 2004 the German magazine *Amiga Insider* called it the Breakpoint demo party, "ehemals Mekka & Symposium" (formerly Mekka & Symposium), and the one place each Easter where Amiga productions still arrived in a flood. Breakpoint ran until 2010. The team that started Revision in Saarbrücken in 2011 included former organisers of Mekka & Symposium as well as of Breakpoint.
