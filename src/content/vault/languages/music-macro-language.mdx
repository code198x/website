A music program need not present a grid of notes. **Music Macro Language**, or **MML**, writes a score as text: note letters, durations and commands for settings such as octave and tempo. Another part of the system interprets or compiles that description and arranges the sound operations at the right times.

The distinction is between the score and the player. MML text does not itself generate an electrical waveform. A sound driver and the machine’s audio hardware do that work.

## One documented dialect: MSX PLAY

Sony’s *MSX-BASIC Programming Reference Manual* describes music strings for the `PLAY` statement. Letters identify notes; commands including `O`, `L` and `T` set octave, default note length and tempo. Variables can hold strings, allowing a phrase to be reused rather than typed into every occurrence.

`PLAY` can take up to three strings for simultaneous parts. That fits the [AY sound generator](/vault/hardware/ay-3-8910) used in the MSX system. The strings describe musical intentions; BASIC’s music machinery schedules the corresponding work. The manual’s examples also distinguish a musical rest from the length of the note before it.

## The tool can change with its composer

[Yuzo Koshiro](/vault/people/yuzo-koshiro) described using a PC-88 and a heavily modified MML-derived language for his work. He called his system Music Love. His account connects the notation to a composer’s own tools; it does not imply that an MSX `PLAY` string can be sent unchanged to a Mega Drive sound chip.

Different MML implementations add different instruments, effects and control commands. A score needs the correct language rules and a compatible player. Its destination might use a programmable tone generator, FM synthesis or another arrangement.

The contrast with [tracker music](/vault/culture/tracker-music) is a useful one: a tracker exposes rows, channels and effect columns visually, while MML makes similar sequencing decisions through text. Both still depend on an implementation that turns musical events into timed updates. The [sound drivers](/vault/techniques/sound-drivers) entry explains that next layer.
