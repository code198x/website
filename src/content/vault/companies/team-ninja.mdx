By the mid-1990s [Tecmo](/vault/companies/tecmo), the company behind *Bomb Jack* and *Ninja Gaiden*, was struggling. *Wireframe*'s history of the company says it "had begun to flag financially", and that its recovery came from a new design group headed by [Tomonobu Itagaki](/vault/people/tomonobu-itagaki), "handed the task of making a rival to Sega's *Virtua Fighter*". The group called itself Team Ninja. Its *[Dead or Alive](/vault/games/dead-or-alive)* "helped pull Tecmo back from the financial brink". Ten years later the same team brought back Ryu Hayabusa in a *[Ninja Gaiden](/vault/games/ninja-gaiden)* so hard that *Edge* warned many players would meet "an impassable difficulty spike" at the first boss. Team Ninja carried on after Itagaki left in 2008, and after Tecmo itself was absorbed into Koei Tecmo in 2010.

## Fast facts

- **Formed:** 1995, inside [Tecmo](/vault/companies/tecmo), Tokyo.
- **Led by:** [Tomonobu Itagaki](/vault/people/tomonobu-itagaki) until 2008; then Yosuke Hayashi.
- **Known for:** *[Dead or Alive](/vault/games/dead-or-alive)* (1996 onwards), *[Ninja Gaiden](/vault/games/ninja-gaiden)* (2004 onwards), *Metroid: Other M* (2010, with Nintendo), *Nioh* (2017).
- **Owner since 2010:** Koei Tecmo, after Koei absorbed Tecmo.

## Dead or Alive

*Dead or Alive* reached the arcades in November 1996 on Sega's own [Model 2](/vault/hardware/model-2) board, which Sega had licensed to Tecmo. Itagaki replaced the usual guard button with a Hold button that caught an incoming attack and turned it into a counter, because, as *Computer and Video Games* reported, he wanted "to recreate the thrill of a Kung-Fu movie, where you see fighters repeatedly countering hundreds of moves". *Edge* gave the Saturn version eight out of ten in 1997, and the PlayStation version followed in 1998.

The series followed Sega's hardware and then Microsoft's. *Dead or Alive 2* ran on Sega's [NAOMI](/vault/systems/sega-naomi) board and the [Dreamcast](/vault/systems/sega-dreamcast), and was later ported to the PlayStation 2. *Dead or Alive 3* was one of the [Xbox](/vault/systems/microsoft-xbox)'s launch games in America in November 2001. Itagaki told the *Official Xbox Magazine* that this time "we really wanted to make it easier and more accessible to casual players", and the magazine found that "Itagaki's claim that it was both easy and deep holds true". *Dead or Alive 4* followed on the Xbox 360.

The fighting was only part of how the series was seen. Magazines remarked on the women fighters' figures from the start, and *Dead or Alive Xtreme Beach Volleyball* (2003) took them on a bikini holiday. *Edge*'s review asked whether it was "intended to titillate, and does it objectify women in a potentially damaging way?" The [Dead or Alive](/vault/games/dead-or-alive) entry follows that criticism through the series.

## Ninja Gaiden

Team Ninja started work on a new *Ninja Gaiden* in 1999, at first on the NAOMI board. Itagaki told *Edge* in 2002 that it was "made very much for the west. It is quite hard, with many functions and a higher level of difficulty." It came out on the Xbox in 2004 and *Edge* gave it nine out of ten. *Ninja Gaiden 2*, published by Microsoft as an Xbox 360 exclusive, followed in 2008. Yosuke Hayashi, who had worked on the 2004 game, directed *Ninja Gaiden: Dragon Sword* for the Nintendo DS and *Ninja Gaiden Sigma*, the PlayStation 3 remake of 2007.

## After Itagaki

Itagaki left Tecmo in 2008 and sued it for unpaid bonuses connected with *Dead or Alive 4*; the two sides settled in 2010, and Itagaki went on to head development at a new company, Valhalla Game Studios. He died in October 2025. Hayashi took charge of Team Ninja. *Edge* reported in 2014 that he then led development on *Metroid: Other M*, *Ninja Gaiden 3* and *Dead or Alive 5*.

*Metroid: Other M* (2010) was a collaboration with Nintendo for the Wii on its *[Metroid](/vault/games/metroid)* series. *Edge* noted the change of direction: after the first-person *Metroid Prime* games, production had passed to Team Ninja and the series had become "a 2.5D shooting brawler". Its review asked "which Team Ninja is going to turn up? The fine-tuning master of virtual combat, the crummy level designer or the clown behind lascivious minigame collections?", and answered that it was the first: Samus could now dodge an attack at the last moment and charge her shots faster for it.

*Ninja Gaiden 3* (2012), the series' "first Itagaki-free series entry" in *Edge*'s words, went the other way. It tried to widen the audience and lost the old one. Hayashi told *Edge* in 2014: "We knew that we should try to get new fans into the series who hadn't played it before, so we tried some different takes on previous *Ninja Gaiden* action ... I think we were not able to give the core fans the experience they expected." The revised *Ninja Gaiden 3: Razor's Edge* put those lessons back in. *Dead or Alive 5* (2012) had stages that broke apart during fights, and Hayashi said its women would be drawn more realistically than the earlier games' "pin-up images".

## Nioh and later

*Nioh* had been announced by Koei in 2005, before Koei and Tecmo merged. After the merger Team Ninja was brought in to help, and later given the go-ahead to lead it. By then, *Wireframe* noted in its history of the game, the challenging action-RPGs of [FromSoftware](/vault/companies/fromsoft)'s *Souls* series had become mainstream, and the game, co-directed by Fumihiko Yasuda, was built in their shadow. *Nioh* came out on the PlayStation 4 in 2017, a samurai action game set in Japan's Sengoku period, and *Nioh 2* followed in 2020.

The studio also took on other companies' characters. *Marvel Ultimate Alliance 3* (2019), published by Nintendo for the Switch, drew 68% from *Wireframe*, which found that "new developer Team Ninja does well to replicate the button-mashing gratification of prior entries". *Dead or Alive 6* (2019) scored 62% in the same magazine. *Wo Long: Fallen Dynasty*, an action-RPG in the vein of *Nioh*, was on *Wireframe*'s list of games to watch at the end of 2022. *Ninja Gaiden 4*, made with PlatinumGames, was released on 21 October 2025.
