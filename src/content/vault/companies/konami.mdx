## Overview
Konami began in 1969 as a jukebox rental and repair business in Osaka, Japan. By the early 1980s, they had pivoted to arcade games and never looked back. Their output—*Frogger*, *Scramble*, *Gradius*, *Castlevania*, *Contra*, *Metal Gear*—defined genres and spawned gaming's most recognisable secret code.

## Fast facts
- **Founded:** 1969 in Osaka by Kagemasa Kozuki.
- **Name origin:** disputed, but possibly derived from the founders' names.
- **Arcade breakthrough:** *Scramble* (1981) pioneered the side-scrolling shooter; *Frogger* (1981) became a cultural phenomenon.
- **Console dominance:** NES-era classics like *Castlevania* (1986) and *Contra* (1987) cemented their reputation.
- **The Code:** ↑↑↓↓←→←→BA—first used in *Gradius* (1986), immortalised in *Contra* for granting 30 lives.

## The Konami Code
Programmer Kazuhisa Hashimoto added the code to the NES port of *Gradius* because he found the game too difficult to test. It was never removed. When *Contra* shipped with the same code granting 30 extra lives, players spread the secret through playgrounds and magazines. The sequence has since appeared in hundreds of games, websites, and even Netflix.

## Home computer ports
Konami's arcade hits were ported extensively to home platforms:
- **Frogger:** Parker Brothers handled the C64 and Atari versions; other publishers covered the Spectrum and Amstrad.
- **Nemesis/Gradius:** Spectrum and Amstrad ports captured the side-scrolling shooter formula.
- **Licensed vs. in-house:** quality varied wildly depending on who handled the conversion.

## Why it mattered
- **Genre definition:** *Scramble* and *Gradius* established the scrolling shooter template. *Castlevania* refined action-platformers. *Metal Gear* pioneered stealth.
- **Cross-platform reach:** Konami's games appeared on nearly every system of the 80s and 90s, from arcade cabinets to home computers to consoles.
- **Cultural impact:** the Konami Code transcended gaming, becoming a symbol of hidden secrets and insider knowledge.
