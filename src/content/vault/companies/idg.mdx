**IDG** — International Data Group — bought the British magazine titles of
[Europress](/vault/companies/europress) at the end of **1994**. *[Amiga
Computing](/vault/magazines/amiga-computing)* announced it in its own news pages
that Christmas, under the heading **"AC joins IDG"**, and reported that ten
other magazines went with it.

The reason given is worth quoting because it states the shape of the deal from
the buyer's side. **Derek Meakin**, described as the founder of the Europress
Group, said:

> "IDG is pre-eminent all round the world, with more than **220 publications in
> 64 countries**. One significant gap in this global network has been the UK.
> With the acquisition of the Europress titles, IDG now has a core business with
> which it can expand to become the major player here as it already is in so
> many other countries."

Britain was the hole in the map, and eleven magazines were the patch.

## Nothing moved

The Christmas 1994 colophon is already IDG's — "Published by IDG Publications",
"©1994 IDG Publications Ltd", "a key member of the IDG communications group" —
at the same **Adlington Park, Macclesfield SK10 4NP** address, the same
telephone number, the same staff list, the same printer in Maidstone. By 1995
the imprint is styled **IDG Media Ltd**. The magazines carried on from where they
already were.

This is the second time a title in this Vault changes owner without changing
anything else about itself. Europress had done the same in 1991, running the
Newsfield magazines from Ludlow rather than bringing them to Cheshire, and the
Italian editions of *Zzap!* and *The Games Machine* changed publisher in
September 1991 with the printer, distributor and registration untouched. What
the colophon records in each case is a change of owner, not a change of
magazine.

## What it means for these entries

*Amiga Computing* ran until **October 1997** and *[Amiga
Action](/vault/magazines/amiga-action)* until **June 1995**. Their last years
belong to IDG, not to Europress — a distinction easy to lose, because the
address, the staff and the look of the page all stayed put.

⚠ This entry covers IDG only where it touches the titles this library holds.
The company was large, American, and long-established before any of this, and
none of that is described here. The count of eleven magazines is the figure
*Amiga Computing* printed; this library does not hold enough Europress titles to
check it.
