In November 1985 *Computer Gamer* reported that [Mastertronic](/vault/companies/mastertronic), the company that had made £1.99 the price of a cheap game, had "launched a new imprint called Mastertronic Added Dimension or MAD games for short". The idea, the magazine explained, was to sell "top quality games at a higher price" while the £1.99 range carried on. The higher price was £2.99. Within weeks MAD had released *[Spellbound](/vault/games/spellbound)*, and *CRASH* told its readers that "for £2.99 you'd be MAD not to buy it".

## Fast facts

- **Launched:** late 1985, as a second label inside [Mastertronic](/vault/companies/mastertronic).
- **Price:** £2.99 on cassette, against the parent label's £1.99.
- **Best-known games:** David Jones's Magic Knight games *Spellbound*, *Knight Tyme* and *Stormbringer*; Binary Design's *Amaurote*; Jeff Minter's *Revenge of the Mutant Camels II*.
- **Best *CRASH* scores:** *Spellbound* 95%, *Knight Tyme* 94%, *Amaurote* 92%.
- **Later life:** in 1988 Mastertronic used the MAD name for its 16-bit games, at £9.95.

## A pound more

*Computer Gamer* said the first range would be seven games "written by existing Mastertronic authors, such as the team that wrote *Chiller* and the authors of *Stunt Biker* and *Finders Keepers*". The author of *Finders Keepers* was [David Jones](/vault/people/david-jones-magic-knight), and his next game was the label's first hit. *Spellbound* came out for Christmas 1985, "part of the new Mad Games range", as *CRASH* put it, and took 95% and a Smash. It looked like a platform game until the player pressed fire and a window of commands opened over it, through which Magic Knight could examine objects, cast spells and give orders to the other characters trapped in the castle with him.

The extra pound was the point. Looking back in 2006, *Retro Gamer* reported that *Spellbound* sold fewer than half as many copies as *Finders Keepers*, but that the higher price still made it "a considerable success".

Jones wrote two more Magic Knight games for the label. *Knight Tyme* (1986), written first for the new 128K Spectrum, scored 94% in *CRASH*, and *Stormbringer* (1987) 86%. *Your Sinclair* called *Stormbringer* "the fourth 'Windimation' arcade adventure from Mastertronic Added Dimension Games". *CRASH* readers kept the three MAD games in their adventure chart for years: in September 1987 *Spellbound* was number 1, *Knight Tyme* number 3 and *Stormbringer* number 6.

## What else £2.99 bought

MAD was not only Magic Knight. In May 1987 *CRASH* reviewed *Amaurote*, an isometric game by Binary Design, credited to "Producer: MAD" at £2.99, and gave it 92%; one reviewer "almost fell off my chair" on hearing that it was a budget game. In October 1987 *ZZAP!64* reviewed *Revenge of the Mutant Camels II*, "the third chapter of the Camels series" by [Jeff Minter](/vault/people/jeff-minter), at £2.99 under the MAD name, and ended its verdict: "Minter has certainly come up with the goods again and at a price that everyone can afford."

The label sold sports games too. *ZZAP!64* reviewed the darts game *180* from "Mastertronic Added Dimension, £2.99" in December 1986, and *Your Computer*, reviewing the Amstrad *Five-a-side Soccer*, said that "MAD Games, otherwise known as Mastertronic Added Dimension, has managed to maintain a surprisingly high quality for the products released".

Not every MAD game earned the higher price. *ZZAP!64* gave the *Star Wars* licence *Droids*, a MAD release at £2.99, 36% in August 1988. In 1987 and 1988 *CRASH* reviewed a run of Spectrum MAD games, among them *Plexar*, *Motos* (licensed from a Namco arcade game), *Flash Gordon*, *U.C.M.* and *Grand Prix Tennis*, and gave the last of these 67%. The label also reissued older games: a 1989 mail-order list in *CRASH* has *Manic Miner* on MAD at £2.99.

## Inside Mastertronic

MAD was a brand, not a separate company. By 1987 Mastertronic also sold games as Bulldog, Entertainment USA and Ricochet, and when it bought [Melbourne House](/vault/companies/melbourne-house) that year, *CRASH* began calling MAD "Melbourne House's budget sister". The magazine's review of 1987 picked out the label: Mastertronic "had come a long way since the days when their budget range was really cheap rubbish. But with labels like M.A.D. and Bulldog they were often putting out quality games at still very low prices."

In October 1988 *ST Action* reported that MAD was "what Mastertronic have decided to call their 16-bit software label". Its review of the sports game *Vectorball*, at £9.95, asked what "this added dimension that the ex-budget boys profess to put in their software" was. The magazines say little about the label after that; Mastertronic itself became Virgin Mastertronic in 1988.

### Two David Joneses

The Magic Knight author is not the [Dave Jones](/vault/people/dave-jones) who founded DMA Design in Dundee and made *Lemmings*. The confusion is old. In December 1986 *Your Sinclair* gave away Magic Knight T-shirts from "David 'Spellbound/Knight Tyme' Jones" in a competition called "Will The Real David Jones Please Stand Up", which asked readers which David Jones was "now a Thin White Duke".
