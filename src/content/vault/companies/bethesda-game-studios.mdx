"Oblivion was simply vast; Skyrim feels epic," *Edge* wrote in the summer of 2011, standing at the foot of a mountain with 7,000 steps up it. Bethesda Game Studios is the team that built both. It is the development arm of [Bethesda Softworks](/vault/companies/bethesda), the publisher Christopher Weaver founded in Maryland in 1986, and since the early 2000s it has made one kind of game above all: a huge, hand-built world that the player can wander for as long as they like, sold with the tools to change it. Its games have been praised for that freedom and criticised, almost every time, for their bugs.

## Fast facts

- **Parent:** Bethesda Softworks, part of ZeniMax Media from 1999 and of [Microsoft](/vault/companies/microsoft) since 2021.
- **Leader:** [Todd Howard](/vault/people/todd-howard), project leader on *Morrowind* and executive producer of the studio's games since.
- **Games:** *The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion* (2006), *Fallout 3* (2008), *The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim* (2011), *Fallout 4* (2015), *Fallout 76* (2018), *Starfield* (2023).
- **Offices:** Rockville, Maryland, with studios in Austin and Dallas, Texas, from 2018, and in Montreal.

## From Bethesda Softworks to Bethesda Game Studios

The studio grew out of Bethesda Softworks' own development team. That team made *[The Elder Scrolls III: Morrowind](/vault/games/morrowind)*, which came out in 2002 credited to Bethesda Softworks as both developer and publisher, with Todd Howard as project leader. Its world of Vvardenfell was built entirely by hand, and it shipped with the Construction Set, the editor the team had used to make it, so that players could make their own changes. *Computer Gaming World* noticed in its review that mods "have already started to appear".

When the separate name came in is not clear from published sources. Wikipedia and the fan wiki UESP both date the studio to 2001, but the period press credited Bethesda Softworks as developer of *Morrowind* and, as late as November 2005, of *Oblivion*. By 2005 "Bethesda Game Studios" was a trademark in ZeniMax's advertising, and by the time of *Skyrim* in 2011 magazines listed it as the developer.

## Oblivion and Fallout 3

*The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion* came out in 2006 for the PC and the Xbox 360. *Edge*, looking back in 2011, called it "primarily a game of mechanisms": its central plot "left many cold" and its voice acting and animation could be stiff, but the systems underneath kept players busy. Before release, Howard, as executive producer, told *Electronic Gaming Monthly* that "a lot of players were lost and confused" in *Morrowind*: "With Oblivion, we're still offering a massive world, but we're going to hold players' hands a little more." He also looked forward to selling extras through Xbox Live: "One of the first mods we're offering is horse armor."

The studio then took on another company's series. Bethesda won the right to make *Fallout 3* in 2004, after Interplay's Black Isle Studios had closed, and bought the *[Fallout](/vault/games/fallout)* series outright in 2007. Fans had worried it would become "*Morrowind* with guns"; Howard promised it would keep the dark humour of the first two games. *Fallout 3* came out in 2008, and *Edge* later picked its town of Megaton, built of scrap metal around an unexploded nuclear bomb, as an example of how much a game world could pack into a small space.

## Skyrim

*The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim* came out on 11 November 2011 for the PC, Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3. *Edge* gave it nine out of ten and summed up the bargain Bethesda's games strike with players: the developer builds "worlds of unrivalled scope, rich in lore and history", and "we do all we can to ignore the rough edges that come, apparently, as the cost of such ambition." *Wireframe* later called *Skyrim* the studio's most successful release, re-released "on every format under the sun".

Its players kept building. By 2013 the Nexus, a fan site for game modifications, hosted more than 28,000 mods for *Skyrim* alone, and *Edge* reported that the network served over five million members.

## Fallout 4 to Starfield

*Fallout 4* followed in 2015 and the online *Fallout 76* in 2018, whose launch *Wireframe* covered for its "spectacular" bugs. The studio grew: in 2018 ZeniMax's BattleCry Studios in Austin became Bethesda Game Studios Austin, and Escalation Studios became Bethesda Game Studios Dallas, which worked on *Fallout 76* and *Starfield*.

In September 2020 Microsoft agreed to buy ZeniMax for $7.5 billion, and the purchase was completed in March 2021. *Starfield*, Bethesda's first new intellectual property in more than 25 years, came out in September 2023 for Windows and the Xbox Series X and S, and reached the PlayStation 5 on 7 April 2026.
