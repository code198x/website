## Overview
Graftgold is the development studio behind a striking share of the best-regarded
British games of the 1980s — *Paradroid*, *Uridium*, *Quazatron*, *Avalon*,
*Ranarama* — and it is worth an entry separate from its two famous programmers
because it is a **different kind of company** from most in this Vault.

It did not publish. It made games under contract, put its own name on the team
rather than on the label, and survived the collapse of more than one publisher
it worked for.

## How it started
[Steve Turner](/vault/people/steve-turner) quit his job as a commercial
programmer in **1983** to write Spectrum games full time, trading as **ST
Software**. He hired a close friend, [Andrew Braybrook](/vault/people/andrew-braybrook),
initially to convert those games to the **Dragon**. ST Software incorporated as
**Graftgold** in **1984**.

*CRASH* caught them at exactly that moment. Its March 1984 issue introduces "the
professional programmer Steve Turner and his assistant, Andrew Braybrook", who
"have recently entered the field of writing software for home computers on a
full time basis" — Braybrook as Turner's hire, which is how it began, rather
than as the co-founder later accounts describe.

That relationship did not stay one-directional for long. Braybrook went on to
write *Paradroid* and *Uridium*, and Turner then reimagined *Paradroid* on the
Spectrum as *Quazatron*.

## The Hewson years
Graftgold's early success came through [Hewson
Consultants](/vault/companies/hewson-consultants), and the pairing defined both
companies. Turner held the Spectrum side from Hewson's first serious year;
Braybrook held the Commodore 64 side through its peak.

Hewson's practice of crediting authors on the inlay is part of why Graftgold's
name is known at all. Most contract development houses of the period are
invisible.

## The Telecomsoft move
In late 1987 Graftgold signed to **Telecomsoft** — British Telecom's software
operation, the parent of [Firebird](/vault/companies/firebird), Rainbird and
[Beyond](/vault/companies/beyond-software).

*CRASH* reported it in December 1987 as a team "signed to Telecomsoft amid
contractual controversy", and by March 1988 was describing *Morpheus* and
*Magnetron* as "the first products of Graftgold's long-term exclusive contract
with Firebird". Leaving the publisher that had made its reputation, for the
phone company, was not a quiet transaction.

The same issue notes a separate dispute involving [Level
9](/vault/companies/level-9), also a Telecomsoft signing — the aggressive
acquisition of British talent by BT's labels was becoming a story in its own
right.

## Who was in it
*CRASH* had to print a correction in December 1987 after leaving **Andrew
Whittaker** out of its list of Graftgold members. The team was always more than
its two famous names, which is the usual condition of development houses and the
usual omission.

## Afterwards
Graftgold continued into the 16-bit era with *Fire & Ice*, *Uridium 2*,
*Virocop* and *Realms*, working across the Amiga and Atari ST. It closed in
**1998** — fifteen years, which is a long life for a British studio of that
generation, and longer than Hewson Consultants, Beyond or Telecomsoft itself
managed.

⚠ The founding sequence is corroborated by *CRASH* in March 1984; the
incorporation dates and the 1998 closure come from present-day sources.
