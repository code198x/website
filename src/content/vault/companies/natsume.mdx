**Natsume** can refer to the Japanese game developer founded on **20 October 1987**, or to **Natsume Inc.**, the American publishing company established in **May 1988**. The Japanese company's published history records both. They should not be treated as interchangeable credits.[^history]

The distinction matters particularly for *Harvest Moon*. Natsume Inc. published the original farming game in North America; it was not a farming game made by the Japanese action-game team.[^harvest][^interview]

## Action games under the Natsume name

*Nintendo Power* introduced *Shadow of the Ninja* in November 1990 as the first offering from new NES licensee Natsume. Its preview highlighted simultaneous two-player action and announced *S.C.A.T.* as another two-player game to follow.[^ninja]

In April 1993 the magazine covered *Pocky & Rocky*, an overhead shooter whose two characters could interact by dashing into each other. In October 1994 it described *Wild Guns* as shooting-gallery action with a character the player could also move to dodge attacks. It used ordinary controllers, not the Super Scope.[^pocky][^guns]

The Japanese development lineage continued through **Tengo Project**. Its official portfolio includes the original *Wild Guns* and *Wild Guns Reloaded*, and records the release of its new *Shadow of the Ninja* in August 2024.[^tengo]

## Publishing Harvest Moon

The original *Harvest Moon* was Yasuhiro Wada's farming-life game. *Edge*'s interview feature dates the Super NES releases to **1996 in Japan, 1997 in the US and 1998 in Britain**. Contemporary *Nintendo Power* coverage carries separate AMCCUS, Pack-In-Video and Natsume copyright credits.[^harvest]

The North American publishing role involved localisation: preparing the game for its new audience. In a 2017 interview, Natsume executives described their long partnership on the Japanese *Bokujō Monogatari* series and the later decision by Marvelous to use its own American publishing operation.[^interview]

## Two farming-game lines

Natsume retained the **Harvest Moon** name and began producing a separate line of farming games with *The Lost Valley* in **2014**. Its own developer diary that August identified Taka Maekawa as producer and described the new animal systems.[^interview][^lost]

Meanwhile, XSEED's October 2014 localisation blog explicitly identified *Story of Seasons* as its new English name for *Bokujō Monogatari*. The North American 3DS release followed on **31 March 2015**. These were separate products, not two regional titles for *The Lost Valley*.[^seasons]

## Later company names

The Japanese Natsume combined with the Osaka amusement-machine developer Atari in **October 2013**, becoming **NatsumeAtari**. It changed its name to **Winning Entertainment Group on 1 January 2026**.[^history]

Separately, Natsume Inc. announced its own Tokyo office in **June 2013**. That office should not be confused with the older Japanese company or its later rename.[^office]
