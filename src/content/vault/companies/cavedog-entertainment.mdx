Cavedog was a games studio that grew out of a company making children's adventures. [Ron Gilbert](/vault/people/ron-gilbert) and Shelley Day had founded Humongous Entertainment in 1992 to make games such as *Putt-Putt* and *Freddi Fish*, and in 1995 they set up Cavedog alongside it for older players. Its first game, Chris Taylor's *[Total Annihilation](/vault/games/total-annihilation)* (1997), built its armies from 3D polygons on hills that changed how they moved and fought, and reviewers called it the best real-time strategy game yet. Cavedog then kept adding to it, posting a new unit on its website every week. Five years after it began, its other games had been cancelled and the studio was folded back into Humongous.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1995, by Ron Gilbert and Shelley Day, as a division of Humongous Entertainment.
- **Location:** Woodinville, Washington, moving to new offices in Bothell in 1998.
- **Owner:** GT Interactive, which bought Humongous in 1996; Infogrames bought most of GT Interactive at the end of 1999.
- **Games:** *Total Annihilation* (1997); its expansions *The Core Contingency* and *Battle Tactics* (1998); *Total Annihilation: Kingdoms* (1999).
- **Cancelled:** *Amen: The Awakening*, *Elysium* and Gilbert's *Good & Evil*.

## Total Annihilation

*Edge*, which visited in 1998 as the first European magazine to be let into the studio, traced Cavedog's start to Chris Taylor arriving "with blueprints for the game back in 1995". Taylor designed *Total Annihilation* and Gilbert produced it. GT Interactive, which had bought Humongous in 1996 for about $76 million, distributed it in September 1997, in English, French and German at once.

The game's reviews were outstanding and its sales were not. *Edge* gave it nine out of ten and *PC Gamer* 94%, but in the United States it sold 83,900 copies by the end of 1997, against more than 178,000 for Microsoft's *Age of Empires*. Gilbert later called it "a huge critical hit, but a very disappointing selling product". Cavedog made up some of the difference with its website. It posted a new unit for the game every week from November 1997, and by early 1998 *Edge* reported that cavedog.com was around the thirtieth most popular site on the internet and the third most popular games site.

The first expansion, *The Core Contingency*, added 75 new units, "as many as most games include to start with", in *Edge*'s words: hovercraft, seaplanes, spies and minelayers among them. It also raised the limit on units per side to 250, a limit players with fast PCs could remove altogether. A second add-on, *Battle Tactics*, followed in 1998.

## Five projects at once

Gilbert did not want a large publisher. "The goal for Cavedog is not to become a massive publisher producing 50 titles a year," he told *Edge*. "We're looking to have maybe five high-quality projects in the works." In mid-1998 those were *Total Annihilation: Kingdoms*, a fantasy sequel; *Amen: The Awakening*, a first-person game set in a near future where, *Edge* was told, players might make progress without killing anyone; *Elysium*, a role-playing game promising 200 hours of play; and *Good & Evil*, Gilbert's own first game since *Monkey Island 2*, a real-time strategy game with adventure and role-playing elements.

The studio had already lost the designer of its one hit. Taylor left in March 1998 and founded Gas Powered Games. *Kingdoms* came out in 1999 without him, with magic in place of the robots and four sides instead of two, and *Edge* later recalled that critics praised it "rather less" than the first game.

## Folded back into Humongous

In February 2000 *Edge* reported that Cavedog had cancelled *Amen*, choosing to concentrate on games "within existing brands such as Total Annihilation". By May *Computer Gaming World*'s gossip column reported that *Elysium* had been cancelled too, that *Good & Evil* was on indefinite hold, and that Cavedog had been "assimilated back into Humongous". None of the three was released. Gilbert told *Edge* in 2002 that Cavedog "was not really closed in the traditional sense": it had shared people and resources with Humongous, "so when we stopped a couple of Cavedog projects it wasn't like we 'closed' anything. We just stopped working on two games and went on with all the stuff we were doing with HE."

## Later history

*Total Annihilation*'s ideas outlived the studio. Taylor's Gas Powered Games returned to the genre with *[Supreme Commander](/vault/games/supreme-commander)* (2007), and in 2012 Uber Entertainment, another team of the game's veterans, funded *Planetary Annihilation* on Kickstarter. The rights passed with GT Interactive to [Infogrames](/vault/companies/infogrames), which renamed itself Atari in 2003. When Atari's American company went through bankruptcy in 2013, the *Total Annihilation* rights were sold at auction to Wargaming, the company behind *World of Tanks*, which had already bought Gas Powered Games.
