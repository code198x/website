## Overview

Anco published unremarkable 8-bit games for most of the eighties. Then in 1989 it released **Dino Dini's *Kick Off***, and became the publisher of the football game against which every other football game was measured for the next decade.

## Fast facts

- **Press evidence in this library:** 8-bit titles in *ZZAP!64* from **1987** (*Thai Boxing*, *Winter Events*, *Magic Madness*), then the *Kick Off* series across the Amiga press to **1994**. ⚠ This library's run begins in 1987; the company was trading from 1983, so its first 4 years are not represented here and nothing in this line should be read as a start date.
- **The series:** *Kick Off* (1989), *Kick Off 2*, *Player Manager*, *Kick Off 3* (1994, £26, A1200, by Steve Screech).
- **Reach:** *Kick Off* is one of the most heavily discussed games in this reference library, and the discussion is not only British.

## The ball is not stuck to your feet

*Kick Off* is viewed from above, and its central decision is that **the ball is a separate object from the player**. Run with it and it travels ahead of you; you are chasing it as much as carrying it. Every other football game of the era glued the ball to the player's boot, which makes the controls obedient and makes the game not football.

That one choice produced everything else: the speed, the difficulty, the need to learn to trap and turn, and the frantic loose-ball scrambles that its critics called pinball and its players called the whole point. It made *Kick Off* hard to pick up and enormously deep once you had, which is precisely the profile of a game people play for ten years.

## Eight years later, in Italian

Anco is here partly to make a point about where this library's evidence comes from.

In April 1997, the Italian magazine *Zeta* opened its *Kick Off 3* review by noting that eight long years had passed since Dini's first *Kick Off*, and that the fame of **"il più insigne gioco di calcio di tutti i tempi"** — the most distinguished football game of all time — showed no sign of rusting.

That is an Italian magazine, in Italian, still treating a British Amiga game as the benchmark most of a decade on. *Kick Off* had a competitive scene in Italy and the Netherlands that outlasted the machine it ran on. A history assembled only from British magazines would record the game and miss the fact that its centre of gravity had moved abroad.

## Kick Off 3

*Amiga Power* reviewed *Kick Off 3* in August 1994 — one page, which the magazine pointedly said was "an excellent pointer to the importance we attach to the game" — and its verdict identifies the mistake exactly. The new game **"successfully defeats the 'pinball' feel of the previous games"**, switching to a side-on view, and *AP* recommended a competitor instead.

Defeating the pinball feel was the failure. The uncontrollable ball was not a flaw the series had been carrying for five years; it was the design. A sequel that fixes the defining characteristic has nothing left that made anyone want the original.

## Why it mattered

Anco is the entry for **a single design decision carrying a franchise** — and for the way sequels lose things nobody thought to write down as features.

It is also a reminder that British games had continental lives their home press never covered. The *Kick Off* community's centre was in Italy and the Netherlands, and the sources that record it are not in English.
