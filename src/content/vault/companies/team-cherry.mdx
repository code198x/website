The hero of *Hollow Knight* started out small. For the [Ludum Dare](/vault/communities/ludum-dare) 27 game jam in 2013, William Pellen, Ari Gibson and Rohan Fraser made *Hungry Knight*, a small Flash game in which a horned, cloaked knight with a nail strapped to its back kills enemies "to eat the berries on their backs". A later three-day jam, on the theme "Beneath the surface", gave them what Pellen and Gibson call "the very beginning of *Hollow Knight*". That game, released in 2017 by a studio of a handful of people in Adelaide, became, in *Wireframe*'s words, "an instant modern classic".

## Fast facts

- **First announced:** 19 November 2014, with the *Hollow Knight* Kickstarter; the founders had been making jam games together since 2013.
- **Base:** Adelaide, South Australia.
- **Directors:** Ari Gibson and William Pellen.
- **Core team on *Hollow Knight*:** Gibson, Pellen and the technical director David Kazi, with Christopher Larkin on music and sound.
- **Kickstarter:** A$57,138, more than one and a half times its target.
- **Games:** *[Hollow Knight](/vault/games/hollow-knight)* (2017) and *Hollow Knight: Silksong* (2025).

## Two friends and a game jam

Gibson was working in animation; on Team Cherry's own page he is described as having run an animation studio, Mechanical Apple, and worked across film, games and music videos. Pellen "was actually doing web development and then in his spare time he was developing games," Gibson told *MCV* in 2018. "I played that and then contacted him about a game jam, just as a fun thing."

The jams gave them a character and a world, and in November 2014 Pellen, Gibson and the programmer David Kazi announced Team Cherry and *Hollow Knight* together, on Kickstarter. The campaign closed with A$57,138. The stretch goal at $56,000 promised a second playable character, Hornet, to be added after release as downloadable content.

## Hollow Knight

*Hollow Knight* came out for Windows on 24 February 2017, published by Team Cherry itself. It is a Metroidvania, a game in which new abilities open up parts of a large connected map, set in Hallownest, a ruined kingdom of insects. The look is hand-drawn. Team Cherry's store page promises "over 130 enemies" and "30 epic bosses"; *Wireframe* thought it a game of "30 or so hours", "part Soulslike and part Metroidvania".

Its makers have been modest about the size of the team. "The core team for the vast bulk of the project was me, Ari and Dave developing the game and Chris on sound and music," Pellen told *MCV*. They also pointed to older influences than the *Dark Souls* series the press compared it with: Pellen said he "hadn't actually played much of Dark Souls" while making it, and the team named *Zelda II*, *Faxanadu*, *A Link to the Past* and *Majora's Mask*.

They kept working on the game after release and gave the results away. Four free content packs, *Hidden Dreams*, *The Grimm Troupe*, *Lifeblood* and *Godmaster*, added bosses, quests and abilities. The Nintendo Switch version came out during E3 in June 2018, and PlayStation 4 and Xbox One versions followed. Team Cherry later added a coder, Jasmine Vine, who worked on the console versions and on *Godmaster*. In February 2026 the studio released new versions for the Nintendo Switch 2, PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, free to existing owners.

## Silksong

Hornet's stretch goal grew into something larger. Making room for her movement abilities needed a world of its own, and Team Cherry chose to make a standalone game. In 2019 Team Cherry showed *Hollow Knight: Silksong* as a full sequel rather than an add-on, with Hornet as its heroine, in a new kingdom, Pharloom. *Wireframe* reported that year that, according to the developer, it would be "much larger than *Hollow Knight*'s". Its development was long and quiet. In 2022 *Wireframe* noted that players had been "pining after" it "ever since *Hollow Knight* was released back in 2017". *Silksong* came out on 4 September 2025.
