In August 1985 a Liverpool company nobody had heard of took a *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* Smash with its first game. *Nodes of Yesod* sent a spaceman in a blazer down through the caves of the moon with a mole that ate walls, and the reviewers said they had to look twice to make sure it was not by [Ultimate](/vault/companies/ultimate). That was the point. Odin had set out to be Liverpool's Ultimate, with lavish adverts and boxes to match, and its next game, *Robin of the Wood*, scored even higher. Within two years the team had signed up to make ten games in a year for British Telecom's games business, the games got worse, and the company was gone.

## Fast facts

- **Based:** Liverpool.
- **Run by:** Paul McKenna, who also ran the publishing label Thor.
- **Formed:** late 1984, named Odin by February 1985.
- **Known for:** *Nodes of Yesod* (1985), *Robin of the Wood* (1985), *The Arc of Yesod* (1986), *Heartland* (1986).
- **People:** [Steve Wetherill](/vault/people/steve-wetherill), Colin Grunes, Stuart Fotheringham, Paul Salmon, Marc Dawson.
- **From 1986:** marketed by [Telecomsoft](/vault/companies/telecomsoft), British Telecom's games business.
- **Closed:** 1987 or 1988.

## Thor, then Odin

Thor was Paul McKenna's publishing label, with games such as *Jack and the Beanstalk*, *Giant's Revenge* and *The House That Jack Built*. When *CRASH* visited in September 1985, it described Thor as "a publishing company, rather than a programming company", concentrating on marketing and distribution. Towards the end of 1984, as the idea for *Nodes of Yesod* took shape, McKenna set up a second company to write original games of its own. It had its name, Odin, by February 1985. Mark Butler, one of [Imagine](/vault/companies/imagine-software)'s founders, had been a director, and had recently left.

The team came from the wreckage of Liverpool's earlier software houses, "some from Software Projects, some from Imagine", as *CRASH* put it. Stuart Fotheringham and Marc Dawson had spent three months at [Software Projects](/vault/companies/software-projects) working with [Matthew Smith](/vault/people/matthew-smith) on a third *Jet Set Willy* game that was never finished. Fotheringham told *Edge* how a character drawn for a lost Noddy licence became the game's hero: Colin Grunes stuck "a space helmet and backpack" on Big Ears, the two of them went to see Thor, and "this meeting led directly to *Nodes of Yesod* and the creation of Odin Computer Graphics". The company's offices sat across a windswept plaza from the empty offices of [Bug-Byte](/vault/companies/bug-byte). In Fotheringham's memory the working day ran into the night: the team went to the pub at 7pm, clubbing until 2am, and then back to the office "to sleep under the desks".

Ultimate was the model. *Edge* wrote that "it was no secret that Odin openly set out to emulate the games produced by Ultimate", and *CRASH*, looking back in 1987, called Odin's launch advert "very classy", run for two months before the game appeared.

## Nodes of Yesod and Robin of the Wood

*Nodes of Yesod* took its premise from *2001: A Space Odyssey*. Charlemagne "Charlie" Fotheringham-Grunes, named after two of the team, explores the caves under the moon's surface for eight crystals, the alchiems, that open the way to a black monolith. A friendly mole chews through cave walls to open new routes. *CRASH* gave it 93% in August 1985 and compared it, without complaint, to Ultimate's *Underwurlde*. Steve Wetherill programmed the Spectrum version, with graphics by Grunes and Paul Salmon. Odin converted it in-house for the Commodore 64 and Amstrad, and Wetherill later wrote a level editor that let the team place every platform by hand and check that each jump could be made.

*Robin of the Wood* followed for Christmas 1985. Robin crosses Sherwood Forest to win the Silver Arrow at the Sheriff's archery contest, buying a bow, a sword and magic arrows from an Ent with bags of gold taken from the Bishop of Peterborough. *CRASH* gave it 94% and wrote that "the programmers are obviously perfectionists". It reached the British sales charts in early 1986, and *The Arc of Yesod*, the sequel to *Nodes*, scored 89% in *CRASH* that March.

## The Telecomsoft deal

In April 1986 *[Zzap!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* reported that "only days ago Odin/Thor announced marketing deals with BT". By the autumn *CRASH* reported that Odin's marketing was "now taken care of by Telecomsoft", the group behind Firebird and Rainbird, and in early 1987 *[ZX Computing](/vault/magazines/zx-computing)* listed Odin with Firebird and Beyond inside the Telecomsoft group.

The deal was for volume. Fotheringham told *Retro Gamer* in 2004 that it "involved producing ten titles" for Telecomsoft, and *Edge* in 2007 described "a deal ... to come up with ten games in one year". Both accounts say the team broke up under it, and *Edge* judged that "the conveyor belt nature of producing so many games in such a short period clearly had a huge impact on creativity and quality". Fotheringham left for [Denton Designs](/vault/companies/denton-designs) and later persuaded Wetherill, Grunes and Paul Salmon to follow him.

The scores show the slide. The arcade adventure *Heartland* (1986) was still praised: *CRASH*'s reviewer of the shoot-'em-up *ICUPS*, which appeared under the Thor name, complained that "Thor have really let me down" when "their stablemates Odin turn out really respectable games like *Heartland* and *Nodes*". Wetherill and Grunes's *Sidewize* came out under Firebird in 1987 and scored 47%, and its sequel *Crosswize* 69% in 1988. By then Odin had stopped trading. In August 1988 *ACE* described it as a label Telecomsoft had "bought and then lost in the mists of time".

*Commodore Format* gave Odin a short obituary in 1992: "ODIN - Or Thor, as they were sometimes known. From their Valhalla came the ace arcade adventures *Nodes Of Yesod*, *Arc Of Yesod* and *Robin Of The Wood* ... But one day, like the people who worshipped the Norse gods after whom the company was named, everyone stopped believing in them."

## Marc Dawson

Marc Dawson's career shows how these Liverpool programmers moved from company to company. In 1989 the Danish magazine *Amiga Interface* interviewed him at 24. He had started at Imagine at 18, then worked for "the now closed software houses, Odin and Thor". He was now setting up his own development company with Steve Wetherill, and their first game, the arcade adventure *Astaroth*, was to be published by [Hewson](/vault/companies/hewson-consultants). He later went back to his birth name, Marc Wilding. He worked on the Commodore 64 versions of *Nodes of Yesod* and *Robin of the Wood*, and on *Mission AD* and *Hypa-Ball*, a C64 sports game loosely based on *2000 AD*'s "Harlem Heroes" strip.

He also warned would-be programmers what the work was like: "The problem with programming is that most of game development consists of boring programming. You start with the interesting part, where you work out all your ideas, but the ending is very, very hard. It is a constant fight against boredom."

## Later history

Paul McKenna re-formed Odin Computer Graphics Ltd in 2005 to make games for mobile phones. Wetherill, who went on to work for Electronic Arts on games such as the *Command & Conquer* series, made a version of *Nodes of Yesod* for the Pocket PC in 2003. He had been working on the game for mobile handsets since, and *Edge* told readers in 2007 that a working demo could be found at the revived company's website, odincomputergraphics.com.
