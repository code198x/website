## Overview
Gilsoft published games briefly and authoring tools for a decade, and it is the
tools that matter: [The Quill](/vault/tools/the-quill), *The Illustrator* and
[PAW](/vault/tools/paw) between them account for a large share of every British
text adventure written in the 1980s.

Their own advertising in *CRASH* called them **"The Welsh Wizards of
Adventure"**, which is both accurate and the best summary available.

## Fast facts
- **Run by:** [Tim Gilberts](/vault/people/tim-gilberts), from South Wales.
- **The Quill's author:** [Graeme Yeandle](/vault/people/graeme-yeandle).
- **Products:** *The Quill* (1983), *The Illustrator* (1984), *PAW* (1987).
- **Early games:** a short line of £5.95 16K Spectrum games credited to T Gilberts.
- **In this library:** *Gilsoft* appears on 276 pages of the Spectrum press.

## A family business

*Sinclair User* profiled the company in April 1985 and found it owned and run by
[Tim Gilberts](/vault/people/tim-gilberts), then twenty. His own account of the
start is the best line anyone has written about the period:

> Gilsoft started mainly as a pocket money venture. I bought myself a Spectrum
> when it came on the market and pretty soon had a full-scale business on my
> hands.

He had left school early to get into computing rather than go to university, and
told the magazine he had no regrets about it. He was seventeen when the company
began.

It was run as a family concern: Tim as technical director, and his father
**Howard Gilberts** as managing director — which is why Howard is the one
demonstrating [PAW](/vault/tools/paw) to *Sinclair User* in December 1986.
[Graeme Yeandle](/vault/people/graeme-yeandle) joined in 1982 to write text
adventures, and The Quill "came along almost accidentally" out of that, through
a friend of Gilberts who was a systems analyst rather than a games professional.

The Quill sold at **£14.95** — a figure the period adverts confirm, listed beside *Valhalla* and *Games Designer* at the same price in a 1984 dealer's page — with no royalty claimed on anything written with
it — which is the decision that made the amateur adventure economy possible.

## Before the tools
Gilsoft published games first. *CRASH* reviewed several across its first issues
in 1984, all 16K Spectrum releases at **£5.95** and credited to T Gilberts,
including the maze game *Styx*. They are competent and unremarkable, and they
stopped once the authoring business took over.

## The piracy problem, and where it led

The 1985 profile records a complaint that reads differently now. Gilberts told
*Sinclair User* there were pirated copies of The Quill circulating that were
"just appalling", that it was hard to do anything about, and that it was worst
abroad — "I'm scared even to send samples to places like Spain or Portugal."

Spain is where the Gilsoft line took root. Gilberts went on to write **DAAD**
for the Spanish studio Aventuras AD, a single-source system targeting eight
different machines, and Spanish adventure writing outlived the British scene by
decades on the strength of it.

Gilsoft itself did not last. The limited company folded and the team went their
separate ways.
