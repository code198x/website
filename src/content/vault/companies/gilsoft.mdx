## Overview
Gilsoft published games briefly and authoring tools for a decade, and it is the
tools that matter: [The Quill](/vault/tools/the-quill), *The Illustrator* and
[PAW](/vault/tools/paw) between them account for a large share of every British
text adventure written in the 1980s.

Their own advertising in *CRASH* called them **"The Welsh Wizards of
Adventure"**, which is both accurate and the best summary available.

## Fast facts
- **Run by:** [Tim Gilberts](/vault/people/tim-gilberts), from South Wales.
- **The Quill's author:** [Graeme Yeandle](/vault/people/graeme-yeandle).
- **Products:** *The Quill* (1983), *The Illustrator* (1984), *PAW* (1987).
- **Early games:** a short line of £5.95 16K Spectrum games credited to T Gilberts.
- **In this library:** *Gilsoft* appears on 276 pages of the Spectrum press.

## A pocket-money venture
*Sinclair User* profiled the company in April 1985 and found it owned and run by
Tim Gilberts, then twenty. His own account of the start is the best line anyone
has written about the period:

> Gilsoft started mainly as a pocket money venture. I bought myself a Spectrum
> when it came on the market and pretty soon had a full-scale business on my
> hands.

He had left school early to get into computing rather than go to university, and
told the magazine he had no regrets about it.

The Quill itself "came along almost accidentally", through Graeme Yeandle — a
friend of Gilberts and a systems analyst by profession — rather than as a
planned product.

## Before the tools
Gilsoft published games first. *CRASH* reviewed several across its first issues
in 1984, all 16K Spectrum releases at **£5.95** and credited to T Gilberts,
including the maze game *Styx*. They are competent and unremarkable, and they
stopped once the authoring business took over.

## The piracy problem
The same 1985 profile records a complaint that reads differently now. Gilberts
told *Sinclair User* there were pirated copies of The Quill circulating that were
"just appalling", that it was hard to do anything about, and that it was worst
abroad — "I'm scared even to send samples to places like Spain or Portugal."

Spain is where the Gilsoft line ultimately took root. PAW's descendant DAAD
became the foundation of a Spanish-language adventure community that outlasted
the British one by decades.
