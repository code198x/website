**DISMAC** was a Brazilian manufacturer. *[Micro
Sistemas](/vault/magazines/micro-sistemas)* dates its beginning precisely — **25
January 1973** — and describes the trajectory in one sentence: it started by
selling **imported** calculators, and went on to make electronic calculators and
computers of its own.

That is the shape of [Brazil's market
reserve](/vault/culture/brazilian-market-reserve) in a single company. An
importer becomes a manufacturer, because the policy made importing the harder
business.

By October 1981 its **D-8000** was on the cover of the first issue of Brazil's
first microcomputer magazine, under the line "O D-8000 é o primeiro lance da
DISMAC na disputa do mercado brasileiro de computadores pessoais" — DISMAC's
first move in the contest for the Brazilian personal computer market. The article
inside calls it *o primeiro computador pessoal do Brasil*.

⚠ That "first" is *Micro Sistemas*'s claim about a machine on its own cover, in
its own launch issue, and this library holds no independent Brazilian source to
test it against. Recorded as what the magazine said, not as settled.

## Its own account of its constraints

The company's commercial director, **Oscar Alegre**, is the source for most of
what this Vault records about how Brazilian machines were designed — that
imported components were avoided even where they were cheaper, that a thermal
printer was ruled out because the paper would be imported, that DISMAC expected
to build its own memory expansion because no peripheral industry existed yet, and
that the company was "não presa a nenhuma matriz no exterior" — not tied to any
parent abroad.

⚠ Beyond the founding date, the D-8000 and that interview, this library holds
nothing about DISMAC: no incorporation record, no production figures, no account
of what became of it.
