System 3 is a London games company that Mark Cale started as a teenager and still runs more than forty years later. Its first hit was a *Star Wars* game in all but name. It went on to make some of the Commodore 64's best-known games: *International Karate*, *IK+*, [*The Last Ninja*](/vault/games/the-last-ninja) and *Myth*. It is still publishing today.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** by Mark Cale, who said he set it up at 17 with two partners. Later accounts date it to 1982; its first advertisements appeared in spring 1984.
- **Based:** London (Marshalsea Road, then Southbank House, Black Prince Road, SE1), later Pinner and Harrow in Middlesex.
- **First game:** *Death Star Interceptor* (C64 1984, Spectrum 1985).
- **Best known for:** [*International Karate*](/vault/games/international-karate) (1985), [*International Karate +*](/vault/games/international-karate-plus) (1987), [*The Last Ninja*](/vault/games/the-last-ninja) (1987) and its sequels, and *Myth* (1989).
- **Later:** *Constructor* (1997), a licence to Epyx's games (2006), *Putty Squad* (2013). Cale is still in charge.

## Death Star Interceptor

Cale told *Retro Gamer* in 2004 that there were three founders, and that they chose the name because "'system' was a trendy word" at the time. By spring 1984 System 3 was advertising from 10 Marshalsea Road in south London. Alongside a BBC Micro game and an Atari one, it sold ***Death Star Interceptor*** for the Commodore 64 at £9.99: "Earth is threatened by an Empire Deathstar, can you penetrate its defences and destroy it before it destroys you". The advertisement promised 12 screens of 3D graphics and "You have not seen what the Commodore 64 is capable of until you have played Deathstar Interceptor!" In October 1984 it was sixth in *Commodore User*'s chart.

It was a *Star Wars* game without a *Star Wars* licence. You launch, fly through space to the Death Star fighting off TIE fighters, then fly down the trench and drop a torpedo into the vent. The Spectrum version followed in 1985. *CRASH* gave it 92% in April 1985 and called it "one of the fastest solid 3D shoot em up games yet available for the Spectrum". One reviewer had seen it earlier on the C64 and thought the Spectrum version "far superior". It played the *Star Wars* theme at the start. *Computer Gamer* found it "rather bad" at first, but thought "it sort of grows on you", and in August 1985 it was number 29 in the Gallup chart. [Domark](/vault/companies/domark) later bought the real licence for its *Star Wars* games.

## International Karate and IK+

System 3's karate game, [*International Karate*](/vault/games/international-karate), arrived on the Spectrum at the end of 1985, months after Melbourne House's *Way of the Exploding Fist*. Archer Maclean rewrote it for the Commodore 64, and [Epyx](/vault/companies/epyx) sold his version in America as *World Karate Championship* from April 1986. Data East sued Epyx over its likeness to *Karate Champ*. A district court ordered both games recalled in 1987; the Ninth Circuit reversed the ruling in November 1988. System 3 was not a party to the case. The *International Karate* entry tells the whole story.

The sequel, [*International Karate +*](/vault/games/international-karate-plus), put three fighters on the screen at once. *ZZAP!64* reviewed it in November 1987 and liked the "three player free-for-all", along with "wonderful little touches on the picture, like the spiders and leaping fish". "A tenner is a high price to pay for a fighting game," the reviewer added, "but they don't come any better than this."

## The Last Ninja

[*The Last Ninja*](/vault/games/the-last-ninja) made the company's name on the C64. Cale conceived it at the end of 1985. It was advertised for January 1987 on five machines, went through two failed development teams, and came out on the C64 in the summer of 1987, after John Twiddy built the game around Hugh Riley's graphics. *ZZAP!64* gave it 94%. The Spectrum version was abandoned half-written. *Last Ninja 2* (1988) moved the ninja to New York and did reach the Spectrum, where *CRASH* gave it 90%. *Last Ninja 3* followed in 1991, on a cartridge for the C64.

## Myth and the move to 16 bits

*ZZAP!64* made ***Myth: History in the Making*** a Sizzler in December 1989: a mix of platform game and arcade adventure that travels through time, in which "all the classic ages come to life", programmed by Peter Baron with graphics by Bob Stevenson. The reviewer praised its "superlative presentation" and noted that "System 3 remain one of the few independent software houses developing original software for the C64". Cale's brother Adrian was then the company's head of PR. System 3 also made Amiga versions of its games. *The One* reported in December 1989 that the Amiga *Myth* would have 90K of music for each level, with the tune on three sound channels and the effects on the fourth.

Its 16-bit record was more mixed. Looking back in 2004, *Retro Gamer* called the 16-bit versions of the *Last Ninja* games "incredibly sloppy ports". Cale told the magazine that Archer Maclean had also made an Amiga conversion, from an Atari ST version that System 3 had developed in Hungary.

## Later years

System 3 kept going through the 1990s, and by 1990 it had moved from London to Pinner in Middlesex. Its later games included *Constructor* (1997), a building and management game, and *International Karate 2000*. For some years around 2000 it worked as Studio 3; Cale said the new name suited a company that now did "developing, publishing and marketing".

In 2006 System 3 licensed some of [Epyx](/vault/companies/epyx)'s old games. Cale told *Retro Gamer* early in 2007 that remakes of *Impossible Mission*, *California Games* and *The Last Ninja* would follow for the PSP, Nintendo DS and Wii. *Impossible Mission* appeared; the *Last Ninja* remakes and a long-promised *Last Ninja 4* did not. The company published the *Ferrari Challenge* racing game in 2008 and a new *Putty Squad* in 2013, and brought *Constructor* back as *Constructor HD* (2017) and *Constructor Plus* (2019). Cale is still chief executive, and System 3 remains an independent publisher based in London.
