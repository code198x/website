When Tetsuya Mizuguchi left Sega in October 2003, the team that had made *[Rez](/vault/games/rez)* and *[Space Channel 5](/vault/games/space-channel-5)* was being folded into Sonic Team. He started again with a new company, Q Entertainment, and a new kind of machine. Its first two games were falling-block puzzles for the handhelds that Sony and Nintendo were about to launch, and the first of them, *[Lumines](/vault/games/lumines)*, became what *Edge* called "the only completely original PSP launch title". Over the next eight years Q carried Mizuguchi's idea of games that feel like music through puzzles, a shooter built from a freeware PC game, a high-definition *Rez* and *Child of Eden*, a shooter played with a Kinect camera.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** October 2003, in Tokyo (its head office was in Meguro in 2009), by [Tetsuya Mizuguchi](/vault/people/tetsuya-mizuguchi) and Shuji Utsumi. The company's own profile gives October 2003; some reference works give 2004.
- **Management:** Utsumi as chief executive, Mizuguchi as chief creative officer.
- **Main games:** *[Lumines](/vault/games/lumines)* (PSP, 2004), *Meteos* (DS, 2005), *Every Extend Extra* (PSP, 2006), *Ninety-Nine Nights* (Xbox 360, 2006, with Phantagram), *[Rez HD](/vault/games/rez)* (Xbox 360, 2008), *Child of Eden* (Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3, 2011), *Lumines Electronic Symphony* (PS Vita, 2012).
- **Later:** Mizuguchi and Utsumi had both left by early 2014; the *Lumines* and *Meteos* rights were sold in January 2015.

## From Sega to the handhelds

*Hyper* summed up the start in early 2005: after *Rez*, Mizuguchi "left Sega to start his own development studio called Q Entertainment", which had two puzzle games in development, *Lumines* for the PSP and *Meteos* for the Nintendo DS. His partner, Shuji Utsumi, ran the business as chief executive while Mizuguchi took the title of chief creative officer.

*Lumines* came out alongside the PSP in Japan at the end of 2004, published by Bandai. It is a falling-block puzzle in which a "timeline" sweeps across the screen in time with the music, clearing matched blocks as it passes, and each set of stages changes the song and the look. *Edge* gave it eight out of ten. Around the same time Q announced a label with Bandai for PSP and DS games, ((qb)), or Quest Beat, meant to combine the handhelds with "new game experiences and collaborations with artists from genres outside of the videogame industry". Mizuguchi told *Edge* that Q was "not a handheld-only development company — it's just at the moment we happen to be working on a handheld platform", and that he wanted to reach players who "just want to play games with joy".

*Meteos* was the second puzzle game. Mizuguchi hired [Masahiro Sakurai](/vault/people/masahiro-sakurai), the designer of the *Kirby* games and *Super Smash Bros. Melee*, who had resigned in 2003 to work freelance. Sakurai told *Edge* that Mizuguchi phoned him and asked him "to come up with a puzzle game" as soon as he went freelance. *Hyper* noted that on *Meteos* Mizuguchi took more of a producer's role, working with Nintendo on a game built around the DS's two screens and stylus.

## Growing up

The third game had a surprising origin. *Every Extend Extra* (2006) grew out of *Every Extend*, a freeware PC shoot-'em-up by a developer called Omega in which the player's ship blows itself up to set off chain reactions. Q rebuilt it around a rhythm, with the flow of enemies following the music, and *Edge* gave the PSP game eight out of ten. In the long interview that accompanied the review, Mizuguchi described the study of how stimulation triggers emotion as "my life's work".

Q also began to take on bigger projects. *Ninety-Nine Nights* (2006), a fantasy battle game for the Xbox 360 published by Microsoft, was made with the Korean studio Phantagram, and *Edge* saw it as the moment Q "revealed its intention to move out of the boutique". A sequel, made with feelplus and published by Konami, followed in 2010. The puzzles kept coming too: sequels and an Xbox Live Arcade version of *Lumines*, a Disney edition of *Meteos*, and *Gunpey*, a continuation of a puzzle game originally made in tribute to the Game Boy's designer, Gunpei Yokoi. In 2008 Q brought *Rez* itself back as *Rez HD* for Xbox Live Arcade. A true sequel was another matter: Mizuguchi had told *Edge* in 2006 that it "would require Sega's approval as the company owns the IP".

## Child of Eden

*Child of Eden* (2011), published by Ubisoft, was the spiritual sequel to *Rez*: a rail shooter in which every shot adds to the music, played either with a controller or by waving at Microsoft's Kinect camera. Mizuguchi started it, he told *Edge*, by writing a 40-page memo "like a poem", which everyone at Q read, and in 2011 he called it "a music shooter, a synaesthesia shooter". A year later *Edge* judged it the first action game to "conquer" Kinect, because keeping the action on rails let the player concentrate on shooting instead of steering.

The last major *Lumines* from Q, *Lumines Electronic Symphony*, came out on the PS Vita in 2012. Asked by *Edge* that year why Q made games, Utsumi answered: "We want to entertain people. We always try to convey feelings of peacefulness, happiness and optimism. We are strongly biased to music when we design games."

## After Mizuguchi

In March 2014 *Game Informer* and *VideoGamer*, following a report by *CVG* that cited an unnamed source inside the company, reported that Mizuguchi had quietly left Q Entertainment the year before, and that Utsumi had also gone after an outside company bought the studio's shares. Mizuguchi founded a new company, Enhance, in the United States in October 2014. In January 2015 the mobile company Mobcast and Enhance acquired the *Lumines* and *Meteos* properties from Q Entertainment, with a new *Lumines* planned for smartphones. Enhance has since made *Rez Infinite* and *Tetris Effect*, and in 2025 a new *Lumines*; the [Tetsuya Mizuguchi](/vault/people/tetsuya-mizuguchi) entry follows that story.
