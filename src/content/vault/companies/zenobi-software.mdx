## Overview

**Zenobi Software** was a UK publisher founded by John Wilson that specialised in text adventures, primarily for the ZX Spectrum and Amstrad CPC. While major publishers abandoned text adventures in the late 1980s, Zenobi kept the genre alive by publishing independently-written games, often using authoring systems like The Quill and PAW. They released hundreds of titles and built a dedicated community of writers and players.

## An address, not an office
The most telling detail about Zenobi is the one *CRASH* printed in its contact
columns: a house number in Rochdale. Adventure players sent cheques there, and
tapes came back.

That is the whole operating model. By the turn of the 1990s *CRASH*'s adventure
column was describing "adventures being released by the sackload and new
adventure labels exploding onto the scene" — a genre the mainstream industry had
written off, sustained by mail order at **£2.49** a title.

Zenobi is therefore best understood as **a distribution service for a
community** rather than a publisher in the ordinary sense. The catalogue is
largely other people's work: what Wilson supplied was duplication, an inlay,
an address that people trusted, and continuity.

## Publishing Philosophy

John Wilson championed:

- Games that commercial publishers wouldn't touch
- New authors getting first releases
- Niche genres within adventures
- Quality control through curation
- Personal relationship with customers
