*Age of Empires* nearly began as a tank game. Bruce Shelley told *Computer Gaming World* in 2001 that Ensemble Studios' first ideas were a tank demo that Angelo Laudon and Tony Goodman, the company's chief executive, had put together, and a game about surviving a shipwreck. "We dropped those after Tim Deen insisted we play WarCraft," Shelley said, "and we decided to add features from Civilization to an RTS." The result, published by [Microsoft](/vault/companies/microsoft) in October 1997, sold more than two million copies in two years and started a series that Microsoft was still publishing in the 2020s. Ensemble itself did not last that long.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** in Dallas, Texas, by Tony Goodman and Rick Goodman; Bruce Shelley joined in 1995.
- **Games:** *[Age of Empires](/vault/games/age-of-empires)* (1997) and *The Rise of Rome* (1998), *Age of Empires II: The Age of Kings* (1999), *[Age of Mythology](/vault/games/age-of-mythology)* (2002), *Age of Empires III* (2005) and *Halo Wars* (2009).
- **Owner:** an independent studio publishing through Microsoft until 2001, when Microsoft bought it.
- **Closed:** by Microsoft, in 2009.
- **Successors:** Robot Entertainment, Bonfire Studios and Newtoy, among others.

## Warcraft meets Civilization

Shelley brought the *Civilization* half. He had co-designed *[Civilization](/vault/games/civilization)* and *Railroad Tycoon* with [Sid Meier](/vault/people/sid-meier) at [MicroProse](/vault/companies/microprose), and in 1995 he joined the new Dallas studio founded by Rick and Tony Goodman. *Computer Gaming World*'s preview of *Age of Empires* in 1997 explained the mix: the controls of Blizzard's *[Warcraft II](/vault/games/warcraft)*, and the research and the ages of discovery of *Civilization*. Shelley and Rick Goodman designed it together. It became the magazine's Multiplayer Game of the Year, and by July 1998 it was "on the verge of breaking the million-copy sales mark". Not bad, *CGW* added, for "Ensemble's first game". The game itself has its own [entry](/vault/games/age-of-empires).

Rick Goodman, the lead designer, left in 1998 to found Stainless Steel Studios, where he made *Empire Earth*. Ensemble stayed with the formula and moved it to the Middle Ages: *Age of Empires II: The Age of Kings* came out in 1999. By then, *PC Player* in Germany reported, two million copies of the first game had been sold, and its expansion *The Rise of Rome* was still in the charts. Shelley told the magazine the studio wanted to make the non-military side, exploring, building an empire and its economy, "much more interesting and important", without shrinking the fighting the hardcore players loved.

## Microsoft's studio

In 2001 Microsoft bought Ensemble outright. *Computer Gaming World* reported that Stuart Moulder, head of Microsoft's PC games division, promised a third *Age of Empires* with a historical setting, perhaps the age of discovery. In the meantime Ensemble licensed its engine to LucasArts for *Star Wars: Galactic Battlegrounds*, and turned to legend. *Age of Mythology* (2002) was drawn in 3D, on a new engine called BANG! that Dave Pottinger had developed; the aim, *PC Player* reported, was to lose none of the detail of the sprite graphics in *Age of Empires*. Gods replaced civilisations, and players could call down natural disasters and send cyclops and minotaurs into battle.

*Age of Empires III* followed in 2005, a game about the European colonisation of America. Ensemble decided that Native American nations would appear as allies on the map rather than as playable civilisations, and Microsoft consulted representatives of different tribes. "It was impossible to get consensus on any issue," the studio's president, Tony Goodman, told *Computer Gaming World*.

## Halo Wars and the closure

Ensemble's last game was its first for a console. *Halo Wars* was a real-time strategy game for the Xbox 360 set in Bungie's *[Halo](/vault/games/halo)* universe, and *Edge* noted that it was the first *Halo* game not made by Bungie. Microsoft announced in September 2008 that it would close Ensemble once *Halo Wars* was finished, and the game came out in February 2009, after the studio's staff had already begun to set up new companies. *Edge* called the closure of the "hundreds-strong veteran RTS developer" a blow to the games industry in Dallas.

The studio's staff did not leave the business. Within a year *Edge* counted more than five new developers in Dallas started by Ensemble veterans. Robot Entertainment made *Orcs Must Die!* and the iOS game *Hero Academy*. Paul and David Bettner, both Ensemble veterans, founded Newtoy in 2008 and made *Words With Friends*; Zynga later bought Newtoy and another Ensemble offshoot, Bonfire Studios.

## The series after Ensemble

Microsoft kept the series going without its creators. *Age of Empires: Definitive Edition*, a remaster of the first game, came out for Windows 10 in February 2018, and [Relic Entertainment](/vault/companies/relic-entertainment)'s *Age of Empires IV* followed in 2021 (see the *Age of Empires* entry for both).
