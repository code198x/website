In 2013 the first playable demo of *Outlast* ran at the PAX East show, in small curtained booths away from the lights of the show floor. *Edge* reported that one player "took the concept of a jump scare a little too literally and crashed through the booth's makeshift wall". The game gave the player nothing to fight with. Its hero, a journalist, carried a video camera whose night-vision mode was the only way to see in the dark, and whose batteries were, in *Edge*'s words, "the game's most vital resource".

*Outlast* was the first game from Red Barrels, a studio of about ten people in Montreal started by three developers who had worked on some of the best-known action games of the 2000s. It has made nothing but *Outlast* games since, and has published all of them itself.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 2011, in Montreal, Canada.
- **Founders:** Philippe Morin, David Chateauneuf and Hugo Dallaire.
- **Before Red Barrels:** Morin worked on *Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time*, *[Assassin's Creed](/vault/games/assassins-creed)* and, at [Naughty Dog](/vault/companies/naughty-dog), *Uncharted: Drake's Fortune*; Chateauneuf was a lead level designer on *The Sands of Time*, *Assassin's Creed* and *Splinter Cell: Conviction*; Dallaire was art director on *[Splinter Cell](/vault/games/splinter-cell)* and *Army of Two*.
- **Games:** *[Outlast](/vault/games/outlast)* (2013), its add-on *Outlast: Whistleblower* (2014), *Outlast 2* (2017) and *The Outlast Trials* (2024).
- **Engine:** Unreal Engine 3 for *Outlast*.

## Leaving the big studios

All three founders came out of Ubisoft Montreal. *Edge*'s 2012 history of *Assassin's Creed* singled out David Chateauneuf, its lead level designer, who had to make sure "almost everything was climbable" in the game's cities. *Edge* added that he had since gone on "to cofound Montreal based indie startup Red Barrels".

Philippe Morin told the studio's story himself in 2015, in an article for *Game Developer*. After shipping *Uncharted: Drake's Fortune* at Naughty Dog in 2007, he went back to Ubisoft Montreal as a creative director, and then joined EA Montreal, where Dallaire had been art director on *Army of Two*, "to work on a new IP based on an original concept by Hugo Dallaire". "EA was undergoing many changes at the time and after a year or so, the project was cancelled." In January 2011 all three quit.

They decided early that "the team would be no larger than 10 people", and that the first game would be horror. With no money coming in, they spent three months making a fake gameplay trailer in [Unreal Engine](/vault/tools/unreal-engine) ("Hugo is a real MacGyver with Unreal," Morin wrote) and finished it in June 2011. Most of the money came from the Canada Media Fund, but only once the founders had raised some of their own: "We managed to find $360,000, which was enough to ask for $1,000,000 from the CMF." The trailer went public at Halloween 2012.

## Outlast

*Outlast* came out for Windows on 4 September 2013. The player is Miles Upshur, an investigative journalist who drives up at night to Mount Massive, a remote Colorado asylum run by the Murkoff corporation, after a tip from an insider. The patients have got loose. Upshur cannot fight them. He can run, hide in lockers or under beds, lean round corners and, while running, look back over his shoulder.

Morin explained the choice: "We decided to go with no combat because it would allow us to build a more focused experience." The camera followed from Upshur's job, and "camcorders also have night vision, so it fit nicely". In practice night vision drains the battery quickly, and most of the asylum is pitch black, so the player keeps scavenging for spares. *Edge*'s review found the moments when the battery ran out "so nerve-shreddingly enjoyable" that it wished batteries were scarcer.

*Edge* noted that the game "owes much to [Amnesia: The Dark Descent](/vault/games/amnesia)", including "Amnesia's door trick" of opening a door slowly with the mouse to peek through. It also credited the team's past. Getting into the asylum means climbing scaffolding to an open window, in a manner "more reminiscent of *Mirror's Edge*'s Faith than, say, Gordon Freeman", and the review closed by praising "the benefits of the diverse experience of its highly talented development team". It thought Red Barrels had done "for modern horror what Visceral did for sci-fi with *Dead Space*". The game could be finished in four to five hours.

A PlayStation 4 version followed early in 2014, through what Morin called "a special deal with Sony to allow PS+ gamers to download the game for free". The add-on *Outlast: Whistleblower* came out on 6 May 2014 for PC and PS4. By January 2015, Morin wrote, "about 3 million units of *Outlast* and *Whistleblower* have been downloaded on all platforms", a count that includes the free PlayStation Plus copies.

## Later games

*Outlast 2* (25 April 2017) moved the series to Temple Gate, a hidden town in the Arizona desert. The player is Blake Langermann, a cameraman and investigative journalist working with his wife, Lynn. They are investigating the murder of a pregnant woman and are led into the desert, to the followers of a man called Sullivan Knoth.

*The Outlast Trials* went co-operative. Set in the Cold War, it has players, alone or in teams of up to four, "involuntarily recruited by the good folks at the Murkoff Corporation" to test brainwashing and mind control. It opened in early access on 18 May 2023 and was fully released on 5 March 2024. Red Barrels describes all its games as "independently funded, developed and published".
