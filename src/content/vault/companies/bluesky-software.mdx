In 1993 *Sega Visions* took its readers to "an unmarked office building about 15 miles from the San Diego Zoo", where a dozen people had spent fifteen months putting dinosaurs on the Mega Drive. That building belonged to **BlueSky Software**, and its *Jurassic Park* was one of a long run of games the studio made for Sega of America: talking American football with Joe Montana, Sega's *World Series Baseball*, a Mega Drive *Shadowrun*, and the robot made of spheres, [*Vectorman*](/vault/games/vectorman). BlueSky made games for other companies' labels, so its name appeared in the small print on someone else's box.

## Fast facts

- **Based in:** San Diego, California
- **Active:** 1988 to 2001, according to public databases
- **Founder:** George Kiss, according to Wikipedia; Sega of America's Joe Miller later credited the *Vectorman* games to "George Kiss and the SOA BlueSky group in San Diego"
- **Main customer:** [Sega](/vault/companies/sega) of America
- **Best known for:** *Joe Montana II: Sports Talk Football*, *World Series Baseball*, *Jurassic Park*, *Vectorman*

## Computer games for other people's labels

BlueSky's early credits are on computer games. *Amiga Power*'s first issue, in May 1991, listed the "programmer" of the Amiga version of Electronic Arts' *PGA Tour Golf* as Bluesky Software. For Disney Software it made two film games distributed in Europe by others: *Arachnophobia*, whose Italian advertisement in 1991 carried "Developed by BlueSky Software", and *Hare Raising Havoc*, an "interactive cartoon" in which Roger Rabbit has to stop Baby Herman escaping before his mother notices, advertised by Infogrames in *ACE* in February 1992 with the same credit.

## Talking football

Its Sega work began around the same time. Sega Retro credits BlueSky with the 8-bit *Joe Montana Football* for the Master System and Game Gear (1990), the Mega Drive conversion of Electronic Arts' space game *Starflight* (1991), and then *Joe Montana II: Sports Talk Football*, released in America in November 1991.

*Sports Talk Football* sold itself on its voice. *Mean Machines* explained in February 1992 that "rather than tackle John Madden head-on, Sega have taken an unusual approach": "Containing the most speech ever heard in a videogame, Sports Talk Football gives you a full commentary as you play! The effect is astounding." BlueSky followed it with *NFL Sports Talk Football '93* (1992) and *NFL Football '94 Starring Joe Montana* (1993), and with Sega Sports' *College Football's National Championship* (1994) and its sequel (1995).

Its biggest sports hit was baseball. *World Series Baseball* came out on the Mega Drive in America in March 1994 under the Sega Sports label, with a large close-up view of pitcher and batter. Two years later *GamePro* introduced BlueSky as "the creator of the stellar World Series games", whose "large, up-close batting view" had become the series' trademark.

## Jurassic Park and the licences

Sega also gave BlueSky its film and cartoon licences. *Ariel the Little Mermaid* appeared on the Mega Drive, Game Gear and Master System in 1992. *Jurassic Park* followed in August 1993, the game *Sega Visions* visited. It let the player choose between Dr Grant, on "a mission to rescue people trapped in the Visitor Center", and a velociraptor. Its lead animator was Doug TenNapel. *Retro Gamer* tells how he was working on it when he was introduced to David Perry, and drew Earthworm Jim as his demo for [Perry](/vault/people/david-perry)'s new studio, Shiny; TenNapel also worked as an artist on BlueSky's *The Ren & Stimpy Show Presents: Stimpy's Invention* (1993). A reworked *Jurassic Park: Rampage Edition* came out in 1994.

The studio also made the Mega Drive version of the role-playing game *Shadowrun* (1994), a different game from the Super Nintendo one, as well as the Road Runner game *Desert Demolition* (1995), and *Spider-Man: Web of Fire* (1996) for the short-lived 32X.

## Vectorman

BlueSky's best-remembered game came late in the Mega Drive's life. [*Vectorman*](/vault/games/vectorman) (October 1995) built its hero from separate glowing spheres, rendered from 3D models, and *Computer and Video Games* called it "one of the best releases of the year". *Vectorman 2* followed in November 1996; *GamePro* previewed it as "good news for Genesis gamers faced with a sparse lineup of fresh titles". Joe Miller, Sega of America's senior vice-president of product development, told Sega-16 in 2013 that both were "big favorites of mine", done by "George Kiss and the SOA BlueSky group in San Diego". A *Vectorman 3* was planned and never released.

## After the Mega Drive

As Sega's 16-bit market faded, BlueSky worked for other publishers on the new machines and the PC. *GamePro* previewed *MVP Baseball '96* and *MVP College Football* for [Data East](/vault/companies/data-east) in June 1996, and a year later reported that, after what it called "the demise of Data East", the baseball game had been "snapped up by [Psygnosis](/vault/companies/psygnosis)", which "sent MVP back to the minors for a graphical overhaul". For Philips Media it developed *Total Control Football* (1996) for the PC, a game, *Computer Gaming World* wrote, in which "you run every aspect of an NFL franchise from the top down".

Public sources say little about the end. According to Wikipedia, the French publisher Titus Interactive bought BlueSky in 1998, and the studio closed in March 2001 as Titus ran into financial trouble; Sega Retro also gives 2001 as the year it ended. No period report of either event has been found.

The name is easy to confuse with others. Blue Sky Productions in New Hampshire, which made *Ultima Underworld* and became [Looking Glass](/vault/companies/looking-glass), was a different company, as was the Blue Sky Software of New Jersey that sold business programs for the Commodore 64 in 1983.
