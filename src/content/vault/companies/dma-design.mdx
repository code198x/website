In January 1997 *Edge* found nine new cars with DMA number plates in the car park at Dundee Technology Park. The cars had been paid for by *[Lemmings](/vault/games/lemmings)*, a game about tiny green-haired creatures that DMA Design had finished more than two years earlier and no longer wanted to talk about. "Don't ask anybody at DMA what their favourite game is," its PR man, Brian Baglow, told the magazine, and he answered his own question with an emphatic, unprintable no. Inside, the team was working on a PC driving game about stealing cars. That game, *[Grand Theft Auto](/vault/games/grand-theft-auto)*, came out later that year. DMA made two of the best-known games of the 1990s in Dundee, and the studio carries on today as [Rockstar North](/vault/companies/rockstar-north).

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1988, Dundee, by [David Jones](/vault/people/dave-jones).
- **First games:** *Menace* (1988) and *Blood Money* (1989), for [Psygnosis](/vault/companies/psygnosis).
- **Best-known games:** *[Lemmings](/vault/games/lemmings)* (1991) and *[Grand Theft Auto](/vault/games/grand-theft-auto)* (1997).
- **Owners:** independent; [Gremlin Interactive](/vault/companies/gremlin-graphics) from 1997; [Infogrames](/vault/companies/infogrames) with Gremlin in 1999; Take-Two Interactive from late 1999.
- **Became:** [Rockstar North](/vault/companies/rockstar-north), in Edinburgh, in 2002.

## Menace and Blood Money

Jones came to the Amiga from the Spectrum, *RAZE* noted in 1990. DMA Design's first game, *Menace*, was a shoot-'em-up for Psyclapse, the second label [Psygnosis](/vault/companies/psygnosis) started in 1988. Psyclapse advertised it in *ACE* in October 1988 as "a pure arcade game". In May 1989 *ACE* reported that DMA, "flushed with the success of *Menace*", was finishing its follow-up, *Blood Money*: four large levels that alternated between horizontal and vertical scrolling, with two players able to play at once. *Blood Money* came out on the Amiga, Atari ST and Commodore 64.

Jones also explained his methods in print. In 1990 *Amiga Format* ran "The Menace Within", in which he used *Menace* to show how an Amiga game is put together, from the vertical blank to hardware sprites and the game's main ship.

By the time *RAZE* visited in November 1990, DMA was known as "the guys behind *Menace* and *Blood Money*". Jones had chosen not to write another game himself but to build up his own company in Dundee, bring in outside programmers and expand the offices. He also wanted DMA's games to come out on consoles, and his favourite at that time was the Mega Drive.

## Lemmings

*Lemmings* came out early in 1991, published by Psygnosis. It began as a few seconds of animation of tiny figures walking to their deaths, and the team turned it into a puzzle game in which the player saves them by giving individuals jobs such as digging or building. The [Lemmings](/vault/games/lemmings) entry tells its story. *The One* gave it 96%, and *Amiga Format* in January 1992 reported sales "in excess of 365,000 copies across all formats". By January 1997 *Edge* was putting the total at 20 million copies across 21 formats, a figure that probably counts the sequels and every conversion.

For DMA the success was also a burden. "We had to have a break for a while after *Lemmings*. Three years of hell," Jones told *Edge*. The team made *Oh No! More Lemmings*, *Lemmings 2: The Tribes* and *All New World of Lemmings*, and wrote several of the conversions itself. *Edge* put Jones's dislike of publishing on every format for the sake of it down to the years of *Lemmings* conversions. "It's very hard to explain to publishers that sometimes games just don't go across all formats well." In the end DMA sold the *Lemmings* rights to Psygnosis.

Not every project was finished. *The One* reported in January 1991 that DMA had shelved *Gore*, because keeping it going with an artist in Kent while working on other projects "was a little too much to manage". An "interactive cartoon" called *Cutie Poo* was planned for the following month.

## Walker and Hired Guns

DMA's other Amiga games for Psygnosis came out in 1993. *Walker* was a scrolling shoot-'em-up in which the player steers a huge mechanical walker. It had no power-ups and "a distinctly un-cute main character", as *Amiga Format* put it in a May 1993 issue that also reviewed *Lemmings 2*. *Hired Guns*, released at the end of the year, was a role-playing game with 3D action about a squad of mercenaries, with up to four players on screen at once. Psygnosis's advertisements quoted scores of 97% in *Amiga Force* and 91% in *Amiga Computing* and *The One*.

## Nintendo

The link with [Nintendo](/vault/companies/nintendo) began with a piece of technical work. Jones told *Edge* in 1997 that DMA had "initially ... did some video compression on the Super NES, just in case they were doing a CD-ROM drive. When they saw that, they quite liked it." DMA also made *Uniracers*, a two-player racing game on unicycles for the Super NES, released at the end of 1994. *Nintendo Power* noted that it was the second game to use the rendering technique behind *Donkey Kong Country*. In April 1995 *Nintendo Power* listed DMA among the developers working on Nintendo's next console, as the studio "famous for creating the award-winning Lemmings games and Uniracers for the Super NES".

Before that, DMA had spent about six months on the 3DO. "We bought the development kits and started looking at titles, but then there were signs coming through that it wasn't going to do what it had promised," Jones said. "At that point we were quite lucky because Nintendo approached us to try some work on the Ultra 64." The result was *Body Harvest*, a shooter in which the player travels through history fighting giant alien insects, and can drive any vehicle found on the way. Nintendo was its publisher at first, and sent back notes on every version. "Mr Miyamoto has a couple of points about the gameplay," John White, the project leader, remembered one saying. According to White the gameplay changed "no less than four times – mostly at the behest of Nintendo". By 1998 Nintendo had let *Body Harvest* go. It came out that year from Gremlin in Europe and Midway in the United States.

DMA also made *Space Station Silicon Valley* for the N64. It came out in 1998, published by Take-Two.

## Grand Theft Auto

At the start of 1997 DMA was working on three games for the music company BMG's games division: *Tanktics*, *Silicon Valley* and *[Grand Theft Auto](/vault/games/grand-theft-auto)*. *Edge* previewed *GTA* as a game about stealing cars and causing havoc in three American cities: 35 vehicles, 2,500km of road, missions from a crime boss that players could take or ignore, and a four-player network mode. "The good thing about *GTA*," said Ian Johnson, one of its programmers, "is that you don't have to go down a predetermined path." *Edge* expected trouble: "with videogame violence once again becoming a contentious issue in Britain, DMA could well face a rough (joy)ride when *GTA* is released next year." Jones was worried about a different market. He thought Americans, who wanted "everything in 3D", might dismiss its overhead view.

BMG Interactive published *Grand Theft Auto* for the PC in 1997. By early 1999 BMG's games business was part of the American publisher Take-Two Interactive.

## Gremlin, Infogrames and Take-Two

In 1997 [Gremlin Interactive](/vault/companies/gremlin-graphics) of Sheffield bought DMA, in what *Edge* called potentially "the most significant takeover of 1997". The magazine said DMA would stay semi-autonomous, keep its name for the time being, and bring Gremlin its ties with Nintendo and its N64 experience. Jones took a seat on Gremlin's board.

Two years later, in 1999, the French publisher [Infogrames](/vault/companies/infogrames) bought Gremlin for £23 million. *Edge* reported "uncertainty concerning the position of Dundee developer DMA Design", and noted that Take-Two "had its eye on DMA if Gremlin was to be broken up". By the end of 1999 Take-Two had bought DMA from Infogrames. *PC Zone* reported that the new owner planned more *Grand Theft Auto* games, among them one in 3D. *GTA 2* followed late in 1999, under Take-Two's Rockstar Games label.

David Jones did not stay with the new owner. In February 2000, *Edge* reported, "former DMA boss David Jones" joined Rage Software to set up two new studios. In 2002 he founded Realtime Worlds, with a core of 15 staff from DMA. Realtime Worlds went on to make *Crackdown*.

## Rockstar North

Take-Two moved the studio from Dundee to Edinburgh, and most of the staff left. *Edge* reported in 2001 that DMA, despite its 3D experience from *Body Harvest* and *Wild Metal Country*, had needed Criterion's [RenderWare](/vault/techniques/renderware) middleware "to bring the team up to speed". The game it was used for, *[Grand Theft Auto III](/vault/games/gta-iii)*, came out on the PlayStation 2 in 2001, published by Take-Two with "Rockstar/DMA" as the developer.

In 2002 the studio took the name [Rockstar North](/vault/companies/rockstar-north). By October 2002 *IGN Unplugged* was writing of "Rockstar North (formerly known as DMA Design)". Under that name it has gone on making the *Grand Theft Auto* series in Edinburgh. The [Rockstar North](/vault/companies/rockstar-north) entry follows its story.
