## Overview

Thalion Software, founded in 1988 in Germany, is one of the clearest cases of the scene staffing the industry. Its people and its sensibility came out of the Atari ST and Amiga scene, and it showed: Thalion games tended to push the hardware the way a good demo would, then wrap that technical flair around real design ambition.

The studio's catalogue ran from the sprawling RPGs *Amberstar* and *Ambermoon* to the lavish platformer *Lionheart* and the speed-blurred *No Second Prize*. Behind them stood scene talent like the musician Jochen "Mad Max" Hippel, whose chip and synth work was scene-famous before it was game-famous.

## Fast facts

- **Active:** 1988–1994, Germany.
- **Platforms:** Atari ST, Amiga.
- **Best known for:** *Amberstar* / *Ambermoon*, *Lionheart*, *No Second Prize*.
- **Scene link:** Staffed by Atari ST / Amiga sceners, including Jochen Hippel.

## Why Thalion mattered

Thalion is a reminder that the scene-to-studio pipeline wasn't only a Scandinavian or Finnish story — the German scene fed it too. The studio's reputation for squeezing beauty out of 16-bit hardware came directly from the demoscene habits its staff brought with them.
