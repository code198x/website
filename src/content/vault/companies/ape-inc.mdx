Ape is the company that turned [Shigesato Itoi](/vault/people/shigesato-itoi)'s idea of a role-playing game set in ordinary places into *Mother* and *[EarthBound](/vault/games/earthbound)*. When *[Nintendo Power](/vault/magazines/nintendo-power)* introduced Itoi to American players in 1995, it explained that he had "teamed up with a company called Ape in Tokyo", and added a detail that sounds odd for a games developer: "Ape is best known for its books on video games, but their artistic talents perfectly matched Itoi's vision." Out of Ape's staff came Tsunekazu Ishihara and [Creatures Inc.](/vault/companies/creatures-inc), one of the three companies that own Pokémon.

## Fast facts

- **Based in:** Tokyo.
- **Founded:** March 1989, according to later accounts, by Shigesato Itoi with backing from Nintendo's president, Hiroshi Yamauchi.
- **Games:** *Mother* (Famicom, 1989), *Mother 2* (Super Famicom, 1994; *EarthBound* in America, 1995) with [HAL Laboratory](/vault/companies/hal-laboratory), and *Mario's Picross* (Game Boy, 1995) with Jupiter.
- **Books:** guidebooks to video games, published with Nintendo.
- **Successor:** [Creatures Inc.](/vault/companies/creatures-inc), founded by Tsunekazu Ishihara in November 1995.

## Mother

Itoi, an advertising copywriter and television presenter, took his idea for an RPG without swords and dragons to [Shigeru Miyamoto](/vault/people/shigeru-miyamoto), and Nintendo's president Hiroshi Yamauchi approved it. Itoi then took the project to Ape. *Mother* came out on the Famicom in 1989. Later accounts date Ape's founding to March of that year, with an investment from Yamauchi, and its first guidebook, *Encyclopedia Mother*, to October 1989; both dates rest on secondary sources.

## Mother 2

The sequel nearly sank. Ape's work on *Mother 2* for the Super Famicom stalled, and Itoi asked [Satoru Iwata](/vault/people/satoru-iwata), then at HAL Laboratory, to look at it. Iwata's verdict, as Itoi remembered it: "If we use what you've got now, it'll take two years to fix. If we start from scratch, we can get this done in six months." HAL helped rebuild the game, and *Mother 2* came out in Japan in August 1994 and went to number one in the Japanese chart that *[Edge](/vault/magazines/edge)* printed that December. Nintendo of America released it as *EarthBound* in June 1995. See the [EarthBound](/vault/games/earthbound) entry for the game itself.

Ape's name also appears in the small print of a quite different Nintendo game. *Mario's Picross*, a picture-logic puzzle game for the Game Boy, carried the copyright line "© 1995 Nintendo © 1994 Ape, Inc. © 1994 Jupiter Co., Ltd." when *Nintendo Power* covered it in April 1995.

## Creatures and Mother 3

Tsunekazu Ishihara, who had worked at Ape, founded [Creatures Inc.](/vault/companies/creatures-inc) in Tokyo in November 1995. Iwata later said he had been "involved in setting up Creatures Inc." and had sat on its board while he was president of HAL. Creatures took a share in Pokémon, which [Game Freak](/vault/companies/game-freak) released in Japan in February 1996, and later accounts describe it as Ape's successor.

Ape's name did not disappear at once. In 1997 *N64 Magazine* reported that "HAL Laboratory along with Shigesato Itoi's APE team" were making *Mother 3* for the Nintendo 64. That version was cancelled in 2000, and *Mother 3* finally came out on the Game Boy Advance in 2006, developed by Brownie Brown with HAL; see the [Shigesato Itoi](/vault/people/shigesato-itoi) entry. Secondary sources say Ape's guidebook publishing ended in January 1997 with a revised guide to the Pokémon games, after which Shogakukan took over the work.
