**Europress** was a British publisher based at Europa House, Adlington Park, **Macclesfield**, Cheshire. It is not a name that appears in most accounts of the British games press, and it should: it owned two of the four magazines this Vault treats as defining, for the last stretch of their lives.

## Two routes into the same story

Europress reaches the games press twice, from different directions.

The first is quiet and early — earlier than its games titles suggest. **Database Publications Ltd**, of the same Macclesfield address, was publishing *[The Micro User](/vault/magazines/micro-user)* for the [BBC Micro](/vault/systems/bbc-micro) from **March 1983**, and launched *[Amiga Computing](/vault/magazines/amiga-computing)* in **June 1988**. Seven issues later the colophon spells out the relationship — "Database Publications is a division of **Europress Ltd**" — so by early 1989 Europress had been publishing a substantial Amiga monthly for a year.

The second is the one people remember. When [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield) collapsed in 1991, *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* and *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* went to Europress. The corpus dates the arrival precisely enough on its own: the word *Europress* appears on 12 pages in 1990, **151** in 1991 and **515** in 1992.

Put together, those two routes correct a common impression. The Ludlow titles were not rescued by an outsider who wandered in. They were bought by a publisher that had been in British computer magazines since **1983** — before *CRASH* existed — and that had started on Acorn machines.

## It kept them in Ludlow

The detail worth having is in the small print of *CRASH* issue 94, December 1991 — the first issue under the new owner. Readers with a faulty covertape are told to write to:

> **EUROPRESS IMPACT LTD, CRASH TAPE CLINIC, Ludlow, Shropshire SY8 1JW**

Not Macclesfield. **Ludlow** — Newsfield's own town, the address the magazine had always used. The acquiring company set up a subsidiary, *Europress Impact Ltd*, and ran the titles from where they already were.

A staff writer in the same issue gives the other half of it, in an aside about his workload: "as CRASH work isn't all I do **at Europress**." The masthead had changed, the postbox had not.

⚠ This entry describes Europress only where it touches the games press. The company published well beyond that, and this library holds its computing titles rather than its business.
