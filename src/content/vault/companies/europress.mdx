**Europress** is a name most readers of the British games press met only once, in December 1991, when it appeared on the masthead of *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* after [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield) went under. By then the group behind it had been publishing computer magazines for most of a decade, from an office in Cheshire, under a different name: Database Publications. It also had a software business that had published *Mini Office*, the *Fun School* series and, under its Mandarin label, [AMOS](/vault/tools/amos).

## Database Publications

The group's founder and chairman was Derek Meakin. In 1991 *Amiga Computing* said he had "founded the group 21 years ago"; a later secondary summary gives 1965, and the public sources do not settle the year.

Its computer magazines came out under the name **Database Publications Ltd**, at Europa House, 68 Chester Road, Hazel Grove, Stockport. In January 1983 Database was publishing *Windfall*, a magazine for Apple computers, with Meakin as managing editor, and a trade paper, *Computer Dealer*. In March 1983 it launched *[The Micro User](/vault/magazines/micro-user)* for the [BBC Micro](/vault/systems/bbc-micro). In 1987 it took over a rival: Newsfield passed its Amstrad magazine *[Amtix!](/vault/magazines/amtix)* to Database, whose *Computing with the Amstrad* absorbed its best features.

By June 1988, when Database launched *[Amiga Computing](/vault/magazines/amiga-computing)*, it had moved to a new Europa House at Adlington Park, near Macclesfield. That November the magazine's small print added "Database Publications is a division of Europress Ltd".

## Software: Database, Mandarin and AMOS

The group sold software too. **Database Software** published the *Mini Office* business suite, and in early 1988 *ACE* reported that "Europress International (owners of Database Software, publishers of Mini Office)" had set up a new label, **Mandarin**, run by Chris Payne. It opened with an exclusive distribution deal for the adventure house Level 9, which had "severed relations" with Rainbird. Mandarin later published *Lombard RAC Rally* and, in 1990, François Lionet's [AMOS](/vault/tools/amos), a BASIC for making Amiga games. By 1991 both labels had become **Europress Software**; *Amiga Format* in February 1992 told readers that AMOS "is published by Mandarin Software, now also known as Europress Software". The same business made the *Fun School* educational series, and *Fun School 3* won the best home education package award reported by *Amiga Computing* in June 1991.

## One banner, 1990–91

In the spring of 1990 a deal between Database and Gollner Publishing of Chichester created Interactive Publishing, which brought Gollner's *[Amiga Action](/vault/magazines/amiga-action)* to Adlington Park and managed *Amiga Computing* for fourteen months. In June 1991 Meakin put everything under one name. "With the creation of new subsidiaries in recent years, all with different names," he said, "there was a growing danger of the parent group losing its identity. From now on all companies forming part of the group will appear under the Europress banner."

*Amiga Computing* listed the result:

- **Europress Publications:** *Amiga Computing*, *[Atari ST User](/vault/magazines/atari-st-user)*, *PC Today*, *The Micro User* and *Let's Compute!*
- **Europress Interactive:** *Amiga Action*, *ST Action* and *Games-X*
- **Europress Software:** formerly Database Software and Mandarin Software
- **Europress Trade Publications:** *Computer Trade Weekly*

Michael Meakin became managing director of the holding company, Europress Ltd.

## Europress Impact and the Newsfield titles

Newsfield went into liquidation in the autumn of 1991. Its *CRASH* and *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* missed their November issues and returned in December from a new Europress company, **Europress Impact**, still in Ludlow. *CRASH*'s December 1991 issue told the story as a space rescue: the staff had been carried off to "the planet GONEBUST" until "Help arrived, lasers set to stun, in the form of Europress". The masthead named Roger Kean as publisher, Jonathan Rignall as managing director and David Wren, who also appears on *Amiga Computing*'s mastheads, as circulation manager. Faulty covertapes went to "EUROPRESS IMPACT LTD, CRASH TAPE CLINIC", Ludlow. One writer mentioned in passing that "CRASH work isn't all I do at Europress".

Europress Impact did not keep *CRASH* long: it ran until April 1992 and then merged into EMAP's *[Sinclair User](/vault/magazines/sinclair-user)*. *ZZAP!64* lasted until November 1992 and was relaunched as *Commodore Force*, which ran until March 1994. With Kean as publishing director, Impact also launched its own console and Amiga titles, among them *N-Force*, *Amiga Force* and *SNES Force*. A later secondary account says Europress Impact went into administration in March 1994.

## The sale to IDG

At the end of 1994 Europress sold eleven of its magazines. *Amiga Computing* reported it in its Christmas 1994 issue under "AC joins IDG": the magazine, "along with 10 other magazines published by the group", had been sold to IDG, "the US-controlled multinational that forms the world's largest computer magazine publisher". Meakin, "founder of the Europress Group", explained the buyer's interest:

> "IDG is pre-eminent all round the world, with more than 220 publications in 64 countries. One significant gap in this global network has been the UK. With the acquisition of the Europress titles, IDG now has a core business with which it can expand to become the major player here as it already is in so many other countries."

The same issue's colophon read "Published by IDG Publications". *Amiga Computing* stayed at Adlington Park under its new owner.

## Software to the end

The software business carried on under the Europress name. In July 1999 Hasbro agreed to buy Europress, by then described as a consumer software house in Macclesfield whose best-known label was *Fun School*.
