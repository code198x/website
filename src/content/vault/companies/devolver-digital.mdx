Devolver Digital was its founders' third attempt at the same idea. In 1998 Harry Miller, Rick Stults and Mike Wilson set up Gathering of Developers in Dallas as a publisher that would put developers first: higher royalties, more credit, and creative control left with the studios. *Computer Gaming World* compared it to United Artists, the film studio founded by actors in 1919. It published *Railroad Tycoon II*, had *Max Payne* in its line-up, ran out of money, and was sold to Take-Two in 2000. Their second company, Gamecock, lasted from 2007 until it was sold in 2008. In June 2009 they tried again in Austin, Texas, this time selling games only as downloads.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** June 2009, Austin, Texas.
- **Founders:** Harry Miller, Rick Stults and Mike Wilson, with Graeme Struthers and Nigel Lowrie.
- **First releases:** *Serious Sam HD* and other *Serious Sam* games, with the Croatian developer Croteam.
- **Breakthrough:** *Hotline Miami* (Dennaton Games, 2012).
- **Listed:** on AIM, the London Stock Exchange's growth market, on 4 November 2021.
- **Studios owned:** Croteam, Dodge Roll, Nerial and Firefly, among others.

## From Gathering of Developers

Wilson had worked at id Software. Gathering of Developers, known as G.O.D., brought together a collective of developers included [3D Realms](/vault/companies/3d-realms), Ritual Entertainment and Terminal Reality. Critics, *Computer Gaming World* reported in 1999, warned that "putting the lunatics in charge of the asylum" would lead to blown deadlines. G.O.D. also co-published Croteam's first *Serious Sam* games. In 2007 *Edge* found Wilson and Miller back with Gamecock and asked what had gone wrong. Despite an initial investment of five million dollars, G.O.D. had run out of money: "The main thing just came down to funding," Wilson explained.

Engadget announced Devolver in June 2009 as a new venture from the founders of Gamecock and Gathering of Developers. Its first project was *Serious Sam HD*, a remake of the 2001 game, which Devolver published for PC while Majesco handled the Xbox Live Arcade version. Devolver's own account, written for its 2021 stock-market listing, describes a "digital-only developer-first publisher" that made its early money from *Serious Sam* titles on Steam before looking for new studios.

## Hotline Miami and after

The studio that changed that was Dennaton Games, a small team in Sweden. *Hotline Miami* (2012) put the player in a top-down, neon-lit 1980s Miami, killing everyone in each building to a pounding soundtrack. *Edge* described it as "a return to genuine tension", with only three states, "waiting, killing and dying when you get it wrong". Devolver took it to the first Rezzed show in Brighton, where it won Game of the Show.

Devolver went on to publish a steady stream of games from small studios, including a 2013 reboot of 3D Realms' *Shadow Warrior* with Flying Wild Hog, Dodge Roll's *Enter the Gungeon* (2016), which earned more than $25 million, Roll7's *Not a Hero*, DeadToast's *My Friend Pedro* and Askiisoft's *Katana Zero* (2019). Several deliberately echo older games. *Wireframe* found *Katana Zero*'s one-hit deaths reminiscent of *Ninja Gaiden*, and Devolver itself told investors that the "retro" styles of *Hotline Miami* and *Loop Hero* gave them lasting appeal.

The biggest success was Mediatonic's *Fall Guys* (2020), a game-show battle royale for up to 60 players that earned $150.5 million in 2020 alone. Devolver sold the publishing rights to Epic Games in March 2021, when Epic bought Mediatonic. The game's earnings and the sale paid for a run of acquisitions that had begun with Croteam in October 2020.

## Mocking the trade show

Devolver became as well known for its marketing as for its games. At E3 in June 2017 it announced no games at all. Instead it staged a 15-minute spoof press conference hosted by a fictional "Chief Synergy Officer", Nina Struthers, that parodied the cheering audiences, frantic camera work and invented services of the big publishers' shows. It later streamed its own showcases, as Devolver Direct, on Twitch.

## A public company

Devolver listed on AIM on 4 November 2021. Mike Wilson had stepped back from the company in 2017, and the listing document named Miller, Stults, Struthers and Lowrie as founders still in senior roles. In 2026 its investor website described the company as having released more than 150 games, with five development studios and two publishing labels.
