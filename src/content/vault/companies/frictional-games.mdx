Frictional Games makes horror games in which the player is usually too weak to fight. Its best-known game, *[Amnesia: The Dark Descent](/vault/games/amnesia)* (2010), gave its hero a lantern and no weapons at all, and the sight of players running and hiding from its monsters, posted as videos on YouTube, shaped first-person horror for the decade after. The studio got there by trial and error. Its first game let the player fight, players fought badly, and Frictional took the weapons away.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 2007, in Helsingborg, Sweden, by Thomas Grip and Jens Nilsson; based in Malmö since 2017.
- **Games:** the *Penumbra* series (2007–2008), *Amnesia: The Dark Descent* (2010), *SOMA* (2015), *Amnesia: Rebirth* (2020) and *Amnesia: The Bunker* (2023); it published The Chinese Room's *Amnesia: A Machine for Pigs* (2013).
- **Engine:** its own HPL engine, through several versions.
- **Source code:** *Penumbra: Overture* and the first HPL engine released under the GNU GPL in 2010; *Amnesia: The Dark Descent* and *A Machine for Pigs* in 2020.

## Penumbra

Grip and Nilsson had worked together at university, and their *Penumbra* tech demo, made for a course, did well enough that they planned a commercial game around it. Frictional, by its own account, "started out in 2007 as a virtual company, with employees scattered across Europe", with only a nominal base in Helsingborg.

*Edge* previewed the result, *Penumbra: Overture*, in February 2007, under the line "As game credits regularly stretch into the hundreds, four Swedes prove it doesn't have to be that way". It called the game a "pacifist's Half-Life 2": a first-person adventure built from physics puzzles, stacking boxes and fitting parts together, using the Newton Game Dynamics physics engine. *Edge* thought it remarkable that "such a physically astute game could have been developed by just four people", and noted that it would sell for just £15. *Edge* listed Lexicon Entertainment as its publisher, and Paradox Interactive published the sequel, *Penumbra: Black Plague*, in 2008.

Physics was the studio's idea of interaction. "Using physics for all interaction came out of the simple idea that the player should be able to open drawers," Grip told *Gamasutra* in 2008, "and we figured that animations would be too expensive." *Edge* found the handling of objects "sensitive and precise". *Overture* also had a little melee combat, and that went less well: players fought the monsters head-on although the designers had not meant them to, and, Grip said, "many thought the fighting system was really bad, even though that was a design choice". For *Black Plague*, Frictional removed the weapons.

## Amnesia

Frictional's next game was *Amnesia: The Dark Descent*, released in September 2010 for Windows, Mac and Linux, and published by the studio itself. Before release it asked fans to fill a "preorder-o-meter" on its website with 2,000 pre-orders to unlock extra content. The hero, Daniel, has no way to fight; darkness and the sight of monsters drain his sanity, so the player must choose between hiding in the dark and keeping his mind. The [Amnesia entry](/vault/games/amnesia) tells that story in full: how it cost $360,000, sold 1.36 million copies within two years counting bundles, and fed a stream of YouTube videos and fan-made mods that, by Grip's account, kept it selling.

Its influence reached well beyond the studio. *Edge* said Red Barrels' *[Outlast](/vault/games/outlast)* (2013) "owes much to Amnesia", and in 2019 *Wireframe* wrote of "the first-person horror mode popularised by more recent games like Amnesia and Outlast". Frictional handed the sequel to The Chinese Room, the studio behind *Dear Esther*, and published its *Amnesia: A Machine for Pigs* in 2013.

## SOMA and later games

*SOMA* (2015) moved the studio from Gothic castles to science fiction. Its hero wakes in PATHOS-II, a research station on the sea bed, after a comet has wrecked the surface of the Earth, and the game asks, as *Wireframe* put it, "What makes us human?" *Wireframe* called it Frictional's "underappreciated first-person horror set beneath the sea" and found it "just as scary as its Amnesia series". In December 2017, when *SOMA* came to the Xbox One, Frictional added a Safe Mode on the console and on PC, in which the monsters still roam but cannot kill the player. Grip said it was for "a group of people that either disliked the monster encounters or didn't feel horror was their genre but still really wanted to play *Soma*", and that "it worked way better than we expected".

*Amnesia: Rebirth* (2020) returned to the series, and *Wireframe* gave it 87%. *Amnesia: The Bunker* followed in 2023. In December 2025 Frictional and the publisher Kepler Interactive announced *Ontos*, described as a spiritual successor to *SOMA*; in July 2026 it was delayed to 2027.

## Released source code

Frictional has published the code of its older games. In May 2010, after the first Humble Indie Bundle passed $1 million in sales, it released the source code of *Penumbra: Overture* and its HPL1 engine on GitHub under the GNU General Public Licence version 3. In September 2020, for *Amnesia*'s tenth anniversary, it released the code of *The Dark Descent* and *A Machine for Pigs* under the same licence. The 2020 release left out the games' art, sound and other content, so players still need a copy of the game.
