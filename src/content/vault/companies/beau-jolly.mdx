Beau Jolly sold other people's games. It bought the rights to games that had already been released, put several on one or two cassettes, and sold the pack for about the price of a single new game. *Computer Gamer*'s reviewer saw where the idea came from in 1987. "The computer business becomes more like the music business with every month that goes by," he wrote: "stars, charts, promotion budgets and, increasingly, exploitation of the back catalogue. Beau Jolly is a bit like K-Tel: grab the rights to some hits, repackage them for maximum appeal and count the profits." Beau Jolly began by buying most of the games of [Imagine Software](/vault/companies/imagine-software) as that company went under in 1984, and it was still making compilations for the Amiga eleven years later.

## Fast facts

- **Company:** Beau-Jolly Limited, incorporated 21 December 1983, still registered at Companies House.
- **Address:** 19A New Broadway, Ealing, London W5, in 1984–85; later 29a Bell Street, Reigate, Surrey.
- **Director in 1984:** Colin Ashby.
- **Ties:** described in 1984 as the sister company of Nouveau Records.
- **Series:** Mega Hits, Computer Hits, Five Star Games, Big Box, Power Pack, The Greatest and the Beau Jolly Compilation.

## Imagine's back catalogue

In the summer of 1984 the Liverpool publisher Imagine was running out of money. *[Sinclair User](/vault/magazines/sinclair-user)* reported in its August issue that, "in a last-ditch effort", Imagine had decided to sell the rights to its games to "London-based Beau-Jolly, sister company to Nouveau Records". Imagine would keep a royalty, "rumoured as being as low as 10 percent of the retail price". Beau-Jolly planned to sell the games in packs of four or six for the Spectrum and Commodore 64.

The sale came before the end. Imagine was wound up by court order on 9 July 1984, and *Sinclair User*'s September issue reported that "most Imagine games have already been sold to distributor Beau-Jolly". So the games did not reach Beau Jolly from a liquidator: Imagine sold them itself, in its last weeks.

For Christmas 1984 Beau-Jolly packed them up. A six-game pack for the 48K Spectrum and a four-game pack for the 16K machine mixed Imagine's established games, such as *Arcadia* and *Alchemist*, with newer ones such as *Cosmic Cruiser* and *BC Bill*. [Mastertronic](/vault/companies/mastertronic) took over the distribution, and *Sinclair User* reported that the packs were backed by £250,000 of television advertising on the ITV network, aimed at more than 20 million viewers. Colin Ashby, a director of Beau-Jolly, said: "Mastertronic got it right from the word go, and we are very pleased to be working with them. The market for budget games is large and growing larger." Ashby also wrote to *Dragon User* offering [Dragon 32](/vault/systems/dragon-32) owners a four-game pack of their own.

## Mega Hits and Computer Hits

In spring 1985 Beau Jolly licensed ten hits from nine other publishers for *Mega Hits*, on two cassettes: *3D Starstrike*, *Blue Thunder*, *Fall Guy*, *Son of Blagger*, *Automania*, *Bugaboo*, *Psytron*, *Wheelie*, *Blade Alley* and *Penetrator*. *Sinclair User* sold it to its readers at £16.49, against a shop price of £19.99, and pointed out that the ten games bought separately "would cost you well over £70". *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* reported a quarter of a million pounds of television advertising for the launch, and noted that six of the ten had been *CRASH* Smashes. The advertisements listed W H Smith, Boots, Woolworth, Asda and Tesco among the shops selling it.

The *Computer Hits* series followed at £9.95 for ten games, in versions for the Commodore 64, Spectrum, Amstrad and the Acorn machines. A typical volume needed many separate deals. *10 Computer Hits 4* for the Commodore 64, which *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* listed in January 1988, held *Bride of Frankenstein* from [Ariolasoft](/vault/companies/ariolasoft), *Alleykat* from [Hewson](/vault/companies/hewson-consultants), *Classic Snooker* and *Magic Madness* from [Anco](/vault/companies/anco), *Starquake* and *Skaterock* from [Bubble Bus](/vault/companies/bubble-bus), *Uchi Mata* from [Martech](/vault/companies/martech), *Iridis Alpha* from [Llamasoft](/vault/companies/llamasoft), *Dandy* from [Electric Dreams](/vault/companies/electric-dreams) and *Sacred Armour of Antiriad* from [Palace](/vault/companies/palace-software): ten games from eight publishers.

## In the charts

The packs sold like new games. In *[Acorn User](/vault/magazines/acorn-user)*'s charts for May 1986, *Computer Hits* was number two among Electron games, down from number one, and number six on the BBC Micro, alongside *Yie Ar Kung-Fu*, *Citadel* and *Repton 2*. In February 1987 *Computer Hits 10 Volume 3* went straight in at number one in the Electron chart, with Beau Jolly's *Five Star Games* at number eight. On the Spectrum, *Computer Hits Vol 4* stood at number seven in the Gallup full-price chart printed by *[Your Sinclair](/vault/magazines/your-sinclair)* early in 1988, between *Road Runner* and *Exolon*.

## The 16-bit years

By 1990 Beau Jolly had moved to Reigate in Surrey and was selling compilations for the Amiga, Atari ST and PC. *Power Pack*, at £24.99, spent the first months of 1991 in *[Amiga Format](/vault/magazines/amiga-format)*'s chart. *The Greatest*, at £32.99, combined *Dune*, *[Lure of the Temptress](/vault/games/lure-of-the-temptress)* and *Jimmy White's Whirlwind Snooker*, and *Amiga Format*'s 1993 games guide listed it as Beau Jolly's best seller, with a review score of 91%. A Commodore 64 pack, *Big Box 2*, was still on sale at £16.99 in 1993.

The last big one was the *Beau Jolly Compilation* of 1994–95, which put *[Cannon Fodder](/vault/games/cannon-fodder)*, *The Settlers*, *[The Chaos Engine](/vault/games/chaos-engine)* and *T2* in one box. It reached number three in *Amiga Power*'s chart in June 1995 and was still in mail-order lists in 1996. The company itself is still registered at Companies House, with a director, Nigel Mason, recorded since before 1992.
