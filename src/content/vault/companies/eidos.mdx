Eidos Interactive transformed from a small British publisher into a gaming powerhouse through one iconic character: Lara Croft. *Tomb Raider* made Eidos a household name and funded an ambitious publishing slate that included *Deus Ex*, *Hitman*, and *Thief*. The company's trajectory traced the rise of 3D gaming and the PlayStation era.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1990 as Eidos plc (video game division active from 1995).
- **Key acquisition:** [Core Design](/vault/companies/core-design) (1996), bringing *[Tomb Raider](/vault/games/tomb-raider)*.
- **Headquarters:** Wimbledon, London.
- **Major franchises:** *[Tomb Raider](/vault/games/tomb-raider)*, *[Deus Ex](/vault/games/deus-ex)*, *[Hitman](/vault/games/hitman)*, *Thief*, *Legacy of Kain*.
- **Acquired by:** Square Enix (2009).

## The Tomb Raider phenomenon

*Tomb Raider* defined Eidos:
- **Launch:** 1996, developed by Core Design.
- **Cultural impact:** Lara Croft became gaming's first mainstream icon.
- **Sales:** tens of millions across the franchise.
- **Media expansion:** films, comics, merchandise.

## Publishing legacy

Beyond Lara:
- **Ion Storm:** published *Deus Ex* (2000), a landmark immersive sim.
- **IO Interactive:** *Hitman* series began under Eidos.
- **Looking Glass:** distributed *Thief* games.
- **Crystal Dynamics:** *Legacy of Kain* series.

## Before Tomb Raider: a video-compression company

The fact the received account leaves out, and both period sources lead with it.

*PC Gamer*, **July 1996**, reporting from E3:

> **The UK video-compression technology firm** have recently **bought Domark, US Gold, Core
> and distributors Centregold** to become one of the largest players in [the industry].

*Digital Press* puts the same thing with dates:

> Before their days as video game developers, **Eidos was best known for their work in the
> area of video compression technology**. In **October 1995 the company acquired Domark**,
> and in **April 1996 acquired CentreGold** (which included **Core Design and US Gold**, the
> pieces they were obviously most interested in).

So the sequence is specific: a compression-technology business bought
[Domark](/vault/companies/domark) in **October 1995**, then **CentreGold in April 1996** —
and CentreGold carried both **Core Design**, who would make *Tomb Raider* that same year, and
**[US Gold](/vault/companies/us-gold)**.

Two things worth drawing out.

**The company that ends US Gold's story was not a games company.** Everything in this Vault's
British publisher layer — the licences, the conversion houses, the budget shelves — is
eventually consolidated by a firm whose expertise was compressing video. *PC Gamer* notes the
identity problem in passing: at E3 that year "**Eidos threw a big one to tell people who they
were**", which is what a company does when nobody in the industry knows its name.

**Core Design came attached to something else.** Eidos did not set out to buy *Tomb Raider*'s
developer; Core arrived inside CentreGold, alongside the US Gold catalogue this Vault covers
in detail. *Digital Press*, writing in retrospect, is blunt that Core and US Gold were "the
pieces they were obviously most interested in" — but the acquisition was of the parent, not
the studio.

⚠ *Digital Press* is a **retrospective** source (2003), not period reporting. The *PC Gamer*
item is contemporaneous (July 1996) and independently carries the same four names.
