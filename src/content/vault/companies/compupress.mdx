**Compupress** was the Athens house behind two of Greece's longest-running computer magazines. Its *Computer για Όλους* (*Computer for Everyone*) was already on its fifth issue in 1983, when a questionnaire in it found that readers wanted more ready-made programs. Compupress answered by launching a second magazine, *[Pixel](/vault/magazines/pixel)*, made up almost entirely of type-in listings. Six years later, as readers moved to IBM-compatible PCs, it launched a third, *PC Master*, with a floppy disk on the cover every month. One name runs through all of them: Nikos Manousos, the publisher.

## Fast facts

- **Based:** Athens: Solomou and Botasi streets in 1985, then Syngrou Avenue 44 from at least 1987; with an office in Thessaloniki.
- **Publisher:** Νίκος Μανούσος (Nikos Manousos), named as publisher on *Pixel* from its first issue to its last and as publisher-director of *PC Master*.
- **Titles:** *Computer για Όλους* (by 1983), *Pixel* (October 1983 to June 1996), *PC Master* (from September 1989).
- **Company form:** a limited company (ΕΠΕ) on *Pixel*'s masthead in 1984, a société anonyme (Α.Ε.) on *PC Master*'s in 1989.

## Computer για Όλους and Pixel

*Pixel* began as an offshoot. The publisher's letter in its first issue, dated October–December 1983, called it an effort by *Computer για Όλους* to cover home and personal computers through programs, and explained where the idea came from: the questionnaire in the fifth issue of *Computer για Όλους*, where "a large proportion of readers asked for more ready-made programs". *Pixel* described itself as the first Greek magazine devoted only to listings. It went from quarterly to bimonthly in 1984 and monthly in 1985, and the [Pixel](/vault/magazines/pixel) entry tells its story, including the 1987 reader party at the Panathinaikos ground.

The two magazines advertised the company as a whole. A Compupress advertisement in *Pixel* in January 1985 ran the line "Η πληροφόρηση στην πληροφορική ονομάζεται Compupress": information about computing is called Compupress. On *Pixel*'s 1984 masthead Βαγγέλης Παπαλιός (Vangelis Papalios) worked in public relations and advertising; by 1989 he was deputy director of *PC Master*.

## PC Master

*PC Master* appeared in September 1989 as "a monthly magazine with a disk for PCs", eleven issues a year at 3,000 drachmas by annual subscription. Its first editorial promised a disk every month of utilities and games chosen from the big public domain libraries, along with programs written by readers and contributors. The first disk included *Lines*, a variant of *Tetris*. *Computer για Όλους* grew in the same direction. In 1992 Compupress advertised an archive of its 1989–91 issues on CD-ROM, with a full-text search program, as "the first Greek magazine on CD-ROM", and by the end of 1993 the magazine came with a 3.5-inch disk and an extra supplement on multimedia and CD-ROM.

*PC Master* outlived *Pixel*. In *Pixel*'s last issue, number 133 in June 1996, Manousos explained that readers who moved to PCs had turned to its "sister" magazine *PC Master*, and that many of *Pixel*'s contributors were already writing for it.

## Later years

Wikipedia, which cites no specific sources, says that Compupress was formed in 1982, that *Computer για Όλους* reached its 300th issue in August 2009, and that its last print issue, number 344, appeared in May 2013, after the magazine had also gone out as a digital edition.
