Maxis exists because nobody wanted *SimCity*. [Will Wright](/vault/people/will-wright) had a working city simulation on the Commodore 64 by the mid-1980s, but it had no enemy, no score and no way to win, and publishers kept waiting for it to turn into a game. "Will couldn't find a publisher," Jeff Braun told *ACE* in 1990, "so we set up Maxis." The company that grew from that decision made a business out of programs that period magazines struggled to name. *Computer Gaming World* settled on "software toys": a city, a planet, an ant colony or a family farm for the player to tinker with. Ten years after *SimCity*, Wright turned the same idea on a household, and *The Sims* became the best-selling PC game there had been.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1987, by [Will Wright](/vault/people/will-wright) and Jeff Braun.
- **Based:** Lafayette, California, in 1988; Orinda in the early 1990s; Walnut Creek by 1995; later Emeryville.
- **First release:** *[SimCity](/vault/games/sim-city)* (1989), distributed in North America by [Brøderbund](/vault/companies/broderbund).
- **Public company:** from 1995, on Nasdaq as MXIS.
- **Bought by:** [Electronic Arts](/vault/companies/electronic-arts), in 1997, for about $125 million in stock.
- **Best known for:** *SimCity* and its sequels, *SimEarth*, *SimAnt*, *The Sims* (2000) and *Spore* (2008).
- **Today:** a studio inside Electronic Arts, making *The Sims*.

## A partnership to publish one game

Braun told *ACE* how it began. "Maxis started in 1987, it's a partnership between Will Wright and myself. Before Maxis I was involved with productivity software on the Amiga but I just didn't see that going anywhere. I then met up with Will at a party." Wright showed him the game that had grown out of the island editor for his helicopter game *Raid on Bungeling Bay*. *Retro Gamer* described Braun as an investor looking for a way into games, and quoted Wright's view of the time: "The idea of a sandbox game was pretty foreign to everybody." Braun, the magazine wrote, saw its potential and persuaded Wright to keep going.

By December 1988 *Computer Gaming World* was listing Maxis Software at a suite on Mountain View Drive in Lafayette, California. The two rewrote *SimCity* for the Macintosh and Amiga, and it came out in early 1989 with Brøderbund, which had published *Raid on Bungeling Bay*, as its distributor. *Retro Gamer* reported that the first 100,000 copies were sold out of Braun's living room. Sales were slow at first and then kept growing: *Info* reported in January 1992 that Maxis had sold its millionth copy. The [SimCity](/vault/games/sim-city) entry tells that story in full.

## Software toys

*SimCity*'s success paid for more simulations, each of a different system. *SimEarth* (1990) modelled a whole planet. *SimAnt* (1991) went down to an ant colony, and *Retro Gamer* later called it the first Maxis sim to give the player a clear set of goals. *SimLife* followed in 1992, and *SimFarm* (1993), in *Computer Gaming World*'s words, was "almost a rural version of SimCity". Previewing *SimFarm* in April 1993, the magazine placed it in "the tradition of Maxis' other software toys in allowing the player to control a model of a real-life system".

Maxis also published other people's simulations. *El-Fish*, a Russian program, was in *Computer Gaming World*'s words "a sort of SimAquarium", a pet "you don't have to feed". *SimTower* (1994) was a skyscraper simulation designed in Japan by Yoot Saito; reviewing it in 1995, the magazine explained that the makers of "engrossing system simulations (SimCity, SimEarth, SimLife, sim-anything)" had seen a game in Saito's idea of an elevator simulator. In early 1996 *Computer Gaming World* previewed Maxis's *Full Tilt! Pinball*, noting that the company did not propose to charge more than $30 for it.

In 1992 Maxis moved out from under its first partner: *Computer Gaming World*'s report on Brøderbund's finances that October mentioned "Maxis leaving Broderbund's distribution network". *SimCity 2000* followed in 1993, and in 1995 *Computer Gaming World*'s Hall of Fame described the "Maxis SimEmpire of SimEarth, SimFarm, SimAnt and SimCity 2000", adding that *SimCity* "may not be a game, but it's certainly a product that revels in creativity for its own sake".

## A public company

Maxis sold shares to the public in 1995. By October that year its press releases came from Walnut Creek, gave the company as "Maxis (Nasdaq: MXIS)" and quoted Sam Poole as president; one announced *SimCity 2000* for the Sega Saturn. Being a small company on the stock market proved hard. *Retro Gamer* counted *SimCopter* (1996), in which Wright returned to helicopters, among some "suspect releases" before the EA takeover, with "poor 3D rendering and bugginess". In July 1997 *Edge* reported that Maxis had made a net loss of $1.7 million for the financial year to 31 March, and that its share price had fallen to $11.

## Electronic Arts

In mid-1997 [Electronic Arts](/vault/companies/electronic-arts) agreed to buy Maxis in a share swap worth about $125 million. *Edge* called it "EA acquires Sim kings" and expected the deal to go through in August; *Computer Gaming World* said the merger should be complete by the end of that month, and that EA would add "the highly successful franchise of Sim products" to its line-up. Maxis's announced titles then included *SimCity 3000* and *Streets of SimCity*.

For Wright the purchase was a rescue. He had been trying for years to build a game about a simulated household, and inside Maxis, he told *Edge* in 2004, "it was kind of a struggle to get one or two programmers devoted to it... we were kind of struggling until EA bought Maxis. They were really, really into it, and for the first time I got resources on the project." *SimCity 3000* came out in 1999 and *The Sims* in February 2000. In June 2002 *Computer Gaming World* reported that *The Sims* had become "the best-selling PC game of all time, according to Electronic Arts, ending the long reign of previous leader Myst", with more than 6.3 million copies shipped. *[Myst](/vault/games/myst)* has its own entry.

## After The Sims

*SimCity 4* followed in 2003. Wright's next project, *Spore*, was a Maxis game when *Edge* previewed it in 2007, and came out in 2008; *Darkspore*, a spin-off action role-playing game, followed in 2011. For a time *The Sims* was made by a separate EA team, The Sims Studio, but by 2013 the series was back with Maxis, and *Edge* previewed *The Sims 4* that October as the studio's work.

The same *Edge* preview mentioned "the problems that plagued Maxis's previous outing, SimCity, at launch". The *SimCity* of March 2013 required a constant internet connection, and many players could not reach its servers. On 4 March 2015 EA closed Maxis's studio in Emeryville, California, where *SimCity* and *Spore* had been made, and moved development of Maxis's games to its studios in Redwood Shores, Salt Lake City, Helsinki and Melbourne. The name survived. EA still presents Maxis as a studio making *The Sims*, which marked its 25th anniversary in 2025.

The code of the first *SimCity* is public: in 2008 EA released it under the GNU General Public License as *Micropolis*.
