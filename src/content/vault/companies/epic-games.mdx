Epic Games' journey spans gaming's modern history. Tim Sweeney started in his parents' basement, creating shareware games. The company built Unreal Engine, which now powers countless games across the industry. Then *Fortnite* made them one of gaming's most valuable companies. Epic's story is one of continuous reinvention.

## Fast facts

- **Traded as:** **Epic MegaGames** until the 1999 rename to Epic Games.
- **Founded:** 1991 as Potomac Computer Systems, renamed Epic MegaGames (1992), then Epic Games (1999).
- **Founder:** [Tim Sweeney](/vault/people/tim-sweeney).
- **Headquarters:** Cary, North Carolina.
- **Key products:** Unreal Engine, *Fortnite*, Epic Games Store.
- **Notable games:** *[ZZT](/vault/tools/zzt)*, *Jazz Jackrabbit*, *[Unreal](/vault/games/unreal)*, *Gears of War*, *Fortnite*.

## The shareware years

Epic began in shareware:
- **ZZT (1991):** Sweeney's first game, with user-created content.
- **Epic Pinball, Jazz Jackrabbit:** successful shareware titles.
- **Business model:** try-before-you-buy distribution.

## Unreal Engine

The technology that changed everything:
- **Unreal (1998):** first-person shooter that showcased the engine.
- **Licensing:** engine became industry standard.
- **Evolution:** UE4 and UE5 power modern AAA development.
- **Accessibility:** free for developers until royalty threshold.

## Fortnite and beyond

*Fortnite* transformed Epic:
- **Battle royale:** became cultural phenomenon.
- **Free-to-play:** billions in revenue from cosmetics.
- **Epic Games Store:** challenged Steam's dominance.
- **Industry influence:** cross-platform play advocacy.

## The shareware benchmark

Before the engine and long before *Fortnite*, Epic's name functioned in the press as a unit of
measurement.

*Amiga World*, January 1995, describing the ambition of a South Carolina shareware group:

> a shareware coding group that aims to become to the **Amiga shareware game market what
> Apogee and Epic MegaGames are to the IBM**.

That is Epic as the reference point rather than the subject — the standard a developer on
another platform measured itself against, in an American magazine, two years before *Unreal*.

Worth recording because it dates the reputation. By 1995 **Epic MegaGames** and **Apogee**
were jointly the definition of what PC shareware publishing looked like, and they managed it
from outside the retail system every other company in this Vault depended on: no distributor,
no shelf, no bundle deal — the first episode free, the rest by mail order.

⚠ This library is a poor source for Epic. Its magazines are overwhelmingly British and
Amiga/8-bit; Epic's shareware years happened on the American PC, and what survives here is
mostly the company being cited by others rather than covered directly.
