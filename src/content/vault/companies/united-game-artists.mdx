United Game Artists made only a handful of games, and two of them are the reason Sega's early-2000s output is remembered for more than *Sonic*. *[Space Channel 5](/vault/games/space-channel-5)* had a 25th-century reporter defeat aliens by dancing; *[Rez](/vault/games/rez)* turned a rail shooter into a way of playing trance music. Both came from a studio of about 60 people in Shibuya, Tokyo, which its head, [Tetsuya Mizuguchi](/vault/people/tetsuya-mizuguchi), filled with visual artists, dancers, DJs and sound specialists as well as programmers. In its awards for 2001, *Edge* put UGA third in its Developer of the Year award, behind Nintendo and Konami, for releasing "a breath of inspirationally fresh air" when other studios were playing safe. Two years later Sega folded it into Sonic Team.

## Fast facts

- **Part of:** [Sega](/vault/companies/sega). A wholly owned company in the Sega group from 2000.
- **Head:** [Tetsuya Mizuguchi](/vault/people/tetsuya-mizuguchi), described by *Edge* in 2002 as its "president and CEO".
- **Earlier names:** AM Annex (Mizuguchi's arcade team from 1996), then Software R&D No. 9 (from 1999).
- **Staff:** about 60 in 2000.
- **Games:** *Space Channel 5* (Dreamcast, 1999), *Rez* (Dreamcast and PlayStation 2, 2001), *Space Channel 5 Part 2* (Dreamcast and PlayStation 2, 2002).
- **End:** merged into [Sonic Team](/vault/companies/sonic-team) in 2003; Mizuguchi left Sega in October 2003.

## From the arcade to Shibuya

The team began in Sega's arcade division. By 1996 Mizuguchi, the producer of *[Sega Rally Championship](/vault/games/sega-rally)*, was running a team of about 15 people called AM Annex, which made *Sega Touring Car Championship* and, in 1998, *Sega Rally 2*. In 1999 he moved to home consoles, set up Software R&D No. 9 to make a game for the Dreamcast, and took the team to Shibuya. The game was *Space Channel 5*, a rhythm game in which the player copies the moves of dancing aliens, aimed, Mizuguchi told *Edge*, at "people who don't play games". Sega launched it in Japan at the end of 1999 with a street event in front of Shibuya station.

## A company of its own

In 2000 Sega broke its development departments up into nine separate companies, each wholly owned by Sega, and R&D No. 9 became United Game Artists. *Edge* interviewed the heads of all nine for its October 2000 issue. Mizuguchi explained that, given a company of his own within the Sega group, he had made his idea its name: "Each member of UGA is a creator, a videogame artist." Its market would not be Japan, America or Europe: "our 'market' is the human being." About 25 people had made *Space Channel 5*, and the company now had around 60, several of whom "don't come from the games industry. They are specialists in various fields: visual, dance, DJ, sound etc." He admitted that setting the company up had been stressful, and said that because UGA was "100 per cent owned by Sega Group", it would "work at taking Sega to the top".

*DC-UK* visited the studio at the end of 2000 and presented it as "the new face of Tokyo games design", its offices "tucked away down a side street not far from the famous Shibuya Square", reached by "a pokey security-pad operated elevator", where Mizuguchi could be found "rubbing shoulders with famous anime creators one minute and DJs the next".

## Rez

In that interview Mizuguchi said UGA had two projects under way, the more advanced aimed at 2001 and "very different from *Space Channel 5*", before his assistant stopped him saying more. The next game to appear was *K-Project*, shown only on video at E3 in 2001 and released at the end of that year as *Rez*. Its team included the American artist Jake Kazdal, who told *Edge* that he had met Mizuguchi at E3 in 1998 and been invited to move to Japan to work on the game, although he spoke no Japanese; he recalled videotaping a Winamp music visualiser for Mizuguchi, and said "that's where the wireframe look for *Rez* originated". The UGA team followed the magazine's coverage closely enough to send *Edge* "a heartfelt email" after reading a translation of its *Rez* preview.

*Rez* won *Edge*'s Graphical Achievement of the Year award, and in the Developer of the Year category the magazine wrote that while others "proved willing to rely on boring, bankable concepts and genres, Tetsuya Mizuguchi's United Game Artists bucked the trend". Both *Rez* and *Space Channel 5 Part 2*, it said, "defy easy categorisation". *Part 2*, released on the Dreamcast and PlayStation 2 in 2002, brought back the dancing reporter Ulala; *Edge* later called it "perhaps his most human title". In 2002 Mizuguchi told *Edge* that he updated the UGA website diary himself "every day".

## Merged into Sonic Team

The games were admired more than they were bought. Reviewing a Game Boy Advance version of *Space Channel 5* in October 2003, *Edge* noted that there "might not have been a reshuffle if the games had been as successful commercially as they were critically", and wondered whether UGA's design ethic would be lost "along with his sub-division's name". In 2003 Sega merged UGA into Sonic Team. Kazdal had spent a year after *Rez* on a GameCube prototype that was never released; *Edge*'s profile of him records that when the merger was announced, many of his friends at the studio left, and so did he. Mizuguchi left Sega in October 2003 and set up [Q Entertainment](/vault/companies/q-entertainment). When Sonic Team released *Astro Boy* for the PlayStation 2 in 2004, *Edge* linked its troubled development to the merger: "It's no secret that *Astro Boy* suffered pains during its conception, with Tetsuya Mizuguchi's Sega division merging into Sonic Team shortly before he left." The result, it wrote, "shows little of the UGA spirit".
