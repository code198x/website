"Talk about bad luck," *PC Gamer* began its profile of Starbreeze in November 2002. "All of Starbreeze's previous projects have collapsed thanks to broken publishers or dropped deals." The Swedish studio, founded in 1998 by Magnus Högdahl, the demo coder Vogue of the group [Triton](/vault/groups/triton), and its team had lost one game to a publisher that went bust and a second to a takeover. Its first release, the Xbox game *Enclave*, scored 3 out of 10 in *Edge*. Two years later it made *The Chronicles of Riddick: Escape from Butcher Bay*, a film tie-in that *Wireframe* was still singling out when the Xbox turned 20. Starbreeze kept its name through the departure of its founder, a merger with the makers of *Payday*, an expansion into publishing and virtual reality, and a financial collapse in 2018 that put it under the protection of the Swedish courts.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1998, by [Magnus Högdahl](/vault/people/vogue); in Härnösand, Sweden, by 2000.
- **Roots:** the PC demo group [Triton](/vault/groups/triton) and its failed game *Into the Shadows*.
- **Later bases:** Uppsala, then Stockholm after the 2012 merger with Overkill.
- **Key games:** *Enclave* (2002), *The Chronicles of Riddick: Escape from Butcher Bay* (2004), *The Darkness* (2007), *Syndicate* (2012), *Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons* (2013), *Payday 2* (2013, by Overkill), *Payday 3* (2023).
- **Reconstruction:** filed December 2018, completed December 2019.

## Härnösand and Sorcery

Starbreeze grew out of Triton's collapse. Triton's programmers had spent 1995 and 1996 on *Into the Shadows* for the American publisher Scavenger, which stopped paying and, in *PC Gamer*'s words, "went spectacularly bust"; *PC Gamer* reported that "the developers, Triton, later became Starbreeze Studios". In August 2000 the company's website described "a privately held game-development company located in the city of Härnösand in Sweden", with nine staff, and stated: "Starbreeze is founded by Magnus Högdahl." He was listed as "President, Lead-programmer, Game design". Among the others were the musician Gustaf Grefberg, who as Lizardking had written music for Triton's *Crystal Dream 2*, and the level designers Jens Matthies and Jerk Gustafsson.

The first project was *Sorcery*, a first-person fantasy role-playing game "funded by Gremlin Interactive since late '98". *PC Zone* reported in May 1999 that it was due out through [Gremlin](/vault/companies/gremlin-graphics) later that year, on an engine "built from scratch" that promised "Quake III-style curves, shadows and the now obligatory dynamic lighting". Then [Infogrames](/vault/companies/infogrames) bought Gremlin. In January 2000 it terminated the contract, keeping the game's artwork, while Starbreeze kept its engine. "8 of the 10 people that was working on Sorcery are still with us," the studio told its fans that autumn.

## Enclave and the O3 merger

Starbreeze started again at once. It announced *Enclave*, a game "set in an evolved fantasy world", in March 2000, and in August the Swedish developer O3 Games, maker of the space strategy game *The Outforce*, agreed to buy the company for 3,250,000 of its own shares. The German publisher Swing! signed *Enclave* that November. It reached the Xbox in 2002, published in America by Conspiracy Entertainment. *PC Gamer* found it "unrelentingly wonderful" to look at but "deeply flawed, thanks to a combination of sudden-death scenarios and no mid-level save"; *Edge* gave it 3 out of 10. By then the team had 55 people and a futuristic strategy game, *Epsilon*, in development.

## Riddick and The Darkness

*The Chronicles of Riddick: Escape from Butcher Bay* began, according to *Retro Gamer*, as a game of the 2000 film *Pitch Black* and became a tie-in for its 2004 sequel. The studio shrank from 80 people to 25 during the game's beta, and Vivendi at one point tried to cancel it. What came out broke the rules of film tie-ins, as *Edge* put it in August 2004: it was a first-person game on the Xbox only, not a third-person game on the PlayStation 2. Starbreeze had won the argument for a first-person game with hand-to-hand fighting by prototyping quickly in its own engine. It used normal mapping, which lets low-polygon models catch the light as if they were far more detailed, and it let players see Riddick through his shadow on the walls. Vin Diesel voiced him.

*Edge* scored it 7 out of 10 and praised the "laconic, rumbling narration". Its reputation grew afterwards. In 2012 *Retro Gamer* made it a "future classic" and wrote that Starbreeze "ended up with a surprising classic on its hands"; in 2021 *Wireframe* called it "a technical marvel, putting other consoles to shame with its incredible visuals".

*The Darkness*, a first-person game whose hero *Edge* summed up as a "Mafia hitman turned demonic shapeshifter", followed from 2K Games on the Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3 in 2007. *Edge*'s studio profile in June 2008 found an 85-person studio in Uppsala, with Johan Kristiansson as chief executive and Högdahl as chief technology officer. A two-game deal with Vivendi shrank to one, a remake of *Riddick* with a new campaign, *Assault on Dark Athena*. It came out in 2009 and, *Edge* reported, sold only 100,000 copies in North America in its first month.

## The founders leave

In 2008 Starbreeze signed with [Electronic Arts](/vault/companies/electronic-arts) for two games, one based on the Jason Bourne novels and a reboot of an EA series codenamed Project RedLime. A couple of weeks after *Dark Athena* shipped in 2009, seven of Starbreeze's most experienced developers, all directors, handed in their notice. They included Högdahl, Matthies and Gustafsson, and they founded a new studio, MachineGames. "I missed the days when we were 20, 30, maybe 40 people who really loved what we were doing," Gustafsson told *Edge*. "When you grow as big as Starbreeze you have to focus more on the business side."

EA cancelled the Bourne game when it cut back its partner publishing, and Kristiansson left the games industry; Mikael Nermark succeeded him. Bethesda bought MachineGames in November 2010, by which time it had hired at least 15 more former Starbreeze staff. In 2011 *Edge* reported that Starbreeze, a studio "synonymous with in-house tech", would move to Epic's Unreal Engine after RedLime; its producer Samuel Ranta-Eskola said the choice had been to rebuild its technology from scratch or use something like Unreal. RedLime was revealed as a first-person reboot of Bullfrog's *[Syndicate](/vault/games/syndicate)* and released in February 2012. The next game was a change of tone: *Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons*, directed by the Swedish film-maker Josef Fares and published by 505 Games in 2013, made by about 30 people where *Syndicate* had used more than 100.

## Overkill and Payday

Overkill Software, a Stockholm studio founded by the brothers Bo and Ulf Andersson, had released the cooperative heist shooter *Payday: The Heist* in 2011. In 2012, Eurogamer later reported, both companies were in trouble, and they merged under the Starbreeze name; the company moved from Uppsala to Overkill's offices in Stockholm, and Bo Andersson became its chief executive. *Payday 2*, published by 505 Games in August 2013, was a hit, and its downloadable content kept selling for years.

Starbreeze spent the money on growth. It became a publisher, invested in a virtual-reality headset, StarVR, with the hardware company Acer, and placed its hopes on *Overkill's The Walking Dead*, a cooperative shooter in the *Payday* style. When it came out in 2018, *Wireframe* gave it 49%.

## Reconstruction

The collapse was quick. At the end of November 2018 Starbreeze announced cost cuts, blaming weak early sales of *The Walking Dead*. On 3 December it replaced Andersson with Nermark as acting chief executive and, on the same day, applied to the Swedish courts for reconstruction, a form of protection from creditors; it warned that "no payments can be made to suppliers for services or goods relating to the period prior to the date of filing". Two days later officers of the Swedish Economic Crime Authority searched the company's Stockholm headquarters in an investigation into suspected insider trading, took away computers and documents, and arrested a person. *Wireframe* noted that the arrest did not place Starbreeze itself under suspicion.

The reconstruction took a year. Starbreeze pulled out of publishing *System Shock 3*, selling the rights back to its developer, OtherSide, and completed the process in December 2019. In 2021 it announced that Koch Media would put €50 million into *Payday 3*, due in 2023. Mikael Nermark, who had led the company through the reconstruction, died of cancer that year.

## Payday 3 and after

*Payday 3* came out in late September 2023. Matchmaking problems marred its launch, and in November Embracer Group, whose Plaion division co-published it, said it had performed below expectations. In February 2024 Starbreeze reported that sales and player activity were "at significantly lower levels than we would like". The company bought the game's publishing rights back from Plaion in May 2025. In October 2025, under its chief executive Adolf Kristjansson, it cancelled its other major project, a game codenamed Baxter, to concentrate on *Payday*, a series it said had reached more than 50 million players.
