Capcom began manufacturing electronic game machines in 1979. Their arcade games—[Ghosts 'n Goblins](/vault/games/ghosts-n-goblins), *1942*, *Street Fighter*—built their reputation. Console franchises—*Mega Man*, *Resident Evil*, *Monster Hunter*—built their empire. Through arcade decline and console ascendance, Capcom remained essential.

## Fast facts
- **Founded:** 1979 as I.R.M. Corporation, Osaka, Japan.
- **Name:** from "Capsule Computers."
- **Arcade breakthrough:** [Ghosts 'n Goblins](/vault/games/ghosts-n-goblins) (1985).
- **Fighting revolution:** *[Street Fighter II](/vault/games/street-fighter-ii)* (1991) — not the first fighting game, but the one that made the genre competitive.
- **Console franchises:** *[Mega Man](/vault/games/mega-man)*, *[Resident Evil](/vault/games/resident-evil)*, *Devil May Cry*, *Monster Hunter*.
- **Modern success:** *Monster Hunter: World* became their best-selling game.

## The arcade era
Capcom's arcade output was prolific:
- **1942 (1984):** World War II shooter, vertical scrolling.
- **[Ghosts 'n Goblins](/vault/games/ghosts-n-goblins) (1985):** brutal platformer.
- **Commando (1985):** top-down run-and-gun.
- **Bionic Commando (1987):** grappling hook innovation.
- **Final Fight (1989):** belt-scrolling beat-em-up.

## Street Fighter II
The 1991 sequel transformed gaming:
- Eight distinct characters with unique moves.
- Six-button control scheme.
- Competitive depth that created tournaments.
- Arcade revenue that seemed impossible.
- Home conversions that sold millions.

*Street Fighter II* didn't invent fighting games, but it invented fighting game *culture*.

## How Capcom reached Britain

Capcom did not sell to British players directly in the eighties. It licensed, and it licensed to two firms in succession.

**Elite Systems** had the coin-ops first. *Commando* arrived at Christmas 1985 as, in *Your Computer*'s words, "developed with the aid of Capcom to create the closest possible duplication of the original arcade game" — the official version, and the distinction mattered, because unofficial lookalikes were the norm. By March 1986 *CRASH* was describing *Ghosts and Goblins* as Elite continuing "their series of Capcom arcade conversions".

The licence then moved to **[U.S. Gold](/vault/companies/us-gold)**, through its **Go!** label. *Your Sinclair* reported it in February 1988: "US Gold (or rather its offshoot, Go!) has signed a huge deal with Jap arcadesters Capcom, so expect to see a number of brainblending koin-op konversions." *Side Arms*, *Speed Rumbler*, *Tiger Road* and *Bionic Commando* followed, the last of them programmed by the British house Software Creations.

One line in *The Games Machine*'s June 1988 preview explains what the licence actually bought, and it is not what you would guess:

> "**Capcom do their own C64 conversions, but GO! prefer to redesign for the UK market.**"

The Japanese versions existed. The British licensee chose to write its own anyway. That is the same judgement Ocean was making about [Konami](/vault/companies/konami) in the same years, and it is the reason a British player's memory of a Capcom game is often of a game Capcom did not write.

## Home computer versions
Capcom games reached 8-bit and 16-bit computers:
- **[Ghosts 'n Goblins](/vault/games/ghosts-n-goblins):** ported everywhere.
- **1942, 1943:** vertical shooters converted with varying success.
- **Street Fighter II:** Amiga and ST versions available.
- **Licensed conversions:** [US Gold](/vault/companies/us-gold), [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software), others handled ports.

## The console transition
As arcades declined, Capcom pivoted:
- **Mega Man (1987):** NES platformer, spawned massive franchise.
- **Resident Evil (1996):** defined survival horror.
- **Devil May Cry (2001):** created stylish action genre.
- **Monster Hunter (2004):** became phenomenon in Japan, then worldwide.

## Legacy
Capcom adapted better than most arcade companies. Their franchises span decades; their design philosophy—challenge, precision, spectacle—remains consistent. From Arthur in his underwear to Ryu's hadouken to zombies in Raccoon City, Capcom's characters define gaming history.
