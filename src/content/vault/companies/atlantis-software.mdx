Atlantis Software came to games from video. Its founders ran a business that distributed horror films on cassette, and in the spring of 1984 they decided that computer games, like videos, should sell in volume at a low price. Atlantis was one of the first publishers to follow [Mastertronic](/vault/companies/mastertronic) to £1.99, and it did something the arcade-minded budget labels rarely tried: alongside the shoot-'em-ups it sold card games, board games and a seafaring trading game. One of its card games won *CRASH* readers' vote as the best of its kind in 1984.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** January 1984, in London, by Michael Cole and Rodger Coghill.
- **First games:** four, released in May 1984 at £1.99.
- **Address:** 19 Prebend Street, Islington, London N1, in 1984–85; later 28 Station Road, London SE25.
- **Formats:** Spectrum and Commodore 64 first, with VIC-20, Amstrad and MSX games planned from 1985.
- **Labels:** Atlantis at £1.99, and Atlantis Gold at £2.99 from 1985.
- **Best-known games:** *Eights* and *Chinese Patience* (David Simon), *Vagan Attack*, *Master Mariner*, *Connect 4* and the *Mafia Contract* adventures.

## From videos to games

*[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* told the company's story in November 1984. Rodger Coghill ran Atlantis Video Productions, a video distributor in Wapping, and Michael Cole had been managing director of one of the largest software distributors in Britain. (*CRASH* added that its own artist, [Oliver Frey](/vault/people/oliver-frey), had once designed video box covers for Atlantis.) *[Sinclair User](/vault/magazines/sinclair-user)* named some of the films: *Nightmare Maker*, *One Dark Night* and *Plague*.

The two men reasoned that with four million home computers expected in British homes by the end of 1984, and half their users under sixteen, games cost too much to sell in the numbers they should. A "pocket-money price" was the answer. Cole spent five months evaluating hundreds of games at the company's Islington premises and picked four to release in May 1984. In July *Sinclair User* reported that Atlantis had matched Mastertronic's £1.99 and was releasing four Spectrum and Commodore games a month. Atlantis advertised in *CRASH* for more, offering "high royalties on world-wide sales". By November it had sixteen games on sale and expected twenty by Christmas. "We were amazed at the high standard of some of the games sent to us for evaluation," Cole said.

## Cards, boards and the high seas

*CRASH* reviewed the first three in October 1984. Its editorial had promised to back "any software house that could produce really good programs at a cheap price", and now it had found "three games from Atlantis Software" that were "worth the asking price and that haven't been written down to match the price". *Vagan Attack*, by John Green, was a *Star Trek*-style space game with real-time battles. "It's amazing that a game of this quality and complexity should be on sale at £1.99," one reviewer wrote. *Master Mariner* was a trading game in which the player sailed a brig in search of a fortune.

The third was a card game. *Eights*, by David Simon, dealt the player and the computer seven cards each. Its *CRASH* reviewer explained why such games usually failed: "in the end it is much easier to drag the real game out and play it rather than play it on the computer. But Eights seems to have overcome that." *CRASH*'s readers agreed, and voted it the best board, card or puzzle game of 1984. Atlantis put the award at the top of its next advertisement as "proof that budget software can be quality software".

David Simon followed it with *Chinese Patience*, a two-player version of patience against the Spectrum, in which, *CRASH* found, "the computer plays a near-perfect game" and "grinds your mistake into your face". *Connect 4* was "simply a computer version of the well known plastic wall divider with holes", as *CRASH* put it. Atlantis also sold adventures, among them *Mafia Contract*, and in January 1987 *CRASH*'s adventure column counted Atlantis, "not least with their famous Mafia games", among the budget publishers that had kept adventuring alive.

Not every reader agreed with the reviewers. In January 1987 one wrote to *CRASH* that *Pro Golf* was among "the most accurate and enjoyable games I have ever played"; *CRASH* gave it 26% the following month.

## Later years

Atlantis kept going after most of its first-wave rivals had gone. By 1987 it had moved to Station Road in south London. In 1989 and 1990 its games turned up on magazine cover tapes: *CRASH* gave away *Sceptre of Bagdad* on its Powertape in September 1989 and *Survivors* and *Luna Atac* in 1990, and *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* put *Rollin'* and *Connect 4* on its Christmas 1990 cassette. *[Your Sinclair](/vault/magazines/your-sinclair)* reviewed a new Atlantis game, *Hobgoblin*, in September 1991, and Atlantis four-game packs were still being sold, and hacked for cheats in *[Amstrad Action](/vault/magazines/amstrad-action)*, in 1992.
