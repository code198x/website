## Overview

Atlantis sold cheap games from 1985 to 1988, at **£1.99 and later £2.99**, and was reviewed steadily in *CRASH* and *ZZAP!64* throughout. Its catalogue is worth reading closely because of what sits in it beside the arcade games.

## Fast facts

- **Press evidence in this library:** *CRASH* and *ZZAP!64*, **1985–1988**, at **£1.99–£2.99**.
- **Arcade-style titles:** *Rattler*, *Rumpus*, *Nightmare*, *Timezone*, *Time Rings*, *Countdown*, *Dickie's Diamonds*, *Cerius*, *Pro Golf*.
- **And also:** *Connect 4* (72% in *CRASH*, £1.99) and *Chinese Patience* (68%, £1.99).

## Card games on the cheap shelf

*Connect 4* and *Chinese Patience* look like filler and are the most interesting things here.

A £1.99 computerised patience game in 1986 is not competing with *Knight Lore*. It is competing with **a deck of cards**, and it wins on exactly one thing: the computer shuffles, deals, enforces the rules and never miscounts. Everything the genre has offered ever since is already present.

The audience is different too. Someone buying *Chinese Patience* is not the teenager the industry thought it was selling to — they may be the parent, or the person who wanted the machine for something other than arcade games and found one thing on the shelf that suited them.

Budget was where this could happen. A full-price publisher could not justify £7.95 for a card game, so it never made one; at £1.99 the arithmetic worked, and the games that nobody was designing a market strategy around got made anyway. **The cheap shelf is where the unfashionable audiences were served**, decades before anyone called it casual gaming.

## The first budget wave

Atlantis also dates the shift. Its *CRASH* reviews start in **early 1985** — *Rattler* and *Rumpus* in issue 14, *Nightmare* and *Battles* in issue 15 — which puts it in the first wave of £1.99 publishing alongside [Mastertronic](/vault/companies/mastertronic), well before the budget market was an established tier with its own labels and imprints.

Reading its run in order shows a market forming in real time: the price settles, the review columns make room, and by 1987 the £2.99 rung has appeared above it.

## Why it mattered

Atlantis is the entry for **breadth at the bottom of the market**. The budget shelf is usually described in terms of quality — cheap games, some good, mostly not — and the more useful description is variety. It carried arcade games, card games, board-game conversions, quizzes and utilities, because at £1.99 anything could be tried.

That range is invisible in a history assembled from review scores and classics lists, and it is a substantial part of what people owned.
