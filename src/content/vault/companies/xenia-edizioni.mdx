**Xenia Edizioni S.r.l.** of Via Dell'Annunciata 31, Milan, is the name on the colophons of the Italian [*Zzap!*](/vault/magazines/zzap-64) and [*The Games Machine*](/vault/magazines/the-games-machine-italy) from September 1991. That was the month [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield), the British company that had licensed both titles, went into voluntary liquidation. The magazines carried on without it. *Zzap!* ran for two more years, and *The Games Machine* stayed with Xenia for fourteen years before it was sold. It is still published.

## A new name on the same magazines

Until July 1991 both magazines named [Edizioni Hobby](/vault/companies/edizioni-hobby) as publisher. The September issues named Xenia Edizioni instead, and nothing else changed. In January 1992 *The Games Machine*'s colophon still listed Roberto Ferri as *direttore responsabile* (the editor legally answerable for the magazine), editorial post to Casella Postale 853, Milan, printing by Rotolito Lombarda at Cernusco sul Naviglio, advertising through Spaziotre on Piazzale Archinto and distribution by ME.PE. on Viale Famagosta. The court registration was still the one Edizioni Hobby had obtained in September 1988: Tribunale di Milano no. 587.

The colophons record the new name but not the business behind it. One of the magazines' writers supplied a clue. In the last Italian *Zzap!*, in December 1993, Fabio Rossi remembered phoning the publisher in 1988, when "si chiamava ancora Edizioni Hobby" ("it was still called Edizioni Hobby"). On his account, Xenia was the same publishing house under a new name rather than a buyer.

The September 1991 issues still printed the line "Su licenza di Newsfield Publications". From October it was gone, and the two magazines were Italian publications with British names.

## What it published

The Italian *Zzap!* closed with its December 1993 issue, a year after the last British *ZZAP!64*.

*The Games Machine* grew instead. Its December 1991 issue came out with extra pages under the seasonal title *The Christmas Machine*, and specials followed: a Christmas 1994 special sold as a supplement to issue 70, and in the summer of 1998 an unnumbered special to keep readers company until September, carrying the reviews that had not fitted into the usual double summer issue. By February 1992 the same staff were producing *TGM Action*, a companion monthly sold with a disk of playable demos. Its editorial admitted that it had begun as an experiment and explained why the disk came separately: fixing it to the magazine, as many foreign titles did, would have pushed the cover price too high for the Italian market. In 1998 the summer special came with a back issue of *PC Force*, which the editorial called Xenia's new title, "dedicata principalmente a trucchi e soluzioni" ("devoted mainly to cheats and solutions").

The colophons show Xenia publishing *The Games Machine* at the end of the 1990s, and Wikipedia records that it sold the magazine to Future Media Italy in November 2005.
