When *Edge* reviewed *Grand Theft Auto: Vice City* at Christmas 2002, the developer box no longer said DMA Design. It said "In-house (Rockstar North)". The studio that had made *[Lemmings](/vault/games/lemmings)* in Dundee now worked in Edinburgh under a new name, and it had just followed *[Grand Theft Auto III](/vault/games/gta-iii)* with a game the magazine called "one of the year's essential games". Rockstar North has made the main *Grand Theft Auto* games ever since. The last of them, *GTA V*, has sold more than 230 million copies, and the next, *GTA VI*, is due in November 2026.

## Fast facts

- **Named:** 2002. Before that it was [DMA Design](/vault/companies/dma-design), founded in Dundee in 1988.
- **Based:** Edinburgh.
- **Owner:** Take-Two Interactive, as part of [Rockstar Games](/vault/companies/rockstar).
- **Best-known games:** the *Grand Theft Auto* series from *Vice City* (2002) onwards, and *Manhunt* (2003).

## From DMA to Rockstar North

The [DMA Design](/vault/companies/dma-design) entry tells the earlier story. Take-Two bought DMA from Infogrames at the end of 1999 and moved the studio from Dundee to Edinburgh. *Edge* reported in 2001 that most of the staff left after the move. The studio had 3D experience from *Body Harvest* and *Wild Metal Country*, but it now needed Criterion's [RenderWare](/vault/techniques/renderware) middleware "to bring the team up to speed" on *Grand Theft Auto III*. That game came out on the PlayStation 2 in 2001, credited in *Edge* to "Rockstar/DMA".

The name changed in 2002. Wikipedia says DMA Design became Rockstar Studios in March of that year and Rockstar North in May. The period press followed: in May 2002 *Edge* wrote of "DMA (now Rockstar)", and by October *IGN Unplugged* was writing of "Rockstar North (formerly known as DMA Design)".

## Vice City, Manhunt and San Andreas

*Vice City* (2002) moved the series to a city modelled on 1980s Miami, all neon, big hair and print shirts. *IGN Unplugged* saw it before release and noted that, "for the first time in the series", players could walk inside buildings. *Edge* gave it eight out of ten. It liked the new lead character, voiced by Ray Liotta, and the properties the player could buy, but found cracks in the city: crash bugs, characters who walked through each other, and a police car that stopped next to a gunfight only because the lights were red.

*Manhunt* (2003) was a different kind of game: a violent stealth game for the PlayStation 2, in which, as *Edge* described it, the longer the player waited before attacking an unsuspecting victim, the more violent the cut-scene that followed. It carried an 18 certificate in Britain. In July 2004, after the parents of a murdered Leicester teenager blamed the game for influencing his killer, the retailers Dixons and Game took it off their shelves. The British Board of Film Classification stood by its rating.

*Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas* followed on the PlayStation 2 on 29 October 2004, with a PC version the next year. *Edge* noted how far the player's character could now be customised, from the car's wheels and paint to a trip to the barber.

## GTA IV, Red Dead and GTA V

*Grand Theft Auto IV* (2008) brought the series to the PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360 and returned it to Liberty City. *Grand Theft Auto V* (2013) had three playable characters and an online mode, *GTA Online*, that Rockstar has kept adding to ever since. Take-Two reported in August 2026 that *GTA V* had sold more than 230 million copies across three generations of consoles, and the series as a whole nearly 475 million.

Rockstar's studios increasingly shared their games. *Red Dead Redemption 2* (2018) was developed by all of Rockstar's studios together, Rockstar North among them. In 2020 Rockstar bought the Dundee developer Ruffian Games, which had broken away from Realtime Worlds, and renamed it Rockstar Dundee, which brought the company back to the city where DMA had started.

## Leslie Benzies

Leslie Benzies, Rockstar North's president, left the company in 2016. In April 2016 he sued Rockstar and Take-Two, claiming more than $150 million in unpaid royalties. Take-Two's annual report for the year to March 2019 records that the case ended that year in a confidential settlement.

## Grand Theft Auto VI and the dismissals

*Grand Theft Auto VI* was first due in 2025. Rockstar delayed it twice, first to May 2026, and in November 2025 announced a release date of 19 November 2026 on the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.

In autumn 2025 Rockstar dismissed 31 staff in Britain, many of them at Rockstar North, all members of the Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain. Rockstar said they had leaked confidential information in a Discord channel that people outside the company could see. The union said they had been sacked for organising, in a private union channel. In January 2026 an employment tribunal in Glasgow refused the dismissed workers interim relief; the full claim was still to be heard.
