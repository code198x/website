Brian Jobling once summed up his company's history as a full circle. "Starting with £1.99 tape cassette games, we've moved up to full price console £59.99 retail games and now, with the build up of mobile gaming, we are back to 99 pence!" The company he had co-founded in 1987 as the Newcastle budget label [Zeppelin Games](/vault/companies/zeppelin-games), spent its second life as Eutechnyx, a studio that made almost nothing but driving games: racing cars, trucks, a James Bond car game and the official NASCAR games.

## Fast facts

- **Same company as:** [Zeppelin Games](/vault/companies/zeppelin-games), registered on 30 September 1987; renamed Merit Studios (Europe) in 1994 and Eutechnyx on 12 March 1997.
- **Run by:** Brian Jobling and his brother Darren.
- **Based:** Newcastle upon Tyne, later Gateshead.
- **Games:** *Total Drivin'* (1997), *Max Power Racing*, *Le Mans 24 Hours* (1999), *007 Racing*, *Big Mutha Truckers* and its sequel, *Ferrari Challenge*, NASCAR games, *Auto Club Revolution*.
- **NASCAR business sold:** 2014.
- **Dissolved:** 20 May 2025.

## A new name for CD consoles

By the mid-nineties Zeppelin had been sold to the American publisher Merit Studios and renamed Merit Studios (Europe). For the new CD-based consoles it took another name. Darren Jobling found it in an old dictionary, "just below Eurythmics", Brian told *Retro Gamer*: "Eutechnics", meaning "a well-ordered sequence of events", which he thought exactly described computer game code. The domain name was taken, so it became Eutechnyx. "We have regretted it ever since, having to spell it at least five times every day!"

The renamed company left Merit, earned a licence to develop and publish PlayStation games and signed a three-year publishing deal with [Infogrames](/vault/companies/infogrames). It had two PlayStation games in development, Brian recalled: a role-playing game called *Sato City* and a driving game, *Total Drivin'*. Infogrames wanted a racing game. "So, Total Drivin' it was and the rest is history!" *Edge* previewed it in August 1997, noting a coding tool that worked out what the driver could see from the car and drew only that.

## Le Mans and the racing years

The Infogrames years set Eutechnyx's course. After *Total Drivin'* and *Max Power Racing* came *Le Mans 24 Hours*, sold in America as *Test Drive Le Mans*. When *Edge* visited the Newcastle studio in early 1999 the team was trying to fit the 24-hour race into a console. The PC version would put all 48 cars on the 13km Circuit de la Sarthe; the PlayStation would manage 16. There was a full 24-hour mode with a save option in the pits, and parts could fall off the cars. "They're limping back into the pits," said the programmer Andrew Perella. *Edge* wondered what would happen to a PlayStation left running for 24 hours. At the end of 1999 *PC Zone* introduced the developer as "formerly known as Merit Software (formerly known as Zeppelin Games)", with "some 300 games" behind it.

When the Infogrames deal ended, *Retro Gamer* reports, the Jobling brothers bought out the French company's interest to regain control. Licences followed: *007 Racing*, built around the cars of the James Bond films, *Ferrari Challenge* and NASCAR. *Big Mutha Truckers*, a comic trucking game, was by Brian's account a million-seller, and the one game of more than 350 that brought the company death threats, from American truckers who took offence at its picture of their lives. A sequel followed in 2005, published in Britain by Empire.

## Free-to-play and the end

By 2010 Brian Jobling was managing director and the company was based in Gateshead. That year it took $10 million of venture capital, which went into *Auto Club Revolution*, a free-to-play racing game run partly in a web browser, with car makers supplying their design data so the models matched the real cars. In 2012, with Darren Jobling as chief operating officer, it made a free standalone game for BMW, *The BMW Experience*, that linked to *Auto Club Revolution*.

The business did not last much longer. Eutechnyx sold its NASCAR business in 2014, and in 2015 some of its staff moved to ZeroLight, a company making 3D virtual showrooms for car manufacturers. The company first registered as Zeppelin Games in 1987 was dissolved on 20 May 2025.
