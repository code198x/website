For a British games player in the 1980s and early 1990s, a surprising number of the magazines on the newsagent's shelf came from one company, and it was not a computer company at all. **EMAP**, short for East Midland Allied Press, was a newspaper group from Peterborough and the market towns around it. It published *[Computer and Video Games](/vault/magazines/computer-and-video-games)* from 1981, bought *[Sinclair User](/vault/magazines/sinclair-user)* and *Commodore User* in 1984, took over *[ACE](/vault/magazines/ace-magazine)* in 1989, and launched *[The One](/vault/magazines/the-one)*, *[Mean Machines](/vault/magazines/mean-machines)* and *[Nintendo Magazine System](/vault/magazines/nintendo-magazine-system)*. For most of that time they were all made in the same building, Priory Court in Farringdon Lane, London.

## From local newspapers

According to later secondary accounts, the company grew out of the Winfrey family's local papers, among them the *Spalding Guardian*, the *Lynn News* and the *Peterborough Advertiser*. In 1947 they were merged to form East Midland Allied Press. Its first magazines came about because its printing presses stood idle between editions of the local papers: *Angling Times* started in 1953, and in 1956 EMAP bought *Motor Cycle News*.

The Peterborough roots showed in the small print of its computer magazines. Readers of *Computer and Video Games*' first issue, in November 1981, sent their subscriptions to Bretton Court in Peterborough, and back issues of *The One* came from EMAP Frontline in the same city.

## Computer and Video Games

*C&VG* was launched in November 1981. Its first issue barred "EMAP employees and their relatives" from its competitions and gave EMAP's office at Durrant House, 8 Herbal Hill, London EC1R, as its address. From the second issue the colophon was explicit: "Published and distributed by EMAP National Publications Ltd", with the copyright held by Computer & Video Games Limited.

Another company also claimed a share in it. ECC Publications, which launched *Sinclair User* in April 1982, advertised itself as "the pioneers of *Practical Computing*, *WHICH COMPUTER?*, and *Computer & Video Games*", and ran that advertisement in *C&VG*'s own pages. The public sources do not explain how the two companies divided the work.

## Buying the rivals

In September 1984 *Sinclair User* told its readers, under the headline "Same paper, new owners", that ECC's Sinclair titles, *Sinclair User* and *Sinclair Programs*, had been "acquired by East Midland Allied Press, the publisher of Computer and Video Games". The new address was EMAP Business and Computer Publications, 67 Clerkenwell Road.

*Commodore User* changed hands the same year. Its first owner was the Paradox Group, in Enfield Road, London N1, whose name was still in the June 1984 issue. By October 1984 the small print read: "Commodore User is published twelve times a year by EMAP Business and Computer Publications Ltd", at Priory Court, 30-32 Farringdon Lane. So EMAP now published the main magazines for both of the rival home computers, and *C&VG*, which reviewed games for both.

The next purchase was *ACE*, which Future Publishing had launched in Bath in October 1987. Future's name was still in the June 1989 issue; by September the magazine was "© EMAP B&CP 1989" and edited at Priory Court.

## Protecting its titles

EMAP was prepared to go to court over its magazines. In the summer of 1985 [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield)'s *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* gave four pages of its August issue to a lampoon of *Sinclair User*. When *Sinclair User*'s editor saw an advance copy, EMAP sought an injunction. It won, and *CRASH* had to recall the issue, cut out the four pages and redistribute it with a sticker explaining why they were missing. *CRASH* retold the story two years later, noting that its own circulation then climbed "from around 50,000 copies a month to over 100,000".

The two magazines ended up under one roof. After Newsfield collapsed, [Europress](/vault/companies/europress) passed *CRASH* to EMAP in March 1992, and *Sinclair User* announced in May that it had "incorporated Crash".

## New magazines for new machines

EMAP launched *The One* in October 1988 for the new 16-bit computers, the [Atari ST](/vault/systems/atari-st) and [Amiga](/vault/systems/commodore-amiga). In 1989 *Commodore User* became *[CU Amiga](/vault/magazines/cu-amiga)*.

Consoles came next. In July 1990 *C&VG*'s editor [Julian Rignall](/vault/people/julian-rignall), the artist Gary Harrod and EMAP's publisher Graham Taylor met at a café in Farringdon to plan a console magazine. *Mean Machines* went on sale in October 1990, and by the first half of 1991 it had audited sales of 50,098 a month. In October 1992 EMAP split it into *Mean Machines Sega* and *Nintendo Magazine System*, which carried Nintendo's characters under licence to "EMAP Images Limited". *Sinclair User* reported a "real fight" at "EMAP Towers" over which of the two would be better.

## The end of the computer titles

As the home computer market shrank, the computer magazines went. *CU Amiga*'s October 1998 issue announced that it was "the last ever issue", closed by EMAP because of falling sales. By then the games titles came from EMAP Images and then EMAP Active, which was still publishing *C&VG* in November 1999. By October 2001 *C&VG* was published by Dennis Publishing. Later secondary accounts date that sale to 2001 and say Future bought the title from Dennis in 2004.

EMAP itself was broken up in 2008. It sold its consumer magazines, radio and television businesses to the German publisher Bauer, and the rest of the business was bought by Apax and the Guardian Media Group; it was renamed Top Right Group in 2012 and Ascential in 2015.
