In 1994 *Edge* was let into a Sega building in Ohta-ku, Tokyo, to meet the man it called "Sega's undisputed coin-op champion". Yu Suzuki was general manager of Amusement R&D Department 2, AM2 for short: about 100 people inside an arcade division of around 600. His department had just made *Virtua Racing* and *Virtua Fighter*, and was finishing *Daytona USA*. For most of the 1990s AM2's name on a cabinet meant the newest arcade hardware Sega had, and the games were built around it.

## Fast facts

- **Parent:** [Sega](/vault/companies/sega).
- **Head:** [Yu Suzuki](/vault/people/yu-suzuki), from its beginnings until 2001.
- **Name:** Amusement Machine Research and Development Department 2; "AM" for amusement, meaning arcade machines.
- **Before the name:** Suzuki's team made *[Hang-On](/vault/games/hang-on)*, *[Space Harrier](/vault/games/space-harrier)*, *[Out Run](/vault/games/out-run)* and *[After Burner](/vault/games/after-burner)* in 1985–87, before it was called AM2.
- **First AM2 game:** *Virtua Racing* (1992), in Suzuki's own account.
- **1990s:** *[Virtua Fighter](/vault/games/virtua-fighter)*, *[Daytona USA](/vault/games/daytona-usa)*, *[Virtua Cop](/vault/games/virtua-cop)*, *Virtua Fighter 2* and *3*, *[Shenmue](/vault/games/shenmue)*.
- **Later names:** Sega-AM2 Co., Ltd. (2001–04), a subsidiary; then a Sega department again, renamed R&D2 in 2011.

## Before AM2

*Out Run* is sometimes credited to "Sega AM2", but no department of that name existed in 1986. Suzuki joined Sega in April 1983. "When I started in 1983 there was only one division — the amusement [coin-op] division," he told *Edge*. His games of the mid-1980s came from a team he led inside it: *Hang-On* and *Space Harrier* in 1985, *Out Run* in 1986, *After Burner* in 1987, then *Power Drift*, *G-LOC* and the rotating R360 cabinet. *Wireframe* describes AM2 as "the division Suzuki set up within Sega during the second half of the 1980s".

Some of that work was done in secret. Interviews from 1988 and 1996, translated by the website Shmuplations, recall that *After Burner* and *Power Drift* were developed at "Studio 128", a separate office about five minutes' walk from Sega's headquarters. Suzuki had ordered that nobody enter without his permission, and one of his team remembered a staff member turning away Sega's president, who had come to see how the game was going: "Sorry, no permission, no entry." Sega Retro, which has traced the company's internal departments, says the team became R&D 8 around 1988 and took the AM2 name around 1990, when Sega reorganised its arcade and console development.

## Polygons

By the end of the 1980s Sega's rivals were ahead in polygon 3D: Sega Retro notes that Atari Games and Namco were already making polygon games, Atari's *Hard Drivin'* among them. Sega's answer was the Model 1 arcade board, which took three years to develop against about 12 months for a typical new board, Suzuki said. It used an NEC V60 processor, "actually pretty slow" at 16 MHz, and did its 3D maths on four Fujitsu signal processors. "Virtua Racing was the first game we produced," he told *Edge*; the team grew from 10 people to 25 during the project. Suzuki researched the 1992 Ferrari and McLaren-Honda engines, talked to drivers and engineers and hired a Honda NSX for a while, and the finished game used about 120 physical parameters. *Wireframe* put it this way: where *Hard Drivin'* "chugged along at a dismal pace", *Virtua Racing* felt like "the helm of a dangerously quick Formula One car".

After *Virtua Racing*, AM2 split into two teams. One made *Virtua Fighter* (1993), whose success, *Wireframe* wrote, "hinged entirely on one question: whether it was possible to render and animate full-3D humans on the Model 1 hardware". The other made *Daytona USA* on a new board, the [Model 2](/vault/techniques/model-2). General Electric had approached Sega with texture-mapping chips, the two companies went into partnership in August 1992, and building the board took a year, Suzuki said, with another year for the game. Model 2 drew almost twice as many polygons as Model 1, about 300,000 a second. He had wanted 40 linked cabinets for *Daytona*, "just like in a real race"; eight proved feasible.

## Arcade and home

AM2 was an arcade department first, but its games also came home. It converted *Virtua Racing* to the Mega Drive, with the help of the SVP chip in the cartridge; "without it, I don't think I would have even attempted it," Suzuki said. When the [Saturn](/vault/systems/sega-saturn) went on sale in Japan in November 1994, *Edge* found *Virtua Fighter* the only worthwhile game on it and called it "a near-perfect conversion". *Virtua Fighter 2*, *Virtua Cop* and *Fighting Vipers* followed in the arcades, as did *Scud Race*, led by [Toshihiro Nagoshi](/vault/people/toshihiro-nagoshi). *Computer & Video Games* reported in September 1996 that Suzuki had unveiled *Virtua Fighter 3* in Tokyo on 26 July; Sega Retro lists it among AM2's games for the new Model 3 board.

## Shenmue

AM2's biggest project was a game for home. Suzuki began an adventure for the Saturn built around the *Virtua Fighter* characters; it moved to the [Dreamcast](/vault/systems/sega-dreamcast) and became *Shenmue* (1999), with around 300 staff and, *Wireframe* reported, a budget of $47 million. *Edge* listed the developer simply as "Sega (AM2)".

## Sega-AM2 and after

Sega restructured its development several times as its console business failed. According to Sega Retro, AM2 briefly merged with the CSK Research Institute in 2000, and in 2001 became a separate, wholly owned company, Sega-AM2 Co., Ltd. *Edge* credited it with *Shenmue II*, *Virtua Fighter 4* for the PlayStation 2, the GameCube's *Beach Spikers* and the arcade sequel *OutRun 2*. Suzuki was no longer in charge: Sega Retro records that Sega's long-serving head of research and development, Hisashi Suzuki, became the company's president in 2001, and that Yu Suzuki left in 2003 to found his own subsidiary studio, Digital Rex.

When Sega folded its studios back into the parent company in 2004, AM2 became a department again, and went on making *Virtua Fighter 5*, *After Burner Climax* and the arcade series *Border Break*. Sega Retro says the AM name was dropped in 2011, when the department became R&D2, though it still sometimes traded as AM2 and used the palm-tree logo it had carried since *Virtua Fighter*. By its account, the department merged with Sega's Ryu ga Gotoku Studio in the early 2020s.
