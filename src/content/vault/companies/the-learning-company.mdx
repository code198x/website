The Learning Company built an educational software empire through engaging games that made learning feel like play. *Reader Rabbit* became synonymous with early literacy software, while acquisitions brought *Math Blaster* and *The Oregon Trail* under one roof. The company's history mirrors the rise and consolidation of the educational software industry.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1980 by Ann McCormick, Leslie Grimm, and Teri Perl.
- **Key franchise:** *Reader Rabbit* (1983-present).
- **Acquisitions:** [MECC](/vault/companies/mecc), Broderbund, [Mindscape](/vault/companies/mindscape).
- **Acquisition:** purchased by Mattel (1999) for $3.5 billion; massive writedown followed.
- **Current:** brands survive under various owners.

## Reader Rabbit

The flagship franchise:
- **Debut:** 1983 for Apple II.
- **Target:** early childhood literacy.
- **Approach:** phonics through mini-games.
- **Longevity:** dozens of titles across decades.

## The catalogue at 1988 prices

*COMPUTE!*'s educational software guide of May 1988 lists three Learning Company titles
together, which gives a clearer picture of the business than the *Reader Rabbit* headline
alone:

| Title | Ages | Platforms | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Reader Rabbit* | 4–7 | Apple II, Apple IIgs, C64, MS-DOS | **$39.95–$59.95** |
| *Math Rabbit* | 4–7 | Apple II, MS-DOS | **$39.95** |
| *Rocky's Boots* | 9 & up | Apple II, C64, MS-DOS | **$34.95–$49.95** |

Two things stand out.

**The company was already running a franchise, not a title.** *Math Rabbit* is in the guide
alongside *Reader Rabbit* in 1988 — the rabbit had been extended into a second subject within
five years of the first game.

**And *Rocky's Boots* is the outlier worth noticing.** Aged nine and up, and about **digital
logic** — building working circuits from gates. A company whose public identity is early
literacy was also shipping a game that taught children how computers work, at the
same price, on the same shelf.

⚠ *Reader Rabbit* and *Math Rabbit* span four platforms here including MS-DOS and the C64;
[MECC](/vault/companies/mecc)'s *Oregon Trail* in the same table is Apple II only. The
company that would eventually buy MECC was already the broader operation.

## Acquisition strategy

Building an empire:
- **MECC (1995):** brought *The Oregon Trail*.
- **Broderbund (1998):** added *Carmen Sandiego*.
- **Mindscape (1998):** more educational titles.
- **Mattel disaster (1999):** overvaluation led to massive losses.
