Arkane Studios was started to make a sequel nobody else would. In 1999 Raphaël Colantonio set up a small company in Lyon to make first-person role-playing games like *Ultima Underworld*, the 1992 dungeon game from the team that became [Looking Glass](/vault/companies/looking-glass). It was, he later told *Edge*, a time "when all the RPG developers were folding, or about to fold". Its rough first game won a cult following, its second was built on [Valve](/vault/companies/valve)'s Source engine, and its first game for Bethesda, *[Dishonored](/vault/games/dishonored)* (2012), scored nine out of ten in *Edge*.

## Not quite Ultima Underworld 3

Colantonio and his team, then four people, did not want a game merely inspired by *Ultima Underworld*: they wanted to make "an actual U3", *Edge* reported in 2006. They approached Paul Neurath, who had founded Looking Glass. "Paul was very excited about it — but he had the IP rights, not the distribution rights," Colantonio said. "So then we met with EA… and that's where that story ends."

What Arkane made instead was *Arx Fatalis*. *PC Zone* previewed it at the end of 2000 as a first-person RPG set in a frozen world "where all life, magical and otherwise, has been forced underground", in the vein of *System Shock 2* and *Deus Ex*, with spells cast by drawing shapes on the screen. No publisher had signed it yet. *PC Gamer* introduced the "French developers Arkane" the following spring. The game came out for the PC in 2002.

*Computer Gaming World*'s review in March 2003 began with everything against it: "The box is ugly. The name is dopey. It's a blatant Ultima Underworld wannabe. It's got a French development team. OK, that's all true, but so are some other things." The reviewer went on to call it "the most original RPG not named Morrowind released in 2002". Colantonio himself admitted it had "a lot of rough edges and a lot of bugs". The Xbox version, published by DreamCatcher in 2003, fared worse: *Edge* gave it four out of ten.

## Source and Might and Magic

The Xbox version brought Arkane its next game. While demonstrating it at E3, Colantonio said, a visitor from Valve stopped to say he had liked *Arx Fatalis*, took the team to see the *Half-Life 2* demo, and put them in touch about the Source engine. Valve let Arkane use it before *[Half-Life 2](/vault/games/half-life-2)* was released. A Source demo caught the interest of [Ubisoft](/vault/companies/ubisoft), which had bought the *Might and Magic* series and suggested combining the two.

The result was *Dark Messiah of Might and Magic* (PC, 2006), a first-person fantasy game built around sword fighting, physics and magic, with a multiplayer mode developed separately at Kuju. "At the end of Arx we were nine people, and now we're about 30, plus external contractors," Colantonio told *Edge* in 2006. He had recruited the *Thief* designer Randy Smith and *Half-Life 2*'s art director Viktor Antonov as consultants, and had persuaded a respected author of *Thief* fan missions to give up his accounting job, sell his house and move his family from the United States to Lyon for a year of level design. Asked how, he said: "You just have to ask."

## ZeniMax and Dishonored

On 12 August 2010 ZeniMax Media, the owner of [Bethesda](/vault/companies/bethesda), announced that it had bought Arkane. By then the studio had offices in Lyon and in Austin, Texas, and was led by Colantonio with Harvey Smith, the lead designer of *[Deus Ex](/vault/games/deus-ex)*. It had also worked on 2K Marin's *BioShock 2*. "After 11 years of independent development," Colantonio said, "we couldn't think of a better publishing partner."

*Dishonored* (2012), co-directed by Colantonio and Smith and published by Bethesda for the PC, Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3, was set in Dunwall, a plague-ridden city run on whale oil. Its hero, Corvo, could teleport short distances with a power called Blink, and every level offered several routes. *Edge* gave it nine out of ten and singled out its Chaos system, "with the city of Dunwall becoming more or less stable in relation to your bodycount", so that a violent player faced more rats and better-defended strongholds.

## Two studios

The two studios then made separate games. Arkane Lyon made *Dishonored 2* (2016) and *Deathloop* (2021); Arkane Austin made *Prey* (2017). Colantonio stepped down as president in June 2017, after eighteen years. In 2021 [Microsoft](/vault/companies/microsoft) bought ZeniMax, and with it Arkane. Arkane Austin's next game was the vampire shooter *Redfall* (2023), and in May 2024 Microsoft closed the Austin studio. A small team was allowed back to finish a last update, with an offline mode, which came out at the end of that month. Arkane Lyon continues.

## The source code of Arx Fatalis

On 14 January 2011 Arkane released a final patch for *Arx Fatalis*, version 1.21, together with the source code of its engine under the GNU General Public License version 3, minus some proprietary components such as EAX sound. The game's art and data were not included, so players still need a copy of the original game. Fans built on the code to make Arx Libertatis, a continued version of the engine, also under the GPL, which still needs the original game's data.
