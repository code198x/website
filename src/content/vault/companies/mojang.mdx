Mojang began as the name on one programmer's spare-time project. Markus Persson put the first version of *[Minecraft](/vault/games/minecraft)* online in May 2009 under the name Mojang Specifications, sold it while it was still unfinished, and kept selling it. Only in October 2010, when the game was already making money, did he sign the papers that made Mojang a company, with two former colleagues. Four years later Microsoft paid $2.5 billion for it.

## Fast facts

- **Founded by:** [Markus Persson](/vault/people/markus-persson), as Mojang Specifications in 2009; Mojang the company was set up in October 2010 with Jakob Porser and Carl Manneh.
- **Based:** Stockholm, Sweden.
- **Games:** *Minecraft*; *Scrolls* (2014), later *Caller's Bane*; *Minecraft Dungeons* (2020, with Double Eleven); *Minecraft Legends* (2023). Mojang also published Oxeye Game Studio's *Cobalt*.
- **Owner:** [Microsoft](/vault/companies/microsoft), since 6 November 2014.
- **Name:** Mojang Studios since May 2020.

## Three friends from Midasplayer

*Edge*'s studio profile of February 2012 tells the story. Persson and Porser had worked together at Midasplayer, the company behind the King.com games portal. Persson left ("They didn't like us making games in our spare time," Porser remembered) and went to work for Carl Manneh, chief executive of a photo-sharing site. About a year later, when *Minecraft* began to take off, Persson looked for partners to start a studio. He asked Porser first, who was still at Midasplayer, and Manneh joined them as chief executive.

When *Edge* visited, on a December morning in Stockholm, Mojang had 13 staff. *Minecraft* had 18 million registered accounts, and on the day of the visit it sold 9,510 copies at £16.95 each. Persson had handed the game over to Jens Bergensten and was deciding what to make next, and Mojang had turned down around 150 offers of venture capital. One investor, Napster's founder Sean Parker, had flown Persson to London by private jet for the night. Mojang was also publishing *Cobalt*, a two-dimensional multiplayer platform game by the local studio Oxeye, and Manneh said the company would "prefer" not to release games in a finished state but to let players in early, as it had with *Minecraft*.

## Scrolls and 0x10c

Mojang's second game was Porser's. *Scrolls* was a collectable card game played out on a battlefield, in which each player builds a deck from a set of scrolls and tries to destroy three of the opponent's five idols. *Edge* found in 2012 that it was "more than a passion project" and that Mojang meant to run a long beta, tuning the game "depending on player feedback". It left beta in December 2014. Mojang closed its online services in February 2018 and re-released it free that June as *Caller's Bane*.

Persson's own next game, the space game *0x10c*, never appeared. He set it aside in 2013, citing a lack of interest and a creative block.

## Microsoft

On 15 September 2014 Microsoft announced that it would buy Mojang for $2.5 billion, and the deal closed on 6 November 2014. Persson, Porser and Manneh, the company's three owners, all left. *Minecraft* carried on under Microsoft as two lines: the original Java version for computers and the Bedrock Edition shared by consoles, phones and Windows. The [Minecraft](/vault/games/minecraft) entry tells that part of the story.

Mojang grew into a group of studios and renamed itself Mojang Studios in May 2020, the same month it released *Minecraft Dungeons*, a dungeon crawler made with Double Eleven. *Wireframe* called it "Mojang's first major spin-off for PC and consoles" and found it "mostly great fun while it lasts" but "strangely incomplete" at £15.99. *Minecraft Legends*, an action strategy game, followed in 2023.
