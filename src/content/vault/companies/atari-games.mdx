## Overview

When Atari split in 1984, the arcade division became Atari Games (later Midway Games West). Free from the home console market's collapse, they continued innovating in arcades—pioneering polygon graphics, creating genre-defining titles, and maintaining Atari's reputation for technical excellence well into the 3D era.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1984 (Atari split).
- **Location:** Milpitas, California.
- **Later name:** Midway Games West.
- **Closure:** 2003.

## Tengen, dated

The section above describes the Tengen dispute. The period press carried it as it resolved,
and the dates and figures are worth fixing in place.

*ACE*, **January 1990**:

> **Nintendo has just won its long court battle with Tengen** over [*Tetris*]. Nintendo now
> has exclusive rights to this addictive game — after some confusion over what company had
> what rights. Although this Nintendo vs Tengen court battle is over, **there is still
> another $100 million lawsuit between the two companies — this time over alleged patent
> infringement**. The final judgment in this case will be in the New Year.

Two separate actions, then: the *Tetris* rights, lost by the turn of 1990, and a **$100
million** patent suit over the lockout hardware still outstanding. See
[Nintendo](/vault/companies/nintendo) for the platform-control regime both were fought over.

The shape of it is worth stating plainly. **Atari Games was suing, and being sued by, the
company whose console had displaced its parent.** The arcade division that survived the 1984
split spent the end of the decade in court against Nintendo over the right to publish on
Nintendo's machine at all.

## Keeping the Lynx alive

A footnote that complicates the antagonism, and it is in the same magazine nine months later.

*ACE*, **October 1990**, surveying the console market, on Atari's handheld: software "is very
scarce — **Tengen is the only software developer really supporting the Lynx**. Conversions of
*Paperboy*, *Klax* and *Vindicators* are on their way before Christmas."

So while Tengen and Nintendo were in litigation, Tengen was also the only publisher keeping
the [Atari](/vault/companies/atari) Lynx supplied — with conversions of Atari Games' own
coin-ops. The arcade division was, by 1990, simultaneously the console division's last
software partner and the industry's most persistent litigant.
