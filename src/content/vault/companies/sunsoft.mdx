In the autumn of 1982 the small print of Atari's club magazine carried a credit: "KANGAROO is manufactured by Atari under license from Sun Electronics Corp." *Kangaroo* was an Atari arcade game in America, but it came from a Japanese electronics company in Kōnan, Aichi, for which games were only one of several businesses. Under the name Sunsoft it went on to make and publish games for Nintendo's 8-bit console, from the tank-and-pilot adventure *Blaster Master* to a run of film and cartoon licences led by *Batman*.

## Fast facts

- **Company:** Sun Electronics Corporation (Sun Corporation), founded in April 1971 in Kōnan, Aichi, Japan. Sunsoft is its games brand.
- **Arcade games:** from 1978, including *Kangaroo* (1982), which Atari made under licence.
- **American arm:** Sun Corporation of America, set up in 1986 by buying an American arcade company, Kitkorp.
- **[NES and Famicom](/vault/systems/nintendo-entertainment-system) games include:** *[Blaster Master](/vault/games/blaster-master)* (1988), *Batman* (1989), *Journey to Silius* (1990) and *Gimmick!* (1992).

## From arcades to the Famicom

Sun began making arcade games in 1978. In the West its best-known was *Kangaroo*, which Atari built under licence in 1982; Atari's advertising in American magazines in 1983 still carried the credit to Sun Electronics.

From the mid-1980s Sun turned to Nintendo's Famicom, and the Sunsoft name went on the boxes. Its early Famicom list mixed its own games with conversions of other companies' arcade hits, including Sega's *Fantasy Zone* (1987), and its name was on Western games too: the NES version of [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software)'s *Platoon* (1988) was a Sunsoft release. In 1986 it bought an American company, Kitkorp, which had sold Sega conversion kits to arcades, and made it Sun Corporation of America. That company later traded under the Sunsoft name.

## The NES years

*[Blaster Master](/vault/games/blaster-master)* was on American shelves by the autumn of 1988. When *Mean Machines* reviewed it for British players in March 1992, it explained the two halves: "a four-way scrolling platform game in which you control the tank", and sections in which the boy Jason leaves the tank to search the drains on foot for his escaped pet frog, grown huge on atomic waste. Its music was by Naoki Kodaka, whose name is in the music credits of many of Sunsoft's NES games.

Then came the licences. Sunsoft won the rights to the 1989 *Batman* film. In May 1989, a month before the film opened, *Nintendo Power* told its readers to look for "a Batman video game by Sunsoft tentatively scheduled for release around October". *Mean Machines* reviewed it in Britain in December 1990 and loved it – "If you own an NES you'd be bats to miss this" – giving the presentation 90 per cent: "brilliant throughout, with slinky animated sequences between levels". The sound got 78 per cent and was "the only let-down in the game". Sunsoft made different *Batman* games for the Game Boy, the Mega Drive and the PC Engine, and the PC Engine version's sound got 94 per cent in *Computer and Video Games*.

The licences kept coming: *Fester's Quest* from *The Addams Family*, *Gremlins 2* (1990), and *Batman: Return of the Joker* (1991). In between came an original game, *Journey to Silius* (1990), which *Nintendo Power* previewed as "an action type game dealing with the quest of Jay, a young man of the future, for the truth behind the death of his father". Kodaka shared its music credit.

### Gimmick!

*Gimmick!* (1992), a Japanese platform game, was one of the few Famicom games with extra sound hardware in the cartridge. Sunsoft's 5B chip, a version of its FME-7 cartridge chip, contained a three-channel sound generator compatible with Yamaha's YM2149, and *Gimmick!* is the only game that used it, for three extra tone channels on top of the console's own.

## Myst, and the retreat

Sunsoft had a hand in one of the biggest computer games of the 1990s. Robyn Miller of Cyan remembered that after Activision had told the brothers to stick to children's games, "it was Sunsoft who approached us asking us if we'd do a game for an older audience". That game was *[Myst](/vault/games/myst)*. Sunsoft kept rights to it: when ClickBOOM wanted to bring *Myst* to the Amiga in 1997, it was Sunsoft it signed with.

The American business did not last. In March 1995 *Nintendo Power* reported that Sunsoft, maker of *Bugs Bunny Rabbit Rampage*, *Taz-Mania* and *Road Runner's Death Valley Rally*, had "dramatically scaled back" its American office, and that *Justice League* and *Porky Pig's Haunted Holiday* might be its last Super NES games. Sega Retro records that Acclaim took over distribution of its American releases that year.

## Later history

Sun Corporation carried on as an electronics company, and Sunsoft went quiet. On 24 December 2021 it asked fans on Twitter which of its old games they would like remade. It has since published new games based on its old series, among them *Ufouria: The Saga 2* in March 2024 and *Gimmick! 2*, made by Bitwave Games, in September 2024.
