Many of Tecmo's best-known games were hard. *Bomb Jack* asked for perfect jumps, *Ninja Gaiden* on the NES killed its players over and over and told its story in film-like cut-scenes between the deaths, and *Dead or Alive* built a fighting game around reading an opponent's attack and turning it back on them. The company that made them started out far from games: *Wireframe*'s history of Tecmo lists cleaning equipment and yacht sales among its early lines of business, and its games first went out under another name, Tehkan.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** July 1967, as Nihon Yacht; Yoshihito Kakihara was its co-founder and president.
- **Names:** Tehkan for its early arcade games; Tecmo from January 1986.
- **First arcade game of its own:** *Pleiads* (1981).
- **Known for:** *Star Force* and *[Bomb Jack](/vault/games/bomb-jack)* (1984), *Tehkan World Cup* (1985), *Solomon's Key* and *[Rygar](/vault/games/rygar)* (1986), *Tecmo Bowl*, *[Ninja Gaiden](/vault/games/ninja-gaiden)*, *[Dead or Alive](/vault/games/dead-or-alive)* (1996), *Monster Rancher*, *Fatal Frame*.
- **Studio:** [Team Ninja](/vault/companies/team-ninja), led by [Tomonobu Itagaki](/vault/people/tomonobu-itagaki) until 2008.
- **Ended:** joined Koei under Koei Tecmo Holdings in April 2009; Koei absorbed Tecmo in April 2010 to form Koei Tecmo Games.

## Tehkan in the arcades

Tehkan's first arcade game developed in-house, *Pleiads* (1981), was a *Galaxian*-style shooter that *Wireframe* called "fairly unremarkable". In America its early games went out through other companies: *Video Games* magazine reviewed *Guzzler* in 1983 as a "licensed conversion kit from Tehkan, Ltd." sold by Centuri, and at the AMOA trade show that winter found Tehkan in "a little spot" with its tank game *Senjyo*, predicting that if the game crossed the Pacific it would carry "a different name and ... a different manufacturer".

The games that made the name came in 1984 and 1985. Much of the talent, *Wireframe* reports, came from the rival developer Universal: Kazutoshi Ueda, who oversaw *Bomb Jack*, had designed Universal's *Mr. Do!*, and around twenty former Universal staff followed him, perhaps half of Tehkan's development workforce. Michishito Ishizuka, who programmed *Tehkan World Cup*, remembered a saying among the staff: "If you didn't come from Universal, then you can't get promoted here." Small teams, the magazine says, turned games round in a few months.

- ***Star Force*** (1984) was a vertically scrolling shooter. Hudson Soft converted it for the Famicom and made it the first game of its touring Caravan tournaments, then built its own *Star Soldier* series on it.
- ***Bomb Jack*** (1984) sent a caped hero leaping and gliding round a single screen to collect bombs, scoring more for taking the bombs with lit fuses in order. *CRASH* reported that it reached the arcades "around Easter 1984" and "although it didn't become a massive success, the game attracted a cult following". Elite Systems' home versions made it familiar in Britain.
- ***Tehkan World Cup*** (1985) was an overhead football game played with trackballs. Sensible Software's Jon Hare told *Retro Gamer* that he and Chris Yates were addicted to it ("A game that hurt if you wore a ring on your hand"), and that it led to their *MicroProse Soccer* and, later, *Sensible Soccer*.

## Tecmo

Tehkan became Tecmo on 8 January 1986, and that April released its first game for a home console, *Mighty Bomb Jack* for the Famicom. The corporate history behind the name is tangled. Japanese company histories trace the Tehkan name to a building-maintenance firm founded in 1964, which became Tehkan in 1977 and Tecmo in 1986; in April 1987 a related company, Tehkan Electronics, which had been founded in July 1967 as Nihon Yacht, merged with Tecmo and took its name. Koei Tecmo counts Tecmo's history from that 1967 company. The arcade games kept coming under the new name. *Solomon's Key* (1986) was a puzzle game in which the hero makes and destroys blocks to build platforms and open paths, and collects keys to unlock doors; [US Gold](/vault/companies/us-gold) sold it on the Spectrum, Commodore 64, Amstrad and Atari ST, quoting *Computer and Video Games*: "An arcade strategy game with enough addictive qualities to turn even the toughest coin-op critic into an arcade junky". *Rygar* (1986) was a run-and-gun arcade game whose hero's weapon is, in *Wireframe*'s words, "a giant, deadly yo-yo"; its NES version added more weapons and exploration.

## Ninja Gaiden and Tecmo Bowl

Tecmo's biggest successes of the late 1980s came on the NES. *Wireframe* tells how *Ninja Gaiden* started: Kakihara saw the ninja craze in America and set two teams to work on different games with the same name. The arcade game was a scrolling fighting game in the style of *Double Dragon*, released in Europe as *Shadow Warriors*, in which the ninja could swing from poles and ledges to kick his enemies. The NES game shared little but the hero. It was a platform game in which Ryu Hayabusa clings to walls and springs between them, and between its levels the story of his father's death is told in what Tecmo called "Cinema Display" scenes. *Nintendo Power* previewed it in early 1989 saying it "got the highest marks of any title we've seen in a long time".

*Tecmo Bowl* began as an arcade game; *Nintendo Power*, awaiting the NES conversion in 1988, said "the arcade version is really great". Both NES games came out in America early in 1989. *Tecmo Super Bowl* (1991) featured all 28 NFL teams, and *Wireframe* noted that players still arranged and streamed *Tecmo Super Bowl* tournaments almost 30 years later.

## Team Ninja and Dead or Alive

By the mid-1990s, *Wireframe* writes, Tecmo "had begun to flag financially". Its recovery came from Team Ninja, a design group led by Tomonobu Itagaki and given the job of making a rival to Sega's *Virtua Fighter*. The result, *Dead or Alive*, ran on Sega's own Model 2 arcade board: *Edge* reported from the AOU arcade show in 1996 that Sega's licensing of Model 2 to Tecmo and Jaleco let it move its best work on to Model 3. The arcade game came out in November 1996. Its central idea was the hold. *Computer and Video Games* explained in 1998 that Itagaki had replaced the usual guard button with "One button, 'Hold'": blocking was done by pulling away from the opponent, and the hold button caught an attack and turned it into a counter, because Itagaki wanted "to recreate the thrill of a Kung-Fu movie, where you see fighters repeatedly countering hundreds of moves". *Edge* gave the Saturn version eight out of ten in 1997 and wrote that its "hi-res, 60fps visuals will both delight and astound Saturn owners", but found its arenas disappointing. *Dead or Alive 2* followed on Sega's NAOMI board and the Dreamcast.

Tecmo had other hits on the PlayStation. *Monster Rancher* (1997) raised monsters from whatever audio CD the player put in the drive; its advertisement promised "EVERY CD YOU OWN CONTAINS A MONSTER". *Fatal Frame* (2001), *Project Zero* in Europe, armed the heroine of a ghost story with only a camera; see [survival horror](/vault/genres/survival-horror). In 2004 Team Ninja brought back Ryu Hayabusa in a 3D *Ninja Gaiden* for Microsoft's Xbox, which Itagaki had shown to an enthusiastic crowd at Microsoft's X03 event in 2003.

## Koei Tecmo

The founder, Kakihara, died in 2006. In 2008 Itagaki left Tecmo and sued the company for 148 million yen in unpaid bonuses; the two sides settled in 2010. In August 2008 Square Enix proposed a friendly takeover of Tecmo, which Tecmo's board turned down in September in favour of talks with another Japanese publisher, Koei. The two companies signed an integration agreement in November 2008, set up a joint holding company, Koei Tecmo Holdings, on 1 April 2009, and on 1 April 2010 merged their game companies: Koei absorbed Tecmo, and the combined company became Koei Tecmo Games.

Team Ninja and Tecmo's series carried on inside it. *Ninja Gaiden 3* (2012) was, *Edge* noted, the series' first "Itagaki-free" entry. *Dead or Alive 6* (2019), published under the Koei Tecmo name, drew 62 per cent from *Wireframe*. Team Ninja also made *Nioh*, a game Koei had first announced in 2005, and *Ninja Gaiden 4*, made with PlatinumGames, was released on 21 October 2025. Koei Tecmo marked Tecmo's 50th anniversary in 2017.
