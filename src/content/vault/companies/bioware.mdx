In 2000 *Computer Gaming World* travelled to "an utterly nondescript office building on 82nd Avenue in Edmonton, Canada" to find out how "a couple of Canadian M.D.s whose only previous programming experience" was medical software, such as a "Gastroenterology Patient Simulator", had ended up "at the top of the gaming heap". Five years after it opened, BioWare had nearly 100 staff, a million-selling role-playing game behind it and the *Star Wars* licence ahead of it. The doctors running it were testing their own game until three in the morning. "I try to find 20 bugs at a time and report them the next morning," Ray Muzyka told the magazine.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1995, in Edmonton, Alberta, by Ray Muzyka, Greg Zeschuk and Augustine Yip, doctors who had qualified at the University of Alberta.
- **First game:** *Shattered Steel* (1996), published by Interplay.
- **Breakthrough:** *[Baldur's Gate](/vault/games/baldurs-gate)* (1998), published by [Interplay](/vault/companies/interplay) under its [Black Isle Studios](/vault/companies/black-isle-studios) label.
- **Later games:** *MDK2* (2000), *Baldur's Gate II* (2000), *[Neverwinter Nights](/vault/games/neverwinter-nights)* (2002), *[Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic](/vault/games/kotor)* (2003), *Jade Empire* (2005), *[Mass Effect](/vault/games/mass-effect)* (2007) and *[Dragon Age](/vault/games/dragon-age)* (2009 onwards).
- **Owners:** independent until 2005; combined with Pandemic Studios under the private equity firm Elevation Partners; bought by [Electronic Arts](/vault/companies/electronic-arts) in 2007.
- **Founders' exit:** Yip returned to medicine in the late 1990s; Muzyka and Zeschuk retired in 2012.

## Doctors and programmers

Muzyka, Zeschuk and Yip had written medical teaching software together before they made games. Zeschuk says the company began informally in the early 1990s and was formed officially in 1995. The doctors were not the only people involved. Trent Oster, a programmer at BioWare from the start, has recalled that he, his brother Brent and Greg Zeschuk's cousin Marcel had already made a rough first-person *Asteroids* game, *Blasteroids 3D*, as a proof that they could make a game at all. In Oster's telling the doctors came in through Greg Zeschuk, and the six of them counted as BioWare's original founders in February 1995.

## Shattered Steel

BioWare's first game grew out of a rendering engine that could draw rolling desert terrain. It became *Shattered Steel* (1996), published by Interplay: a game about piloting giant walking war machines, in the style of *MechWarrior*, in which the player, a mercenary, finds a planet overrun by robotic insects. *Computer Gaming World*'s reviewer thought its most striking feature was the graphics engine, with voxel landscapes of hills and trenches that could be used for cover and blown apart with mortars. Looking back in 2000, the same magazine called it "a decent but not spectacular debut".

## Baldur's Gate

The next project was a real-time strategy game, *Battleground: Infinity*, set during Ragnarok, the Norse end of the world. Trent Oster told *Retro Gamer* that the demo went round the major publishers and none was interested, until Feargus Urquhart, BioWare's producer at Interplay on *Shattered Steel*, asked to see it. Urquhart had just been made head of Interplay's new role-playing division, which held the Dungeons & Dragons licence and would later be named Black Isle Studios. "Feargus had the D&D licence and BioWare had an RTS-style RPG," the Black Isle producer Chris Parker recalled. The strategy game's engine became the Infinity Engine, and the game became *[Baldur's Gate](/vault/games/baldurs-gate)*, released at the end of 1998. Its party of adventurers fought in real time, but the player could stop the clock at any moment with the space bar and give orders.

*Baldur's Gate* sold more than a million copies. It was hard to make: "With BG there were longer hours, a lot more stress, and tempers ran higher," said the co-lead designer, James Ohlen. For the sequel, *Baldur's Gate II: Shadows of Amn* (2000), *Computer Gaming World* found the team working 66-hour weeks, but more calmly. In the same year BioWare released *MDK2*, a third-person action game published by Interplay, and began work on a *Star Wars* role-playing game.

## Leaving Interplay

At the end of 2001 BioWare broke with its only publisher so far. *Computer Gaming World* reported that BioWare had terminated its contract with Interplay, and *PC Gamer* that BioWare had sued Interplay for breach of contract and for distributing its games to third parties without its consent. *[Neverwinter Nights](/vault/games/neverwinter-nights)* (2002), a Dungeons & Dragons game with a toolset that let players build their own adventures and run them as dungeon masters for other players online, came out from Infogrames instead. Interplay's debts to BioWare were still unpaid in 2004, when *Computer Gaming World* listed a BioWare claim for $156,000 in *Baldur's Gate* royalties among Interplay's troubles.

## Consoles and new worlds

*[Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic](/vault/games/kotor)* (2003) was published by LucasArts, first on Microsoft's [Xbox](/vault/systems/microsoft-xbox). Set some 4,000 years before the films, it kept a *Baldur's Gate*-style pausable combat system under a *Star Wars* surface. *Edge* called it "a remarkable distillation of BioWare experience and excellence in the development of videogame RPGs", and thought it captured the films "more than almost any other 'Star Wars' title to date". *Jade Empire* (2005), a martial-arts role-playing game published by Microsoft for the Xbox, was BioWare's first world of its own since *Shattered Steel*.

In 2005 BioWare and Pandemic Studios, the developer of *Full Spectrum Warrior*, were brought together under a holding company by Elevation Partners, a private equity firm whose partners included John Riccitiello, a former president of Electronic Arts. *Edge* reported the price as $300 million. BioWare opened a studio in Austin, Texas, to make an online role-playing game. It was also finishing *[Mass Effect](/vault/games/mass-effect)* (2007) for the Xbox 360: a science-fiction role-playing game planned from the start as a trilogy, whose saved games were meant to carry the player's choices from one instalment to the next.

## Electronic Arts

Riccitiello returned to EA as chief executive in 2007, and in October EA announced that it would buy the holding company. EA said it would pay up to $620 million in cash and issue up to $155 million in shares to employees; the two developers had about 800 staff in four studios between them. Muzyka said the deal was "consistent with BioWare's focus on crafting the highest quality story-driven games in the world".

Under EA, BioWare finished the *Mass Effect* trilogy (2007 to 2012), started the *Dragon Age* fantasy series with *Dragon Age: Origins* (2009) and launched the online game *Star Wars: The Old Republic* (2011) from its Austin studio. In September 2012 Muzyka and Zeschuk announced together that they were leaving. "I've reached an unexpected point in my life where I no longer have the passion that I once did for the company, for the games and for the challenge of creation," Zeschuk said. Muzyka said he had "personally achieved what [he] wanted in videogames". Zeschuk turned to beer, and within a year *Edge* was introducing him as an "ex-BioWare boss and beer buff".

## Later history

After the founders left, BioWare released *Dragon Age: Inquisition* (2014), then *Mass Effect: Andromeda* (2017), led by its Montreal studio, which met a lukewarm response from the press, and *Anthem* (2019), an online action game. *Dragon Age: The Veilguard* came out in October 2024. In January 2025, after EA said the game had not met its expectations, BioWare's general manager, Gary McKay, announced that a core team led by veterans of the original *Mass Effect* trilogy would make the next *Mass Effect*, and that many other staff were being moved to other EA teams; others were laid off. Bloomberg reported that the studio had fallen to fewer than 100 people. The first *Baldur's Gate* games, meanwhile, lived on outside the studio: Beamdog released *Baldur's Gate: Enhanced Edition* in November 2012, with BioWare's early programmer Trent Oster heading its development.
