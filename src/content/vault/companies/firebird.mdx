## Overview
Firebird was British Telecom's software label, set up in the spring of 1984 by
**Dr Ederyn Williams**, General Manager of BT's New Information Services
Division. It is a stranger thing than it now sounds — a nationalised telecoms
monopoly selling £2.50 games in newsagents — and *CRASH*, profiling them in May
1985 under the headline "The Boys from Buzby", said as much: it was not
immediately obvious why BT should want to be "The Power Behind the Joystick".

The magazine's own answer was that BT was already in electronic publishing
through Prestel, Micronet and Gamestar, so selling software in shops was not as
illogical a move as it first appeared.

## Fast facts
- **Founded:** spring 1984, by BT's New Information Services Division.
- **Launch price:** £2.50 — twenty titles available by November 1984.
- **Catchphrase:** "Seeing is believing".
- **Labels:** Firebird Silver (later Silverbird) for budget, Rainbird for premium.
- **Breakout release:** *Elite*, published under the Firebird label.

## Open submissions, at scale
Firebird advertised for programs in May 1984 and, by its own account to *CRASH*,
received **nearly two thousand** in the first three months. That is the budget
model working as designed: no development studio, no advances, a wide net cast
across the bedroom-coder population, and an editorial filter applied afterwards.

**Tony Rainbird** was persuaded to leave his own label, Micro-Gold, and join in
June 1984 to get the operation moving. The premium **Rainbird** imprint carries
his name.

## Managing a budget range
Firebird treated the Silver range as inventory rather than a catalogue. Every
few months it planned to replace eight or twelve of the titles — dropping
whatever sold least and introducing better games to lift the average. It also
told *CRASH* it might take on games from other houses that had "exhausted the
market" at full price, which is budget republishing described plainly a decade
before the practice became routine.

By May 1985 the label had taken **three CRASH Smashes** in the few months it had
been operating, which is what prompted the profile.

## Legacy
Firebird's output ran from £2.50 tapes to *Elite*, and the Rainbird imprint
carried simulations and adventures at full price. Telecomsoft, BT's software
arm, was sold in 1989 and the catalogue passed to MicroProse.

The label matters here because it is the clearest case of a large institution
running the bedroom-coder pipeline deliberately: an advert, two thousand
submissions, a filter, and a rotating shelf.
