**Electric Dreams Software** was a British label operating inside
[Activision](/vault/companies/activision)'s UK organisation. Its advertising makes the
arrangement plain: orders went to **Activision (UK) Ltd, Units 3 & 4 Lloyds Close, Finedon
Road Industrial Estate, Wellingborough**, and the small print identifies Electric Dreams as
an "Authorised User" of Sega properties.

So this is the third distinct thing Activision's British operation was doing at once — its
own catalogue, distribution for [Infocom](/vault/companies/infocom), System 3, Sierra and
Lucasfilm, and a separate imprint of its own with licences attached.

## Fast facts

- **Run from:** Activision (UK) Ltd, Wellingborough, Northamptonshire.
- **Licences held:** Sega properties, credited as an authorised user.
- **Best known for:** ***Spindizzy*** (1986).
- **Typical prices:** **£9.99** cassette across Spectrum, C64 and Amstrad; £12.99–£14.99 on disc.
- **Also published:** *Dandy*, which turned up on [Beau Jolly](/vault/companies/beau-jolly) compilations.

## Spindizzy

*Spindizzy* is the game Electric Dreams is remembered for. You steer a spinning top across a suspended
landscape of ramps, towers, catwalks and trampolines, with lethal drops on every side and a
time limit forcing the pace — an isometric exploration game with the handling model of a
physics toy.

*ACE* placed it in its recommended list and gave the development cost: it **took a year to
build**, which for a 1986 8-bit game is an unusual investment. The magazine's verdict was
that a whole world had been crammed into an 8-bit micro, and called the result a milestone.

## It won everything

The clearest measure of *Spindizzy*'s standing is that it did not win one award; it won the
set. *Retro Gamer*'s month-by-month reconstruction records ***ZZAP!64*** awarding it the
**Gold Medal at 98%**, and notes that it took top honours again elsewhere, giving it "**a
complete set of awards spanning all formats**".

⚠ Worth putting beside the other sweeps in this Vault. [Martech](/vault/companies/martech)'s
*Zoids* took a *CRASH* Smash, a *ZZAP!64* Sizzler and an *Amtix!* Accolade. *Spindizzy* did
the same and took the Gold Medal — the tier above a Sizzler, and one *ZZAP!* declined to
award at all in some months.

## What the label was for

Electric Dreams shows a pattern this Vault sees repeatedly in the second half of the
eighties: **a large publisher operating several names at once**, so that different kinds of
product can sit at different price points without confusing the parent's identity.
[Mastertronic](/vault/companies/mastertronic) ran M.A.D., Bulldog and Ricochet;
[Telecomsoft](/vault/companies/firebird) ran Firebird, [Rainbird](/vault/companies/rainbird)
and Silverbird; [US Gold](/vault/companies/us-gold) ran [Kixx](/vault/companies/kixx).

Activision's version put a British imprint with Japanese arcade licences alongside an
American catalogue and a distribution arm — and the imprint's best game was neither a licence
nor an American import, but an original that took a year to make.
