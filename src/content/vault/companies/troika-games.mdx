Troika Games lasted seven years and made three role-playing games, each for a different publisher. Each found admirers among critics, and each shipped with serious bugs. The studio was founded in 1998 by three of the people who had made Interplay's *[Fallout](/vault/games/fallout)*, and it built its games the way *Fallout* had been built: big worlds, many ways through every problem, and characters who could talk, sneak or fight their way to the end. What it never managed was a hit. Early in 2005, unable to find a publisher for its fourth game, it closed.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** April 1998, by [Tim Cain](/vault/people/tim-cain), Leonard Boyarsky and Jason Anderson.
- **Based in:** Irvine, California.
- **Games:** *[Arcanum: Of Steamworks and Magick Obscura](/vault/games/arcanum)* (Sierra, 2001); *The Temple of Elemental Evil* (Atari, 2003); *[Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines](/vault/games/vampire-the-masquerade-bloodlines)* (Activision, 2004).
- **Closed:** February 2005.

## Leaving Interplay

Cain had led *Fallout* as its producer and lead programmer; Boyarsky and Anderson had worked on its art and design. Early in 1998, as work on *Fallout 2* was starting, the three left [Interplay](/vault/companies/interplay). Boyarsky later told *The Escapist* that Interplay "had been a great place to work, and we felt that it was losing a lot of what we felt was great about it". Anderson said they "were unable to come to an agreement with Interplay as to how our next team should be structured". Sierra's newsletter *InterAction* recorded that Troika Games LLC was founded in April 1998.

For months there was no game to make, only a search for someone to pay for one. "Over the course of six months I looked at a lot of different publishers," Cain told *InterAction*. "I went with Sierra Studios for one reason—trust." According to *The Escapist*, the Sierra contract let Troika grow to a team of 12.

## Arcanum

*[Arcanum: Of Steamworks and Magick Obscura](/vault/games/arcanum)* (2001) put a fantasy world in the middle of an industrial revolution. Elves and wizards shared it with steam engines, railways and pistols, and a character who leaned towards technology found magic harder to use, and the other way round. *PC Zone* called it "Jules Verne meets JRR Tolkien". *Computer Gaming World*'s preview saw the *Fallout* team's hand at once, from the view from above to the row of action-point bubbles in turn-based fights.

The reviews were mixed. *Edge* found the steampunk setting "a refreshing change", but thought the game had missed how far *Baldur's Gate* and *Planescape: Torment* had raised the bar, and complained about its "sprawling" rules, its awkward inventory and its fights. *PC Gamer* in America liked it enough to name it the best role-playing game of 2001.

## The Temple of Elemental Evil

Troika's second game, for Atari, adapted a famous *Dungeons & Dragons* adventure module, *The Temple of Elemental Evil*, and used the tabletop game's then-current 3.5 edition rules. The player built a whole party of first-level characters and led it through the village of Hommlet and down into a huge dungeon, in fights that were turn-based. Australia's *Hyper* thought Troika had done "an exceptionally great job with the character creation, levelling up, and the deep, deep strategic combat", but found little else in the game to do.

The bugs overshadowed everything else. *Computer Gaming World*'s Jeff Green called Troika's team "hardcore RPG fanatics" whose "dedication shows", then described "innumerable game-stopping crashes" on two different PCs and broken pathfinding that left party members "hopelessly stranded". A patch made by fans had not fixed the crashes. "With just a little more time and care, *Temple of Elemental Evil* could have been great," he wrote, and called it "yet another poster child" for publishers that release games before they are finished.

## Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines

For Activision, Troika made a game in the *Vampire: The Masquerade* tabletop setting, built on Valve's new Source engine, the technology behind *[Half-Life 2](/vault/games/half-life-2)*. It was, *PC Gamer* noted, Troika's first first-person role-playing game. The player was a newly made vampire in a night-time Los Angeles, bound by the "masquerade", the rule that vampires hide what they are from humans. According to *The Escapist*, Troika worked with a prototype of the engine, and Activision, "fearing their license was in jeopardy", advanced it more money in 2003. The game came out on 16 November 2004, the same day as *Half-Life 2*.

*Computer Gaming World*'s Robert Coffey loved the world it built, "a nightmarish hell lurking just beneath the seedy surface of nighttime Los Angeles", and its character building. He also found "so many rough edges you could use the CDs as saw blades": loading times of close to two minutes, frame rates that could fall to about one a second, and enemies running on the spot. His verdict was that it "might go from 'really good' to 'great' once they patch it". Troika's last patch for *Bloodlines*, *The Escapist* noted, was the last code the studio ever released. Its players kept it alive: *Wireframe* wrote in 2020 that the game "withers on the vine, but lives on through community support and intense fandom".

## Closing down

By then Troika was running out of work. In 2005 Boyarsky told *Hyper* that the studio had a new post-apocalyptic role-playing game, isometric and with a new 3D engine, but no publisher, because publishers were "looking for successful titles in terms of sales and not just positive reviews". "Being original is risky," he said. Before the article went to print, Troika had closed. *The Escapist* reported that two rounds of layoffs had left only the founders by the start of 2005, and that the company shut in February. Anderson's explanation was blunt: "Publishers aren't interested in games from developers that consistently turn out B titles."

## After Troika

The founders stayed in role-playing games.

- **Tim Cain** became design director on the online game *WildStar* at Carbine Studios, joined [Obsidian Entertainment](/vault/companies/obsidian-entertainment) in 2011 and co-directed *The Outer Worlds* (2019) with Boyarsky. The [Tim Cain](/vault/people/tim-cain) entry follows his career.
- **Leonard Boyarsky** went to Blizzard, where *Edge* interviewed him in 2012 as world designer on *[Diablo](/vault/games/diablo) III*. He left for Obsidian in April 2016.
- **Jason Anderson** returned briefly to Interplay, then joined Brian Fargo's inXile Entertainment as creative director in March 2009, and sat beside Fargo when publishers turned down the idea of a new *[Wasteland](/vault/games/wasteland)*. He later rejoined inXile, and in 2023 was named principal designer of its steampunk role-playing game *Clockwork Revolution*, in the announcement, as one of the makers of *Arcanum*.

*Bloodlines* also got its sequel in the end. *Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines 2*, made by other studios, came out in October 2025.
