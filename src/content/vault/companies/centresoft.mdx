**CentreSoft** was the games distributor **Geoff and Anne Brown** built in **Halesowen**, in
the West Midlands, from **1983**. It matters here for something other than its own trade: the
publisher it financed. [U.S. Gold](/vault/companies/us-gold) exists because CentreSoft could
raise a Letter of Credit and a start-up publisher could not.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** early 1983, Halesowen, West Midlands, by Geoff and Anne Brown.
- **Business:** wholesale distribution of computer games and hardware to British retail.
- **Financed:** [U.S. Gold](/vault/companies/us-gold)'s first three licences in 1984.
- **First employee:** Jenny Richards, later head of sales.
- **Became:** **CentreGold plc** in 1993, merged with U.S. Gold ahead of a flotation.
- **Named in the British press** continuously from 1983 to the mid-1990s.

## Milk crates in Halesowen

The accountant Martyn Savage — who would join the company two years later — remembers the
first meeting in early 1983:

> I went over to a very small office where all they had was a table to gather round and milk
> crates to sit on. We went through certain figures and put through a business plan to raise
> some money.

The plan projected a cash flow from 1 April to 31 December 1983 and an opening balance of
**£20,000** in share capital, "assuming they would be ready to incorporate a new company at
that point, which as it turned out they were not". CentreSoft traded as a name before it was
a limited company, which is why its founding resists a single date.

Anne Brown was still working at a bank through the early months, so the operation ran on one
pair of hands: "Delivery notes, invoices etc. — I just entered the details in a little blue
book."

## Distribution first, publishing second

The idea was borrowed rather than invented. Geoff Brown had been selling Atari 800s, watched
a rival move from selling machines to distributing them, and followed: "I sort of stole the
idea of distributing from Mike really."

Sourcing product is half of distribution; the other half is a retailer. **Currys** — Britain's
largest electrical chain until Dixons took it over in 1984 — was the account that made the
volumes work.

By the time [U.S. Gold](/vault/companies/us-gold) was founded in March 1984, CentreSoft was
established enough to underwrite it. The licensors wanted their guarantees locked in through
Letters of Credit, a bank issues those only against funds on deposit, and the funds were
CentreSoft's.

## Why the two are one story

Most accounts treat U.S. Gold as a publisher that happened to have a sister distributor. The
sequence says otherwise: the distributor came first, paid for the publisher, and the two ended
as divisions of the same listed company when the group was renamed **CentreGold plc** in 1993
ahead of its flotation.

A British software house of the eighties usually died when its distribution failed. This one
started at the other end.
