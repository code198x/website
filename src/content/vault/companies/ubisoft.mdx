Ubisoft started as a sideline to a distribution business. The Guillemot brothers were already among France's biggest sellers of other people's software when, in 1986, they set up Ubi Soft to publish games of their own. Two years later they flew the European computer press to a chateau in Brittany where the company's programmers lived and worked, and showed them *Iron Lord*. The same family runs it forty years on. In between, Ubi Soft grew from a publisher of French adventure games for the Amstrad and Atari ST into one of the largest game companies in the world, with *[Rayman](/vault/games/rayman)*, *[Prince of Persia](/vault/games/prince-of-persia)* and *[Assassin's Creed](/vault/games/assassins-creed)*, and twice held off bigger companies that bought large parts of it.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1986, by the brothers Christian, Claude, Gérard, Michel and Yves Guillemot, from Brittany.
- **Run by:** Yves Guillemot, founder and president in 1988, still chairman and chief executive in 2026.
- **First game:** *Zombi* (1986), an icon-driven adventure.
- **Stock market:** floated in Paris in 1996.
- **Bought:** Red Storm Entertainment (2000), Blue Byte (2001) and The Learning Company's games division, with *Myst* and *Prince of Persia* (2001).
- **Best-known series:** *Rayman*, *Prince of Persia*, *Tom Clancy's Rainbow Six* and *Splinter Cell*, *Assassin's Creed*, *Far Cry*, *Just Dance*.

## From distributors to publishers

The brothers grew up in Carentoir, a village in Brittany, where their parents ran a farm supplies business. According to later accounts, they started selling computers and software by mail order in the mid-1980s after finding that games cost far less in Britain than their French supplier charged, and imported them direct. By 1988 the family's Guillemot group was, *ST Action* reported, "reputed to be the top software distributor in France", and *ACE* listed the British and American publishers it handled there: Electronic Arts, Mirrorsoft and Elite.

Ubi Soft was the publishing arm, set up in 1986 "as an independent publishing company within the Guillemot group". The name was chosen, *Edge* later explained, to suggest that the company's games would be everywhere. Its first game was *Zombi*, which *Amstrad Action* previewed in September 1986 for the Amstrad CPC as a chance to "take part in a night of the living dead"; the same report mentioned a sprite designer, *Graphic City*, and a role-playing game, *Fer et Flamme*. Ubi Soft also did conversions for other publishers: in 1988 it was programming Cinemaware's *Defender of the Crown* for Mirrorsoft's Amstrad CPC release.

## The chateau

In the summer of 1988 Ubi Soft invited journalists from Britain, Denmark, Finland, Germany, Italy, Spain and Sweden to Brittany. Its developers lived and worked in a 19th-century chateau near Rennes, set in 700 acres of grounds. Yves Guillemot, "Founder and President of UBI Soft", explained that the headquarters stayed in the centre of Paris: "with a telephone, modem and Facsimile, it is not really necessary to be in the city." *ST Action* counted 250 in-house and freelance developers working for the company, and *ACE* reported that it had released about 20 titles and hoped to double its turnover, from about $5 million to $10 million, by selling them across Europe.

The games on show were for the Atari ST and Amiga. *Iron Lord*, after eighteen months of artwork, was a medieval adventure with archery, arm-wrestling and swordfighting. *Puffy's Saga* was a *Gauntlet*-style maze game with 140 levels, and *Skateball* crossed football with *Rollerball* on an ice rink. At the Personal Computer Show in London that September, Electronic Arts announced that it had signed the British distribution rights to Ubi Soft as an affiliated label, and *Iron Lord*, *Skateball* and *Puffy's Saga* were among the first releases.

## Rayman

Ubi Soft's first big hit was a platform game. *Rayman*, designed by [Michel Ancel](/vault/people/michel-ancel), came out in 1995 for the Atari Jaguar, the PlayStation and the Saturn, developed and published by Ubi Soft itself. *Computer and Video Games* called it "superb on Saturn and PlayStation. Cool on Jaguar too." *Edge* said in 2000 that it was one of the best-selling PlayStation games of all time and one of the first to be reissued as a Platinum title, though "not well regarded for its innovation or gameplay".

In 1996 Ubi Soft floated on the Paris stock exchange. When *PC Gamer* visited that summer, "most of the employees" had bought shares, and a project manager was ruefully tracking the price: "On the first day the price went up 10%. On the second it was 8%. Today it is only 4% more."

## Buying its way to the top five

By December 2000 Ubi Soft had about 1,700 staff, studios in 18 countries, 65 per cent of its developers outside France, and, Yves Guillemot told *Edge*, the aim of being one of the world's top five publishers by 2006. It bought its way there. In 2000 it took over Sinister Games and Grolier Interactive, and in August Red Storm Entertainment, the maker of *Tom Clancy's Rainbow Six*. "It's one more target that we didn't reach," Guillemot said of Red Storm's audience. In February 2001 it bought the German publisher Blue Byte, maker of *The Settlers*.

In March 2001 Ubi Soft bought the games division of [The Learning Company](/vault/companies/the-learning-company), which Gores Technology Group had taken over from Mattel. That brought it the games of [Brøderbund](/vault/companies/broderbund) and [Mindscape](/vault/companies/mindscape), among them *[Myst](/vault/games/myst)* and *Prince of Persia*. Ubi Soft published *Myst III: Exile* later in 2001 and *Myst IV: Revelation* in 2004; the [Cyan](/vault/companies/cyan) entry tells that story. Its own Montreal studio remade Jordan Mechner's prince as *Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time* (2003), which *Edge* said "felt thrillingly modern" but whose sales never matched its reputation.

## Electronic Arts and Vivendi

By then the company wrote its name as one word, Ubisoft. At the end of 2004 [Electronic Arts](/vault/companies/electronic-arts) bought 19.9 per cent of its shares. Ubisoft called the purchase "hostile". *Edge* reported that the French media expected the government or Vivendi to help defend it, and Infogrames' chief executive Bruno Bonnell said EA "did not enter Ubisoft's capital to just stay at" that level and offered to help fight off a takeover. No bid came.

Vivendi itself was the next threat. The French media group began buying Ubisoft shares in 2015 and built up a stake of more than a quarter of the company. On 20 March 2018 Ubisoft announced that Vivendi had agreed to sell all of it, to Ubisoft, Tencent and other investors, and not to buy any more shares for five years. *Wireframe* described Ubisoft as having "saw off the threat of a Vivendi takeover".

## Assassin's Creed and after

Ubisoft Montreal's *Assassin's Creed* (2007) became the company's biggest series. Its assassin, Altaïr, ran, climbed and leapt across three cities of the Third Crusade, Acre, Damascus and Jerusalem, and *Edge* called his animation "a triumph". Ubisoft also took on Crytek's *Far Cry* (2004), first as its publisher and then as the owner of the series, and found an unexpected hit in the dance game *Just Dance*, which in Christmas 2009 "significantly outperformed our expectations", its British managing director said. The company kept publishing smaller projects beside the blockbusters: [Éric Chahi](/vault/people/eric-chahi)'s god game *From Dust* (2011) came from Ubisoft Montpellier, and [Julian Gollop](/vault/people/julian-gollop) led the 3DS tactics game *Ghost Recon: Shadow Wars* (2011) at Ubisoft Sofia.

In July 2020, after newspaper reports of harassment and misconduct at several of its studios, Ubisoft's chief creative officer Serge Hascoët and two other senior executives resigned, and the company said it would change its workplace culture. *Wireframe*'s columnist noted that a staff survey had found that about a quarter of employees said they had witnessed or experienced misconduct.

In 2025 Ubisoft moved *Assassin's Creed*, *Far Cry* and *Rainbow Six* into a new subsidiary, Vantage Studios, in which Tencent paid €1.16 billion for a 25 per cent stake; it began operating on 1 October 2025, with Yves Guillemot's son Charlie as one of its two chief executives. In January 2026, facing heavy losses, Ubisoft announced a restructuring into five "creative houses", closed studios in Halifax and Stockholm, and cancelled six games, among them a remake of *The Sands of Time*. On 19 June 2026 Claude Guillemot, one of the five founding brothers and head of the family's hardware company, Guillemot Corporation, maker of Hercules and Thrustmaster products, was killed when his plane crashed near La Baule.
