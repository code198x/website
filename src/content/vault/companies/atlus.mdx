In most role-playing games a monster is something to kill. In Atlus's, it might be something to talk to. When Atlus brought *Revelations: Persona* to America in 1996, its advertisement explained the idea: the player could "converse with them before doing battle to determine your best course of action. Fight them or enlist their aid in your mission." That idea came from the *Megami Tensei* games, which Atlus had been making since the Famicom, and which put demons, gods and figures from many religions into a modern Tokyo. For years the series was little known outside Japan; *Persona 5* and its expanded edition went on to sell more than ten million copies.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 7 April 1986, Tokyo.
- **Series:** *Megami Tensei* and *[Shin Megami Tensei](/vault/games/shin-megami-tensei)*; *[Persona](/vault/games/persona)*; *Etrian Odyssey*; *Catherine*.
- **Western arm:** Atlus USA, founded in 1991.
- **Owners:** Takara (2003), Index (2006), [Sega Sammy](/vault/companies/sega) (2013).
- **Best-selling:** *Persona 5* and *Persona 5 Royal*, more than ten million copies together by August 2025.

## From contract work to Megami Tensei

Atlus was founded on 7 April 1986. It started by developing games that other companies published, and its first *Megami Tensei* games came out on the Famicom under Namco's name; *Megami Tensei II* (1990) shipped on one of Namco's own cartridges, with Namco's 163 sound chip. In 1992 Atlus published *Shin Megami Tensei* for the Super Famicom itself. A 2006 Japanese readers' poll of the hundred best games, printed in *Edge*, still placed *Megami Tensei II* 58th and *Shin Megami Tensei* 59th.

The games were not easy to export. *Retro Gamer* noted that Japanese role-playing games such as *Shin Megami Tensei* were "rife with risqué humour, irreverent use of religious symbols, and blunt depictions of murder", and that Japanese games were often cut or edited to fit Nintendo of America's rules. When *Shin Megami Tensei: Nocturne* was finally confirmed for America in 2004, *Edge* listed the series' "traditional cast", which it expected would do it no favours there: "devils, deaths and gods from every possible religion, a tyrannical creator and a slightly sympathetic portrayal of Lucifer Morningstar". It praised the "unique design" of the series artist Kazuma Kaneko and the "freedom to throw your lot in with any of the game's disparate dogmas or strike out on your own moral path". Atlus took a relaxed view of fans who translated its older games themselves: "if fans choose to translate game text for their own personal use, then more power to them," a spokesperson told *Edge* in 2006.

## Persona

*Revelations: Persona* (1996) was the first *Megami Tensei* game released in America, and *GamePro* explained to its readers that it had "roots in the Megami Tensei games that hit the NES" years before. Its advertisement promised "over 300 different monsters" and a party who could "morph" into more powerful beings, the Personas of the title.

The series changed with *Persona 3* (2006) on the PlayStation 2. Its director, Katsura Hashino, also wrote much of the scenario, and Kaneko's assistant Shigenori Soejima took over the art direction with modern-looking young characters. The player lives through a year as a Japanese schoolchild: by day, classes, clubs, part-time jobs and friendships; by night, the monster-filled Tartarus, where the friendships made in the day make the player's Personas stronger. *Retro Gamer* called it "against the tide of pretty much everything in its own genre" and concluded: "Only Atlus is making games like this." Its "Evokers", with which teenage characters summon their Personas by appearing to shoot themselves in the head, drew some controversy as well as attention.

## Other series

Atlus kept other lines going alongside the demons. *Etrian Odyssey* (2007) on the Nintendo DS was a first-person dungeon game of the kind *Edge* called a "mono-dungeon experience"; its series ran to a fifth game in 2016 and HD versions of the first three in 2023, with music by [Yuzo Koshiro](/vault/people/yuzo-koshiro). *Catherine* (2011), with music arranged by *Persona*'s composer Shoji Meguro, combined a story about a 32-year-old man unsure whether to commit to his girlfriend with nightmare block-climbing puzzles; *Edge* thought it "confidently explores what, in gaming, is all but uncharted territory". Atlus USA also published other Japanese studios' games in the West, among them Nippon Ichi's *Disgaea: Hour of Darkness* in 2003.

## Owners

Atlus changed hands several times. The toy company Takara took control in 2003, and the mobile-content company Index bought it in 2006 and absorbed it in 2010. When Index went bankrupt in 2013, Sega Sammy bought it for ¥14 billion at an auction, and by 2014 Sega had taken over publishing Atlus's games in Japan. *Edge* noted at the start of 2014 that *Persona 5*'s release in the West would now depend on "Sega, Atlus's new parent company".

*Persona 5* came out in 2016. By December 2023 the *Persona 5* series, counting its spin-offs, had sold more than ten million copies, and in September 2025 Atlus said that *Persona 5* and its expanded edition *Persona 5 Royal* had together sold 10.46 million by the end of August.
