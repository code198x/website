**GCE** (General Consumer Electronics) was the company that created the Vectrex, the only home console with built-in vector graphics display. Founded in 1981, GCE released the Vectrex in 1982 before being acquired by Milton Bradley that same year.

- **Founded:** 1981

- **Acquired by:** Milton Bradley (1982)
- **End:** 1984 (with MB exit from games)

## What BYTE said at launch

*BYTE* ran a First Look at the Vectrex in **December 1982**, and the verdict is worth having
in full because of how the story ends:

> **Here is one of the greatest game machines we have seen this year.** With superb vector
> graphics, excellent sound, cartridges for some of the best coin-operated arcade games, and
> a suggested retail price of **$200**, the Vectrex Arcade System is a good bet to score big
> with the consumer… Developed and distributed by **General Consumer Electronics (GCE), a
> subsidiary of the Milton Bradley Company**, the Vectrex **comes closer to duplicating a
> real arcade game than any other game system on the market**.

Two details the spec table above does not carry, both of which were selling points rather
than trivia:

**It did not need the television.** *BYTE*: "Because the Vectrex is a stand-alone system,
**you won't have to fight other people for use of the television set**. Just take your
Vectrex and plug it in wherever you have an electrical outlet." In 1982, in a house with one
television, that is the whole pitch.

**The controller detached, and a second one plugged in.** A detachable control panel with a
self-centering joystick and four buttons, plus an optional second panel for two players.

⚠ *BYTE* describes GCE as **already a Milton Bradley subsidiary at launch**, which refines
the timeline in the table above: the acquisition was not a later rescue of a successful
product but the arrangement the Vectrex shipped under.

## Eighteen months later

Set that launch review beside what *Big K* reported in **June 1984**:

> In America, Vectrex [company] **Milton Bradley has stopped production of the system and is
> dumping redundant stock at low prices.**

**"One of the greatest game machines we have seen this year" to dumped stock in eighteen
months.** Nothing in the hardware changed. What changed was the market it had been launched
into — see [Milton Bradley](/vault/companies/milton-bradley) for how the British end of that
was handled, and the [1983 crash](/vault/phenomena/1983-crash) for the wider collapse.

The Vectrex is usually filed as a machine that was too strange to succeed. The period record
suggests something less flattering to the market and more flattering to GCE: it was
**reviewed as the best thing of its year, and killed by timing**.

## Legacy

GCE's brief existence produced one of gaming's most unique consoles. The Vectrex's singular technology ensured its place in gaming history, and the active collector/homebrew community keeps the platform alive decades later.
