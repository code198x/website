## Overview

Beyond published for about three years and is remembered almost entirely for two games — **Mike Singleton's *Lords of Midnight* and its sequel *Doomdark's Revenge*** — which did something with a 48K Spectrum that nobody had managed and few managed afterwards.

## Fast facts

- **Seen in the press:** reviewed in *CRASH*, *ZZAP!64* and *Amstrad Action* from **1985 to 1987**, at **£5.95–£9.95**.
- **Known for:** *Lords of Midnight*, *Doomdark's Revenge*, *Shadowfire*, *Enigma Force*, *Spy vs Spy*, *Dante's Inferno*.
- **Fate:** absorbed into British Telecom's software operation, alongside [Firebird](/vault/companies/firebird).

## Landscaping

*Lords of Midnight* (1984) gave the player a world of some **4,000 locations**, each viewable from **eight directions**, on a machine with 48 kilobytes of memory. Storing 32,000 pictures was obviously impossible. Storing none of them was the answer.

Singleton's technique — he called it **landscaping** — held only a map of what was where, and *drew* each view when you looked: the mountains behind, the forest in front, the tower on the horizon, sized and layered by distance. What you saw was generated from the geometry, not retrieved.

The effect on a player in 1984 was of standing somewhere. Turn around and the landscape moved coherently. Walk toward a distant peak and it grew. That is unremarkable now and was close to unprecedented then, on hardware with no 3D capability whatsoever.

It also made the game **enormous in a way the cassette could not explain** — one of the era's recurring magic tricks, where a program smaller than a modern photograph contains a world you can get lost in.

## The rest, and the end

*Shadowfire* and *Enigma Force* were real-time squad games driven entirely by an icon interface, unusual for the Spectrum and better regarded than they are remembered. *Spy vs Spy* brought the *MAD* magazine strip across from the US.

Beyond did not last. Like several publishers of the period it was drawn into **British Telecom's software operation** — the same corporate move that produced Firebird and Rainbird — and the name stopped appearing.

## Why it mattered

Beyond is the entry for a specific idea: that the way past a memory limit is often **not to store the thing but to describe how to make it**. Landscaping is procedural generation, arrived at from necessity, years before the term was in common use.

Singleton's games are also a reminder that the British 8-bit scene produced work of real ambition alongside the arcade conversions — and that a single programmer with a good idea could still, in 1984, out-think an entire industry.
