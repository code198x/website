## Overview
Gargoyle Games were **Roy Carter** and **Greg Follis**, working out of
Birmingham. *CRASH* introduced them in May 1984 as a new company whose first
game, *Ad Astra*, was a £5.95 space combat game written in machine code — and
called it "quite a debut".

What followed was a run of arcade adventures with a common temperament: large,
slow, literate, and built around a rotoscoped walking animation that gave their
characters a distinctive gait. They are the clearest example in this Vault of a
studio whose commercial position was set by its genre rather than its quality.

## Fast facts
- **Founders:** Roy Carter and Greg Follis, credited together in *CRASH* review boxes from 1984 onwards.
- **Base:** Birmingham.
- **First game:** *Ad Astra* (1984), £5.95, 48K, machine code.
- **Best known for:** *Tir Na Nog*, *Dun Darach*, *Marsport*, *Heavy on the Magick*.
- **Typical price:** £9.95 — full price, not budget.

## The catalogue
*Tir Na Nog* (1984) established the house style: an Irish-mythological
landscape explored by a large, smoothly animated Cuchulainn, with an inventory
system that expected forward planning across some 150 objects. *CRASH*'s review
credits Follis and Carter jointly and dwells on the strategic thinking the
object limit forces.

*Dun Darach* (1985) refined it into a city. *Marsport* (1985) moved the same
engine to science fiction and, by *CRASH*'s account, beat Hewson's *Dragontorc*
in that year's reader voting. *Heavy on the Magick* (1986) gave the formula a
sorcerer, Axil, and demons who bargain rather than attack.

## Where they charted
Gargoyle's games did well, and did well in a particular place. [*Heavy on the
Magick*](/vault/games/heavy-on-the-magick) held **#1** in *CRASH*'s Adventure
chart three separate times while sitting at **#16** in the general Hotline
chart. [*Dun Darach*](/vault/games/dun-darach) peaked at #5 in the general chart
in 1985 and was still in the Adventure Top 30 in early 1987.

That is the signature of a house with a devoted audience rather than a broad
one. The reviews were not the constraint — *Heavy on the Magick* took 5/5 from
*Sinclair User* and *Your Computer* and 9/10 from *CRASH* and *Your Sinclair* —
the shelf was.

*CRASH* thought them interesting enough to profile the crew in its 1985/6
Christmas Special, which is not something that happened to every two-person
company.
