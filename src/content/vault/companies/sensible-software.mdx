Sensible Software was two school friends from Essex, [Jon Hare](/vault/people/jon-hare) and Chris Yates, and for most of its first five years it was hardly more than that. Hare drew and designed, Yates programmed. Working at first from Yates's house, they made *[Wizball](/vault/games/wizball)* and *MicroProse Soccer* for the [Commodore 64](/vault/systems/commodore-64), then *Mega-lo-Mania*, *[Sensible Soccer](/vault/games/sensible-soccer)* and *[Cannon Fodder](/vault/games/cannon-fodder)* for the [Amiga](/vault/systems/commodore-amiga). By 1994 *[AMIGA POWER](/vault/magazines/amiga-power)*'s all-time top 100 had *Sensible Soccer* at number one and *Cannon Fodder* at number two. The magazines knew the pair by their nicknames: *[ACE](/vault/magazines/ace-magazine)* introduced them in 1991 as "the long-haired duo of Jon 'Jovial Jops' Hare and Chris 'Cuddly Crix' Yates".

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** March 1986, by [Jon Hare](/vault/people/jon-hare) and Chris Yates.
- **Based:** Essex. By 1994 the office was in Saffron Walden.
- **Commodore 64 years:** *Parallax* (1986) and *[Wizball](/vault/games/wizball)* (1987) for [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software), the *Shoot-'Em-Up Construction Kit* (1987) for Palace's Outlaw label, *MicroProse Soccer* (1988) for [MicroProse](/vault/companies/microprose).
- **Amiga years:** *International 3D Tennis* (1990), *Mega-lo-Mania* (1991), *Wizkid* (1992), *[Sensible Soccer](/vault/games/sensible-soccer)* (1992), *[Cannon Fodder](/vault/games/cannon-fodder)* (1993), *Cannon Fodder 2* (1994), *Sensible World of Soccer* (1994), *Sensible Golf* (1995).
- **Ended:** bought by [Codemasters](/vault/companies/codemasters) in 1999.

## A band first

Hare and Yates met at school. "I knew Jon from the fifth year of school," Yates told *[ZZAP!](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* in 1990, "we formed a band." The band, which Hare said went through names including Zeus, Hamsterfish, Dark Goose and finally Touchstone, formed in Chelmsford and played around Essex. Music stayed part of the company: when [Martin Galway](/vault/people/martin-galway) wrote the *Wizball* music, the pair played him the guitar and bass lines.

Their first computer work was for other people. Yates took a job as a Spectrum programmer at LT Software in Basildon, and Hare was brought in to draw the graphics. Between them they converted a game to the [ZX Spectrum](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum), drew graphics for System 3's *International Karate*, and wrote a game called *Twister: Mother of Harlots*, which, Hare said, went out under someone else's name. Then they heard about a government scheme that helped people start small businesses, and in March 1986 Sensible Software became official.

## Ocean and the Commodore 64

The first game to carry the Sensible name was *Galax-i-birds*, released by Firebird in August 1986. The one that mattered was *Parallax*, a Commodore 64 shoot-'em-up. Hare remembered driving back from Ocean's Manchester office with "a £1,000 cheque after just signing up Parallax". Ocean released it in October 1986 after six months' work, with music by Ocean's Martin Galway. *[Wizball](/vault/games/wizball)* followed in 1987, took nine months, and went on the cover of *ZZAP!64* with 96%.

At the end of 1987 Sensible gave Palace Software's new label, Outlaw, its first release: the *Shoot-'Em-Up Construction Kit*, sold at £14.95. It began, *ZZAP!* reported, as "a series of graphic code utilities written by Chris", and it let anyone with a Commodore 64 design and share their own scrolling shooter. In August 1988 *ZZAP!* announced that Galway had joined Sensible as its third member, and he was there while they wrote *MicroProse Soccer*. In 1991 *ACE* still called it "quite simply, the best 8-bit football game ever". Getting paid for it was harder. "Writs were involved to get the money out of MicroProse," *ZZAP!* reported.

## Amiga years

Sensible's first 16-bit game was *International 3D Tennis* for Palace in 1990. The Commodore 64 version was theirs, and David Korn wrote the Amiga and ST versions. *Mega-lo-Mania* (1991), a god game published by Mirrorsoft, was the first Sensible program by Chris Chapman, who later programmed *Sensible Soccer*. *AMIGA POWER* gave it 90% and later called it "one of the most accessible, and best, God-sims ever created". *Wizkid*, the sequel to *Wizball*, followed in 1992.

*[Sensible Soccer](/vault/games/sensible-soccer)*, published by [Renegade](/vault/companies/renegade) in 1992, was Hare's answer to *Kick Off 2*. It topped the charts for four months, and Sensible kept updating it, then built it into *Sensible World of Soccer* (1994), which *AMIGA POWER* gave 95%, its highest score. *[Cannon Fodder](/vault/games/cannon-fodder)* (Virgin, 1993) put the same tiny figures into a war game that counted its dead, and drew protests over the poppy on its packaging. By then the team had grown. *AMIGA POWER* introduced some of them in 1994: Julian "Jools" Jameson, the programmer of *Cannon Fodder*; the artist Stoo Cambridge; the programmer Chris Chapman; and Richard Joseph, who was writing the music and sound effects for their golf game. Hare's own entry in that list said his "ultimate ambition is to cut a single and get it into the Gallup Top 10".

*Sensible Golf* (Virgin, 1995) was, *AMIGA POWER* reported, "the last ever Amiga game Sensible intend to release", and the magazine was disappointed with it.

## The last years

The project Hare cared most about in the late 1990s never came out. *Sex 'n' Drugs 'n' Rock 'n' Roll* was, *[Edge](/vault/magazines/edge)* reported, "a labour of love" for Hare. Time Warner first signed it, then found it too risqué, and Sensible took it to around 20 other publishers without an acceptable offer. As early as 1995 Hare had told *[Amiga Computing](/vault/magazines/amiga-computing)* that publishers liked the game but feared the media reaction to it.

In 1999 [Codemasters](/vault/companies/codemasters) bought Sensible Software for an undisclosed sum, with the rights to its back catalogue, and Hare went on to lead new projects for Codemasters. In a list of his top ten moments for *Edge* the following year, Hare described the sale: "after 13 years, the last two of which had been living hell, Chris and I had managed to emerge unscathed… without ever having had a single argument." In 2000 *PC Zone* previewed a *Cannon Fodder 3D* from Sensible for Codemasters, which was never released, and Codemasters put *Cannon Fodder* on the Game Boy Color. The Sensible name lived on in re-releases, including a new *Sensible Soccer* in 2006 with Hare back on board. Hare's later career is covered in his own entry.
