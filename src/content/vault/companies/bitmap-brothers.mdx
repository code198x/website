The Bitmap Brothers were not brothers. Mike Montgomery, Eric Matthews and Steve Kelly were three programmers who, *[Amiga Computing](/vault/magazines/amiga-computing)* explained in 1988, were "like the Thompson Twins, … not really related". What they shared was an idea about how games should be sold. "As developers we felt that publishers often took the limelight," Montgomery said later. "After all, you buy music albums and singles for the artist not the record label they're published on." So they gave themselves a band's name, posed for photographs like pop stars, and made sure theirs was the name people remembered. Between 1988 and 1993 their games, from *Xenon* to *The Chaos Engine*, were among the best reviewed on the [Amiga](/vault/systems/commodore-amiga) and [Atari ST](/vault/systems/atari-st), and by 1990 they had their own publishing label.

## Fast facts

- **Formed:** 1987, by Mike Montgomery, Eric Matthews and Steve Kelly.
- **Publishers:** [Melbourne House](/vault/companies/melbourne-house) (*Xenon*), then [Mirrorsoft](/vault/companies/mirrorsoft)'s Image Works (1988–1990), then their own label, [Renegade](/vault/companies/renegade) (from 1991), which Warner bought in the mid-1990s.
- **Artists:** [Dan Malone](/vault/people/dan-malone) and Mark Coleman drew much of the metallic look; Richard Joseph did sound and music programming.
- **Main games:** *Xenon* (1988), *Speedball* (1988), *[Xenon 2: Megablast](/vault/games/xenon-2)* (1989), *Cadaver* (1990), *[Speedball 2: Brutal Deluxe](/vault/games/speedball-2)* (1990), *[Gods](/vault/games/gods)* (1991), *Magic Pockets* (1991), *[The Chaos Engine](/vault/games/chaos-engine)* (1993), *Z* (1996), *Speedball 2100* (2000), *Z: Steel Soldiers* (2001).
- **Now:** Rebellion bought the Bitmap Brothers brand and its games from Mike Montgomery in November 2019.

## Before the Brothers

Montgomery had been a project leader at Virgin, where he worked with Matthews on the ZX Spectrum version of *Scalextric*; Kelly had worked on *Chequered Flag*, a Spectrum driving game for Psion. Between them, *Amiga User International* listed, they had also written versions of *Scrabble*, *Monopoly*, *Cluedo*, *Backgammon* and *Match Point*, and a karate game for the QL and Amiga. Kelly told *Amiga Format* that he had come to computers by accident: he was a warehouse foreman until he hurt his back, and started programming while he recovered. Montgomery remembered the name as the hardest part: "At first we thought this sounded a bit crap, but after some time we decided we rather liked it."

## Xenon and Speedball

Their first game as a team was *Xenon*, a vertically scrolling shoot-'em-up started in June 1987, written for the ST first and then converted to the Amiga. It was the first release on Melbourne House's 16-bit label, early in 1988, with music by [David Whittaker](/vault/people/david-whittaker), and the Bitmaps told *Amiga User International* they had spent most of their time on the scrolling and sprite routines. It also went into Arcadia's Amiga-based coin-op machines, and onto the children's television programme *Get Fresh*, which needed a special version in which you lost points instead of lives.

*Xenon*'s success, *Amiga Computing* reported, "attracted the big bucks from Mirrorsoft", which outbid Melbourne House and its owners and signed the Bitmaps to its new label, Image Works. Their first game there was *Speedball* (1988), a violent future sport that borrowed, the magazine said, from *Rollerball*, football and Montgomery's own sport, squash. It was voted 16-bit Game of the Year at the [Golden Joystick Awards](/vault/events/golden-joystick-awards) in April 1989. *[Xenon 2: Megablast](/vault/games/xenon-2)* (1989), with Bomb the Bass on the title screen, followed, and then two very different games in 1990: *Cadaver*, an isometric adventure that Montgomery later called one of his favourites to work on, and *[Speedball 2: Brutal Deluxe](/vault/games/speedball-2)*, the game most people still associate with them.

## Renegade

Working with Bomb the Bass on *Xenon 2* brought the Bitmaps into contact with Bomb the Bass's record label, Rhythm King. In the autumn of 1990 they left Mirrorsoft and set up [Renegade](/vault/companies/renegade), their own label, with Rhythm King and the former Mirrorsoft marketing man Tom Watson. It promised developers half the profits and put their names first, as a record label does its artists. Renegade's own entry tells its story.

Their first two Renegade games came out in 1991. *[Gods](/vault/games/gods)* was a platform game that quietly adjusted itself to how well you played. *Magic Pockets* was a lighter platform game, with Rhythm King's Betty Boo on the soundtrack: Richard Joseph sampled her hit "Doin' the Do" for the Amiga. *[The Chaos Engine](/vault/games/chaos-engine)* (1993), a two-player shoot-'em-up in a steam-driven Victorian England, was, as *Retro Gamer* counted them, their eighth release.

## PC years

The Bitmaps' next game took years. *Z*, a real-time war game with comic cut-scenes, was shown at four European Computer Trade Shows in a row before it finally came out on the PC in 1996. *[Edge](/vault/magazines/edge)* gave it eight out of ten, but found it "still very much rooted in the Bitmaps' golden Amiga age" after *Command & Conquer* had arrived. The same year Time Warner Interactive published *The Chaos Engine 2* for the Amiga.

*Speedball 2100* (2000) remade *Speedball 2* for the PlayStation. Montgomery did much of the programming and said it was his favourite *Speedball*; *Edge* gave it 4/10. *Z: Steel Soldiers*, a 3D sequel published by EON Digital in 2001, fared better: *Edge* found its environments "at the cutting edge" and noted that, unlike *Z*, it had not arrived looking dated. In 2004 Montgomery, by then the company's managing director, told *[Retro Gamer](/vault/magazines/retro-gamer)* the studio was working on *World War II: Frontline Command* and still "tinkering away" on a new *Speedball*, and that it was bringing its old games to handhelds and pocket PCs.

## Later years

Public sources record that Montgomery's involvement with the Bitmap Brothers ended in 2004, when he and John Phillips set up Tower Studios, which by 2005 they were running with [Jon Hare](/vault/people/jon-hare). The old games carried on without new ones: *Speedball 2* was remade for the PC as *Speedball 2 Tournament* in 2007, and appeared on phones and tablets from 2010 and as *Speedball 2 HD* in 2013. In November 2019 Rebellion announced that it had bought the Bitmap Brothers brand and its games, *Xenon*, *Speedball*, *The Chaos Engine* and *Z* among them, from Montgomery, and said it would bring the classic games to new platforms and make new ones.
