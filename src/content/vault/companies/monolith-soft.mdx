Monolith Soft began as a group of people who wanted to finish a story. Tetsuya Takahashi had directed *[Xenogears](/vault/games/xenogears)* at [Square](/vault/companies/square), a 1998 PlayStation role-playing game whose second disc ran short of time and money, and which its makers had described as one episode of a longer saga. In 1999 he and other Square staff set up their own company, with [Namco](/vault/companies/namco) as its backer, and started again with *Xenosaga*. Eight years later [Nintendo](/vault/companies/nintendo) bought the studio. It went on to make *Xenoblade Chronicles*, and to become one of the developers Nintendo leans on hardest for its own biggest games.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1 October 1999, in Tokyo.
- **Founders:** Hirohide Sugiura, who became its president and still heads it; [Tetsuya Takahashi](/vault/people/tetsuya-takahashi); and Yasuyuki Honne, all formerly of Square.
- **Owners:** Namco at first; [Nintendo](/vault/companies/nintendo) from 2007; now wholly owned by Nintendo.
- **Games for Namco:** *Xenosaga Episode I*, *II* and *III* on the PlayStation 2 (in America 2003, 2005 and 2006); *Baten Kaitos* (GameCube, 2003, with tri-Crescendo) and its sequel (2006); *Namco x Capcom* (PlayStation 2, 2005).
- **Games for Nintendo:** *Disaster: Day of Crisis* (Wii, 2008), *Soma Bringer* (DS, 2008), *Xenoblade Chronicles* (Wii, 2010), *Xenoblade Chronicles X* (Wii U, 2015), *Xenoblade Chronicles 2* (Switch, 2017), *Xenoblade Chronicles 3* (Switch, 2022).
- **Studios:** headquarters in Tokyo, a second Tokyo studio at Osaki, and a studio in Kyoto.

## Leaving Square

The founding story, as Sugiura told it to *Famitsu* in 2020, starts with *Xenogears*. He had not worked on the game but "fell in love" with it, and when a sequel proved impossible at Square, which was putting its money into the film *Final Fantasy: The Spirits Within*, he set out to make a company that could carry the idea on. Monolith Soft's own company profile says that Sugiura, who had worked at ASCII, Konami, Warp and Square, founded it in 1999, and that Takahashi and Honne, both from Square, took part in the founding. It was incorporated on 1 October 1999.

The money came from Namco. *IGN Unplugged* reported in 2001 that "Takahashi and a great many of *Xenogears*' staff left Square after the game's development concluded" and, "as Monolith Soft, a new company under the wing of Namco", worked in secret on a project known only as Project X. It got its name, *Xenosaga*, at the Tokyo Game Show in the spring of 2001.

## Xenosaga and the Namco years

Square kept the rights to *Xenogears*, so *Xenosaga* was a new story, though one that *Hyper* noted was full of references to the old one. Its episodes took their subtitles from books by Friedrich Nietzsche. *Episode I: Der Wille zur Macht* reached America in 2003, when *Edge* gave it seven out of ten. Its long cut-scenes were the point and the problem. Reviewing *Episode II* in 2005, *Edge* wrote that "cut-scenes are the new king, gameplay elements little more than lines to link the drama", and warned that the episodic plan was "a rod for its own back". *Xenosaga* had been planned in six parts; *Electronic Gaming Monthly* reported at the end of 2005 that Namco would "wrap up the planned six-part space opera series" with the third, due in America in the autumn of 2006.

Monolith made other things for Namco too. *Baten Kaitos* (2003), made with tri-Crescendo for the GameCube, fought its battles with cards drawn at speed from a deck, which *Edge* found demanded "beat 'em up accuracy, unhesitating speed and mathematical awareness". *Namco x Capcom* (2005), a strategy role-playing game for the PlayStation 2, gathered characters from both companies' games; *Edge* described Monolith as the "Namco subsidiary" making it, and pointed out that its designers had to create most of the attacks themselves: Capcom's Ryu had plenty of source material, but "how Klonoa handles himself in a fight is another matter".

## Nintendo

In 2007 Nintendo bought Monolith Soft from Namco Bandai. *Gamasutra* reported in April that Nintendo had taken 80% of the shares and that Namco Bandai, which had held 96%, would keep 16%. *Edge* reported later that year that Monolith had become a Nintendo subsidiary in May and was making *Disaster: Day of Crisis*, an action game about surviving natural disasters, for the Wii.

The big project was *Xenoblade*, released in Japan on 10 June 2010. *Edge* called it "the first major project to result from the acquisition". Its world was the bodies of two dead giants, frozen mid-battle, with the people living on them, and Takahashi set out to keep the story from getting in the way of the game. "Ahead of the game's Japanese release," *Edge* wrote in its review, "he's reported to have said that the cutscene-heavy approach of his previous titles was a 'dead end'." *Edge* thought the result showed that "Takahashi's revised approach has led to focus and a return to creative form", and that no role-playing game of the previous decade had shown "such a concerted focus" on redefining the genre "by learning from past mistakes". It came to Europe in 2011 as *Xenoblade Chronicles*; at the end of that year *Edge* counted it among the year's best role-playing games and noted that it had still not reached America. Takahashi went on to direct *Xenoblade Chronicles X* for the Wii U, in which the player could pilot transforming robots in an open world, and two numbered sequels for the Switch.

## Building Nintendo's games

Much of Monolith Soft's work since 2007 does not carry its name on the box. The studio's own list of games includes a long column of titles where it did part of the development under contract. For Nintendo these include *Super Smash Bros. Brawl* (2008), *The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword* (2011), *Animal Crossing: New Leaf* (2012), *Pikmin 3* (2013), *The Legend of Zelda: A Link Between Worlds* (2013), *Splatoon* (2015) and its sequels, *[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild](/vault/games/breath-of-the-wild)* (2017), *Animal Crossing: New Horizons* (2020), *The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom* (2023) and *Mario Kart World* (2025). It also worked on Namco Bandai's *Project X Zone* games for the 3DS.

Nintendo bought out the remaining shareholders over the years that followed, and Monolith Soft's company profile now lists Nintendo as its sole owner, with Sugiura still its representative director and Takahashi, a co-founder, one of its directors.
