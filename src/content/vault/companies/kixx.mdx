In September 1988 *Commodore User* reviewed a game most of its readers already knew. U.S. Gold's conversion of Atari's arcade game *[Gauntlet](/vault/games/gauntlet)* had been reviewed at full price early in 1987. Now it was back at £2.99, on a new label. "This is US Gold's new cheapo label," the reviewer wrote. "And what a first release!"

The label was **Kixx**, and its business was giving old games a second life. [U.S. Gold](/vault/companies/us-gold) had built its name on expensive American imports and arcade licences. Kixx took games whose full-price sales had run out and sold them again at a pocket-money price. It kept doing so for the rest of the 8-bit era, then did the same on the Amiga, the Atari ST and the PC.

## Fast facts

- **Owner:** [U.S. Gold](/vault/companies/us-gold), and from 1996 [Eidos](/vault/companies/eidos).
- **Launched:** summer 1988, at £2.99 a cassette.
- **First managers:** John Mearman and Lesley Bossen, both formerly of [Mastertronic](/vault/companies/mastertronic).
- **First releases:** *[Gauntlet](/vault/games/gauntlet)* and *Metrocross*.
- **Later ranges:** 16-bit Kixx at £7.99 from 1991; Kixx XL at £12.99 to £14.99 from 1992; Kixx Classics on the PC at £4.99 by 1998.

## A cheap label from a dear publisher

A budget label was an odd fit for U.S. Gold, which had always charged more than its rivals. According to *The Story of US Gold*, the idea grew out of its compilations. Tim Chaney, U.S. Gold's managing director, complained that a game sold in April was unwanted by September. The *They Sold a Million* compilations, made with [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software), showed that old hits could sell again. Mastertronic had already proved that games could sell at £1.99. Chaney chose £2.99, or £4.99 on disk.

*CRASH* announced the label in its September 1988 issue, calling it "a very selective budget venture. Known as Kixx (not sure why)". It expected about 12 titles in the first year, starting with games *CRASH* had already reviewed: *Gauntlet* (92%), *Metrocross* (77%), *World Games* (71%), *Ace of Aces* (62%) and *Tenth Frame* (55%). They would sell at £2.99 from "some time in the middle of August". *Amstrad Action* reported in October that *Gauntlet* and *Metrocross* were out.

*ACE* named the people behind it in November. "Kixx has been formed by ex-Mastertronic employees John Mearman and Lesley Bossen, who reckon the Kixx philosophy of quality not quantity is just the thing the market needs." The first games came from "the U.S.Gold and Gremlin back catalogue", so from the start Kixx sold [Gremlin Graphics](/vault/companies/gremlin-graphics) titles as well as U.S. Gold's own. *ACE*'s plan was bigger than *CRASH*'s: 24 titles over the next year, "approximately a third of those being original titles", all at £2.99 on cassette.

## New games at budget prices

The original games did appear. In February 1989 *ACE* reported that Kixx was continuing "its re-release theme" with *Bravestar*, *World Games*, *Way of the Tiger*, *Rygar* and *Super Cycle*, and that the Spanish developer Topo Soft, which had written U.S. Gold's *Pepsi Challenge* game *Mad Mix*, had been signed "to produce Kixx's first original games, *Blackbeard* and *Colosseum*".

*ACE* reviewed *Blackbeard* the next month: "Kixx's first original product", £2.99 on the Spectrum and Amstrad. The player guides a pirate around the decks of HMS *Victory* to recover his stolen treasure map, and drinking too much of the rum that restores his energy leaves him staggering. "Entertaining — if a little slow", the review concluded, with an ACE rating of 670 out of 1,000.

## Everyone's back catalogue

Kixx did not only sell U.S. Gold games. By the early 1990s its 8-bit list included [Thalamus](/vault/companies/thalamus) games such as *Hunter's Moon*, *Creatures* and *[Turrican](/vault/games/turrican)*, [System 3](/vault/companies/system-3)'s *Flimbo's Quest* and MicroProse's *MicroProse Soccer*. In September 1992 *ZZAP!64* reviewed *Flimbo's Quest* and Sega's *Super Monaco GP* as Kixx cassettes at £3.99, and its readers' chart for that month put *MicroProse Soccer*, *Creatures* and *Turrican* on Kixx in its top five.

The price rose slowly. *ZZAP!64*'s May 1991 issue had Kixx games at both £2.99 and £3.99, and £3.99 became the usual 8-bit price. The label also sold compilations: in 1991 *Kixx Multimix 1* collected *World Class Leaderboard* and other *Leaderboard* golf games.

Some re-releases came back to judgement. *[Out Run](/vault/games/out-run)*, the arcade licence *CRASH* had given 72% at full price in 1988, returned on Kixx at £2.99 and scored 58% in December 1990.

## The 16-bit labels

In March 1991 *Amiga Computing* reported that U.S. Gold was "about to release the first games on their Kixx label" for the Amiga, "all priced at £7.99". The first batch included *RoadBlasters*, *Thunder Blade*, *Blasteroids*, *World Class Leaderboard*, *Gauntlet II* and *Out Run*.

A dearer range followed. *The Story of US Gold* dates **Kixx XL** to November 1992, a label "to cater for more serious gamers". *Amiga Format*'s December 1992 budget guide had Kixx XL selling *Midwinter* and *Battlehawks 1942* at £12.99, while plain Kixx sold *Rick Dangerous* at £7.99. In September 1994 *[Amiga Power](/vault/magazines/amiga-power)*'s chart had *[Street Fighter 2](/vault/games/street-fighter-ii)* and *Battle of Britain* on Kixx at £14.99, and the magazine gave the Kixx re-release of MicroProse's *Stunt Car Racer* 91%. In 1995 the label was selling CD32 games at £10. According to *The Story of US Gold*, Kixx XL's sales grew from £1.8 million in its first year to £3.1 million by 1994.

## Under Eidos

In 1996 [Eidos](/vault/companies/eidos) bought CentreGold, the group that owned U.S. Gold. Eidos dropped the U.S. Gold name but, according to *The Story of US Gold*, kept Kixx with the rest of the subsidiaries, and re-released U.S. Gold's last game, *Olympic Games: Atlanta 1996*, on it. The label carried on selling PC games. In 1997 *PC Zone* advertised an Our Price promotion with prize vouchers inside every Kixx box, and in November 1998 it reviewed the *Titanic* adventure as a £4.99 "Kixx Classics" release. The period and later sources checked for this entry do not record when Eidos stopped using the name.
