## Overview

When crackers cracked a game, the better ones didn't stop at removing the copy protection — they improved it. A **trainer** is a bundle of cheats patched into the game itself: infinite lives, unlimited energy, frozen timers, level skips. The count became a boast. A crack advertised as "+5" promised five cheats, and the number, displayed proudly in the [crack intro](/vault/communities/crack-intros), was a measure of how much extra work the group had put in.

For a lot of players, the trainer was the whole appeal — the reason you sought out a particular group's release over another's.

## How it works

A trainer is memory surgery. The cracker finds where the game stores a value — the lives counter, the energy bar — usually by freezing the machine and searching memory for the number that changes when you take a hit. Replace the instruction that *decrements* lives with one that does nothing, and lives become infinite. A front-end menu, added before the game starts, lets the player toggle each cheat on or off.

## Why it mattered

Trainers turned a crack from a copy into a *product* with features, and competition over them pushed groups to add ever more (and ever more obscure) cheats. They are also a direct ancestor of the cheat menus, trainer programs, and memory-editing tools that long outlived the cracking scene that invented them.
