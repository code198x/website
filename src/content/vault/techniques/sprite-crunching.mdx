**Sprite crunching** makes a C64 sprite *shorter* than the 21 lines the hardware
gives you, by deliberately corrupting the VIC-II's internal sprite counter at a
cycle-exact moment.

> Sprite crunching is based on a glitch in the VIC-chip (as usual) that appears on a
> cycle-exact position every rasterline.

## Why it is hard

The counter normally advances by three each line, ending the sprite when it reaches
`$3f`. Triggering the glitch changes what gets added — and not predictably:

> Sometimes 3 will be added (as normal), sometimes something else. Sometimes a
> negative value will be added(!).

Miss `$3f` and the sprite does not end. The counter wraps to `$00` or `$01` and "the
sprite will continue to be displayed for some 21 more lines", misaligned.

So the technique is not parametric. You cannot ask for a 17-line sprite; you have to
find a path through the counter's behaviour that happens to land on one.

## Crossbow mapped the table by hand

That path exists because somebody wrote down what the glitch does at every one of
the counter's 64 values. In *Krestage* by **Crest** you can find

> exact documentation of what happens on each value of the sprite-graphics-counter
> when triggering the glitch, **hand-haxxored by Crossbow himself**. Finding the
> (probably) shortest path through the table, he managed to shrink the sprites from
> 21 lines to 17.

Codebase64 reproduces the table, and then leaves the record standing open:

> Now play away with it. Can you make a sprite smaller than 17 lines and beat
> Crossbow? ;D

## Three productions, three different uses

| Production | What it did |
|---|---|
| ***Rutig Banan*** / Fairlight | Glitch on the third sprite line adding −1, so the sprite draws "almost 3 times" — about 120 lines of sprite area to work in |
| ***Krestage*** / Crest | "The first example of sprite crunching when used for shrinking sprites" |
| ***Edge of Disgrace*** / Booze Design | Glitch at position `$1e` adding −9 inside a 4×4 plasma, so `$d017` is written "twice every 4 lines" instead of twice per line, "saving some life supporting cycles" |

## The record it set

*Rutig Banan* (Pernod/Fairlight, **5 November 1989**) is the first recorded use.
Eight years later *Krestage* used the technique to put **144 sprites** on screen,
against 112 for conventional sprites.
