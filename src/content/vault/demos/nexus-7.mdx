At The Party 1994, the Danish scene gathering held between Christmas and New Year, twenty-one other Amiga demos lost to *Nexus 7*, and none came close. The official results gave it 1,443 points. The runner-up, Virtual Dreams' *Psychedelic*, scored 710, and Bomb's *Motion* came third with 501. *Nexus 7* was made by [Andromeda](/vault/groups/andromeda), a group from Norway, for the AGA Amigas, the A1200 and A4000. Three months later *[Amiga Format](/vault/magazines/amiga-format)* told its readers that it was "worth more than a look, it's worth a purchase and a study".

## Fast facts

- **Group:** [Andromeda](/vault/groups/andromeda), from Norway.
- **Platform:** Amiga with the AGA chipset (A1200 and A4000).
- **Released:** at [The Party](/vault/events/the-party) 1994, Denmark, December 1994.
- **Placing:** first in the Amiga demo competition, 1,443 points to 710.
- **Credits:** code by Dr. Jekyll, Mr. Hyde and View; graphics by Archmage; music by Interphace.

## Made for AGA

AGA, the [chipset](/vault/hardware/amiga-chipset) in the A1200 and A4000, put 256 colours on screen instead of 32, from a palette of 16.7 million. *Nexus 7* was Andromeda's first production for the new machines; the group's earlier demos, such as *D.O.S.*, were written for the older chipset. The Party's results list it under Dr. Jekyll of Andromeda, and Demozoo credits the code to Dr. Jekyll, Mr. Hyde and View, the graphics to Archmage and the music to Interphace. The field it beat included demos from [Sanity](/vault/groups/sanity), [Oxyron](/vault/groups/oxyron) and Polka Brothers.

## What Amiga Format saw

Steve McGill and Jason Holborn's PD Select column in *Amiga Format* reviewed *Nexus 7* in March 1995, from the Freestyle PD library. The review found "loads of top techie trickery", presented "in a slick, professional manner (admittedly more for the sake of impressiveness than entertainment)", and picked out four routines:

- **Gouraud Pulse:** irregular shapes, one like a Möbius strip, pulsing with shading "that resembles a hot coal changing heat from cherry red to furnace white hot".
- **Motion Blur:** a rotating, blurring picture of a tortured face. Scaled and rotated texture-mapped images were "a bit of a cliché in demos these days", but this one was "Digital high art indeed".
- **Texture-Mapped Bouncy Ball:** a ball whose transparency showed the background through it as it passed. "So much for the Sony PlayStation and its feature-laden hardware."
- **Plasma Zoom:** which carried the message "Use your perception to penetrate the void of your mind", and which the reviewer suggested viewing like a stereogram.

The verdict: "I think *Nexus 7* is a stonker of a demo."

British public-domain libraries kept it on their lists through 1995. One advertised it in *Amiga Format* as "A must for AGA Amiga!" and another in *CU Amiga* as "voted 1st at the Party 94! Absolute must". The [Andromeda](/vault/groups/andromeda) entry has more on those listings and on the group's later work.
