## Overview

*Jesus On E's* (1996), by Rebel, is one of the most talked-about ZX Spectrum demos of its era — partly for its cheerfully provocative, pure-mid-90s-rave title, but mostly for what it pulled off on the humblest hardware in the range. Much of the Eastern European scene's best work leaned on the 128K machine's AY sound chip and the favourable video timing of Pentagon clones. *Jesus On E's* did its multicolour conjuring on the plain 48K Spectrum.

That constraint is the whole point. Squeezing smooth, colourful, beam-synced effects from the cheapest Spectrum, with the least to give, is exactly the kind of stubborn ingenuity the scene prized.

## Fast facts

- **Group:** Rebel.
- **Platform:** ZX Spectrum (48K).
- **Released:** 1996.
- **Noted for:** Ambitious multicolour on bare 48K hardware.

## Why it stuck

Demos are remembered for the gap between what the hardware should allow and what ends up on screen, and *Jesus On E's* has one of the wider gaps on the Spectrum. It is a regular fixture in conversations about the platform's finest — and the title guaranteed nobody forgot the name.
