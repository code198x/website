## Overview

Released in October 1992 by the British group LSD, *Jesus On E's* is less a conventional demo than a piece of early-90s rave culture pressed onto two Amiga floppies. Built around a concept by Echo (Graham, from Grantham), it runs about half an hour: a continuous breakbeat and hardcore mix, sample-heavy and relentless, locked to visuals that anticipate the VJ sets the same music would soundtrack in clubs.

It was — and remains — divisive. To some it is a groundbreaking multimedia production and a genuine VJ tool; to others it is repetitive and overlong next to the era's tighter demos. Either way, it is one of the demoscene's most vivid intersections with the wider youth culture of its moment.

## Fast facts

- **Group:** LSD.
- **Platform:** Amiga.
- **Released:** October 1992.
- **Form:** Rave "musicdisk" / extended music-video demo, across two disks.

## A demo of its moment

Where most demos sold technical spectacle, *Jesus On E's* sold a *vibe* — the breakbeat, the samples, the strobing visuals of the 1992 rave. It pointed at a future in which real-time graphics and dance music would keep meeting, on dancefloors and screens alike.
