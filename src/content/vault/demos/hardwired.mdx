Just after Christmas 1991, 1,252 people came to The Party in Aars, Denmark, and the demo competition came with a rule printed at the top of the results: "Productions with greetingscrollers not allowed." *Hardwired* came second, behind Alcatraz's *Odyssey* and ahead of Razor 1911's *[Voyage](/vault/demos/voyage)*. It was the work of two groups, the Danish [Crionics](/vault/groups/crionics) and [The Silents](/vault/groups/the-silents), and it filled two floppy disks. Public-domain libraries sold it for years, and in 2015 *The Amiga Book* put it among its five best Amiga demos, calling it "a seminal work that practically defined the demo scene for years to come".

## Fast facts

- **Groups:** [Crionics](/vault/groups/crionics) and [The Silents](/vault/groups/the-silents), Denmark and Sweden.
- **Platform:** Amiga with 1 MB of memory, on two disks.
- **Released:** at [The Party](/vault/events/the-party) 1991, Aars, Denmark, in December 1991.
- **Result:** second in the demo competition.
- **Credits:** code by The Spy, Deftronic, Murphy, Saxs and Guzzler; graphics by Mikael Balle, Sionic and Zycho; music by [Jesper Kyd](/vault/people/jesper-kyd).

## The Party 1991

The Party was new in 1991. Its results name the hosts as "Melon Dezign/Crystal, Trix/Anarchy Dk and Silents". They list the top three in the demo competition and then another 31 productions without placings, from demos to short intros and a music disk: Alcatraz's *Odyssey* first, "Hard Wired by The Silents" second and *Voyage* third. The results give no points. The ban on greetings, the scrolling lists of hellos to other groups that ran through most demos of the time, is the only rule the results record. See [demo parties](/vault/communities/demo-parties) for how the competitions worked.

The results name only The Silents, but the demo was a joint production. The scene database Demozoo credits it to both groups. All five coders were members of Crionics, and two of the graphic artists, Mikael Balle and Sionic, and the musician, Jesper Kyd, were members of The Silents. Zycho, the third artist, belonged to Crionics. In Britain at least one public-domain library listed it the other way round, as "Hardwired by Crionics".

## What it looks like

*The Amiga Book* describes it as "the epic Hardwired", which "clocks in at nearly eleven minutes and runs the gamut of Amiga programming tricks, throwing around complex geometry like it was child's play. In 1991, this was jaw-dropping stuff and it was hard to believe it fit on just two floppy disks." Demozoo's tags for the demo list the kinds of effect on screen: glenz vectors (see-through 3D shapes), shade bobs, a star scroller, a cube and a 3D scene with shadows. No public source describes how its code produced these effects.

## Reception

*Hardwired* reached most Amiga owners through [public-domain](/vault/distribution/public-domain) libraries, which copied demos onto disk and sold them by post. Through 1992 British libraries advertised it in *Amiga Shopper* and *Amiga User International*, marked as needing 1 MB. In July 1992 the Wakefield library 17-Bit Software ranked it third in its "all time top 10" demos in an advertisement in *The One*, one place above *Odyssey*. American buyers could get it too: in 1994 an advertiser in *Amiga Game Zone* sold a ten-disk demo pack that "includes Odyssey, Hardwired, and 3 others".

It also counted in the scene's own charts. Mark Hellewell, who ran the rival group Anarchy, recalled that The Silents held first place among groups in the Eurochart, a chart voted by sceners and distributed on disk, in January 1992, and that after they "teamed up with Crionics to create the ground-breaking demo Hardwired", it "inevitably pushed them to No.1 once again".

## Afterwards

Crionics broke up early in 1992, and Demozoo records that three of its coders, The Spy, Murphy and Deftronic, joined The Silents. Several of the people behind *Hardwired* later made games. Demozoo lists The Spy, Deftronic, Murphy, Guzzler, Sionic and Jesper Kyd as later members of the Copenhagen developer Zyrinx, whose first game was the Mega Drive shoot 'em up *Sub-Terrania* in 1994. Kyd told *Wireframe* in 2019 that he met the future founders of [IO Interactive](/vault/companies/io-interactive) in the demo scene and started Zyrinx with them. He went on to compose the music for IO Interactive's *[Hitman](/vault/games/hitman)* games.
