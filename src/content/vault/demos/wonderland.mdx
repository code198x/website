The first *Wonderland* was not a Censor Design demo at all. It came out on 28 June 1989 under the name of the Swedish group [Triad](/vault/groups/triad), with code by Psycho, on the day the scene database CSDb gives for the founding of [Censor Design](/vault/groups/censor-design). Bob, one of the new group's founders, explained years later that releasing it as Triad was "something Psycho was very keen to do, as a farewell thing", even though Bob had tried to talk him into putting the Censor label on it. Censor Design took the name with it. It released nine more *Wonderland* demos in four years, declared the series finished, and then brought it back two decades later.

## Fast facts

- **Group:** [Censor Design](/vault/groups/censor-design), Sweden; the first part was released under [Triad](/vault/groups/triad).
- **Platform:** [Commodore 64](/vault/systems/commodore-64).
- **Parts:** *Wonderland* (1989) to *Wonderland X* (1993), then *Wonderland XI* (2012) to *Wonderland XIV* (2023).
- **Competition winners:** *Wonderland II* (DatorMagazin Competition, 1989), *Wonderland III* (Shape and TRC party, 1989), *Wonderland V* (Phenomena & Censor Party, 1990), *Wonderland X* (The Computer Crossroads, 1993) and *Wonderland XII* (BFP, 2013).
- **Regulars:** Bob, a founder of Censor Design, is credited with code on every Censor part from *Wonderland II* to *Wonderland XIV* except *IV*; Swallow, another founder, on most of them.

## 1989 to 1993

The series came quickly at first. CSDb dates *Wonderland II* to July 1989, *III* to October and *IV* to November, and *Wonderland V* to 7 January 1990, when it won the C64 demo competition at the party Censor Design held with the Amiga group [Phenomena](/vault/groups/phenomena). *Wonderland VI*, *1 Year in Wonderland* and *Wonderland VII* followed in 1990, then *VIII* in May 1991. The early parts were group efforts, with guests from other groups: CSDb credits code on *Wonderland III* to six people and its music to seven, among them JCH of Vibrants.

*Wonderland IX* came second at the Brutal and Hurricane summer party in June 1992. In October *ZZAP!64*'s public-domain column reviewed it. "Censor Design are one of the older groups in the scene," it said, "and consequently they've released a huge number of demos, including the Wonderland series. These demos have always featured great design and several new inventions." It liked the intro best, a "movie-like text-flasher, complete with rotating and transforming logos", and noted Bob's "remarkably strange colour animation in the shape of a planet". Most of the demo was sampled music, and two parts had, the reviewer thought, the best sample quality he had heard on the C64. His verdict: "very professionally executed", though it "missed that bit extra which made the other Wonderland demos so special. Besides that it's brilliant!"

*Wonderland X* made up for it. At The Computer Crossroads in Gothenburg, from 29 to 31 May 1993, it won the C64 demo competition ahead of [Booze Design](/vault/groups/booze-design)'s *Totally Stoned II*. The Hungarian magazine *576 KByte* reported that it deserved to win because it contained things never seen before on the C64. At the same party [Oxyron](/vault/groups/oxyron)'s *Coma Light 10* did not place.

## "The last in the series"

Censor Design's next party demo, *Spasmolytic*, came second at [The Party](/vault/events/the-party) in Denmark in December 1993. *576 KByte*'s scene column expected readers to wonder why it did not carry the usual *Wonderland* name. The answer, it reported, came from the group's own programmer at the party: *Wonderland X* had been the last in the series, and from then on the line would carry on as *Spasmolytic*. The column still found the Wonderland hallmarks in it, top-class design and flawless code, with 8-bit sampled music. After that the group's releases thinned out, and the next *Wonderland* came nineteen years after the tenth.

## The return

*Wonderland XI* appeared on 28 October 2012 at [X](/vault/events/x-party)'2012 in the Netherlands, where it came fifth; Oxyron's *[Coma Light 13](/vault/demos/coma-light-13)*, another old series revived, won. Its coders included Bob, CRT and Swallow, all three founders of Censor Design in 1989. *Wonderland XII* won the C64 demo competition at BFP in August 2013, and its credits show the two groups drawing closer: Oxyron's coder Axis and artist Yazoo both worked on it. The next year the two groups made *[Comaland](/vault/demos/comaland)* together and won X'2014.

*Wonderland XIII* came third at X in 2016, and *Wonderland XIV* came third at X in June 2023, with nine coders credited, Bob and Swallow among them. In September 2026 CSDb users' chart of C64 demos placed *Wonderland XIV* 24th.

No published source explains how the *Wonderland* demos' routines work; the period reviews describe what viewers saw and heard. The [SID entry](/vault/hardware/sid-chip) explains how the C64 plays sampled sound.
