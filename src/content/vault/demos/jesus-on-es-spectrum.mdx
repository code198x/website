This *Jesus On E's* (1996), by Rebel, is one of the most talked-about ZX Spectrum demos of its era — partly for its cheerfully provocative, pure-mid-90s-rave title, but mostly for what it pulled off on the humblest hardware in the range. Much of the Eastern European scene's best work leaned on the 128K machine's AY sound chip and the favourable video timing of Pentagon clones. This demo did its multicolour conjuring on the plain 48K Spectrum.

It shares its name with — but is otherwise unrelated to — [LSD's famous Amiga rave demo](/vault/demos/jesus-on-es) of 1992. Two scenes, two machines, one irresistible title.

## Fast facts

- **Group:** Rebel.
- **Platform:** ZX Spectrum (48K).
- **Released:** 1996.
- **Noted for:** Ambitious multicolour on bare 48K hardware.

## Why it stuck

Demos are remembered for the gap between what the hardware should allow and what ends up on screen, and this one has one of the wider gaps on the Spectrum. Squeezing smooth, colourful, beam-synced effects from the cheapest Spectrum, with the least to give, is exactly the stubborn ingenuity the scene prized.
