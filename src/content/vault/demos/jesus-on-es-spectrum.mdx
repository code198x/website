In June 1996 a Spectrum owner who signed himself Reb of Technium 220 took LSD's *[Jesus on E's](/vault/demos/jesus-on-es)*, a half-hour Amiga rave demo from 1992, and brought pictures from it to the 48K ZX Spectrum. He did not rewrite the effects. He pointed a video digitiser at the Amiga's picture and saved what he saw as Spectrum screens. The title screen says so: "AMIGA ORIGINAL - LSD", "SPECTRUM - REB OF TECHNIUM 220", "VIDEOFACE DIGITISED 6/1996".

## Fast facts

- **By:** Reb (also known as Rebel) of the group Technium 220.
- **Converted from:** LSD's Amiga demo *[Jesus on E's](/vault/demos/jesus-on-es)* (1992).
- **Platform:** 48K ZX Spectrum, on tape.
- **Released:** June 1996, the date on the title screen.
- **Made with:** a VideoFace video digitiser.

## Pictures from a video signal

The VideoFace was a small interface that turned a video signal into a Spectrum screen. The Dutch firm Data-Skip advertised it in *[Your Sinclair](/vault/magazines/your-sinclair)* in May 1987 for £69: plug in a video recorder, a camera or "even another computer" ("What would you think of a Commodore screen in your Spec??"), and it would capture a 256×192 picture in 0.27 seconds. Romantic Robot, the makers of the [Multiface](/vault/hardware/multiface), also sold it, and by 1990 their advertisements in *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* promised that the pictures "can be frozen, saved, printed, altered, animated".

Reb used it for exactly that. The tape carries a short BASIC program and one block of machine code of 26,629 bytes. Once loaded, the program shows a title screen and then works through a list of 75 steps in its BASIC. Each step calls one of sixteen machine-code routines that put pictures on the screen, and some steps also change the border colour. Some pictures repeat several times in a row and some alternate, which gives short bursts of movement. When the list ends, it starts again. The screens range from black-and-white line patterns, such as a warped set of nested squares, to the coloured "Loading" logo from the Amiga original. There is no music: the soundtrack that made the Amiga original famous stayed on the Amiga.

## Where it fits

Reb made several other digitised slideshows for the Spectrum in 1995 and 1996, according to Demozoo, including ones based on *The X-Files*, *Rugrats* and *The Snowman*. As Equinox he was still making Spectrum graphics in 2020. *Jesus on E's* belongs with that work: a fan's tribute to a famous Amiga demo, made with the tools a Spectrum owner had.
