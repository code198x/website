Razor 1911 entered *Voyage* in the demo competition at the first [Party](/vault/events/the-party), held in Aars, Denmark, just after Christmas 1991. It came third, behind Alcatraz's *Odyssey* and *[Hardwired](/vault/demos/hardwired)* by Crionics and The Silents. Razor 1911 was a Norwegian group, founded in 1985, that cracked games as well as making demos. Within a few months *Voyage* was on sale from British public-domain libraries, and in April 1992 *The One* described what buyers got: "a few 3D polygon spacecraft and robots, some neat variations on the parallax/rotational starfield effect, a cute, superbly-animated block-man, and plenty of music."

## Fast facts

- **Group:** [Razor 1911](/vault/groups/razor-1911).
- **Released:** at [The Party](/vault/events/the-party) 1991, Aars, Denmark, in December 1991.
- **Result:** third in the demo competition.
- **Platform:** Amiga with 1 MB of memory.
- **Credits:** code by Sim; music by Codex, Tip and Mantronix; graphics by Drake and Murdock.

## What it shows

*The One* reviewed *Voyage* in its public-domain pages in April 1992, as disk 1604 from the Wakefield library 17-Bit Software, "a contribution from Razor 1911 to a demos competition in Aars, Denmark". Besides the polygon spacecraft, robots and starfields, the magazine picked out the block-man and the soundtrack: "you also get a lengthy musical track and a cute animated block-man." Library adverts described it in the same terms. One in *Amiga Format* in August 1992 called it "Very good fx and music", and a list that ran in *CU Amiga* in 1994 called it a "Brilliant demo" with "lot of fill vector", meaning solid, filled 3D shapes rather than wireframes.

No period source describes texture mapping in *Voyage*, and no public source describes how its code worked. The Amiga's [blitter](/vault/hardware/blitter) could draw a shape's outline in line mode and then [fill it](/vault/hardware/blitter#area-fill) with a single colour, which is the kind of flat-shaded 3D object the magazines describe.

## Selling the demo

Most Amiga owners saw *Voyage* on a disk bought by post. In the summer of 1992 *Amiga Format* printed 17-Bit Software's ten best-selling demos, and "Voyager (Razor 911)", disk 1604, was sixth. *Hardwired* was first and *Odyssey* second. Libraries listed it as needing 1 MB of memory, and many sold it next to the other two demos from the top of The Party's results, *Odyssey* and *Hardwired*, into 1995.
