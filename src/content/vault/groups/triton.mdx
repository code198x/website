In the summer of 1992 three Swedes signed off the text file for their first serious demo with their real names and a set of house rules: contact us by our real names, we do not swap software, and "we won't join any other group either". The group was Triton, and the demo, *[Crystal Dream](/vault/demos/crystal-dream)*, came second to Future Crew's *Unreal* in the PC demo competition at the first Assembly party in Finland that July. Triton is remembered for more than its demos. Its coder Mr. H wrote *Fasttracker*, and with Vogue he wrote *Fasttracker 2*, a PC music program the Italian press was calling legendary by 1997. Vogue, Magnus Högdahl, later ran [Starbreeze Studios](/vault/companies/starbreeze), the company Triton turned into.

## Fast facts

- **From:** the Timrå area, northern Sweden.
- **Formed:** winter 1991–92, from an earlier group called The Physical Crew.
- **Members in 1992:** Vogue (Magnus Högdahl), Mr. H (Fredrik Huss) and Loot (Anders Aldengård); Lizardking wrote music for *Crystal Dream 2* and later releases.
- **Best known for:** *[Crystal Dream](/vault/demos/crystal-dream)* (1992), *Crystal Dream 2* (1993), *Fasttracker* and *Fasttracker 2*.
- **Became:** [Starbreeze Studios](/vault/companies/starbreeze).

## From The Physical Crew

Triton told its own origin story in the README that came with *Crystal Dream*. The group formed in the winter of 1991–92 out of The Physical Crew, whose earlier releases, *PC Demo* and *Vectra*, it admitted "were no products of some serious work". Working with [Vogue](/vault/people/vogue) went well, the partnership turned into a demo, and the group took a new name for it, after one of the moons of Neptune. Vogue had started out programming the Amiga and had been a member of the Amiga group [Phenomena](/vault/groups/phenomena) in 1991.

Each member had a job. Vogue rendered the demo's ray-traced pictures in *Synthetic Image*, a ray-tracing scene editor he had written as his final project at upper secondary school. Mr. H wrote the music software and designed the hardware to go with it, and the README printed his circuit diagrams for anyone who wanted to build a sound device for the printer port. Loot is listed with the others; the rules told coders to get in touch, "(Not LOOT)".

## Crystal Dream and Crystal Dream 2

*Crystal Dream* was meant for Hackerence 5, a party in Härnösand, but, the group wrote, "we ran out of time and couldn't show a 100% version". It was finished in July 1992 and entered at Assembly '92, where the organisers' results placed it second behind *Unreal*. Its sequel, *Crystal Dream 2*, won the PC demo competition at The Computer Crossroads, held at the Swedish Exhibition Centre in Gothenburg at the end of May 1993. Triton's demos reached the British public-domain libraries: in October 1994 one advertised "CRYSTAL DREAMS (2) - Triton Demo - excellent" in *PC Gamer*, on the same list as Future Crew's *Unreal* and *Second Reality*. The [Crystal Dream](/vault/demos/crystal-dream) entry covers both demos.

## Fasttracker

Mr. H's music routines grew into a program for writing music. Demozoo dates the first *Fasttracker* to December 1992 and credits it to him. In February 1994 the Italian magazine *The Games Machine*, surveying music programs for its readers, listed "Mr H's Triton Fast Tracker" for the PC: a tracker made almost identical to the Amiga's [ProTracker](/vault/tools/protracker), so that anyone who knew that program could use it straight away, but with eight channels of sound instead of four.

*Fasttracker 2*, by Mr. H and Vogue, followed in November 1994, according to Pouët and Demozoo. It saved music in its own XM module format, and by 1997 module players advertised support for files from both *Fasttracker* versions. A reader's classified advertisement in the Italian *MCmicrocomputer* in September 1995 offered version 2.03 of "Triton Fasttracker, the famous program for composing mods", "direct from Sweden by modem". By February 1997 *The Games Machine* was calling it "the legendary FastTracker II".

Development stopped at the end of the decade. On 23 May 1999 the Starbreeze website announced that *Fasttracker 2* had been "put on hold indefinitely", because "it's impossible to maintain the program while I run Starbreeze and Fredrik is working at Ericsson". Magnus Högdahl wrote that they were considering releasing the source code, but had not decided "how, when or if". A development version, 2.09, had already leaked from a beta tester, and Starbreeze put it on its own download page. The program still has users: the modern *ft2-clone* for Windows, macOS and Linux says its XM player was "directly ported from the original source code".

## Into the Shadows

The step into games came through a demo. In 2002 Högdahl told *Edge* that at the end of 1993 the American publisher Scavenger, impressed by one of Triton's demos, contacted him with a proposal to turn his and Fredrik Huss's skills to game development. Scavenger turned down a first-person shooter, and at the end of 1994 Triton Productions, by then a company, started work on *Into the Shadows*, a 3D action role-playing game for the PC. The team moved to the United States at the start of 1995. "We were all rookies and didn't have a clue what a game development company should look like," Högdahl said. Scavenger, he found, had only three employees; the four development teams it presented as its own were separate companies.

*Into the Shadows* was praised at E3 in 1995, and *Edge* reported that it was generally seen as the best of the four games Scavenger had agreed to make for GT Interactive in a $6.5 million deal. Högdahl was its only full-time programmer. Then the money stopped. "We didn't get paid. That is a pretty strong sign," he told *Edge*. The team went back to Sweden, and Scavenger fell silent in the autumn of 1996. The Italian magazine *Zeta* reported the game's cancellation in April 1997.

## Starbreeze

Triton became [Starbreeze Studios](/vault/companies/starbreeze), *PC Gamer* reported in 2002. The new studio's next project, *Sorcery*, for [Gremlin](/vault/companies/gremlin-graphics), was cancelled when [Infogrames](/vault/companies/infogrames) bought Gremlin, and its first game to reach the shops was the fantasy action game *Enclave*, which *Edge* reviewed on the Xbox in September 2002. Starbreeze followed it with *The Chronicles of Riddick: Escape from Butcher Bay* (2004), *The Darkness* (2007), *Syndicate* for [Electronic Arts](/vault/companies/electronic-arts) (2012) and, with the film director Josef Fares, *Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons* (2013).

Högdahl did not stay to the end of that run. In 2009, *Edge* reported, seven senior employees, "including Starbreeze founder Magnus Högdahl", left to form MachineGames, another Swedish studio. Starbreeze later became a publisher as well as a developer, and in December 2018, after disappointing early sales of *Overkill's The Walking Dead*, it replaced its chief executive and applied to the Swedish courts for reconstruction, a form of protection from its creditors.
