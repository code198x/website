Red Sector Inc — RSI — were among the German groups that gave the early Amiga scene its swagger. Their 1989 *Megademo* was a landmark, a relentless run of effects and attitude that travelled across the continent and showed newcomers what the machine could really do. They also released the RSI Demomaker, a tool that let others assemble their own demos, spreading the form to people who couldn't code the effects themselves.

In time RSI joined forces with Tristar to become TRSI — Tristar & Red Sector Incorporated — one of the most prolific names spanning both the demo and cracking worlds.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1987, Germany.
- **Platform:** Amiga (later PC).
- **Best known for:** *Megademo* (1989); the RSI Demomaker.
- **Later became:** TRSI, after merging with Tristar.

## Why RSI mattered

By shipping a demo-construction tool alongside their own productions, RSI lowered the barrier to entry and helped the megademo style propagate faster than hand-coding ever could. The TRSI merger then showed how fluidly the scene's demo and cracking halves could combine.
