## Overview

Scoopex sit among the founding names of the Amiga demoscene. Formed in 1988, they arrived as the platform's creative scene was finding its feet, and they helped define the era's signature form: the megademo, a sprawling collection of effects, music, and credits stitched into one disk. Where earlier productions showed a single trick, Scoopex showed everything they had at once.

Their 1990 production *Mental Hangover* became one of the most-copied demos of its day, passed hand to hand across Europe on floppies. Scoopex outlasted most of their contemporaries, staying active long after the Amiga's commercial life ended.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1988.

- **Best known for:** *Mental Hangover* (1990).
- **Status:** Still active in the Amiga scene.

## Why Scoopex mattered

The megademo defined how a generation experienced the Amiga's hardware. By chaining effect after effect — copper gradients, sprite multiplexing, scrolltexts, tracked music — Scoopex turned a demo into a tour of everything the machine could do. That maximalist instinct became the scene's house style for years.
