In June 1988 *Commodore User* told its readers about "Germany's TRIAD group", publisher of a cracking-scene fanzine called *It's Illegal*. A few pages later it reported that "German hackers TRIAD" were spreading copies of an unreleased game. Triad was in fact Swedish. The magazine's mistake is easy to trace. The fanzine's editor, Jeff Smart, lived in West Germany and was a Triad member that year, and when he wrote to *Commodore User* in October he signed off "Jeff Smart, TRIAD, West Germany".

The mix-up shows how the group looked from outside: a name on disks, in swapping ads and on a magazine's map of the European piracy network, with members spread across more than one country. From inside, Triad was a [Commodore 64](/vault/systems/commodore-64) crew that cracked games, made intros and demos, and kept releasing work for decades afterwards.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 28 July 1986, in Sweden, by seven members of smaller Swedish groups.
- **Platform:** [Commodore 64](/vault/systems/commodore-64), with some Amiga work.
- **Known for:** Cracks in the 1980s; demos such as *Red October*, *Justice*, *Red Storm* and *Lickpipe* in the early 1990s.
- **Status:** Still releasing work in the 2010s.

## Founding

Scene databases date Triad to 28 July 1986 and name seven founders: Ixion, Skydive, The Arrow, Fred, Lucifer, Mr. Z and RND. They came from three smaller Swedish crews, and a scene message from a few days earlier announced the merger as "the golden Triangle of SWEDEN". Demozoo explains the name the same way: the three groups made a triangle on the map of Sweden. These dates and names come from scene sources, not from the period press.

Like other cracking groups, Triad removed the copy protection from commercial games and passed them round the swapping network with its own intro attached. One of its first releases, dated October 1986 in the Commodore 64 scene database CSDb, was a crack of Epyx's *World Games*. The same database now credits the group with more than 2,600 releases of all kinds.

## Seen from Britain

British readers could spot the name well before *Commodore User*'s feature. In the classified "Microsell" pages of *Computer and Video Games*, swappers sent greetings to other crews: an April 1987 ad greeted "SC9 Triad Gamebusters and Omega Man", and one from Denmark in September signed off "Hi: Triad New Bencor FP Alliance TTI".

*Commodore User*'s June 1988 piracy feature, by Mike Pattenden, put Triad on its map of "the European hacking circuit" alongside [Fairlight](/vault/groups/fairlight), Fusion and others. Its chart of the top five European demos placed *F.A.M.E.*, credited to F.A.M.E. and Triad, at number two. It also named Triad in a story that embarrassed the software industry. A demo of *Katakis*, a shoot-'em-up that [Rainbow Arts](/vault/companies/rainbow-arts) had not yet released, was going round the network: "We're not even supposed to know of its existence, but German hackers TRIAD are pouring out disks!" The feature used the case to show how much pre-release software leaked from inside the industry. See the [cracking scene](/vault/communities/cracking-scene) for the rest of the report.

## Illegal

Triad's link with [*Illegal*](/vault/magazines/illegal) lasted less than a year. CSDb lists [Jeff Smart](/vault/people/jeff-smart), the fanzine's editor, as a Triad member from January to October 1988, and credits issues from that spring to "Jeff Smart and Triad". *Commodore User* described the April 1988 issue as carrying "news, interviews with hackers, a report on the Venlo copy party, a review of *Rolling Thunder*, a debate on the relative merits of 64 and Amiga, classified ads, news of the latest cracked games and charts". *Illegal*'s own chart for February 1988, in a later transcription of issue 26, placed Triad third among cracking groups, behind Fusion and Fairlight.

Smart's letter in *Commodore User*'s October 1988 issue opened by expecting the editors' reaction: "Damn it. Yet another stupid pirate going on my nerves!" The magazine printed only those opening lines and his signature, then added: "Too late."

## Demos

By the early 1990s the British magazines were writing about Triad as demo makers. *Commodore Format*'s August 1992 guide to the demo scene listed Triad among the groups whose early demos on Compunet, the Commodore online service, "still hold up well", and picked out *Red October*, which "uses striking images and music, in pop video style".

In November 1992 *ZZAP!64*'s public domain column recommended *Justice*, a Triad demo protesting against the police assault on Rodney King in Los Angeles. Before the demo proper, the viewer paged through text about the incident; then images and text flashed on screen in time with the music. The reviewer admitted it was not "a big demo, or a very good one", but said "it has a meaning, a message", and wanted to see more like it. In August 1994 *Commodore Format* singled out the "colourful cones and spheres" of Triad's *Lickpipe* on a compilation tape of classic demos.

## Still releasing

Triad did not stop when the Commodore 64 left the shops. According to C64-Wiki, the group announced on its 25th anniversary, 28 July 2011, that it had released something in every year of its existence, and Demozoo records a 30th-anniversary celebration at the Gubbdata party in 2016.
