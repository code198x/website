Few groups can claim to have an effect named after them. Kefrens can. The "Kefrens bars" — stacks of vertical sinus bars that smear and trail as they move — became standard scene vocabulary, and coders still reach for the term today. Formed in Denmark in 1986, Kefrens were veterans by the time the Amiga scene peaked, having cut their teeth in the Commodore 64 era first.

Their 1993 production *Desert Dream* is their monument: a polished, confident demo that arrived at the height of the Amiga scene and remains one of the platform's best-loved productions.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** 1986, Denmark.
- **Platforms:** C64, then Amiga.
- **Best known for:** *[Desert Dream](/vault/demos/desert-dream)* (1993); the Kefrens-bars effect.
- **Legacy:** Lent their name to a permanent piece of scene jargon.

## Why Kefrens mattered

Longevity and craft. Kefrens bridged two platforms and two eras without losing their identity, and their willingness to refine a single effect until it was unmistakably theirs is exactly the kind of obsessive polish the scene rewarded.
