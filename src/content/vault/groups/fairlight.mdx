Fairlight began with a train. In the group's own telling, retold by *Computer Sweden* on its 25th anniversary, the founder, Strider, lived in Malmö and his cracker, Gollum, lived in Ronneby, further up the coast. To get a new Commodore 64 game from one to the other fast, Strider talked a conductor at Malmö's central station into carrying it. Gollum collected it at the other end, removed the protection, and the cracked copy was ready to go round the world.

That speed made the name. Within a year of its founding in 1987, Fairlight stood second in a scene fanzine's chart of cracking groups, and it went on through the Amiga, the Super Nintendo and the PC. It also made demos, and its demo work ran alongside its cracking for more than thirty years. Cracking and distributing commercial games was illegal, and in 2004 the group was one of the targets of an international police operation.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** April 1987, in Sweden, by Strider; a scene history names Black Shadow as co-founder.
- **Platforms:** [Commodore 64](/vault/systems/commodore-64), [Commodore Amiga](/vault/systems/commodore-amiga), Super Nintendo and PC.
- **Known for:** Fast cracks and [crack intros](/vault/communities/crack-intros); demos such as *Megademo 3* (with North Star), *Full Moon* (with Virtual Dreams), *Love* and *Agenda Circling Forth* (with CNCD).
- **Status:** Still active. Its Commodore 64 section had releases dated 2026.

## The Commodore 64 years

The founding dates come from scene sources, not the period press. The scene database CSDb gives 14 April 1987; a history of the group in the diskmag *Hugi* gives April 1987 and names two founders, Strider and Black Shadow, who had been in an earlier Swedish group, West Coast Crackers.

By early 1988 the group was a name the European swapping network knew. The fanzine *[Illegal](/vault/magazines/illegal)* ranked groups by readers' points, and its issue of 7 March 1988 put Fairlight second as "Cracker of the Month" for February, behind Fusion and ahead of [Triad](/vault/groups/triad). In June that year *Commodore User*'s feature on software piracy printed a map of "the European hacking circuit", and Fairlight was on it with Fusion, Triad and others. The [cracking scene](/vault/communities/cracking-scene) entry describes how that network worked: groups raced to be first with each new release, and the winner's name went on the loading screen.

## The Amiga years

On the Amiga, Fairlight's name reached magazine readers through demos as well as cracks. In July 1989 *Amiga Computing*'s public domain column mentioned trying "the two Fairlight demo discs" from a mail-order library. By November 1989 the libraries were selling *Megademo 3*, a two-disk production credited to North Star and Fairlight, for £5.

The cracks themselves turn up in developers' and suppliers' memories. Jimmy Maher's history of the Amiga records that Fairlight "cracked and widely distributed" *Pinball Dreams*, the first game from [Digital Illusions](/vault/companies/dice-studio), whose founders had come out of the Swedish demo group [The Silents](/vault/groups/the-silents). One of them, Animal, reflected that "considering the amount of people that played this game on copy parties, I guess I could have been retired by now!" A supplier of original disks for the rival group Crystal recalled that Fairlight beat Crystal to Core Design's *Thunderhawk*, which he credited as "a hardish MFM crack". Ocean's *Hook* was protected with [Copylock](/vault/techniques/copylock), and its programmer, Bobby Earl, explained that a hidden second check removed one of three mugs of cocoa the player needed midway through the adventure; his interviewer describes Fairlight's crack of the game as one that could not be completed.

The demos kept coming after the Amiga 1200 arrived. UK public domain libraries listed a "Fairlight 242 demo" from late 1993, and in October 1994 a library advertisement in *Amiga Format* described *Full Moon*, made with Virtual Dreams, as "Superb. Fairlight do it again." The French magazine *Dream* named *Fullmoon* in 1995 among the demos A1200 and A4000 owners could sit back and watch. *Amiga Computing* was cooler about *Love*, an AGA demo it reviewed in November 1994: "Hardly original but fairly well done", with end credits over "an impressive rendered — and rotating, funnily enough — Fairlight logo". Its caption set the group's two sides against each other: "Much more constructive than cracking and pirating software, but Fairlight's Love AGA demo still made me feel a strange sense of déjà vu".

## The PC years and Operation Fastlink

By the 2000s the group's releases were mainly PC and console games; the Justice Department later described it as specialising in "the illegal distribution of computer games, including PC and console games". On 22 April 2004 the United States Department of Justice announced Operation Fastlink: more than 120 searches in the previous 24 hours, in 27 states and ten other countries including Sweden, and more than 200 computers seized, 30 of them servers. Fairlight was among the groups it named, which it described as "first-providers": "the original source for most of the pirated works traded or distributed online". *Computer Sweden* later reported that several Fairlight members were charged. In March 2005 a man the Justice Department described as a senior member of Fairlight and Kalisto pleaded guilty in federal court in Connecticut, along with another man who had run servers for Fairlight members.

The group did not stop. In September 2004 AfterDawn reported a statement from Fairlight's council: the group's section releasing disc images, "ISO", was "lethally wounded, but the group as such is still alive and kicking!"

## Later scene work

Fairlight's demo section outlived its best-known cracking years. *Agenda Circling Forth*, a Windows demo made with the group CNCD, won the PC demo competition at Breakpoint 2010 in Germany, and took the Scene.org Awards for Best Demo, Best Effects and Best Technical Achievement that year. When about thirty members met in 2012 for the group's 25th anniversary, *Computer Sweden* found Pantaloon still leading the demo section and the headline was a member's line: "We might be old, but we're still the elite." The Commodore 64 side carried on as well: CSDb lists more than 1,800 Fairlight releases, and those dated 2026 include new cracks of 1980s games such as *Mindwheel* and *Brimstone*.
