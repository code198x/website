On the Commodore 64, the name Censor Design carries the weight Fairlight or Phenomena carry elsewhere. The Swedish group's *Wonderland* demo series became a benchmark — productions where coding, graphics, and SID music all hit the top of their game simultaneously, wringing effects from the 1982 machine that seemed to defy it.

Censor helped prove the C64 scene could match the Amiga's for craft and ambition, on hardware with a fraction of the headroom.

## Fast facts

- **Founded:** late 1980s, Sweden.
- **Platform:** [Commodore 64](/vault/systems/commodore-64).
- **Best known for:** the *Wonderland* demo series.
- **Standing:** Among the most revered C64 groups.

## Why Censor mattered

The *Wonderland* demos are routinely cited when people argue the C64 scene's best work rivals anything on more capable machines. Censor set a ceiling that later C64 groups — Oxyron, Booze Design and others — kept pushing against.
