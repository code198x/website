The **Neo Geo Pocket Color** was SNK's 1999 handheld, the colour successor to the original Neo Geo Pocket. Famous for its micro-switched clicky joystick—considered the best portable D-pad ever—and excellent fighting games, it was critically acclaimed but SNK's bankruptcy cut its life short.

## Fast facts

- **Released:** 1999
- **CPU:** Toshiba TLCS-900H @ 6.144 MHz
- **Screen:** 160×152, 146 colours
- **Battery life:** 40 hours (2 AA), unverified; *Edge* gives around 25 hours for the monochrome unit
- **End:** 2001 ([SNK](/vault/companies/snk) bankruptcy)

## The machine it launched beside

The colour unit had a monochrome predecessor, and the predecessor arrived at the
same moment as a machine almost identical to it. *Edge* put the two side by side
at Christmas 1998: the Neo Geo Pocket and Bandai's WonderSwan "share remarkably
similar hardware specifications: both have 16bit processors, eight-shade
monochrome LCDs, weigh 130 and 110 grams respectively, measure within millimetres
of each other and consume batteries in around 25 hours."

Two companies with no connection to each other had, independently, built the same
handheld. What separated them was a price: ¥7,800 for SNK's, ¥4,800 for Bandai's.

Edge described the Neo Geo Pocket's 160×152 screen as "not offering a
particularly high resolution", smaller than "the original Game Boy". That last
comparison does not hold. Nintendo's own programming manual gives the Game Boy's
display as "160 x 144 dots" — the same width, eight lines shorter. The Neo Geo
Pocket's screen was the larger of the two.

## The verdict that came true in four months

The unit's British distributor gave *Edge* the argument against his own product,
in the same feature in which he announced an order for 220,000 of them:

> People are not going to go out and spend money on another monochrome system
> when they can buy one in colour.

He was right, and SNK evidently agreed: the Neo Geo Pocket Color reached Japan on
16 March 1999, roughly four months later. The monochrome machine's whole life was
shorter than the lead time on its own launch order.

There was a second obstacle, and it belonged to a chip that was ten years old.
Western studios were slow onto the new handhelds because their portable
experience was in the wrong instruction set — "the number of western programmers
experienced in coding for the Game Boy's [Z80](/vault/hardware/z80) processor".
The skill that made a developer valuable on the Game Boy was worth nothing here,
and the same wall would face
[Game Boy Advance](/vault/systems/game-boy-advance) developers from the other
direction three years later.

## Legacy

The NGPC proved handheld fighting games could work with the right controls. Its joystick design remains legendary among portable gaming enthusiasts, and its library—though small—contains genuine classics.
