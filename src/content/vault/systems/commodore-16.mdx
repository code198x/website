The **Commodore 16** and **Plus/4** are remembered as the machines Commodore got
wrong — the 264 series, incompatible with the C64, discontinued quickly. This
Vault can test how that reputation varies, because it holds eighteen magazines
across ten territories that all wrote about Commodore machines.

Counting mentions of the C16 and Plus/4 as a proportion of mentions of the C64 in
the same pages:

| Magazine | Country | C16 + Plus/4 vs C64 |
|---|---|---:|
| *[Mikroszámítógép Magazin](/vault/magazines/mikroszamitogep-magazin)* | Hungary | **62%** |
| *[Commodore Computer Club](/vault/magazines/commodore-computer-club)* | Italy | 52% |
| *[Your Commodore](/vault/magazines/your-commodore)* | **Britain** | **51%** |

## The gap is not between countries

The obvious reading — a machine that flopped in Britain and found a home in
Eastern Europe — does not survive the table. Hungary is highest at 62%, but
Britain's *Your Commodore* is third at 51%, ahead of every non-British title
except two.

The widest gap in the whole set is **inside Britain**: *Your Commodore* at 51%
against *ZZAP!64* at 5%. Ten times the attention, same country, overlapping
years. And the two Polish titles sit at 15% and 1%.

What separates them is what the magazine was for. *Your Commodore* covered
Commodore's range; *ZZAP!64* covered C64 games. A budget machine with a small
games library is invisible to one and unavoidable for the other.

This is the same result this Vault keeps arriving at from different directions:
[the institution and audience of a magazine predict its
contents](/vault/culture/institutional-computer-press) better than its country
does.

## What survives the caveat

Two things do look territorial rather than editorial.

**Hungary at 62%** is the highest figure in the set, and the Hungarian press
covers the 264 series more heavily than any British general Commodore magazine.
Hungary is one of the few markets where the C16 is remembered as genuinely
common.

**Argentina had the C16 without the Plus/4.** *Drean Commodore* mentions the C16
191 times and the Plus/4 four times. [Drean built Commodore machines under
licence](/vault/magazines/drean-commodore), and its magazine suggests the cheap
member of the pair crossed and the expensive one did not.

⚠ Mention counts measure editorial attention, not sales, and a ratio against the
C64 measures attention *relative to the flagship* rather than absolute interest.
A magazine may discuss a machine because it is popular or because its readers
need help with it.

⚠ The magazines cover different year spans, and the 264 series was current only
from 1984. Titles running mostly after 1987 — *576 KByte* at 2%, *Amiga Joker*
at 3% — are low partly because they started too late to care.
