## Overview
The Apple II wasn't the first personal computer, but it was the first that ordinary people could use. Steve Wozniak's elegant engineering, combined with Steve Jobs's vision for a consumer product, created a machine that launched Apple, defined educational computing, and proved the personal computer could be more than a hobbyist curiosity.

## Fast facts
- **Manufacturer:** Apple Computer.
- **Released:** June 1977.
- **CPU:** MOS 6502 at 1.023 MHz.
- **RAM:** 4KB expandable to 48KB (later 64KB+).
- **Graphics:** 280×192 high-res, 40×48 low-res, text modes.
- **Sound:** Built-in speaker, 1-bit toggle.
- **Storage:** Cassette, then Disk II floppy drive.
- **Price:** $1,298 (4KB), $2,638 (48KB).
- **Discontinued:** 1993 (Apple IIe).

## The 1977 trinity
Three machines launched in 1977:
- **Apple II:** Consumer-friendly, colour graphics.
- **TRS-80:** Radio Shack's affordable entry.
- **Commodore PET:** All-in-one business focus.

The Apple II outlasted both, remaining in production for 16 years.

## Wozniak's engineering
The Apple II reflected Wozniak's genius:
- **Minimal chips:** Elegant design using fewer components.
- **Expansion slots:** Eight slots for third-party hardware.
- **Colour graphics:** NTSC colour from digital signals—a clever hack.
- **Open architecture:** Published schematics encouraged tinkering.

## The Disk II revolution
Wozniak's floppy drive (1978) transformed the platform:
- Faster, more reliable than cassette.
- Enabled serious software development.
- VisiCalc (1979) required disk storage.
- Cost $495—expensive but essential.

## VisiCalc and business
The first spreadsheet made Apple II essential:
- Dan Bricklin and Bob Frankston's creation.
- Businesses bought Apple IIs specifically for VisiCalc.
- Proved personal computers had professional applications.
- "VisiCalc sells Apple IIs" became industry wisdom.

## Educational dominance
Apple II became the classroom standard:
- Apple's education discounts and donations.
- Oregon Trail, Where in the World is Carmen Sandiego?
- A generation learned computing on Apple II.
- Schools kept machines running into the 1990s.

## Gaming library
Despite business focus, games thrived:
- **Ultima series:** Richard Garriott's RPGs.
- **Wizardry:** Dungeon-crawling pioneer.
- **Karateka:** Jordan Mechner's cinematic action.
- **Prince of Persia:** Later Mechner masterpiece.

## Model evolution
The line expanded and improved:
- **Apple II+ (1979):** More RAM, Applesoft BASIC.
- **Apple IIe (1983):** Enhanced, most popular model.
- **Apple IIc (1984):** Compact, portable.
- **Apple IIGS (1986):** 16-bit upgrade, sound chip.

## Legacy
The Apple II established Apple as a company and personal computing as an industry. Its longevity—16 years in production—speaks to the original design's quality. VisiCalc proved business applications could drive hardware sales. Educational adoption created a generation of computer-literate users.
