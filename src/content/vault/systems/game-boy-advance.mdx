The Game Boy Advance represented Nintendo's leap to 32-bit handheld gaming. The ARM7TDMI processor and improved graphics capabilities enabled ports and original games that rivalled the SNES. Backwards compatibility with Game Boy and Game Boy Color games protected existing libraries. The GBA dominated handheld gaming until Nintendo's own DS superseded it.

## Fast facts

- **Manufacturer:** [Nintendo](/vault/companies/nintendo).
- **CPU:** ARM7TDMI at 16.78MHz.
- **Screen:** 240x160 pixels, 32,768 colours.
- **Memory:** 256KB WRAM, 96KB VRAM.
- **Backwards compatible:** Game Boy, Game Boy Color.
- **Variants:** GBA, GBA SP (lit screen), Game Boy Micro.

## The end of hand-written Z80

The GBA's most consequential change was not a specification. Edge visited
Crawfish Interactive in Croydon in October 2001, a studio that had spent years
shipping Game Boy Color titles, and the first advantage its people named was a
subtraction: the machine "no longer requires developers to master an idiosyncratic
version of [Z80](/vault/hardware/z80)."

What replaced it was one sentence long. "Programming-wise obviously it's all
written in C."

The original [Game Boy](/vault/systems/game-boy) ran a processor that was Z80-like
without being a Z80, and every game on it was hand-written for that dialect by
people who had learned it and could not use the knowledge anywhere else. An ARM
with a C compiler ends that. It also ends the value of the expertise — which is
why the same visit finds Crawfish turning to the 16-bit back catalogue instead:
*Speedball II* on GBA was built "based on the Bitmaps' original Mega Drive and
Amiga code", one of the titles "being turned into potential revenue streams thanks
to Nintendo's 32bit architecture."

A generation of Amiga and Mega Drive source found a second market on a handheld,
because for the first time the handheld spoke the same language.

## The limit was the cartridge, not the chip

The constraint developers actually fought in 2001 was commercial. Nintendo's
"cartridge pricing scheme" set the cost of the ROM a publisher had to buy, and
Edge found the industry split by it: "for almost every publisher or developer
eager to jump on the GBA bandwagon, there is another that has ruled Advance
development out owing to the low margins."

For the studios who did ship, it acted exactly like a hardware limit. "We might
get squeezed on the amount of cartridge space that we can" use — a game's size
decided by a price list rather than by the machine.

## Capabilities

What GBA could do:
- **Mode 7-style effects:** rotation and scaling.
- **Sprite capabilities:** 128 sprites, various sizes.
- **Sound:** 6 channels (4 PSG + 2 PCM).
- **Link cable:** multiplayer connectivity.

## Library highlights

Notable GBA games:
- **Metroid Fusion / Zero Mission:** 2D Metroid excellence.
- **Fire Emblem:** series came West.
- **Golden Sun:** portable RPG epic.
- **Advance Wars:** tactical strategy.
- **Pokemon Ruby/Sapphire:** third generation.
