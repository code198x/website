Microsoft's Xbox entered a market Sony dominated with hardware power and online infrastructure. The console featured PC-derived architecture, a built-in hard drive, and an ethernet port standard. Halo: Combat Evolved justified the platform. Xbox Live created console online gaming as a mainstream service. Though outsold by PlayStation 2, Xbox established Microsoft as a permanent gaming industry player.

## The hard disk is three machines pretending

Microsoft sold the drive to the public as a memory card that happened to be
enormous. Seamus Blackley, who ran the Xbox's Advanced Technology Group, told
*Edge* in June 2001 that this was a deliberately partial account, and that the
gap between the two stories was giving them trouble: "we have talked to so many
developers over the last year and now we have just started talking to consumers
and we are not sure what to talk about."

What a developer sees is three areas, and none of them is a disk.

| Area | What it impersonates |
|---|---|
| "You Don't Touch" | ROM — "it's not ROM because it is on the hard drive" |
| "Save" | a memory card |
| "Scratch" | RAM, "as it gets reformatted every time you turn the machine on" |

The third is the one that matters. The Scratch area carries "all the streaming
and virtual memory stuff", and it is wiped on every power-on: "the thing with the
Scratch area is it is non-persistent."

So the Xbox does not have a hard disk in the sense a PC has one. It has a slab of
cheap magnetic storage cut into a fake ROM, a fake memory card and an extension
of main memory that the machine deliberately forgets. Console architecture had
spent twenty years working around having too little RAM; this is the first
generation with the option of buying more of it by the gigabyte and accepting a
seek time.
