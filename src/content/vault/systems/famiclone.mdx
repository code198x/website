**Famiclones** are unlicensed clones of Nintendo's Famicom/NES, produced primarily in Taiwan and China from the mid-1980s onwards. Often marketed as "learning computers" or educational devices, they introduced gaming to regions where official Nintendo products were unavailable or unaffordable.

## Fast facts

- **Origin:** Mid-1980s
- **Primary source:** Taiwan, China
- **Marketing:** Often as "educational"
- **Reach:** Global, especially developing markets
- **Notable:** [Subor](/vault/companies/subor), Micro Genius, Dendy
- **Cartridges:** 60-pin, the Famicom's edge connector rather than the NES's 72-pin

## In Russia the clone was the market

Famiclones are usually told as a story about places official hardware never
reached. The Russian trade press of the period describes something stronger than
substitution. A console survey in *Games* for April 1995 puts Dendy at the top:
*Самым большим спросом у нас пользуются Dendy — около 30% рынка* — Dendy is in
greatest demand, around 30% of the market — and attributes it to arriving two
whole years ahead of the competition rather than to being cheap.

**And the clones were themselves counterfeited.** The same survey opens with
fakes because it reckons roughly half of all consoles then sold in Russia and
the near abroad were counterfeit — *приблизительно 50 процентов — фальшивки* —
with Dendy accounting for about half of that volume against Sega's 30%. Dendy
was already an unlicensed Famicom clone. What the market was faking was not
Nintendo's product but somebody else's copy of it.

## Legacy
