The **Galaksija** was a Yugoslav home computer designed by Voja Antonić in 1983 and published as complete schematics in January 1984. Readers built their own computers from locally available parts—no manufacturer required. This represents the ultimate "bedroom computing" achievement.

## Fast facts

- **Designer:** [Voja Antonić](/vault/people/voja-antonovic)
- **Published:** January 1984, price 200 dinars
- **Where:** *Računari u vašoj kući* ("Computers in Your Home"), a special edition of the popular-science magazine *Galaksija*
- **CPU:** Z80 @ 3.07 MHz
- **RAM:** 2-6 KB expandable
- **Built:** Estimated 8,000+ units

## The publication was a campaign, not a one-off

The special edition presents the machine as the culmination of something already
running: two years of popularising home computers in *Galaksija*, crowned by a
concrete project readers could build. Its cover sells it plainly — *za
samogradnju kućnog kompjutera*, for self-building a home computer — at 200
dinars, in January 1984.

The editorial is also clear that this was one person's design rather than a
committee's or a company's: *Računar „Galaksija" je projektovao jedan čovek,
Voja Antonić*.

## Voja Antonić

The designer intended Galaksija as accessible computing for everyone. His philosophy: if you can't buy a computer, build one. The design prioritised buildability over features.

## Legacy

The Galaksija represents computing democracy at its purest—complete instructions published freely, built by individuals. It demonstrated that computing knowledge could spread without commercial channels and inspired a generation of Yugoslav engineers.
