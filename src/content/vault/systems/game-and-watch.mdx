**Game & Watch** was Nintendo's line of dedicated LCD handheld games produced from 1980 to 1991. Created by Gunpei Yokoi, these devices established the template for portable gaming, introduced the D-pad controller, and generated the profits that funded Nintendo's home console ventures.

## Fast facts

- **Era:** 1980-1991
- **Creator:** [Gunpei Yokoi](/vault/people/gunpei-yokoi)
- **Units:** 60+ games
- **Innovation:** [D-pad](/vault/hardware/d-pad) invention (1982)
- **Sales:** 43+ million units

## Withered technology

Yokoi named his approach during this project: *Lateral Thinking of Withered
Technology* — using mature, cheap, well-understood components in unexpected
ways. LCD was ordinary by 1980 and therefore affordable, and Retro Gamer records
that this was a fight inside Nintendo rather than an obvious choice: some there
"wanted to use fancier technology in the Game & Watch, technology that would
have reduced battery life and raised costs, but Yokoi insisted that affordability
was key".

The same discipline produced the control. A joystick "would impede the Game &
Watch's portability", so Yokoi "began looking for solutions that would take up
less space".

**The cross was not yet a D-pad.** What appeared in 1982 was "four buttons
arranged in a cross shape, with each one corresponding to up, down, left and
right"; the single rocking pad came later. And on which game introduced it, the
sources disagree — Retro Gamer says *Donkey Kong Jr*, where this Vault's own
[D-pad](/vault/hardware/d-pad) entry says the *Donkey Kong* Multi Screen. Both
are 1982, and nothing held here settles it.

## Yokoi's Philosophy

"Lateral thinking with withered technology" — use mature, cheap technology in creative new ways rather than chasing cutting-edge specs. This philosophy defined Nintendo's handheld strategy.

## Legacy

Game & Watch established portable gaming, generated funding for the NES, introduced the D-pad, and inspired the DS naming ("Developers' System" and "Dual Screen" — a G&W homage).
