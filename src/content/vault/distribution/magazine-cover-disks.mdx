Before digital downloads, magazines brought software to readers. Cover-mounted disks—later CDs—transformed computing publications from pure text into software delivery systems. Demos introduced players to new games, public domain software built communities, and the occasional full game justified the cover price alone.

## Who made them

A cover disk was not something the magazine ran off in the office. *The One* printed
its supply chain on the Coverdisk page: "Coverdisks compiled by Grants Computing",
with faults going to a separate address entirely — "The One Coverdisk Returns, PC
Wise, Dowlais Top Business Park, Dowlais, Merthyr Tydfil" in August 1993.

Eighteen months later the address had changed to Diskxpress in Bourton-on-the-Water.
Duplication was a contract, and contracts moved.

The editorial office was emphatic about the division. Readers with a bad disk got
"humble apologies, but PLEASE DON'T SEND THEM TO US! Instead, send them to this
address with a SAE for 28p" — the magazine's own capitals. Issue 59 puts it more
bluntly: "any coverdisk problems sent to us will remain unanswered."
