## Overview

What began as signature screens on cracked software evolved into an art form. The demo scene brought together coders, graphicians and musicians to make **demos** — programs that generate their audio and visuals as they run, rather than replaying a recording.

That real-time constraint is the point, and it is what a demo is judged on: anyone can play a video, so the achievement is doing it live, on hardware that was never meant to. ⚠ It is a convention rather than a universal law — parties run separate categories for animations, wild entries and video, and demo rules commonly permit precalculated data — but within the demo compos themselves, generating the thing live is the whole game.

## Fast facts

- **Origins:** Crack intro evolution (early 1980s).
- **Peak platforms:** C64, Amiga, Atari ST.
- **Modern:** PC, retro platforms still active.
- **Recognition:** added to **Finland's National Inventory of Living Heritage** in 2020 — the national register kept under the UNESCO intangible-heritage convention, and a first for a digital culture. ⚠ Often reported as "UNESCO recognition"; UNESCO itself has not inscribed the demoscene on its international list.
