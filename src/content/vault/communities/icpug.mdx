At **Biggin Hill Library**, a Commodore owner could attend an ICPUG lecture night or an open evening to ask for help. *Amiga Shopper*’s March 1992 directory lists those meetings alongside regional groups. The national name connected people who could still meet in a local room.

**ICPUG** developed from an independent **PET** users’ group into support for a wider range of Commodore computers. Independence was part of the point: owners could exchange knowledge through an organisation distinct from the manufacturer’s own support and promotion.

## More machines, a broader name

The North Herts group’s history dates the organisation’s origins to 1978 under **IPUG**, the Independent PET Users Group. It records the change to **Independent Commodore Products Users Group** in 1982, as the community’s interests extended beyond the PET.

That history also records a later change from “Commodore” to “Computer”. The initials stayed useful while the range of machines changed. These successive names describe stages in the organisation, rather than rival expansions of an abbreviation with one timeless meaning. (North Herts, “A Short History”.)

## Members supplied the material

The same account describes a volunteer-written journal carrying technical help, reviews, news and readers’ correspondence. Membership also connected people with software, other users and practical assistance.

The directory gives that general description a physical setting. Biggin Hill’s entry offered lectures and open help nights; regional entries provided other places to meet. The arrangement let printed explanations and face-to-face help support each other. A reader who could not finish a job from an article might bring the remaining question to a meeting.

The wider [user-group](/vault/culture/user-groups) history includes similar exchanges around the [C64](/vault/systems/commodore-64) and [Amiga](/vault/systems/commodore-amiga), without making every local club a branch of ICPUG.

## National closure and regional continuation

*Commodore Scene* 23 carried a notice quoting chairman **John Blackstaff**: the management committee had decided to close the national organisation at the end of the year and would not seek membership renewals for 1999.

The North Herts history distinguishes that decision from regional groups continuing. A national subscription structure, a journal and a local circle of people were connected parts of the organisation, but they did not all have to end in the same way.
