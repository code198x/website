GameMaker began as something else. In the summer of 1999 Mark Overmars, a computer scientist at Utrecht University in the Netherlands, started writing a program called Animo: "my first idea was to make a simple system for creating 2-d animations". By the time he released it that November it had a built-in programming language and could run simple games, and it had a new name, Game Maker. Hardly anyone noticed. In December 1999 it was downloaded 366 times.

Three years later it was being downloaded about 2,000 times a day. Overmars kept it free, and his website promised that users could make games "without the need to write a single line of code", by dragging and dropping actions, then move on to the built-in language when they wanted more. Children made their first games with it, and the program itself grew into the engine behind *Spelunky*, *Hotline Miami* and *Undertale*.

## Fast facts

- **Creator:** Mark Overmars, Utrecht University.
- **First release:** version 1.1, 15 November 1999, for Windows PCs; YoYo Games' later versions export games to other platforms.
- **Written in:** Delphi; the editor alone was "slightly over 25.000 lines of code" by 2002.
- **How it works:** drag-and-drop actions for beginners, and a built-in scripting language, the Game Maker Language (GML), for more.
- **Price:** free from Overmars; later sold by YoYo Games, and since November 2023 free for non-commercial use.
- **Owners:** Overmars until 2007; YoYo Games from 2007, now based in Dundee; YoYo was bought by Playtech in 2015 and by Opera in 2021.
- **Known games:** *[Spelunky](/vault/games/spelunky)* (2008), *[Hotline Miami](/vault/games/hotline-miami)* (2012), *Undertale*, *Hyper Light Drifter*.

## Overmars's program

Overmars kept a public diary of the program's growth on his website. The first version played games inside its own window: it "did not use DirectX and it did not have a separate runner nor could it create executables." Version 1.4 in early 2000 drew more attention, and version 2.0 followed around September. Downloads that year came to about 40,000.

Version 3.0, in 2001, was the first to draw its graphics through DirectX. Then Overmars started again. "In Jan 2001 I decided that I had to redo Game Maker," he wrote. "I rewrote it completely from scratch, using all that I learned over the years." Version 4.0, released in July 2001 with a red icon to mark the break, could not load the old version's files. Version 4.1 added multiplayer games in December. Downloads for 2001 came to about 270,000, and by the end of 2002 the site was counting some 2,000 a day.

The idea stayed the same through every version. Beginners built a game by dragging ready-made actions into place instead of typing code, with a collection of free images, sounds and example games to start from; when the actions ran out, the built-in language gave "full flexibility". By 2004 Overmars's site listed scrolling shooters, platform games, puzzle games and isometric games among what users had made, and in October 2004, with version 6.0 just released, it added a tutorial for a first-person shooter. Overmars made the case for the approach in education, in an April 2004 article in the IEEE journal *Computer* titled "Teaching Computer Science through Game Design", and in 2006 he and Jacob Habgood published a book of tutorial projects, *The Game Maker's Apprentice*.

## YoYo Games

In 2007 Overmars's site announced that "Game Maker and all its related files are from now on distributed by YoYo Games". YoYo was a new company, founded that year by a group of games and entertainment executives including Sandy Duncan, its chief executive. In the founders' own words it was set up by "a group of games industry friends" and "never had any external investors aside from our own funds", so GameMaker's sales paid for its growth. In 2010 YoYo opened an office in Dundee, Scotland, where the company is now based, and veterans of the city's games industry worked on the program: [Mike Dailly](/vault/people/mike-dailly), one of DMA Design's first staff, was lead developer of *GameMaker: Studio* from 2010 to 2018, and by 2021 Russell Kay was its technical lead.

*GameMaker: Studio*, released in 2012, could build the same game for several platforms, and *GameMaker Studio 2* followed in 2017. By then it had a reputation. "In the years since YoYo Games' GameMaker first emerged in 1999, it's gained a reputation as a low-cost and easy-to-use platform for making 2D games," *Wireframe* wrote in 2019, listing "such indie darlings as Hotline Miami, Undertale, Downwell, and Spelunky". In 2019 and 2020 the magazine also interviewed two developers who had started with it as children: Nick Walton, whose "early experiments with GameMaker as a 10-year-old led to a lasting interest in development", and AJ Norman, who at nine "would spend hours tinkering with GameMaker and trying to make his own Zelda homage".

## Owners and prices

The gambling software company Playtech bought YoYo Games in 2015, when YoYo had 30 staff. "We've relied heavily on generating revenue, mainly from GameMaker sales," the founders told users, and said Playtech was "the first investor who didn't want to alter our vision". In January 2021 the Norwegian browser maker Opera bought YoYo for $10 million to start a gaming division. *VentureBeat* reported that YoYo had gained more than 400,000 new registered users in 2020 and that GameMaker had been downloaded more than 10 million times since 2012.

In November 2023 GameMaker became free for non-commercial use, with a one-time licence for commercial releases replacing its subscriptions. Russell Kay, by then head of GameMaker, said the team had seen "other platforms making awkward moves with their pricing and terms" and had decided to go the other way.
