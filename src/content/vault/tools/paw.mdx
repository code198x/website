## Overview
*PAW* — the **Professional Adventure Writer** — is [Gilsoft](/vault/tools/the-quill)'s
successor to [The Quill](/vault/tools/the-quill), and the tool that most British
adventures of the late 1980s were built with.

*CRASH*'s adventure column introduced it in May 1987 with an unusually direct
claim: that it would have a bigger impact on the adventure scene than everything
else the column had covered put together. The magazine then scored it **97%**.

## Fast facts
- **Producer:** [Gilsoft](/vault/companies/gilsoft).
- **Price:** £22.95 — roughly twice the price of a full-price game.
- **Authors:** [Tim Gilberts](/vault/people/tim-gilberts), [Graeme Yeandle](/vault/people/graeme-yeandle), Phil Wade, Alex Williams, Kevin Maddocks and Dicon Peeke.
- **Spectrum versions:** 48K, Plus, 128 and Plus 2.
- **Ports:** *CRASH* listed Commodore 64 and Amstrad as "coming soon" at review time.

A utility scoring 97% is worth pausing on. *CRASH* was not rating a game; it was
rating what the tool made possible for its readers, which is the same judgement
the magazine had made about The Quill four years earlier at a lower ceiling.

## What it added over The Quill
The Quill's parser was verb-noun. PAW's was a parser in the fuller sense, and
*CRASH*'s review works through the specifics:

- **Adjectives** — a GREEN jumper, a LIT or UNLIT torch.
- **Adverbs** — HAPPILY married.
- **Prepositions** — talk TO him.
- **Pronouns** — the vocabulary carries numeric values so that the program knows SWORD is the object referred to by IT.
- **Multiple objects** — including GET ALL and DROP ALL.
- **Synonyms**, so that varied input still receives a reply.

The vocabulary values do real work: nouns below 50 and above 49 separate
categories, and values under 20 are "conversion nouns" that the program treats
as verbs — which is how NORTH functions as a command rather than a thing.

Beyond the parser:
- **The full 128K** of the newer Spectrums, rather than 48K.
- **Object weight**, so carrying capacity becomes a designed constraint.
- **Worn, removable and container objects** — *CRASH*'s example is trousers with pockets.
- **Independent characters** who interact rather than sit still.
- **256 flags**, with diagnostic display during testing.
- **Graphics**, which *CRASH* described as "quick, easily-drawn", with rubber-band drawing.

## Why it mattered
The Quill let people who could not program ship an adventure. PAW let them ship
one that did not feel like a Quill game — which was the ceiling the earlier tool
had imposed on its own users. The parser limitations of Quilled adventures were
a standing complaint in the adventure columns, and PAW answered them directly.

Its descendant **DAAD**, written by Tim Gilberts for the Spanish studio
Aventuras AD, carried the same design onto eight machines from one source file
and became the foundation of a Spanish-language adventure community that
outlived the British one by decades. DAAD was later released into the public
domain and repackaged for modern use, which is why PAW's design is still in
service.
