*3D Construction Kit* is [Freescape](/vault/techniques/freescape) sold as a
tool. [Incentive](/vault/companies/incentive-software) had built four games on
the engine — [*Driller*](/vault/games/driller), *Dark Side*, *Total Eclipse* and
*Castle Master* — and then packaged the thing itself, published by **Domark** at
**£24.99** in 1991.

*CRASH* reviewed it in May 1991 and called it "an excellent game creation
utility". *Your Sinclair* gave it **92%** and *Sinclair User* **91%**.

## Fast facts
- **Publisher:** [Domark](/vault/companies/domark), for Incentive.
- **Price:** £24.99.
- **Engine:** [Freescape](/vault/techniques/freescape).
- **Output:** stand-alone programs — yours to distribute.
- **Also sold as:** *Virtual Reality Studio*, the title used on DOS.
- **Bundled:** a **VHS tape**, presented by [Ian Andrew](/vault/people/ian-andrew) himself.

## How you built a world
*CRASH*'s review walks through it, and the vocabulary is recognisably that of a
modern 3D editor thirty years early:

- **Shapes** from a list — cubes, hexagons, triangles, pentagons, lines — which you "stretch, shrink, turn and shade" and position. The magazine notes complex buildings build up quickly this way.
- **Pull-down menus and a pointer**, with an object placeable in as few as two key presses.
- **Global objects** to avoid rebuilding repeated geometry: define four walls and a ceiling once, reuse them per room. The review is explicit that this is a memory measure — storing every object per area is "very memory hungry".
- **Conditions**, the scripting layer: `IF SHOT THEN GOTO AREA 2`.
- **Sensors**, so objects react when the player comes near.
- **A resizable window** onto the 3D world: "the smaller you have the window your player uses to see into your world" the faster it scrolls. The frame-rate trade-off is a design control, not a hidden constant.
- **Border and status panel** customisation, with text and score lines for energy and lives.

## The video in the box
The package included a **VHS tape** — around half an hour of introduction and
tutorial, presented by [Ian Andrew](/vault/people/ian-andrew), the designer of
Freescape, walking buyers through the Amiga and Spectrum versions.

It is a period-perfect object: a 3D engine complex enough that the manual was
not thought sufficient, in a year when the way to show somebody moving
three-dimensional graphics was to post them a videotape.

On DOS the same product was sold as ***Virtual Reality Studio***, which dates it
precisely. 1991 is the year "virtual reality" became a phrase a marketing
department would put on a box.

⚠ The tape's exact running time is unsettled — sources give both 23 and 30
minutes — so it is described here as about half an hour. The VHS itself and the
*Virtual Reality Studio* title are well attested, but not from the period
Spectrum press held in this library, which mentions 3D Construction Kit mainly
in classified ads.

## Why it is interesting
Two things.

First, it is a **commercial 3D engine sold to the public in 1991**, at £24.99, on
8-bit hardware — and the review frames the output as something you might make
"for pleasure or even monetary gain", because the kit compiles stand-alone
programs with no runtime licence attached.

Second, it is the second time Incentive did this. Five years earlier they had
taken adventure authoring and sold it as
[GAC](/vault/tools/graphic-adventure-creator). The company's instinct was
consistently to ship the tool as well as the work — which is a rarer instinct
than it sounds, and the reason both products are still discussed.
