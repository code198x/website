The **Graphic Adventure Creator** — GAC — was
[Incentive Software](/vault/companies/incentive-software)'s rival to
[Gilsoft's](/vault/tools/the-quill) authoring tools, and its distinguishing
feature is in the name: it generated illustrated adventures, with a picture
editor built in rather than sold separately as an add-on.

## Fast facts
- **Publisher:** Incentive Software.
- **Designed and written by:** Sean Ellis and Brendan Kelly.
- **Price:** £22.95 on cassette.
- **Spectrum release:** 1986.
- **Origin:** the Amstrad CPC came first.

## It went the other way round
Most British software of this period reached the Spectrum first and was ported
outward. GAC did the opposite. *CRASH* reported in May 1986 that Incentive were
"about to release a Spectrum version of Graphic Adventure Creator, which has
already won acclaim on the Amstrad", with the Spectrum build still in bug
testing.

That is a small fact with a large implication: by 1986 the Amstrad CPC was a
large enough market to originate a serious tool on, and the Spectrum was the
port. The machine that had defined British home computing was starting to be
one platform among several.

## What it did
The vocabulary handling splits into three areas — **nouns, verbs and adverbs** —
each able to hold a substantial word list. *CRASH* noted the obvious
consequence for the author: it is your job to trap nonsense input and give
intelligent responses, because the tool will happily accept whatever you fail
to think of.

The picture generator is the part the advertising led on: dot, circle, elastic
line, ellipse, fast fill and shading. Incentive's own advert lists "step by step
review, easy editing and the ability to merge pictures", so common elements need
drawing only once. The same advert promised "fast, compact adventure games with
the minimum of effort and the maximum of creativity".

## GAC against the Gilsoft tools
The Quill needed *The Illustrator* bought separately to produce graphics; GAC
had drawing built in from the start, and arrived while The Quill was still the
default and a year before [PAW](/vault/tools/paw) answered it.

The two houses drew different conclusions about what adventure authors wanted.
Gilsoft's line went towards parser sophistication and ended at PAW's adjectives
and pronouns. Incentive's went towards presentation. Both cost £22.95, and both
reviewed at the top of the scale.
