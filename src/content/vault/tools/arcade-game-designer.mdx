**Arcade Game Designer** — AGD — is
[Jonathan Cauldwell](/vault/people/jonathan-cauldwell)'s game-authoring system
for the ZX Spectrum, and it occupies exactly the position
[The Quill](/vault/tools/the-quill) held in 1983 and the
[Graphic Adventure Creator](/vault/tools/graphic-adventure-creator) held in
1986: the thing that lets somebody who cannot write assembly finish and release
a game.

The difference is when. The Quill served a machine at the height of its
commercial life. AGD serves one that has not been sold new since 1992.

## What the modern press says
*ZX Spectrum Gamer* describes "the superb Arcade Game Designer by Jonathan
Cauldwell" as having "been the glue behind many a recent Spectrum homebrew
title", and reports **a Next version in development**. It names *Zombo* by
Malcolm Kirk as an example, and the magazine's own *Aeon* carries the credit
line "made mostly in Jonathan Cauldwell's Arcade Game Designer".

The same article makes the argument this Vault has been making from the other
end of the decade, that authoring tools are what keep a machine alive:

> I believe that both these pieces of software could be crucial in the long
> term, allowing average Joes like you and me to create essential content that
> will keep the machine and its community alive.

"Both" is AGD and **PAW** — the writer's other hope being
[Tim Gilberts](/vault/people/tim-gilberts) bringing
[the Professional Adventure Writer](/vault/tools/paw) to the
[Next](/vault/systems/zx-spectrum-next). Forty years after Gilsoft and
Incentive were selling authoring systems at £22.95, the same two categories —
the arcade-game maker and the adventure writer — are what a Spectrum magazine
identifies as the machine's future.
