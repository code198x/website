**BRender** is a 3D rendering library from [Argonaut](/vault/companies/argonaut). It supplies machinery for describing and drawing a scene, leaving the game to decide what happens in it. Its name expands to *Blazing Render*.

The preserved credits name **Sam Littlewood**, **Dan Piponi**, **Simon Everett** and **Philip Pratt** for programming. They also identify work on ports, testing, documentation and support: a reusable library needed more than a fast drawing routine to serve another development team.

## Describe the scene

BRender’s source distinguishes a *model* from an *actor*. A model holds geometry: points in space and triangular faces connecting them. Faces can refer to materials, and vertices carry coordinates that place a texture on the surface.

An actor places something in the scene. It can refer to a model, light or camera, and carries a transform describing its position and orientation relative to its parent. Actors form a hierarchy. Moving a parent can therefore move its attached parts together, while each child keeps a local position of its own.

That separation is useful whenever several objects share a shape. The geometry describes what they look like; their actors describe where they are. [Texture mapping](/vault/techniques/texture-mapping) supplies surface detail without requiring every mark to become another triangle.

## A shared part of the work

A rendering library does not supply the rules of a race, an opponent’s decisions or a game’s story. It gives those systems a way to show their results. [RenderWare](/vault/tools/renderware) belongs to the same broader history of teams licensing reusable graphics technology.

The BRender source release preserves a version identified as approximately November 1998. Its maintainers record that **Foone Turing** and **Scott Hanselman** helped make the release available with **Jez San’s** approval. The archive makes the library’s data structures and implementation available for study alongside the games and company history.
