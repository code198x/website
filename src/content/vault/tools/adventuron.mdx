**Adventuron** is **Chris Ainsley’s** system for writing [text adventures](/vault/genres/text-adventure) in a web browser. Authors describe locations, connect them, place objects and write rules for what happens when the player acts. An editor and a play panel keep the source close to the result.

In *The Classic Adventurer* issue 5, Ainsley recalled wanting a browser-based environment with the feel of the adventures he had known on the [Spectrum](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum). He connected its structure to tools such as [The Quill](/vault/tools/the-quill), [PAW](/vault/tools/paw) and the Graphic Adventure Creator.

## A world described in tables

The user guide separates locations, connections and objects from event-handling rules. An object can begin in a room, be carried or be created later. Commands test conditions and change that state: taking a key, for example, can make it available to a later rule for opening a door.

Pictures, fonts and themes control the presentation. They do not replace the adventure’s model of where things are and what the player can do. The guide also distinguishes browser features from the subset that can be mapped to older machines; an 8-bit export is not a promise that every browser game transfers unchanged.

## A way back to adventure writing

[John Wilson](/vault/people/john-wilson), the writer behind [Zenobi](/vault/companies/zenobi-software), was an early user. In the same magazine feature, he said that Adventuron let him revise older games and include graphics which had originally sat separately on a tape’s other side. He credited it with making *Ramsbottom Smith and the Yellow Spheroid* possible, followed by a Spectrum version.

That is Wilson’s account of his own work, rather than a claim that every conversion is automatic. It shows why an authoring tool can matter after a machine’s commercial life: it offers a place to revise, test and extend a body of work. [DAAD](/vault/tools/daad) supplies another route between contemporary development and small target computers.
