When Sega's programmers were given the job of fitting its arcade hits onto the Master System, the man they relied on was Yuji Naka. Mutsuhiro Fujii, who managed the conversion of *Space Harrier*, put an arcade cabinet next to Naka's desk so that he could play it whenever he liked. [Mark Cerny](/vault/people/mark-cerny), who worked alongside them in Tokyo, remembered "a magician in our little team, named Yuji Naka, who somehow could bring those state-of-the-art experiences to the home console". Naka went on to program *[Sonic the Hedgehog](/vault/games/sonic-the-hedgehog)*, led [Sonic Team](/vault/companies/sonic-team) for more than a decade, and put Dreamcast owners around the world into the same game with *Phantasy Star Online*. His later career ended in a court case.

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 17 September 1965, in Osaka Prefecture, Japan.
- **Sega:** from the mid-1980s to 2006, as programmer, then producer and head of [Sonic Team](/vault/companies/sonic-team).
- **Programmed:** *Girl's Garden* (1985), Master System conversions of *Space Harrier* and *Out Run*, *[Phantasy Star](/vault/games/phantasy-star)* (1987), the Mega Drive *[Ghouls 'n Ghosts](/vault/games/ghouls-n-ghosts)*, *[Sonic the Hedgehog](/vault/games/sonic-the-hedgehog)* (1991) and *Sonic the Hedgehog 2* (1992).
- **Produced:** *NiGHTS into Dreams* (1996), *Sonic Adventure* (1998), *ChuChu Rocket!* (1999), *Samba de Amigo* (1999) and *Phantasy Star Online* (2000).
- **Later:** founded Prope (2006); joined Square Enix (2018) and directed *Balan Wonderworld* (2021).
- **Conviction:** insider trading, July 2023, with a suspended sentence.

## A programmer without a degree

Naka taught himself to program and went straight into the industry instead of spending four years at university. According to *Retro Gamer*, [Namco](/vault/companies/namco) turned him down because he had no degree. [Sega](/vault/companies/sega) took him on as an entry-level programmer in the mid-1980s. His first game, the Australian magazine *Hyper* reported, was *Girl's Garden* for Sega's SG-1000 console, begun as a training exercise and judged good enough to finish and sell.

The Master System made his name inside Sega. He programmed its conversions of the arcade games *Space Harrier* and *[Out Run](/vault/games/out-run)*, which Cerny later called "amazing", and worked on *[Phantasy Star](/vault/games/phantasy-star)* (1987), Sega's big role-playing game. Takashi Shoji, a younger Sega programmer, remembered the "smooth movement" of its 3D dungeons and Naka, "a senior programmer", working on the dungeon animation in the next booth. On the Mega Drive he converted Capcom's *Ghouls 'n Ghosts*. In 1988, *Retro Gamer* says, he moved to the new AM8 department, where he worked with the artist Naoto Ohshima on *Phantasy Star II*.

## Sonic

*Sonic the Hedgehog* (1991) was made by a group of about 15 in AM8, and its staff roll credits the program to "Yu2", Naka's nickname. Ohshima designed the hedgehog and Hirokazu Yasuhara planned the game and its levels; the [Sonic the Hedgehog](/vault/games/sonic-the-hedgehog) entry tells how it was made. The idea, Naka told *Retro Gamer*, came from playing the first stage of *[Super Mario Bros.](/vault/games/super-mario-bros)* and wondering why he could not get through it faster. *Hyper* summed up the design: the player, not the game, would control the speed.

Success did not keep him at Sega Japan. Unhappy with a pay system that rewarded seniority, Naka left, and Cerny re-hired him at Sega Technical Institute in California, where he and Yasuhara made *Sonic the Hedgehog 2* (1992), *Sonic 3* and *Sonic & Knuckles* (1994). The Americans and the Japanese on *Sonic 2* did not always get on, and Cerny recalled that "from *Sonic 3* onwards, Naka just worked with Japanese staff". Naka still gave Yasuhara his due: "Even now, we call the enemy layout of the traditional Sonic 'the Yasuhara Setting'," he told *Retro Gamer*.

## Head of Sonic Team

Sega brought Naka back to Japan in 1994 as a producer, and with Ohshima he revived the Sonic Team name. Their first game was not a Sonic game. "It would have been easier to use Sonic," Naka told *Edge* in 1996, "but we thought that people were expecting more, so we created a new character and a new world." *NiGHTS into Dreams* (1996), with Naka as executive producer, came with an analogue controller the team designed because the standard Saturn pad could not give its flying the "smooth and comfortable" feeling he wanted; the [analogue control](/vault/techniques/analog-control) entry has the story. *Hyper* later described it as the last game on which Naka was also lead programmer.

From then on he was a producer and a manager. Sonic Team made *Burning Rangers* (1998), *Sonic Adventure* (1998), the first 3D Sonic game, the puzzle game *ChuChu Rocket!* (1999) and the maraca-shaking rhythm game *Samba de Amigo* (1999). When Sega split its studios into separate companies in 2000, Naka became chief executive of SONICTEAM Ltd. That December *Edge* interviewed him about *Phantasy Star Online*, in which up to four players from different countries explored and fought together over the internet. The system that translated players' messages had been "a nightmare for us", he said, and his hopes for it were modest: "I'd like PSO to sell a million... but who knows."

He kept thinking about playing together. "I hope that in the future network games are just called games," he told *Edge* in 2003, when he was also running Sega's quality control as general manager of its Development Control Center. *Hyper* found him in 2005 still president of Sonic Team, overseeing more than a hundred developers in Japan and the United States and wishing he could go back to coding.

## Prope and Square Enix

In 2006 Naka left Sega to set up his own studio, Prope, with Sega's backing; *Retro Gamer* dated the announcement to 8 May 2006 and reported that ten or more Sonic Team staff went with him. Prope made smaller games, several of them for Nintendo's Wii. *Edge* counted *Let's Tap* (2008), published by Sega, among the rare original ideas that used the Wii remote "without making you look a (complete) fool"; after *Ivy the Kiwi?* and some games for the iPhone, the studio's *Rodea the Sky Soldier* was on the magazine's wanted list in 2011.

In January 2018 Naka joined Square Enix, announcing that he was programming again for the first time in twenty years. There he directed *Balan Wonderworld*, a 3D platform game released on 26 March 2021, which *Wireframe* counted among the year's most notable launch disasters. In April 2022, after he had left Square Enix, Naka said on Twitter that he had been removed as director about six months before release, that he had sued Square Enix, and that "it is a true disgrace that Balan Wonderworld was released in the state that it was in". By his account, he was taken off the game after disputes over its promotional music and over the co-developer Arzest submitting builds with problems still unfixed.

## Insider trading

In November 2022 Tokyo prosecutors arrested Naka on suspicion of insider trading, and in December re-arrested him on a second charge. While working at Square Enix, he had learned that the company was working with two other developers on mobile games: Aiming on *Dragon Quest Tact*, and Ateam on *Final Fantasy VII: The First Soldier*. He had bought shares in both companies before the games were announced. He pleaded guilty in March 2023. On 7 July 2023 the Tokyo District Court sentenced him to two and a half years in prison, suspended for four years, and ordered him to pay about ¥173 million in a fine and forfeited profits. Naka apologised in court for "harming the fairness and credibility of the marketplace".
