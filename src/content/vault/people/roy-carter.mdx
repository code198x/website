**Roy Carter** co-founded [Gargoyle Games](/vault/companies/gargoyle-games) with
[Greg Follis](/vault/people/greg-follis), working alongside **Ted Heathcote** in the Dudley
office *Amstrad Action* visited in 1985. In the interviews the division is consistent: Follis
describes the company, Carter describes the code.

## Fast on a new machine

The clearest measure of Carter as a programmer is a timing detail. Amstrad had a policy of
not routinely supplying machines to software houses, so Gargoyle bought a CPC464 themselves
in the summer of 1985 — and *Amstrad Action* records Carter having *Dun Darach* running on it
**within two weeks**.

His reason for liking the machine is the one working programmers give: it was **well
documented**, so you could find what you needed quickly.

⚠ That is a small remark with a large implication, and it recurs across this Vault. The
machines that attracted good third-party work were the ones whose internals were published —
which is the same argument the [Acornsoft](/vault/companies/acornsoft) and
[Superior Software](/vault/companies/superior-software) entries make about the BBC's supply
problem from the other direction.

He also, by Follis's account, developed the technique behind one of their games inside ten
days.
