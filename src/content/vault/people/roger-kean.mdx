The first issue of *CRASH*, dated February 1984, opened with an editorial signed by Roger Kean. He explained the name first: *CRASH* was "short, sharp and to the point, and anyway it makes it the only computer magazine without the word computer stuck somewhere in it". Then he set out the rule that made the magazine different. Every major review would be done by at least three reviewers, so that "the resulting review will even out personal preferences", and they would be "games players, not professional reviewers in the usual sense".

Kean had trained as an artist and worked as a film editor. With the brothers [Oliver](/vault/people/oliver-frey) and Franco Frey he founded [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield), which grew out of a mail-order business in Ludlow, Shropshire, into the publisher of *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)*, *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)*, *[Amtix!](/vault/magazines/amtix)* and *[The Games Machine](/vault/magazines/the-games-machine)*.

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1948. **Died:** 3 January 2023.
- **Founded:** Newsfield, in Ludlow, with Oliver and Franco Frey.
- **Editor of *CRASH*:** issue 1 (February 1984) to mid-1985, and again from April 1987; then editorial director.
- **Editor of *ZZAP!64*:** from late 1985 to autumn 1986.
- **Editor of *The Games Machine*:** May to December 1989.
- **After Newsfield:** publisher at Europress Impact in Ludlow from late 1991; later an author of illustrated history books.

## From film to Ludlow

When *Amtix!* introduced its staff in November 1985, its profile of Kean described a period at Hornsey College of Art, then the London Film School, and a first career as a film editor. His school's obituary adds that he edited television sports films in London for about ten years.

Kean moved to Ludlow in October 1982, as he recalled in a later interview. The first business he, Oliver Frey and Franco Frey started there was not a magazine. It was a mail-order company, Crash Micro Games Action, launched in about April 1983 to sell ZX Spectrum games at a time, Kean said, when shops did not stock them. Each order went out with a catalogue of reviews. Matthew Uffindell, a 17-year-old who came to the door to buy games and was given a job in mail-order dispatch, remembered in 2004 that he soon started reviewing for the catalogue. In June 1983, Kean recalled, the managing director of a magazine distributor told him that WH Smith's buyer would sell the catalogue if it became a proper monthly magazine.

## Editing CRASH

The magazine that followed was made in a very small space. Uffindell described the room he and Kean shared as "smaller than your average bathroom", with "a rented TV, a 16K Spectrum with the dreaded 32K RAM pack, and a typewriter". He would describe a game while playing it and Kean would type, then take his own turn at the game before writing his view.

The first masthead lists Kean as Editor, Oliver Frey as Designer and Franco Frey as Consultant Editor. By the summer of 1985 Graeme Kidd had moved up from assistant editor to editor, and from the autumn Kean appears as Publishing Executive, above Kidd, with Franco Frey as Technical Editor and Oliver Frey as Art Editor. By January 1987 the three founders shared a single masthead line as the publishers.

Kean came back to the editor's chair in April 1987, when the magazine told readers that the role of editor had "been an unclear one" while Kidd oversaw all of Newsfield's computer titles. That summer, reviewing Nintendo's NES for *CRASH*, he asked why a Spectrum owner should buy one, and answered that it was worth it if "virtually flawless arcade conversions" were what you wanted. From October 1987 he was listed as Editorial Director. He still turned up in the pages: when a rumour went round in October 1987 about who had modelled for the *Renegade* cover, the letters page admitted it was Kean, "well his arms anyway".

That reputation opened a door that stayed shut to most of the press. In April 1988 *CRASH* ran the story of Ultimate Play the Game, which, the magazine said, had asked "former CRASH Editor Roger Kean" to hear "the inside story on its history and future". Ultimate had avoided interviews for years, and Tim Stamper told him why: "CRASH always gave us fair reviews, but with some of the other magazines, if we didn't advertise, the product got a bad review." "So we steered clear of speaking to anyone," he went on.

## Lloyd Mangram

*CRASH*'s letters page, "Lloyd Mangram's Forum", and many of its features carried the byline of a staff writer who did not exist. In 2004 Uffindell wrote that "Lloyd's input was the joint combination of text and opinion from both Roger and myself". The mastheads print Mangram like any other member of staff. [Lloyd Mangram](/vault/people/lloyd-mangram) has his own entry.

## ZZAP!64, Amtix! and The Games Machine

*ZZAP!64* launched in May 1985 with Kean and Franco Frey on its masthead as "Money bags" and [Chris Anderson](/vault/people/chris-anderson) running the editorial side as "Slavedriver". By September 1985 Kean was answering readers' letters as "the editor", and the August 1986 masthead lists him as Editor with Gary Penn as his deputy. The magazine's own back-issue list later summed up the September 1986 issue with "Roger Kean Leaves".

That August 1986 issue also announced a new software house, Thalamus, which took ZZAP!'s Gary Liddon as its technical executive. The news pages said Thalamus was "independent of Newsfield and the magazines", while a competition in the same issue called it "the new Newsfield software house". The magazines of the time do not say what part Kean played in it.

*Amtix!*, for Amstrad owners, launched in November 1985, and its staff profile made Kean responsible for overseeing the launch. *The Games Machine* followed in October 1987 under Graeme Kidd as editor. Kean wrote for it: in March 1988, with Nik Wild, he interviewed Ultimate about "their past and their resurrection", and in June 1988 the magazine introduced him as a "life-long home movie buff" when he tried out a video-production program. By August 1988 he was on *The Games Machine*'s masthead as Editorial Director, and from May to December 1989 its mastheads named him as the magazine's editor.

## After Newsfield

Newsfield went into liquidation in the autumn of 1991. *CRASH* missed its November issue, and the December 1991 issue came from a new company, Europress Impact, still in Ludlow. Its masthead listed the publisher as "Roger 'Sir' Kean". Over the next two years he was publishing director of Europress Impact's console and Amiga titles, among them *N-Force* (1992), *Amiga Force* (1993) and *SNES Force* (1994), whose masthead still listed Oliver Frey as art director.

Kean and Oliver Frey were partners in life as well as in business. In the late 1990s Frey was publishing director of Thalamus Publishing in Shropshire, a publisher of illustrated history books, and Kean turned to writing them. He co-wrote *The Complete Chronicle of the Emperors of Rome* (2005) and *Forgotten Power: Byzantium* (2013), both illustrated by Frey, and wrote *The Fantasy Art of Oliver Frey* (2006). The two later set up their own imprint in Ludlow, Reckless Books. In 2017 he edited the crowdfunded *CRASH Annual 2018*, with a cover by Oliver Frey. Frey died in August 2022, and Kean died on 3 January 2023, of motor neurone disease.
