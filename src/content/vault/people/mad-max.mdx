Mad Max — Jochen Hippel — is the name most associated with squeezing real music out of the Atari ST's sound hardware. Where the ST's stock sound chip was widely considered a poor relation of the Amiga's Paula, Hippel coaxed remarkable results from it, building custom playroutines that made the machine sing in demos and games alike.

That scene reputation became a professional one at [Thalion](/vault/companies/thalion), the German studio drawn from the same ST and Amiga scene, where his soundtracks helped define the house style.

## Fast facts

- **Handle:** Mad Max.
- **Scene:** [Atari ST](/vault/systems/atari-st) (and Amiga).
- **Discipline:** Chip music and custom sound routines.
- **Later:** Soundtracks for Thalion.

## Coaxing music from the chip

Hippel's signature was technical as much as musical: bespoke replay routines that pushed the ST's hardware past what its designers intended, the same trick the scene's coders pulled with graphics. His work is a reminder that demoscene music was an engineering discipline as well as an artistic one.
