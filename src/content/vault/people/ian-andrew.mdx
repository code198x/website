## Overview
Ian Andrew founded and ran [Incentive Software](/vault/companies/incentive-software),
wrote *Mined-Out*, and designed the [Freescape](/vault/techniques/freescape)
engine — solid, freely navigable 3D on 8-bit hardware.

*CRASH* profiled him in May 1985, in a piece that opens with an apology: the
magazine had promised him a profile before its first issue ever appeared, "in
the mistaken belief that at 21 he was among the youngest Spectrum programmers of
the day". He wrote to editor Roger Kean to point out that he was "quite a bit
older than 21". The profile took another sixteen issues to arrive.

## Before computers
He was a **deltiologist** — a postcard dealer. *CRASH*:

> He started out with a couple of filing cabinets in his bedroom and then moved
> into shop premises in his home town of Reading as the volume of trade grew.

That shop is the origin of the software company. He was among the first people
to buy a **ZX81**, having seen it advertised in the *Daily Mail*, and wrote
*Mined-Out* for the Spectrum — *Personal Computer News* gave it 5/5 in April
1983, published by Quicksilva. His brother wrote a Dragon version; Ian Rowlings
converted it to the BBC; Lynx and Oric versions followed.

He then sold the postcard business and ran Incentive out of the shop.

## The shop as a company
An advert in the Reading local paper for a machine-code programmer brought **Ian
Morgan** in, and work began on *Splat!* — Incentive's first game, which *CRASH*
rated in the low 80s across three issues in 1984.

The 1985 profile describes a company of three full-time staff besides Andrew:
Darryl Still on the shopfront, administration and accounts, and Dave Baines as
resident technician. Andrew's account of how it worked is the most attractive
description of a software house in this Vault:

> There's a very informal atmosphere in the shop, and it has been a definite
> advantage having a shopfront. Programmers tend to wander in and hang around
> drinking coffee and playing our arcade machine, which means that the place is
> a melting pot of ideas.

He gives a worked example: *Confuzion* was devised by Paul Shirley on the
Amstrad, and its Spectrum version written by **Brendan Kelly**, who had popped
into the shop and stayed. Kelly later co-wrote
[GAC](/vault/tools/graphic-adventure-creator).

## What he was aiming at
> We're constantly looking for new ideas, new approaches to games. We want our
> games to be playable and addictive, but we also try to make things a bit
> harder.

*CRASH* noted his "modest approach", and closed the piece with him leaving in
"his humble Fiesta" — the magazine's joke about an industry then acquiring
Porsches.

## Freescape
Two years later Incentive shipped [*Driller*](/vault/games/driller), the first
game on the Freescape engine, and *CRASH* gave it **97%**. Three more Freescape
games followed, and then the engine went on sale to the public as
[3D Construction Kit](/vault/tools/3d-construction-kit), with Andrew presenting
the tutorial video that came in the box.
