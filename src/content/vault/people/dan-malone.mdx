Dan Malone had never touched a home computer when he answered the advert. After leaving art college in Ipswich he had tried to draw comic strips, pestering *2000 AD* and Marvel and getting occasional work from Knockabout Comics, who, he said, paid "if we've got any money left over at the end of the month". Then a tutor pointed out a notice in the trade paper *Campaign*, placed by Pete Stone of [Palace Software](/vault/companies/palace-software): "2000AD style artist wanted — no previous experience with computers needed." A year later *CRASH* ran a feature on him headed "Designing games the Malone way". By 1990 he was drawing *[Speedball 2](/vault/games/speedball-2)* for the [Bitmap Brothers](/vault/companies/bitmap-brothers).

## Fast facts

- **At [Palace Software](/vault/companies/palace-software):** sprites for *Cauldron*; designer and artist of *The Sacred Armour of Antiriad* (1986), and the comic packed with it.
- **At the [Bitmap Brothers](/vault/companies/bitmap-brothers), from about 1990:** graphics for *[Speedball 2](/vault/games/speedball-2)*, *Cadaver*, *The Payoff*, *[The Chaos Engine](/vault/games/chaos-engine)* and *The Chaos Engine 2*.
- **Not *Gods*:** the graphics for *[Gods](/vault/games/gods)* were by Mark Coleman.
- **Later:** freelance art for companies including System 3, Electronic Arts, Sony, Revolution Software and HotGen.

## Palace and Antiriad

Malone told *CRASH*'s Lee Paddon in December 1986 that his first job at Palace was designing sprites for the Amstrad version of *Cauldron*, with a sprite editor so poor that "you could only draw in one colour", and you only saw the result with the program running. For his own game, Palace programmer Stanley Schembri wrote a better art tool that showed and animated the whole sprite.

That game was *The Sacred Armour of Antiriad* (1986). Malone's love of *2000 AD* pushed it towards an arcade adventure with "a mythical theme ... with hi-tech overtones", set in a world where civilisation had come and gone, in which a barbarian, Tal, finds a suit of armour. He drew Tal's movements first (throwing rocks, crouching, running), then the screens, and when the programmers joined he kept a hand in the design: an editor let him move a monster or change a background and test it straight away. "The game's playability came about by trial and error," he said. He also drew the comic that came with the game and worked out a history for each monster. *Computer & Video Games* called *Antiriad* "a triumph for former freelance comic artist Dan Malone".

Pete Stone described Palace's working method at the time as "a sort of production line", with Malone and Steve Brown each generating games for a team of programmers to convert. By late 1987 he said there was no Palace house style: "It's more a case of there being a Steve Brown game, a Dan Malone game."

## The Bitmap Brothers

In spring 1990 *Amiga Action* reported that "Dan Malone, ex-graphics man with Palace", would do the graphics for the Bitmap Brothers' *Speedball 2*, and *Atari ST User* added *Cadaver*. He joined as the studio was growing. Mike Montgomery, one of the founders, later explained: "Dan Malone brought a lot of expertise in games to *Speedball 2*... That's not to put Mark Coleman down — he was working on *Gods* and that game has superb graphics." *CU Amiga* credited *Speedball 2* to "the Bitmaps, with graphics by Dan Malone and programming by Rob Trevellyan" in December 1990.

His next large job was *The Chaos Engine* (1993), a steampunk shooter for which *ACE* credited "graphics wizard Dan Malone" with "creating a period atmosphere". Other artists noticed. In a 1994 *Amiga Format* feature on how game graphics are drawn, an Amiga artist told readers to "look at the work of Dan Malone who did *Chaos Engine*. He is the king." For *The Chaos Engine 2* he drew the characters and backgrounds, and he told *The One* in 1995 that cutting the cast from six to four had been partly his decision.

The heavy, metal-and-rivets style of *Speedball 2* and *The Chaos Engine* is the look most people mean by "the Bitmap Brothers". It was the work of more than one person: Montgomery credited it to "colleagues such as Dan Malone" and Mark Coleman, who drew *Xenon 2* and *Gods*.

## After the Bitmaps

*Time Extension* reported that Malone left the Bitmap Brothers in 1995. He was lead artist on *Hordes*, a strategy game that *Edge* previewed in 1998 as a System 3 project, and designed *Rebound!*, a game bundled with the Orange SPV smartphone in 2003. He has worked since for companies including Electronic Arts, Sony, Revolution Software and HotGen, drew concept art for an unmade *Speedball* game in 2000, and contributed to the history books *The Bitmap Brothers: Universe* and *The Bitmap Brothers: Odyssey*. When Rebellion began a new *Speedball* in 2024, he said he had had "no input whatsoever" and had not known it was being made.
