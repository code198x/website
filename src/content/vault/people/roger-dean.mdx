In the late 1980s a Psygnosis game was easy to spot on a shop shelf: a painting of a strange landscape on the front, sometimes a poster or a T-shirt inside, and an owl for a logo. Roger Dean designed the owl and painted many of the pictures. He was already famous for the covers of Yes's albums, and he came to games because a software company thought its packaging was letting it down. Psygnosis explained to *The One* in 1990 that it had hired him "because we were disenchanted with the quality of computer game package artwork - if you're asking so much for a piece of software, you can't package it like a Brillo pad."

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 31 August 1944, Ashford, Kent, according to public reference works.
- **Training:** Canterbury School of Art, then the Royal College of Art in London.
- **Music:** covers for Gun and Osibisa, then Yes from *Fragile* (1971); also Asia.
- **Books:** *Views* (1975), *Magnetic Storm* (1984), *Dragon's Dream* (2008).
- **Games:** the Psygnosis name and owl logo; box art for Psygnosis games including *Brataccas*, *Barbarian*, *Terrorpods*, *Blood Money* and *[Shadow of the Beast](/vault/games/shadow-of-the-beast)*; later the *Tetris* logo for The Tetris Company.

## Floating islands

Dean trained as a designer. He told *Retro Gamer* in 2008 that his industrial design course kept a strict approach to drawing while other art schools were moving away from it, so he learned perspective, shadows and hand lettering. "I was lucky to have a discipline-intensive training," he said. His furniture, including a sea urchin chair, led to work for Ronnie Scott's jazz club, and through that to people in the music business. He painted album covers for Gun and Osibisa before Yes asked him for *Fragile* in 1971.

The covers he made for Yes over the next years, among them *Close to the Edge*, *Tales from Topographic Oceans* and *Relayer*, and the band's curving logo, made his name: rock landscapes floating in the sky, arches of stone, strange plants and creatures. They were sold as posters and collected in his book *Views* in 1975. Dean insisted the music did not paint the pictures. "With Yes, the cover always had to be finished long before even rough mixes were available," he said. His official biography also credits him with the logo of Virgin Records.

Games programmers grew up with those posters. [Andrew Braybrook](/vault/people/andrew-braybrook), asked by *ZZAP!64* in 1986 which book he would take to a desert island, chose *Views*: "He's an awfully good artist." Mike Richardson hid a rock shaped like Dean's painting *Pathways*, from the Yes album *Yessongs*, on the horizon of his Spectrum game *Thanatos* (1986). Yu Suzuki's fantasy scenery in *[Space Harrier](/vault/games/space-harrier)* owed a debt to his album covers, and Bill Williams said the city built on a giant creature's bones in *Knights of the Crystallion* (1991) was partly inspired by the cover of *Relayer*.

## Imagine and Psygnosis

Dean's first games work was for [Imagine Software](/vault/companies/imagine-software) in Liverpool, which planned to sell two "megagames", *Bandersnatch* and *Psyclapse*, in large boxes at a high price. *CRASH*'s account of Imagine's collapse in 1984 says Dean had already designed the boxes and the advertisements before the company failed. Looking back in 2008, *Retro Gamer* said the bankruptcy meant nothing he made for Imagine was ever used.

When Imagine's [Ian Hetherington](/vault/people/ian-hetherington) and his partners set up [Psygnosis](/vault/companies/psygnosis), Dean worked for them too; in *The One*'s words his involvement "came about through the MegaGames". He designed the company's identity. "They kept throwing names at me, and in the end I did the name and the logo," he told *Retro Gamer*. "They wanted something that said 'knowledge', 'the future', 'wisdom', 'fun', so the owl has to be the obvious choice." The owl was chrome and robotic, "a way of having a futuristic owl", and the "nosis" in the name meant knowledge: "It's really just a fake word, but it did carry a sense of being about technology, about knowledge, about wisdom and the future." He gave Hetherington and Jonathan Ellis credit for using it well: "they didn't just ask me to do the logo, they made good use of it once they got it."

The first game was *Brataccas*, sold in 1986 with "original poster by Roger Dean included". Its gloomy, stark cover was unlike his album work, but the next ones were not. His favourite remained the cover for Psygnosis's *Barbarian*, which began as "lots of globs and squiggles" until he "gradually teased the details out of it" and found a landscape with a red dragon in it. It came in the box as a poster, and according to Dean the developers put the dragon into the game. *Terrorpods* (1987) had "the usual excellent graphic designs and packaging by the talented team of Roger Dean and Tim White", as *Your Computer* put it, and the opening screen of *Blood Money* (1989) was based on Dean's box painting.

Dean usually painted before a game was finished, sometimes before he had seen anything of it. "I'd be doing packaging for a game which was, at the most, at a conceptual level, and had no opportunity to look at it before I started," he said, and admitted that a buyer who expected the game to look like its cover had a fair complaint. Games packaging had begun to make its painters well known: *CRASH* observed in 1986 that just as the record industry had made young artists into household names, "Roger Dean being one", so too was the software industry.

### Beast and the T-shirts

*[Shadow of the Beast](/vault/games/shadow-of-the-beast)* (1989) went furthest. Psygnosis sold it at £34.99 in a box twice the usual size, with Dean's painting on the front and a T-shirt carrying more of his art inside. According to Martin Edmondson, the title gained its "Shadow of the" after a legal check, and Dean painted a shadow into the cover to match. Asked about the painting in 2008, he remembered its two creatures as dogs and said he preferred his cover for the sequel.

The T-shirts became a Psygnosis habit, and not every reviewer was grateful. *Zero*'s listing for *Obitus* in 1991 noted its £34.99 price "includes yet another Roger Dean designer T-shirt. Yuk!" By then the look had become shorthand: in 1991 *Amiga Power* introduced *Ork* by its "bizarre Roger Dean-style graphics". When Sony bought Psygnosis in 1993, he and the company parted ways.

## Tetris

His next long partnership was with [Henk Rogers](/vault/people/henk-rogers), first on the cover of Rogers's role-playing game *The Black Onyx* and then on *[Tetris](/vault/games/tetris)*. In 1997 Rogers's Blue Planet Software was preparing a new game, *The Black Onyx*, whose world, *Computer Gaming World* reported, was "being designed by none other than Roger Dean". The same year, according to The Tetris Company's history of the game, he designed "a bold and distinctive logo for Tetris".

He found *Tetris* hard to draw for: "four square blocks in various configurations! You can't get simpler and blander." His answer was a simple mark, the T, with each version of the game given its own lettering, silhouette and heraldic device. "It would be very odd now to imagine doing packaging for a game where I didn't do the content," he said in 2008, "except for something like *Tetris*, where the content isn't in any way illustrative."

## Avatar

In 2013 Dean sued James Cameron, 20th Century Fox and Cameron's company Lightstorm Entertainment in a federal court in New York, seeking $50 million. He claimed that 14 of his paintings had been copied in the look of the planet Pandora and its creatures in *Avatar* (2009). In September 2014 Judge Jesse M. Furman dismissed the case. He found that Dean's comparisons were "plainly misguided", relying on images from books about the film rather than the film itself, and that "not all copying results in copyright infringement".

## Later work

Dean told *Retro Gamer* in 2008 that he saw his future "far more in content than covers", and that he was designing landscapes for a virtual-world project with Henk Rogers. His retrospective book *Dragon's Dream*, published that year, included work for Psygnosis, Bullet-Proof Software and Blue Planet Software. In 2023 he joined Rogers and Alexey Pajitnov to talk about *Tetris*, and in 2025 he was still telling interviewers the story of the owl.
