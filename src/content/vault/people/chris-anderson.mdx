**Chris Anderson** edited three British computer magazines and founded the publisher of a fourth, in the space of about eighteen months, and the period press records each step as it happened.

## The sequence

| When | Where |
|---|---|
| 1984 | Editor, *Personal Computer Games* |
| March 1985 | Announced as heading the *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* editorial team at [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield) |
| May 1985 | Editor, *ZZAP!64* issue 1 |
| October 1985 | *[Amstrad Action](/vault/magazines/amstrad-action)* issue 1, for his own [Future Publishing](/vault/companies/future-publishing) |

*CRASH* announced the middle step in March 1985 with a line that also explains it: "Heading up the editorial team is **Chris Anderson who was editor of *PCG* until he heard about *ZZAP! 64***."

Five months after *ZZAP!64* issue 1 reached the shops with his name on the masthead, *Amstrad Action* issue 1 reached them with cheques payable to a company of his own, from an address in Somerton, Somerset.

## Why the sequence matters

The magazine he launched for someone else and the company he left to start became direct rivals. *ZZAP!64* was [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield)'s Commodore title; Future went on to publish *[Amiga Format](/vault/magazines/amiga-format)* and *[Amiga Power](/vault/magazines/amiga-power)*, which competed with Newsfield's and then [Europress](/vault/companies/europress)'s output for the rest of the 8- and 16-bit era.

It also puts one person's name on the founding of two of the three publishing houses this Vault treats as defining the British games press — as launch editor at one and as founder of the other.

⚠ This entry covers only what the library holds, which is his work in the British computer press between roughly 1984 and 1986. Anderson's career continued well beyond that and well beyond this project's scope.
