Eiji Aonuma's way into *[The Legend of Zelda](/vault/games/legend-of-zelda)* was to imitate it. Given the chance to direct his own Super Famicom game, he made *Marvelous*, an action role-playing game he openly modelled on Zelda. "I really wanted to incorporate some essence of Zelda into it," he told *Edge*. [Shigeru Miyamoto](/vault/people/shigeru-miyamoto) noticed, and came to him with an offer: "Come on, if you want to make this type of game, why don't you try the real thing? We're making the new Zelda game." The new game was *[Ocarina of Time](/vault/games/legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time)*, and Aonuma has worked on the series ever since. Since *Twilight Princess* he has been the series' producer.

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1963, in Nagano. *Edge* gave his age as 42 in June 2005.
- **Joined [Nintendo](/vault/companies/nintendo):** 1988, from art school in Tokyo.
- **First Zelda:** *Ocarina of Time* (1998), as dungeon designer.
- **Director:** *Majora's Mask* (2000), *The Wind Waker* (2002), *Twilight Princess* (2006).
- **Producer:** *Twilight Princess* and the series since, including *Skyward Sword* (2011), *A Link Between Worlds* (2013), *Breath of the Wild* (2017) and *Tears of the Kingdom* (2023).

## From art school to Marvelous

Aonuma studied design at the Tokyo National University of Fine Arts and Music and joined Nintendo straight from college in 1988. *Edge* tells the story that Miyamoto had his eye on him from his job interview, where Aonuma turned up with a mechanical doll he had made as his final course project. His first task was as an artist, adapting the sprites of *Mario Bros.* for a special Famicom Disk System version made to promote a food company.

The game that set him on his path came out after he joined. "The first Zelda game I played" was *A Link to the Past*, he said in 2010. "I was really impressed, especially with the potential I saw. I realised that the game was doing many things I did not think videogames could do." *Marvelous* followed, released only in Japan, and led to Miyamoto's invitation. By then, Aonuma said, his own job had been working with Nintendo's outside developers, and here was a chance to make a game himself: "It took no time at all for me to say yes, of course."

## Ocarina of Time's dungeons

On *Ocarina of Time* Aonuma was "mainly responsible for the dungeon design". He traced the dungeons back to his course: at art school he had worked with 3D objects and "became fascinated with the relationship between architecture and 3D shapes", including the mechanisms inside them. The game took three years. "In order to expand the field we need manpower, we need data, we need additional hours of hard work," he told *Edge* in 2005. "In the end we spent three years in order to complete Ocarina Of Time, one single game." *Edge* reminded its readers that he had been "the chief architect of Ocarina's notorious Water Temple".

## Director: Majora's Mask and The Wind Waker

The follow-up had to be made faster, on the same engine. "If we cannot expand the area, the game field, what should we do? Our conclusion was, let us use time." Aonuma directed *Majora's Mask*, with Miyamoto as producer: three days that repeat, a moon that will fall on the world at the end of them, and a town full of people living their own schedules. The darkness, he said, came from that premise. The townspeople cannot understand their fate, "which is a tragedy of its own kind. Only Link, or the player, is aware of the world's fate."

For the GameCube he directed *The Wind Waker*, whose cel-shaded cartoon look divided fans. Aonuma explained the choice in terms of the hero: once the team decided on a child Link, they wanted to show "the very cheerful side of his character" with the exaggerated animation of Japanese cartoons, and toon shading also let them draw the open sea without the processing cost of realistic water. When *Edge* put to him in 2005 that the game felt unfinished, he took the blame: "To tell the truth, originally we were thinking about something bigger, and in order to make it on time we had to make it more compact-sized... that was my fault."

## Producer

Aonuma also co-produced *Four Swords Adventures* and advised on *The Minish Cap*, which was made outside Nintendo. After three Zelda games he asked Miyamoto whether he could "graduate" from the series. Miyamoto agreed, then handed him the next one, *Twilight Princess*: "I say that you are going to be the producer, not the director, this time." The team had grown past 40 people. Aonuma told *Edge* that Zelda kept pulling him back: "sometimes I think it is a kind of... fate for me, so I should give up escaping from that!" Nintendo now describes him as having been both director and producer of *Twilight Princess*, released in 2006.

He has produced the series since. *Edge* called him "series producer" on *Skyward Sword* for the Wii, and in 2013 interviewed him about *A Link Between Worlds* for the 3DS, a sequel to the game that had first inspired him: "It's the game that inspired me to make games." He produced *Breath of the Wild* (2017) and *Tears of the Kingdom* (2023) for the Switch; Hidemaro Fujibayashi directed *Tears of the Kingdom*. Introducing himself for Nintendo's *Ask the Developer* series in 2023, he summed up the career: "My first role in the series was designing dungeons for The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. I served as the director and producer for The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess, and I've been the series' producer ever since."

One habit has stayed the same throughout: Link does not speak. "Because Link is you!" Aonuma told *Edge*. "I do not want the players to feel the existence of a wall between you and Link by having Link speak with a stranger's voice."
