Mike Lamb converted arcade games to the Spectrum for [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software) and [Imagine](/vault/companies/imagine-software) at the point where those conversions were the biggest-selling software in Britain. A *CRASH* reader, compiling a top fifteen of Ocean and Imagine's staff, put him at **number one**:

> **1 MIKE LAMB — programmer of RoboCop, Combat School, Renegade, Target Renegade.**

## Fast facts

- **Programmed:** *RoboCop*, *Renegade*, *Target Renegade*, *Combat School*.
- **For:** Ocean and Imagine.
- **Also credited by *CRASH*** on *Renegade*'s graphics discussion, and named as "Mike Lamb (he of *Combat School*)".

## The hardest job in British software

Ocean's business was licences — films, arcade cabinets, television — and a licence is a deadline you do not control. The arcade original was in the arcades **now**, the film was in cinemas **now**, and the Spectrum version had to be in shops while that was still true.

So Lamb's job was to reproduce, on a 48K machine with no hardware scrolling, no sprites and 8×8 colour cells, a game built on dedicated arcade hardware that cost more than a car — and to do it to a date set by a film distributor.

*RoboCop* is the one that shows what that could achieve. It was the best-selling Spectrum game of its year and it holds up, which is a strange thing to be able to say about a tie-in written against an immovable deadline.

## The list itself

The reader's letter matters as much as Lamb's position on it. It ranks fifteen individuals and studios, and it names the people the credits usually lose:

**Andrew Deakin** (*Operation Wolf*, *Rambo 3*), **Ivan Horn** — graphic artist, **Jonathan Dunn** — "writes fantastic music/FX on nearly all", **Paul Owens** (*The Vindicator*, *Gryzor*), **Mark R Jones** — graphic artist, **Jonathan Smith** (*Cobra*, *Batman*, *Firefly*), **William Harbison** — graphic artist, **Steve Lamb** (*Typhoon*, *Madballs*), **Alison Jeftha** — graphic artist, **[Jon Ritman](/vault/people/jon-ritman)**, and the studios Denton Designs and Sentient Software.

*CRASH*'s reply is worth quoting, because it is the magazine stating the principle this Vault is built on:

> your list is a timely reminder of the work of the programmers, graphic artists and musicians who wrote the games for Ocean. However much money a software company puts into a game, **the ultimate responsibility for a game's presentation and playability must largely lie with the programming team involved**.

## Why he matters

Lamb is **the licensed conversion done properly** — the work that paid for the British industry and that its own histories treat as beneath notice.

He is also the way into a list that no credit field in this library contains. Four graphic artists and a composer are named there and nowhere else — one of them a woman, in a record that otherwise makes the era look entirely male — and they are named because a reader in Humberside sat down and wrote to a magazine.
