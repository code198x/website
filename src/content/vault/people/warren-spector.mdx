In the autumn of 1991, players of Origin's *Martian Dreams* met a villain called Dr Johann Spector. *Computer Gaming World*'s reviewer played along: Spector was "obviously a fictional creation who bears no resemblance to any real person, living or dead". The real Warren Spector had co-designed the game. He spent most of his career in a less visible job, as the producer who kept a team together until a game shipped, and the games he produced or directed include *Ultima Underworld*, *[System Shock](/vault/games/system-shock)* and *[Deus Ex](/vault/games/deus-ex)*, the line of first-person games now called [immersive sims](/vault/genres/immersive-sim).

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1955; grew up in Manhattan.
- **Before games:** studied and taught film history and criticism at the University of Texas at Austin.
- **Tabletop games:** Steve Jackson Games (*Toon*) and TSR (a *Buck Rogers* board game).
- **[Origin Systems](/vault/companies/origin-systems), 1989–1996:** producer of *Ultima VI*, *[Wing Commander](/vault/games/wing-commander)*, *Ultima Underworld*, *System Shock*, *Wings of Glory* and *Crusader*; co-designer of *Martian Dreams*.
- **[Looking Glass](/vault/companies/looking-glass), 1996–1997:** staff member, working on what became *[Thief](/vault/games/thief)*.
- **[Ion Storm](/vault/companies/ion-storm) Austin, 1997–2004:** studio head; director of *Deus Ex* (2000).
- **Junction Point, 2005–2013:** founder; *Epic Mickey* (2010) and *Epic Mickey 2* (2012) for Disney.
- **OtherSide Entertainment, 2016–2026:** *System Shock 3* (not released) and *Thick as Thieves* (2026).

## Film, then dice

Spector came to games from the cinema. *PC Player* reported in 1998 that he had studied film history and criticism and taught both at the University of Texas at Austin. He told *Edge* that he had written his thesis on cartoons. "Films are my life," he told the Italian magazine *K* in 1993. "I'm a student of film history, and it's natural that this interest shows in the games I produce."

His first games were played on a table. Before Origin he worked for Steve Jackson Games, where he designed the cartoon role-playing game *Toon*, and for TSR, the publisher of *Dungeons & Dragons*, where he designed a *Buck Rogers* board game. In February 1991 *ACE* called him "one of the most experienced role-playing game designers and writers in America".

## Producer at Origin

Spector joined [Richard Garriott](/vault/people/richard-garriott)'s Origin Systems in Austin, Texas, in 1989. In 1995 he wrote that in six and a half years there he had worked on "over a dozen games": role-playing games seen from above, interactive movies and flight simulators. *Computer Gaming World* nominated *Ultima VI* for its role-playing game of the year in 1990 and credited it to "Richard Garriott and Warren Spector (producers)". He also produced [Chris Roberts](/vault/people/chris-roberts)'s *Wing Commander*, the First World War flight game *Wings of Glory*, and the action game *Crusader*.

He was a hands-on producer. When *ACE* visited in 1991, he and the project leader Jeff George were "still ironing the creases out of the game's plot" of *Martian Dreams*, a Victorian science-fiction adventure built on the *Ultima VI* engine, with a team of about 25 designers, artists, musicians, testers, writers and programmers. "I'm head whip-cracker," Spector said.

## Underworld and System Shock

The work that defined him was done with a studio in Massachusetts. Blue Sky Productions, soon renamed Looking Glass, was making *Ultima Underworld* for Origin, and *Edge* reported that Origin went through two producers on the project in little over a year. The third, requested by the studio's founder Paul Neurath, stayed the course: Spector. He moved to Cambridge, Massachusetts, to finish the game. "Paul Neurath and I determined that the only way to bring *Ultima Underworld* to completion was to get the team out of the Blue Sky offices in New Hampshire and into one room," he told *Edge*. He "basically lived with the team in a draughty basement room in a government welfare office". "The last few months of *Underworld*'s development were the most personally and professionally satisfying time of my life."

He produced *System Shock* (1994) with the same studio. In 1995 he wrote in *Computer Gaming World* that of all the kinds of game he had produced, "there's one kind of game I live for — first-person, real-time POV games". What he wanted to make next was a game set in the real world, and he admitted it "bugs the hell out of me that we can't do a convincing real-world simulation".

In 1996 he joined Looking Glass itself, charged with setting up an Austin office. He stayed about a year and worked on *The Dark Project*, released in 1998 as *Thief*. *PC Player* credited him as its producer, and in 2004 he described himself as having "worked for a year on the original *Thief* at Looking Glass". Spector told *PC Player* that some of his ideas, such as enemies who run away and come back with friends, went into it before he left, and that his last projects at Origin had been put on ice by Electronic Arts.

## Deus Ex

In the summer of 1997 Spector joined [John Romero](/vault/people/john-romero)'s Ion Storm and set up its Austin studio. His first project there was code-named "The Real World plus 50": a game set in places players knew, 50 years in the future, where a telephone or a coffee machine would work the way a player expected. Its test level was the London Underground.

That game became *Deus Ex* (2000), and Spector was its director, with Harvey Smith as lead designer. In November 2000 BAFTA named it the PC game of the year. As studio head Spector oversaw two more games: *Deus Ex: Invisible War* (2003), directed by Harvey Smith, and *Thief: Deadly Shadows* (2004), whose project director was Randy Smith. Spector was open about his part in the work. "Harvey Smith is a better designer than I am," he told *Computer Gaming World* in 2003. "But I do know this: I don't know anyone who can pull it all together — personnel, design, and production — like I can. I make everyone better. That's my talent."

Spector left Ion Storm at the end of 2004, and Eidos closed the Austin studio early in 2005.

## Mickey Mouse

In 2005 Spector founded Junction Point Studios in Austin, and in 2007 he joined Disney, which bought the studio. Its game *Epic Mickey* (2010), a Wii exclusive, sent Mickey Mouse into a wasteland of forgotten Disney characters with a brush of paint and a bottle of thinner, and brought back Walt Disney's early star Oswald the Lucky Rabbit. Spector brought his old ideas with him. "In *Deus Ex* there were as many people who thought you were a jerk for killing everyone as for killing no one," he told *Edge* in 2010. "The game doesn't judge — you judge yourself." *Edge* later wrote that for all its faults, nobody could accuse it "of playing it safe". Its sequel, *Epic Mickey 2: The Power of Two* (2012), came out on several formats, and *Edge* thought it had steered "into gentler waters, all while making the same mistakes as last time". Disney closed Junction Point in January 2013. "The Junction Point journey is over," Spector wrote.

## Teaching, System Shock 3 and retirement

Spector then went back to the University of Texas, where he set up and ran the Denius-Sams Gaming Academy, a games course for graduates. In 2016 he left to join OtherSide Entertainment, Paul Neurath's studio, to make *System Shock 3*. The publisher Starbreeze invested in the game and then, during its own financial trouble, handed the publishing rights back early in 2019. Development stopped that year, and the rights to future *System Shock* games passed to Tencent. "Tencent is taking the franchise forward," Spector said in 2022.

OtherSide's last game under Spector was *Thick as Thieves*, a stealth heist game, released in May 2026 at $5 after a large cut in its scope. The studio cancelled its other project, *Argos*, and laid off most of its staff. In August 2026 Spector announced his retirement. "The game business has changed and it's just not as much fun for me anymore," he wrote.
