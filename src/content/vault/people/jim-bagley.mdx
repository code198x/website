Jim Bagley programmed arcade conversions for [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software) and Special FX at the peak of that trade. *CRASH* introduces him in 1991 with the industry's own shorthand for standing: "**He's got an impressive track record with smash hit games like *Midnight Resistance* and *Cabal* to his credit.**"

## Fast facts

- **Credited by *CRASH* 53 (June 1988)** on *Gutz* — "Producer: Ocean/Special FX… **Authors: Jim Bagley and Chas Davies**", £7.95.
- **Named for:** *Cabal*, *Midnight Resistance*, *Hudson Hawk*.
- **Still working on the machine:** among the programmers writing new Spectrum and [Spectrum Next](/vault/systems/zx-spectrum-next) software decades later.

## Saying what the game is for

The *Hudson Hawk* preview in *CRASH* 92 is unusual because the programmer explains his intent, in his own words, before the game exists:

> Jim's gone for a cartoony look to the game, emphasising the comedy elements of the movie. Says Jim, "**It's really going to be a fun game, not too difficult — something everyone'll find playable and nearly everyone should be able to complete!**"

That is a design position, and a slightly contrarian one for 1991. British 8-bit games were frequently brutal, partly by taste and partly by economics: a game that takes forty hours to beat justifies £7.95 better than one that takes four, and difficulty was the cheapest way to add length.

Bagley is stating the opposite priority — **finishable by nearly everyone** — and saying it to a magazine whose readers took pride in hard games. It is the sort of thing that only turns up in a preview interview, and it tells you more about how a programmer thought than any review of the finished product.

## Two sources, one game, different credits

*Gutz* also provides a small lesson in reading the record.

*CRASH*'s own review box credits it to **Jim Bagley and Chas Davies**. A reader's letter to the same magazine, ranking Ocean and Imagine's staff, lists *Gutz* among the games of **Jonathan Smith**. Both are period sources; both are printed in *CRASH*.

They need not be in conflict — a Liverpool studio's games passed through several hands, and "programmer of" meant different things to a credit box and to a fan writing from memory. But it is a reminder that **a single attribution is a claim, not a fact**, and that this Vault's confidence in any credit should scale with how many independent places it appears.

## Why he matters

Bagley is **the conversion programmer who is still here**. Most of the names in this Vault stopped, moved into management, or left the industry when the 8-bit market ended. He is among the people who kept writing for the Spectrum after the commercial reason to do so had gone, which places him in both the era this Vault documents and the community that keeps it alive.

That continuity matters to a project like this one. The machine's programming knowledge was never fully written down at the time — it lived in the people who had it, and some of them are still using it.
