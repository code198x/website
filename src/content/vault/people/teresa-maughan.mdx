## Overview

Teresa Maughan edited *Your Sinclair*. Across the magazines held in this library she appears on **48 mastheads as Editor** and a further 11 as Deputy Editor — **more than any other individual**, in any role, at any magazine here.

## Fast facts

- **Editor,** *Your Sinclair*, having come up through the deputy's chair.
- **48 masthead appearances as Editor**, 11 as Deputy Editor.
- **Colleagues on the same mastheads:** Matt Bielby, Kevin Cox, Andy Ide, James Leach, Marcus Berkmann, [Mike Gerrard](/vault/people/mike-gerrard) on adventures, Andy Ounsted and Sal Meddings on art.

## What Your Sinclair was

*Your Sinclair* is remembered for a tone — jokey, self-deprecating, fond of its readers and unimpressed by the industry — that is unusual enough to have outlived the magazine, the machine and the format.

A tone is an editorial product. It comes from who gets hired, what gets sent back, which jokes survive the sub, and whether the person in charge protects a voice when an advertiser or a publisher would prefer something blander. That work leaves almost no trace in the pages themselves, which is why editors are the least-recorded people in a well-recorded industry.

The masthead is the trace. Maughan is on it, as Editor, more times than anyone else in this library — and the years she is on it are the years the magazine sounds most like itself.

## The women the mastheads name

Maughan is also the entry point to something the development record hides completely.

The Vault's entire structured credit layer — 1,200 credits, every magazine — names **one woman**: Alison Jeftha, a graphic artist, once. Read the mastheads instead and *Your Sinclair* alone gives Maughan as Editor, **Jackie Ryan** as Deputy Editor, **Linda Barker** as Staff Writer, **Sara Biggs** as Production Editor, **Judith Middleton** and **Sonia Hunt** as Production Managers, **Melissa Parkinson** as Production Coordinator, and **Catherine Higgs** and **Sal Meddings** as Art Editors. *ZZAP!64* adds **Glenys Powell** as Editorial Assistant.

Two conclusions follow, and only one of them is about the past. Women were plainly present in senior and production roles in the games press. And the **development** record's near-silence is at least partly a recording failure, and not a straightforward account of who was there — the credit boxes that would have named developers were the same boxes that, as the [Ivan Horn](/vault/people/ivan-horn) entry sets out, could not name an artist at all.

## Why she matters

Maughan is the entry for **editing as authorship**, and for the fact that the most-attested person in this whole corpus of British computing magazines is an editor whom no history of the era mentions.
