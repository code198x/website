In January 1991 a British public-domain library advertised a new music demo in *Amiga Format* on the strength of one name: "Sound of the Silents... Great music from Jesper Kydd". The magazines spelled him Kid, Kydd and Kyde, but the name was already the selling point. Jesper Kyd was a Danish teenager writing tracker music for Amiga demo groups. Within a decade he was scoring *[Hitman](/vault/games/hitman)* for the friends he had met in the scene, and in 2005 one of those scores won a BAFTA.

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1972, Denmark.
- **Scene groups:** music for Kefrens demos from 1988, then [The Silents](/vault/groups/the-silents).
- **Demos:** *Sound of Silents* (1990), *Global Trash* and *[Hardwired](/vault/demos/hardwired)* (1991), *Optic Nerve* (1993).
- **Games:** *Sub-Terrania* and *Red Zone* for Zyrinx; the *Hitman* series (2000–2006); *Freedom Fighters* (2003); four *[Assassin's Creed](/vault/games/assassins-creed)* games (2007–2011); *Borderlands* (from 2009).
- **Award:** BAFTA for original music, *Hitman: Contracts*, 2005.

## The scene years

Kyd started on the family piano. He told *Wireframe* that a Commodore 64, which he got at 13, changed what he could do: "You could have a theme, and you could have something arranged around the theme. You could have a bass line... It was almost like you didn't have to sit and play everything." In 2006 he told *Edge* that "the emotion game composers were able to put into C64 scores is what attracted me to the medium", with an "experimental approach that reminded me of artists such as Vangelis and Jean Michel Jarre".

The demo scene took him further. The scene database Demozoo lists his first credits in 1987 and music for the Danish group [Kefrens](/vault/groups/kefrens) from 1988, including *Megademo VIII* in 1990. He then joined [The Silents](/vault/groups/the-silents), the Swedish group with a Danish section. *CU Amiga* reviewed the group's music demo *Sound of Silents* in December 1990, "featuring music by Jesper Kid", and found "some fantastic tunes, a great intro, and some really wild effects on the selection screen".

In 1991 he wrote the music for two of The Silents' best-known productions. Demozoo credits him with *Global Trash*, and with *Global Trash II*, which was made on videotape and, according to Jimmy Maher's history of the Amiga, reportedly shown on MTV Europe. At [The Party](/vault/events/the-party) in Denmark that December, *[Hardwired](/vault/demos/hardwired)*, made with the Danish group [Crionics](/vault/groups/crionics), came second, with Kyd's music under nearly eleven minutes of vector graphics. He also wrote the music for *Optic Nerve*, the PC demo that came second to Future Crew's *Second Reality* at [Assembly](/vault/events/assembly-party) '93.

## Zyrinx and America

Through the scene Kyd met David Guldbrandsen, Karsten Hvidberg and Jesper Jørgensen, who later founded [IO Interactive](/vault/companies/io-interactive). With them, he told *Wireframe*, he started the game developer Zyrinx. Its first game was the [Mega Drive](/vault/systems/sega-mega-drive) shoot 'em up *Sub-Terrania*, published by [Sega](/vault/companies/sega) in 1994. *Edge* called it the work of "little-known programming team Zyrinx", and said it had "some great graphics and sound to back up the gameplay". Several of the *Hardwired* team were at Zyrinx too, among them the artist Mikael Balle.

"Sub-Terrania's success prompted the team to move to America," *Wireframe* wrote. Zyrinx worked as the internal team of Scavenger, an American company that *Edge* described in 1995 as having a "Sega background". The in-game credits of the Mega Drive shoot 'em up *Red Zone* (1994) and the [Saturn](/vault/systems/sega-saturn) racer *Scorcher* list Kyd for the music, and the Sega Retro wiki records that *Red Zone* was developed under the working title *Hardwired*. He also wrote the music for Sega's *The Adventures of Batman & Robin* on the Mega Drive in 1995. *Scorcher* reached the shops in 1997, by which time *Edge* was writing of "the dream that was Scavenger" collapsing. The rest of the team returned to Copenhagen and set up IO Interactive. Kyd stayed in New York.

## Hitman

IO asked him to score its first game, *Hitman: Codename 47* (2000). "The only brief I was really given was that they wanted something unique and original," he told *Wireframe*. "I had been working with these people from the demoscene days, so I had proven myself and they trusted my instincts." The game's music was interactive, built with Microsoft's DirectMusic system, which he found "almost like a database programme", so he wrote the music outside it and fitted it back in afterwards.

For *Hitman 2: Silent Assassin* (2002), IO's chief executive Janos Flösser proposed a full symphonic score with the Budapest Symphony Orchestra and Choir. "I had never written a symphonic score before," Kyd said, "and I'm like, 'Are you sure you have the right guy?'" For IO's *Freedom Fighters* (2003), a game about an invasion of New York, he worked with the Hungarian Radio Choir, and he wrote much of the score after 11 September 2001, when he was in Europe and struggling to get back to Manhattan. *Hitman: Contracts* (2004) turned darker and more electronic, and in 2005 it won the BAFTA for original music. *Hitman: Blood Money* (2006) was his last in the series. In his words, he wanted "to combine Contracts' electronics with the orchestra of Silent Assassin". For *Hitman: Absolution* IO turned to the Danish composers Peter Peter and Peter Kyed, and the game's director, Tore Blystad, told *Edge* in 2011: "Kyd is a great composer and we will work with him again."

## Assassin's Creed and Borderlands

Ubisoft Montreal pitched him its next game "in a hotel room at E3", he recalled: "You can go anywhere in the environment that you want? You can scale buildings?" For *Assassin's Creed* (2007) the studio gave him three words, "tragic", "war" and "mysticism", and the in-game machine that replays history, the Animus, led him to make everything "sound like it's coming through a filter to remind you that you're in some kind of simulator". He scored four games in the series between 2007 and 2011. The theme "Ezio's Family", from *Assassin's Creed II* (2009), became the series' recurring motif and is now played in concert halls.

He has scored the *Borderlands* shooters from the first game in 2009 to *Borderlands 3* in 2019, and *Edge* listed *Darksiders II* among his work in 2012. He came back to *Assassin's Creed* for *Valhalla* in 2020. His later work includes *Warhammer 40,000: Darktide* (2022) and the music for *Samson: A Tyndalston Story* (2026) by the studio Liquid Swords. Talking to *NME* about *Samson*, he looked back at the scene of his teens: "It was always about who can do something unusual, who can surprise people and who can blow the lid off stuff."
