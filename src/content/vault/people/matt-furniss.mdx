In the early 1990s, if an American or British publisher wanted a film licence or an arcade conversion on Sega's consoles, the music very often came from one room in Rotherham. Matt Furniss was sixteen when he joined the conversion house Teque in 1989, and within four years he could tell *CU Amiga* that "just about anything you'll hear on the Megadrive has come from here." His credits run from *Mortal Kombat* and *The Lion King* to *Puggsy* and *The Second Samurai*, on the Mega Drive, Master System and Game Gear, alongside a stack of Amiga games. His own count for the Master System alone was more than thirty.

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1973.
- **Worked for:** Teque and [Krisalis Software](/vault/companies/krisalis), Rotherham, from 1989.
- **Machines:** Commodore 64, Amiga, Mega Drive, Master System, Game Gear and Mega-CD.
- **Known for:** music and sound effects for Western-made Sega console games, many of them written by [Probe Software](/vault/companies/probe-software) and published by [Acclaim](/vault/companies/acclaim), US Gold, Virgin and others.
- **Not his:** *[Streets of Rage](/vault/games/streets-of-rage)*, whose music is by [Yuzo Koshiro](/vault/people/yuzo-koshiro). Furniss has named it among his favourite Mega Drive soundtracks.

## Rotherham, 1989

Furniss left school in 1989 and posted a demo tape of his Amiga music to developers around Britain. "Luckily for me Teque Software were looking for a sound designer," he told Arcade Attack in 2019. He was paid "about £7,000. Not much money but as a sixteen-year-old fresh out of school I didn't complain." His first Commodore 64 music was typed in as source code for Teque's in-house SID driver, using nothing more than a C64 and an Action Replay cartridge; he found it hard going and soon moved to a tracker-style system, which he used for *Shadow Warriors*, *Space Harrier II* and his later C64 games.

His accounts of which game came first differ. In 2015 he named Domark's *Toobin'* (1989) as the first game released with his music, and in 2019 the Amiga *[Laser Squad](/vault/games/laser-squad)*. In 1993 he told *CU Amiga* that "the tracks for *Chase HQ* for Ocean, *Manchester United* for Krisalis, and *Laser Squad* for Blade were all written at about the same time."

## The Amiga years

On the Amiga Furniss worked with a sampler, a hard drive and *ProTracker*, and composed straight on the machine: "I find it's quicker and easier to do it that way." He liked the sample quality and disliked the four channels, and he knew where the real contention lay. Programmers often wanted the blitter to draw the sprites, he explained, "so you've got the blitter and the sound chip both after the same bit of Chip memory", and the soundtrack usually ended up with about 50K.

The magazines noticed. *Computer and Video Games* found his music for Krisalis's puzzle game *Revelation* "very *Countdown* like" in March 1991. Reviewing the Amiga *Ultima VI* in August 1992, *Amazing Computing* wrote that "musician Matt Furniss should be congratulated for the Amiga version's superlative sound and music." *AMIGA POWER* credited him on *OutRun Europa*, *Graham Taylor's Soccer Challenge*, *Alien³*, *Arabian Nights* and *Soccer Kid*, and *The One* reported that Psygnosis's *Innocent Until Caught* would have "proper soundtrack 'themes' written by Matt Furniss", one for each location and character. By February 1993 *CU Amiga* was calling him "one of the Amiga scene's most prolific musicians". Of his own soundtracks he rated *Manchester United Europe* and *Cytron*, for Psygnosis; the one that made him cringe was the "really cheesy tune, with some samples" for Domark's James Bond game.

## A sound system for Sega's consoles

In 1991, Furniss recalled, Krisalis was already selling KASM, its own unofficial development kit for the Master System and Game Gear, and there was "new demand in the UK and USA for Master System game music". With Krisalis's technical director, Shaun Hollingworth, he built a music development program. His first Master System job was Probe's *SEGA Chess*: three songs, for the title screen, winning and losing, which took him a couple of days. "From there I went on to create audio for a further 30 SEGA Master System games, such as *Mortal Kombat*, *The Lion King*, *Alien3*, *Lemmings* and *The Terminator*."

The Mega Drive got the same treatment. Furniss wrote the music on an Atari Mega ST, and Hollingworth's system sent it "in real-time to the Sega console, similar to a MIDI sequencer and synthesizer", so he could hear exactly how it would sound in the game. Hollingworth, he said, "wrote most of the sound engines I used in the early years". The Mega Drive, he wrote later, "had excellent sound hardware for its day. A six-channel stereo sound chip made by Yamaha, three channel PSG and a Z80 co-processor." (The [YM2612](/vault/hardware/ym2612) entry explains the FM chip.) He already knew Yamaha's FM chips from the PC's AdLib and Sound Blaster cards, and his rule for drums was to "use samples for snares and kicks, FM for hats, toms, etc." *The Second Samurai*, he said, was "very 'FM.' Lots of big sweeping synths and FM tricks."

Much of the work was conversion, often done at a distance. If he was lucky, the developer sent an early version of the game by modem to Krisalis's bulletin board and he ran it on his development system; for other projects he had "only a written description of the game delivered by fax", and he posted the finished music back on floppy disk. He converted music by ear: *OutRun Europa* from [Jeroen Tel](/vault/people/jeroen-tel)'s Commodore 64 score to the Amiga and Sega's 8-bit machines, and *The Incredible Hulk* from the Super NES version to the Sega consoles. "If I liked the original music, I'd convert it faithfully." For US Gold's Mega Drive *Gunship* he wrote original music, two songs for each level, taking his cue from its settings in the Middle East, Asia, South America and the Arctic.

## Credits

The in-game credits and manuals transcribed by Sega Retro name Furniss on more than fifty Sega console releases. They include:

|---------|-------|

| Master System and Game Gear | *Back to the Future Part III*, *Prince of Persia*, *The Terminator*, *Smash T.V.*, *Alien³*, *Chuck Rock* (1992), *RoboCop 3*, *Wolfchild* (1993), *Mortal Kombat II*, *The Lion King*, *Dragon: The Bruce Lee Story* (1994) |

Several of these, *Mortal Kombat* among them, were Probe Software conversions: Furniss worked for Krisalis and supplied the sound to other developers. None is a game from Sega's own Japanese teams.

## After Krisalis

Around 2001, Furniss told Sega-16, he "went into software engineering and web development full time". By 2015 he was working for Sony Computer Entertainment America in southern California, on the team behind the cloud-gaming service Gaikai, which Sony turned into PlayStation Now. In 2019 he told Arcade Attack he worked in software design for a Californian tech start-up, and sang and played bass in a local covers band.

A different Matt Furniss, an artist, worked at Gremlin in Sheffield in the same years; the magazine credits for Gremlin's *The Legacy of Sorasil* and *Re-Loaded* are his, not the composer's.
