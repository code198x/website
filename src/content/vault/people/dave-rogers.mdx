## Overview
Dave Rogers wrote the Spectrum music for
[Raffaele Cecco](/vault/people/raffaele-cecco)'s Hewson games — the scores that
are routinely held up as the high-water mark of what the Spectrum's one-bit
beeper could be made to do.

They are also routinely credited to somebody else.

## The evidence
Three independent period sources, all naming Rogers:

**Cybernoid.** *CRASH*, October 1988, reporting on *Cybernoid II*'s progress,
describes incorporating "the new music soundtrack by **Dave Rogers** (who also
put together the original *Cybernoid* tune)".

**Cybernoid II.** *Your Sinclair*'s preview prints the credit block outright:

> Graphics and code — Rafaelle Cecco. Music — **Dave Rogers**.

**Stormlord.** [*Cecco's Log*](/vault/games/stormlord), the development diary
*CRASH* ran while Cecco built the game, records the delivery in January 1989:
"sound effects and music for *Stormlord* from **Dave Rogers**. I hastily
incorporate them into the program to have a listen."

Searching the Spectrum press held in this library for *Stormlord* alongside
*Follin* returns **nothing at all**.

## What Tim Follin did do
[Tim Follin](/vault/people/tim-follin) is a genuinely extraordinary Spectrum
composer and none of this diminishes him — the point is only that these
particular games are not his. The corpus credits him with *Agent X*, and
*Sinclair User* in November 1989 sets out the Software Creations arrangement on
*Ghouls & Ghosts*: programming by **Mike Follin**, music and sound effects by
**Tim Follin**, additional sounds by **Geoff Follin**. Three brothers on one
game. He also scored *Bionic Commando* and *LED Storm* for U.S. Gold, both of
which reached *CRASH*'s music chart.

## Why the mix-up matters here
The Shadowkeep curriculum sets its audio bar by reference to these games. A bar
described as "Tim Follin's *Cybernoid II* music" points at the right sound and
the wrong person, and if the reference is going to be cited it should be cited
correctly.

It is also the ordinary fate of game composers. Where programmers of this period
appear on dozens or hundreds of pages of the Spectrum press — Steve Turner on
120, Matthew Smith on 88, Cecco on 44 — the composers appear on single figures.
Rogers is credited in passing, inside somebody else's diary, in a preview's
small print. That is how the attribution came loose in the first place.
