Romeo Knight took his name from a record. "It's the title of a rap disc of the rather unknown rap group 'Boogie Boys', released in 1988 or so," he told the diskmag *Hugi*. "I was so fond of this disc that I made the title my nickname." Under that name he wrote music for [Red Sector Inc.](/vault/groups/red-sector-inc), including two of the songs in its 1989 *Megademo*, which British public-domain libraries were still selling years later. In February 1991 the French magazine *[ST Magazine](/vault/magazines/st-magazine)* asked the Atari ST musician [Mad Max](/vault/people/mad-max) which Amiga composers he liked. He named three: "Chris Hülsbeck, Romeo Knight of the group Red Sector, and the musician from Kefrens."

## Fast facts

- **Country:** West Germany; he went to school in Düsseldorf.
- **Started:** on a Commodore 128 in 1985, composing in C64 mode with Chris Hülsbeck's Soundmonitor.
- **Groups:** Red Sector Inc. and its successor TRSI; also Masque, TCC Design, Brainstorm, Silverstar Design and Samsara Circle.
- **Best known for:** two songs for the RSI *Megademo* (1989).
- **Voted:** best musician in the *EuroChart* scene polls of March and November 1990.
- **Later:** sound engineer in advertising from 1995, running his own studio with partners from 2001.

## From the C128 to the Amiga

He got a Commodore 128 at 14, in 1985, "which I basically only ran in C64 mode", and started composing on it with [Chris Hülsbeck](/vault/people/chris-huelsbeck)'s Soundmonitor. The C64 music that made him want to try was by Rob Hubbard and Martin Galway; he named *Commando*, *Zoids* and *Last V8* among the tunes he listened to. "All of the about 20 SID tunes I created were lost," he said later.

On the Amiga he wrote music as modules, the tracker files that kept their own instrument samples alongside the notes. His route into Red Sector was ordinary: "It was basically one of these knew someone from school who knew someone who was part of the scene-stories." He went to school in Düsseldorf, "the headquarters of Red Sector back then", and was introduced to the group's Irata, Flynn and Hamster. In the *Hugi* interview he credited Skyhawk of Prime Evil with handing his Amiga modules to the Red Sector core.

He was not, by his own account, very interested in the scene itself. "Sometimes I brought my Protracker mods with me which often ended up in demos, so most of the time I didn't even know which demos my tracks were used in," he told Remix64. "Apart from the positive feedback I got from the guys I knew personally, I honestly didn't care a lot about what happened outside of my personal realms."

## The Megademo and the charts

The RSI *Megademo* is dated 9 September 1989 and filled two disks with separate parts, each with its own effects and music. The scene database Demozoo credits its music to Bit Arts, SCS, Mark II and Romeo Knight. Asked by *Hugi* about his most successful early work, he answered: "Maybe the two songs for the RSI Megademo? It took a couple of days." Eight years later the Italian magazine *Amiga Magazine* still listed the demo in its history of the Amiga scene as "Red Sector Megademo, code by Delta, music by Romeo Knight".

The scene noticed. The *EuroChart*, a diskmag poll of the European scene compiled by the group Crusaders, placed him first among musicians in March 1990 and again in November 1990.

## Later work

Demozoo lists more than 300 productions with his music between 1988 and 2026, mostly for Amiga demos, intros and music disks, but also for the Atari ST, PC and Nintendo DS. He moved from the Amiga to MIDI synthesisers and Cubase on an Atari ST, a step he called "just natural for the type of music I wanted to make". "I have been a sound technician in the advertising industry since 1995 and have been running my own studio together with partners since 2001, mostly in order to make the sound accompanying TV spots and to produce radio spots," he told *Hugi*. He also played guitar in local crossover and metal bands, made guitar-based remixes of C64 music such as *Bionic Commando* and *Monty on the Run*, and has "occasionally" written soundtracks for independent games.
