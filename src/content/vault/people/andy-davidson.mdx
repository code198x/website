In September 1994 a 21-year-old from Bournemouth walked up to Team17's stand at the ECTS trade show in London with, as *Amiga Computing* later put it, "two skanky looking diskettes". "You're probably not going to like this," Andy Davidson remembered telling them, "it's a bit weird." Team17 played it for five minutes and asked whether he wanted it published. The game was *Total Wormage*, and a year later, as *[Worms](/vault/games/worms)*, it was on its way to most of the computers and consoles of the day.

## Fast facts

- **Known for:** creating *[Worms](/vault/games/worms)* (1995), published by Ocean and developed with [Team17](/vault/companies/team17).
- **Tools:** [Blitz Basic 2](/vault/tools/blitz-basic-2), with some assembler, and *[Deluxe Paint](/vault/tools/deluxe-paint)* on the Amiga.
- **At Team17:** 1994 to 1999, then again from 2012 for *Worms Revolution*.
- **Born:** about 1973; *Amiga Format* gave his age as 21 in its January 1995 issue.

## Before Worms

Davidson told *The One* in 1995 that he had "always fiddled, since I had a Spectrum and a Commodore 64", and that he had owned an Amiga since 1987. He chose Team17 because it was, to him, the company that put the Amiga first: "All the people who are knocking the Amiga should remember that it's the only machine that people who have ideas can use to turn them into games."

The game that became *Worms* started, he told *Retro Gamer*, as *Artillery*, a tank game on a Casio graph-plotting calculator. On the Amiga it grew into a game for teams of small, fast, jumping worms, drawn in *Deluxe Paint* and inspired by how much character *[Lemmings](/vault/games/lemmings)* got into a few pixels. *PC Zone* quoted him on why he kept at it: "It started as a simple 'tank game' to keep me and my mates amused while we should have been doing our A-Levels." He worked in a computer shop at the time and let customers play each new version. In November 1995 *Amiga Format* printed a reader's memory of the Caltec computer shop in Wimborne, near Bournemouth, where the shop's A1200 was running "a strange game called Total Wormage". Asked whether it was a public-domain game, "the spotty assistant" replied: "No, I programmed it."

## Getting it published

*Amiga Format*'s November 1993 cover disk carried a full copy of Blitz Basic 2, with a competition to write a game in it. Davidson started work on *Total Wormage* in August 1993, he told *The One*, and entered it the following February. "Some of it's in *Blitz*, but some of it's in assembler," he said. It did not win, "which was rather gutting". A copy sent to Acid Software, the makers of Blitz Basic, in New Zealand brought no reply either. ECTS was his last try, and it worked.

Team17 let him develop the Amiga version his own way. "Team17 basically gave me a free hand with the game," he told *PC Zone*; "I would go back to Bournemouth for a while and then go up to show them the new version." He added the sheep, custom levels drawn in any paint program, and, by his own account, the Concrete Donkey. The worms' voices in the 1995 game were his, re-recorded by the musician Bjørn Lynne. *The One* described the game in March 1995 as "the brainchild and sole work of Andy Davidson"; *Worms* went on sale late that year.

## After Worms

Davidson stayed at Team17 through *Worms 2* (1997), which he told *Edge* was his attempt to use the ideas he had not had time for in the first game, and *Worms: The Director's Cut* (1997), a last Amiga version in which, Marcus Dyson told *Retro Gamer*, he was given "a free reign to do whatever was in his slightly strange brain". He left after *Worms Armageddon* (1999). He told *Retro Gamer* in 2005 that he had worked on ideas for a different game that was not given the go-ahead, that leaving "was one of the hardest decisions I've had to make", and that he had always argued against putting *Worms* into 3D.

In 2012 he rejoined Team17 to work on *Worms Revolution*, saying he wanted to "scratch the creative itch that's been bothering me for the last fourteen years". In August 2026 he released a free expansion pack for *Worms: The Director's Cut* on the Amiga, made with Ben Paddon, with new levels, teams and a level-making tool.
