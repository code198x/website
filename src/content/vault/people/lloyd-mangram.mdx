For eight years the letters page of *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* was "Lloyd Mangram's Forum". Lloyd opened the post, printed the best letters and argued with readers. He was listed on the masthead as a staff writer, answered the letters in *[ZZAP!64](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* as well, and had a magazine named after his initials. He was not a real person. Lloyd Mangram was a name used by [Newsfield](/vault/companies/newsfield)'s staff, and the magazines never said which of them was writing as him at any given time.

## The Forum

Lloyd was there from the start. In the first *CRASH*, dated February 1984, he introduced the Forum and promised to send the writer of the best letter each month £12 worth of software: "I've had to grovel on bended knee to the editor for this, so you'd better be grateful!" Letters went to "Lloyd Mangram, CRASH" at PO Box 10, Ludlow. From the April 1984 issue the masthead listed him as "Staff writer", above the contributing writers, and he was still there in October 1991, in the last issue Newsfield published.

The Forum mixed real questions and complaints with long-running jokes. One was the Ludlow gibbet, where staff who made mistakes were threatened with hanging. Another was Lloyd's typewriter. In October 1985 the contents page promised "Your views become public, courtesy of Lloyd's Hermes", and that month Lloyd wrote of "the shaky carriage of my 1922 Hermes schreibmaschine" (German for typewriter). Replies were signed "LM".

## The Hall of Slime, the tips and the previews

In July 1984 Lloyd opened "Lloyd Mangram's Hall of Slime" for readers who wanted to boast about their high scores, with a warning that the scores had to be honest. It ran for nearly three years, until the May 1987 Forum announced that it was "being rethought, and takes a holiday this issue after three years of hard work".

That same May 1987 issue shows how much the name was expected to carry. Lloyd was credited with a 32-page Playing Tips Supplement, and "Merely Mangram" returned to write the previews, and the Forum admitted that "was quite enough Mangram for one month". When *CRASH* looked back over its tips pages in its last issue, in April 1992, it named Lloyd as their first editor, "the lorefather of faulty POKE listings", succeeded by his "helper", Robin Candy, then Hannah Smith, then Lloyd again for a while, and then Nick Roberts.

## ZZAP!64 and Amtix!

When *ZZAP!64* moved its editorial office to Ludlow in the summer of 1985, Lloyd took over its letters page, ZZAP! Rrap. He introduced himself in the August 1985 issue: "To anyone who has read CRASH Magazine, I probably need little introduction, being none other than Lloyd Mangram." He added that he had edited the CRASH Forum "since it started in issue 1" and that his qualification for the new job was "one and a half years playing Commodore 64 games (don't tell a CRASH reader)". The first issue of Newsfield's Amstrad magazine, *[Amtix!](/vault/magazines/amtix)*, in November 1985, mentioned Lloyd's Hermes and his work on the letters pages of *CRASH* and *ZZAP!*, but Lloyd had no column in *Amtix!*: its letters were answered under other names, and by 1987 readers addressed them to Saffron.

## LM

With its Christmas 1986 issue *CRASH* gave away "Issue 0" of *LM*, a new Newsfield magazine that covered music, films, video and comics. *CRASH*'s back-issue list described that Christmas issue as including a look behind the scenes "where they make the magazine that bears Lloyd's initials".

## Did he exist?

Readers wondered. In November 1986 one wrote to ZZAP! Rrap that "there has been a lot of controversy about Lloyd Mangram's existence". LM replied: "of course I exist". In the same issue ZZAP!'s high-score keeper, the Scorelord, remarked that "the so-called Lloyd Mangram" might have some connection with golf. The golf joke fits a claim made in public sources since, that the name was borrowed from the American golfer Lloyd Mangrum, but none of those sources gives a first-hand account.

When the magazines showed Lloyd, they hid him. A ZZAP! team photograph in March 1989 includes "Lloyd (with paper bag)". *CRASH* changed publisher after Newsfield collapsed in 1991, and its December 1991 issue announced that Lloyd had gone on a world cruise "with a massive supply of paper bags", leaving the letters page, renamed "Lucy's Letters", to the new editor, Lucy Hickman. By 1993 he was back as "our man of letters" in Europress's *Commodore Force*, "what with that bag on his head and all", and told a reader who asked to see his face that he had "a severe aversion to photographs": "The paper bag stays, pal."

Roger Kean, *CRASH*'s first editor, kept up the story. Asked in 1998 who had thought up "the staff member who didn't actually exist", he answered: "Of course Lloyd Mangram existed. He was as real as anyone — the paper bag is still in a special case in the Ludlow Museum."

## Who wrote as Lloyd

The only first-hand account comes from Matthew Uffindell, who joined the Ludlow business at 17 and reviewed games for *CRASH* from the start. Writing in *Retro Gamer* in 2004, he said the early reviews were written by three people, "Roger Kean, myself and the legendary Lloyd Mangram", and went on:

> Lloyd was a character, well, sort of. A little, if at all known fact was that he never existed! Lloyd's input was the joint combination of text and opinion from both Roger and myself. I don't think anyone else has ever admitted to that before.

Uffindell was describing the early reviews. He does not say who wrote as Lloyd later, in the Forum, the tips or *ZZAP!*, and no public source names everyone who did. The magazines sometimes played with the arrangement in print: in August 1984 *CRASH* ran a look back at old reviews under the byline "MATTHEW UFFINDELL and LLOYD MANGRAM".

The name outlived the magazines. In *Edge*'s 100th issue, in 2001, one of the contributions on the future of games was signed "Lloyd Mangram, industry veteran", and the first issue of *[Retro Gamer](/vault/magazines/retro-gamer)*, in 2004, ran a history of video games as "Lloyd Mangram's Videogames History Lookback".
