**Jonathan Dunn** was a British game composer who served as Ocean Software's in-house musician, creating soundtracks for their licensed games across multiple platforms. His ability to adapt to different sound chips—from the C64's SID to the Amiga's Paula—made him invaluable as games were ported across systems.

## On the Spectrum
Dunn is remembered as a Commodore composer, and the Spectrum work is
substantial: *CRASH* was a Spectrum-only magazine, and it named him repeatedly.

Its December 1988 reviews praise "typically atmospheric **Jonathan Dunn** music"
in one game and "an excellent **Jonathan Dunn** title tune and some very good
(and informative) ingame [effects]" in another. The first of those carries a
qualification that says as much about the workload as the work — the music is
good, "which admittedly is a bit like that on" another recent title.

He reached *CRASH*'s reader-voted **Top 10 Music** chart in February 1989 with
***RoboCop*** for Ocean, one of seven composers the magazine listed by name that
month.

## Named inside the games
Ocean's habit of naming characters after its own staff puts him in the fiction
as well as the credits: a *CRASH* map from November 1988 lists building
occupants in pairs of joke-name and real-name, and "Nathan Junnod" sits beside
**Jonathan Dunn** on the second floor, among Ocean colleagues including Mike
Lamb, Simon Butler, Paul Owens and Martin McDonald.

It is a small thing, and it is one of the few places where a composer appears in
a game's content rather than in an inlay nobody kept.
