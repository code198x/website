Jonathan Dunn arrived at [Ocean Software](/vault/companies/ocean-software) by luck of timing. He was studying music at college and making music on his Commodore 64 when friends talked him into sending demos to software houses. "It turns out I sent in my demo just as [Martin Galway](/vault/people/martin-galway) was preparing to leave the company," he told *Retro Gamer*. "I was offered the job that day." He joined Ocean's in-house team in October 1987, and within a year *CRASH* reviewers were naming him in their verdicts on Ocean's biggest games. He is best remembered for a tune he wrote for a handheld: the title music of Ocean's *RoboCop* on the Game Boy, which later sold washing machines on British television.

## Before Ocean

Dunn taught himself music, he told *ST/Amiga Format* in 1989, although he had taken a course in "Performance Music and Technology" at college, and by then he had been writing music for four or five years, "not always for sale and not always professionally". His first published work was the music and sound effects for *Subterranea*, a game on Hewson's budget label Rack-It, which *ZZAP!64* reviewed in 1988. His interest was always as much in the computer as the music. He told *Retro Gamer*'s *Commodore 64 Book* that he "was at college studying music and programming, and I combined the two things", and that he had meant to go on to university a year later, "but I never went."

## Olympic Challenge, Operation Wolf and RoboCop

His first Ocean game was *Daley Thompson's Olympic Challenge* (1988). Besides the in-game music, the game came with an audio tape. Dunn recorded its tune, "The Challenge", with a Korg M1, a mixer and a drum machine, and the vocal sample, he said, was Gary Bracey, who ran Ocean's software development. The tune had a life outside the game: when Keith Chegwin's television programme *Chegwin Checks It Out* visited Ocean, Dunn was filmed with Chegwin miming to it, and the programme used the clip in its opening titles every week. *CRASH* liked the Spectrum version's "great Jonathon Dunn title tune and tunelets between events, plus a separate audio tape".

*CRASH*'s Christmas 1988 issue reviewed two more Ocean arcade licences with his music. On the 128K Spectrum *Operation Wolf* played "typically atmospheric Jonathan Dunn music" on its title screen, though the reviewer added that it "admittedly is a bit like that on *Daley Thompson's Olympic Challenge*"; the review's summary box praised "an excellent Jonathan Dunn title tune". *RoboCop*, which *CRASH* gave 92%, had a 128K title tune that *Retro Gamer* later called "well known", and on the C64 a "much deeper and menacingly resounding SID chip arrangement". When *CRASH* started a readers' Top 10 Music chart in February 1989, Dunn had two entries, *RoboCop* and *Operation Wolf*.

The work came at speed. In 1989 he told *ST/Amiga Format* that he was given roughly two weeks for each tune and would have liked much longer. On the 16-bit machines he had around 40K for music, much of it taken up by samples, and for *[Chase H.Q.](/vault/games/chase-hq)* he had, for the first time, been sent the arcade producer's music score to convert.

## Batman: The Movie and the 16-bit years

Dunn wrote the Amiga music for *[Batman: The Movie](/vault/games/batman-the-movie)* (1989); Matthew Cannon wrote the Commodore 64 version's. When *ZZAP!*'s readers voted in August 1990, Dunn's Amiga *Batman: The Movie* came top of their Amiga music chart, ahead of [David Whittaker](/vault/people/david-whittaker)'s *Xenon 2* and *Shadow of the Beast*. That is a readers' chart, not an award, but it shows how his music was heard at the time.

He stayed on Ocean's film licences. *ZZAP!* reported in 1991 that "veteran Ocean music man Jonathan Dunn" was scoring *Terminator 2*, and *The One* credited him with the music for Ocean's Amiga *The Addams Family* in 1992. His tunes were distinctive enough that *ZZAP!* printed a reader's program, "The Jolly Jon Dunn Music Player", that pulled his music out of Ocean's C64 games and played it.

## RoboCop on the Game Boy

The best-known Dunn tune comes from the Game Boy *RoboCop*. He wrote its chord riff, he told *Time Extension* in 2020, at the piano in his mother's restaurant in Preston, after it had closed for the night. An advertising executive heard it when his son was playing the game, and the tune ended up in a television advert for Ariston washing machines. In 2012 it resurfaced in a track by the American rapper Lil B.

## After Ocean

Dunn stayed at Ocean after Infogrames bought it in 1996, and according to public reference works left in 2000. He later worked in managerial roles in the games industry, including as an executive producer, and around 2014 moved into casino gaming. He has also released house music under the name Soularis. Asked in 2012 for his favourite of his own compositions, he chose the title music of Ocean's *Total Recall*.
