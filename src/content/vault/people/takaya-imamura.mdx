Takaya Imamura spent 32 years drawing Nintendo's science fiction. He designed Captain Falcon for *F-Zero* in 1990 and Fox McCloud's team for *Star Fox* in 1993, was art director on *[Star Fox 64](/vault/games/star-fox-64)*, and gave *The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask* its strangest resident, the map-seller Tingle. He came to Nintendo with a manga under his arm, and when he left in 2021 he went back to drawing one.

## Joining Nintendo

Imamura joined Nintendo in 1989. He brought manga he had drawn to his interview, and told the French site *Full Frontal* in 2022 that it impressed Shigeru Miyamoto; Nintendo, he said, was looking for artists who were "not too much influenced by manga and anime art", so that its games could reach a wide audience.

## F-Zero and Star Fox

His first game was *F-Zero* (1990) for the Super Famicom, where he worked as a graphic designer and created its pilot hero, Captain Falcon. He stayed with the series for years: he was chief designer on *[F-Zero X](/vault/games/f-zero-x)* for the Nintendo 64, and in 2002 *Edge* reported that, on the GameCube *F-Zero* that became *F-Zero GX*, Nagoshi's team at Sega would do the programming while Nintendo, Miyamoto and Imamura among them, looked after the artistic direction.

For *[Star Fox](/vault/games/star-fox)* (1993), built with the British programmers of [Argonaut](/vault/companies/argonaut), Miyamoto "enlisted the help of artwork designer Takaya Imamura, who had previously designed the characters for Nintendo's space-themed racer F-Zero", as *Retro Gamer* put it. He designed the parts of the game that were not drawn in polygons, Fox McCloud and his team among them, and the game's overall atmosphere.

## Star Fox 64 and Majora's Mask

On *Star Fox 64* (1997), known in Europe as *Lylat Wars*, Imamura was art director. Germany's *Club Nintendo* introduced the game's creative team as three people: Miyamoto as producer, Imamura for design and a third member for story and concept. At the E3 show that year Imamura and Miyamoto talked to *Nintendo Power* about the game. Asked what made Nintendo's EAD group special, Imamura answered: "Mr. Miyamoto's supervision. He doesn't involve himself with everything, but he knows when something should be done a certain way and he points that out." He later called *Star Fox 64* "the game of my life", because he had been so involved in making it.

He was art director again on *The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask* (2000). There he designed Tingle, the game's map-seller, after the director, Yoshiaki Koizumi, asked for a character who sold maps.

## Producer, director, supervisor

In his later years at Nintendo Imamura produced *Star Fox: Assault* and *Star Fox Command*, and directed *Steel Diver* and *Tank Troopers*. He also supervised later *F-Zero* and *Star Fox* games, and the *Super Smash Bros.* games for 3DS and Wii U and *Super Smash Bros. Ultimate*.

## After Nintendo

Imamura retired in January 2021. "Today is my last day coming to work," he posted, with a photograph of the empty office, and he wrote that he was tired after 32 years. He became a professor at the International Professional University of Technology in Osaka. His first manga, *Omega 6*, about two bounty hunters searching space for a planet to replace Earth, came out in French from Omaké Books in 2022. The studio Happymeal turned it into a game, *Omega 6: The Triangle Stars*, with Imamura as art director; it was released in Japan in July 2024 for the Switch and PC, and in the West in 2025. He told *Time Extension* that "it would have been impossible to make this game at Nintendo".
