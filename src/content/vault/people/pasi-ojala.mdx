**Pasi Ojala**, also known as **Albert**, explains the [Commodore 64](/vault/systems/commodore-64) through effects, algorithms and working implementations. His signed **Demo Corner** articles in [C=Hacking](/vault/magazines/c-hacking) examine display techniques; his **[pucrunch](/vault/tools/pucrunch)** work tackles a different constraint, the cost of storing and unpacking data.

Both kinds of work make the conditions visible. A raster effect depends on where the display is in its cycle. A compressed program depends on the memory and code needed to restore it.

## Showing the mechanism

The sprite-stretching article in *C=Hacking* 5 identifies its timing as PAL-specific while explaining that the principles also matter on NTSC machines. That qualification is part of the method, not a footnote to discard when copying the code.

Later Demo Corner pieces discuss **DYCP**, where individual letters move at different heights, and **tech-tech** effects. These names describe results; the articles examine the representation and timed work that produce them. An effect with more independently moving characters than the hardware has sprites needs a different way to build the picture. (*C=Hacking* 5–7.)

## Compression with a small destination

Ojala’s pucrunch documentation describes a hybrid of **LZ77**-style references to earlier data and **run-length encoding** for repeated values. The compressor can perform substantial work on its host machine, but the decompressor must fit and run on the target.

That is why the smallest compressed file is not automatically the most useful result. The destination also needs the unpacker, working space and enough time to recover the data. The documentation explains those choices rather than promising the best ratio for every input.

The site’s revision history and the compression articles in *C=Hacking* 16 and 17 make the development traceable. They are Ojala’s own technical accounts, so they support statements about his implementation and reasoning; they are not independent evidence for a broader claim of priority.

His work connects [demoscene](/vault/communities/demo-scene) effects with everyday development concerns. In both, understanding the limit is what makes a technique reusable: another programmer needs to know not only that it worked, but under which conditions.
