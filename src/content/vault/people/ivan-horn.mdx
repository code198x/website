When *Your Sinclair* previewed Ocean's Spectrum *Operation Wolf* in July 1988, it singled out the artist: "The graphics, by a very talented young man called Ivan Horn, are frankly stunning, and as true to the original graphics as you can get." The game was a conversion of Taito's arcade shooting gallery, and that Christmas it was the best-selling game in Britain. Horn drew it with the programmer [Andrew Deakin](/vault/people/andrew-deakin), and the two worked as a pair for most of six years at [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software), "Ivan in charge of graphics, and Andrew does all the code", as *Sinclair User* put it.

## Fast facts

- **Role:** Spectrum graphic artist at Ocean and its Imagine label, in Manchester; earlier, author of budget games.
- **Usual partner:** Andrew Deakin, programmer.
- **Before Ocean:** *Labyrinthion* (Budgie, 1986, with Deakin); *Megabucks* (Firebird, 1987).
- **At Ocean:** *Athena* (1987), *Combat School* (1987), *Operation Wolf* (1988), *Rambo III* (1988), *Renegade III: The Final Chapter* (1989), *Operation Thunderbolt* (1989), *RoboCop 2* (1990), *The Addams Family* (1992).

## Budget games first

Horn's name first appears in *CRASH* in August 1986, as joint author with Deakin of *Labyrinthion*, a £2.99 maze game from the budget label Budgie that the magazine gave 60%. Early in 1987 he was the sole credited author of *Megabucks*, a £1.99 game in Firebird's Silver Range. Its hero, Rock Carrington, has to work his way through the trap-filled mansion of his late uncle, Professor Maxbillion III, to claim his inheritance. *Sinclair User* and *CRASH* both reviewed it under his name.

By the summer of 1988, *Sinclair User* reported, Horn and Deakin had been working for Ocean full time for about a year. One of their first jobs there was *Athena*, an arcade conversion. Ocean's artist Mark Jones later recalled that he had started on mock-ups for *Athena* before it had a programmer, and that "Ivan Horn and Andrew Deakin took on that project in the end". They then joined [Mike Lamb](/vault/people/mike-lamb) on Konami's *Combat School*, which *CRASH* gave 93% at Christmas 1987; [Jonathan Dunn](/vault/people/jonathan-dunn) and David Whittaker were also on the team.

## Operation Wolf and the arcade conversions

*Operation Wolf* set the pattern for the pair's work. Ocean shipped an arcade machine over from Japan, and the team played it, videotaped it and drew from it. The game was written on Atari STs and moved to the Spectrum afterwards, which left the whole of the Spectrum's memory for the game itself. The Spectrum can give each 8×8 block of the screen only two colours, so a busy, moving scene in many colours produces [attribute clash](/vault/techniques/colour-clash). The Spectrum *Operation Wolf* mostly did without colour in the action. *CRASH* noted that "the sprites, despite being monochrome, are very well drawn and animated", and *Your Sinclair* accepted "a lack of colour in the Speccy version, but we're used to that, right? What colour there is, is nicely employed and appropriate." *CRASH* made it a Smash at 91%, and *Operation Wolf* and Ocean's *RoboCop* were first and second in the Gallup charts that Christmas.

*Rambo III*, the film licence that followed, was credited in *CRASH* to "Andrew Deakin, graphics by Ivan Horn, music by Jonathan Dunn", and disappointed: "After the excellent *Operation Wolf* and *RoboCop* this is a bit of a disappointment." The pair recovered with *Renegade III: The Final Chapter*, which *Sinclair User* said they took "from ideas on paper to tapes on high street shelves in only 2½ months", again developing on STs. *CRASH* opened its 91% review: "After the disappointment of *Rambo III* Andrew Deakin and Ivan Horn have returned to form in considerable style."

Taito's sequel came next. *CRASH* reviewed *Operation Thunderbolt*, credited to Deakin and Horn, at Christmas 1989, and gave it 91% as well: "Again, the detailed monochrome accurately recreates the feel of the coin-op."

## RoboCop 2 and The Addams Family

In 1990 the pair spent most of the year on *RoboCop 2*. Programming began in June, and *CRASH* reported that the touches meant to capture the feel of the film were added after "Andrew and graphic artist Ivan Horn had seen the film". Their last Spectrum game together was *The Addams Family* in 1992, previewed by *CRASH* as the work of "your friendly neighbourhood programmer, Andrew Deakin, and his arty sidekick, Ivan Horn".

## Named in the game and in the letters page

Ocean's staff sometimes hid themselves in their games. *The Vindicator* (1988) contained a list of names, arranged by floor, that were anagrams of the Ocean team; *CRASH* and *Your Sinclair* printed readers' solutions, among them "Ron H Vain" for Ivan Horn.

In May 1989 a *CRASH* reader, Richard Dobbs of Humberside, sent in his own top 15 of the people behind the games Ocean and Imagine had published since 1987. At number three, behind Mike Lamb and Andrew Deakin, he put:

> 3 IVAN HORN – graphic artist for *Operation Wolf*, *Rambo 3* and *Combat School*.

The magazine called the list "a timely reminder of the work of the programmers, graphic artists and musicians who wrote the games for Ocean". Horn was one of five graphic artists on it, with Dawn Drake, Mark R. Jones, William Harbison and Alison Jeftha. In 1988 he was photographed with the rest of the Ocean team; the picture appears in *The History of Ocean Software*.
