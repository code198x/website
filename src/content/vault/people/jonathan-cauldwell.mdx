## Overview
Jonathan Cauldwell arrived the way the magazines advertised that you could:
by sending a game in. *CRASH* ran his work on its **PowerTape** covertape in
February 1990, in the same breath as its standing invitation — "if you've
written a game you think is completely wizzo why not share it with the Speccy
[world]".

Thirty years later he wrote the tool that most new Spectrum games are built
with.

## Egghead
The *Egghead* series is what the covertape route produced. *CRASH* trailed
*Egghead 2* in November 1990 as its author's "first step into the software
publishing world", and by December was announcing "he's back in an awesome
arcade adventure by Jonathan Cauldwell!! It's ten months since the first
Egghead game".

*Your Sinclair* was the more quotable: his work was "just about the most
spankily-programmed **reader game** we've seen for quite a while. Slick,
smooth…"

*Egghead* appears on **405 pages** of the Spectrum press held here — more
coverage than many commercial series managed, for games that arrived through
the reader route rather than a publisher.

## Arcade Game Designer
[AGD](/vault/tools/arcade-game-designer) is Cauldwell's authoring system, and
the direct modern descendant of [The Quill](/vault/tools/the-quill) and the
[Graphic Adventure Creator](/vault/tools/graphic-adventure-creator): a tool that
lets somebody who cannot write Z80 assembly ship a real Spectrum game.

*ZX Spectrum Gamer* calls it "the superb Arcade Game Designer" and reports that
it "has been the glue behind many a recent Spectrum homebrew title" — naming
*Zombo* by Malcolm Kirk — with **a Next version reportedly in development**. The
same magazine's *Aeon* credits it directly: "made mostly in Jonathan Cauldwell's
Arcade Game Designer".

## The shape of it
This is the Vault's cleanest closed loop. The magazines of the 1980s ran two
things at once: a route in for readers who wrote games, and reviews of the
tools that let readers write games without learning assembly.

Cauldwell used the first, and then built the second. The reader who got in
through the covertape is the reason the current generation has a door.

⚠ The Arcade Game Designer material comes from *ZX Spectrum Gamer*, a modern
magazine held in this library but outside the period archive. Reported progress
on a Next version is the kind of claim that decays.
