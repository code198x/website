In February 1990 *CRASH* put a platform game called *Egghead* on its covertape and asked who its author thought he was. The answer was an 18-year-old, Jonathan Cauldwell, who had been writing and selling his own Spectrum games since 1987 under the name Graphix Wizards. He duplicated the tapes himself, designed and photocopied the inlays, and posted copies to clubs and fanzines for review. "It's hard work," he told the magazine, "but it's been great fun so far!" He hoped for a career in software. He kept writing Spectrum games long after the magazines had gone, and eighteen years after *Egghead* he released the tool that hundreds of other people have since used to write theirs.

## Fast facts

- **Machine:** [ZX Spectrum](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum), and later the Amstrad CPC and other 8-bit computers.
- **First label:** Graphix Wizards, his own, from 1987.
- **Best-known games:** the *Egghead* series, beginning with *Egghead* on *CRASH*'s covertape in 1990.
- **Covertape games:** for *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)*, *[Your Sinclair](/vault/magazines/your-sinclair)* and, by his own account, *Sinclair User*.
- **Tools:** Platform Game Designer (2005) and [Arcade Game Designer](/vault/tools/arcade-game-designer) (2008).

## The covertape years

*CRASH* had been asking for games like his. Its December 1989 issue told readers: "If you've written a game you think is completely wizzo why not share it with the Speccy world??" If a game "passes the reviewers' test", the magazine promised, "we'll put it on the Power Tape so thousands can enjoy it!" *Egghead* followed two months later, a 40-screen platform game "in a style not unlike Manic Miner", as Cauldwell later described it. *CRASH* thought he had "started the same way the Darlings did before CodeMasters", a reference to the [Darling brothers](/vault/people/darling-brothers), who had founded [Codemasters](/vault/companies/codemasters).

The sequel came at Christmas. "It's ten months since the first Egghead game appeared on the Powertape label," *CRASH* wrote in December 1990, "and due to overwhelming popular demand, programmer Jonathan Cauldwell has brought the little fella back to life in a smashing game that's bigger, better and harder than the original."

*Your Sinclair* took up his games too. In November 1992 its covertape carried *Haunted House*, "just about the most spankily-programmed reader game we've seen for quite a while", and the magazine reported that Cauldwell had "had his rather large talents recognised by the industry" and was writing games for a company called Beyond Belief. Through 1993 *Your Sinclair* ran more of his games, *Pipework* in July and *Megablast* in August, introducing him as "a programmer whose name you'll recognise". By September it noted that he "recklessly insists on writing new games for the Speccy at a rate of knots".

## After the magazines

Cauldwell did not stop when the magazines did. "After the final Spectrum magazine closed down in the summer of 1993 I continued to write games for the machine," he told *Retro Gamer* in 2005. Early in 2003 Simon Ullyatt asked on the World of Spectrum forums for programmers to supply a new publisher of 8-bit games, Cronosoft. Cauldwell had just finished *Egghead in Space*, the third game in the series, and it became Cronosoft's first release. Cronosoft published more of his games on tape at budget prices, among them the *R-Type*-style shooter *More Tea, Vicar?*, *Gloop!* and *Fun Park*, a 16K game inspired by *Theme Park* that was being ported to the Amstrad CPC in 2004. Others, such as *Jurbomania*, a *Rally-X*-style driving game that ran even on a 16K Spectrum, he put up for download on his own website. *Retro Gamer* called him "probably the best-known active home-brew programmer currently around" and wrote that he "deserves a medal, for services to the Spectrum scene". Citing the World of Spectrum archive, he said in 2005 that only the Shaw brothers had written more Spectrum games.

## Game designers

In mid-2005 Cauldwell released Platform Game Designer, in two versions: one for games of flip-screen rooms like *[Jet Set Willy](/vault/games/jet-set-willy)*, the other for single-screen games like *[Manic Miner](/vault/games/manic-miner)*. *Retro Gamer* called it his most successful recent product.

[Arcade Game Designer](/vault/tools/arcade-game-designer), first released in 2008, went further. It lets an author who has never written Z80 assembly build an arcade game from sprites, scenery blocks and screens, with short scripts for what everything does, and turns the result into a game that runs on a real Spectrum. Cauldwell gives it away and takes no royalties on the games made with it. In 2017 the free magazine *ZX Spectrum Gamer* called it "the superb Arcade Game Designer by Jonathan Cauldwell" and said it "has been the glue behind many a recent Spectrum homebrew titles", reporting that a version for the [ZX Spectrum Next](/vault/systems/zx-spectrum-next) was in development. The magazine's own cover game in 2018, *Aeon*, was "made mostly in Jonathan Cauldwell's Arcade Game Designer". Its Windows successor, the Multi-Platform Arcade Game Designer, builds games for the Spectrum, the Amstrad CPC, the MSX, the BBC Micro and other machines. The Spectrum Computing database credits AGD with more than 400 games.
