## Overview
Bob Pape wrote the Spectrum conversion of [*R-Type*](/vault/games/r-type),
which is the game people reach for when they want to argue that the Spectrum
could hold its own against arcade hardware. *Sinclair User* named him in
October 1988, while the game was still in development, in a feature that ran
his biography alongside the preview.

## What the period record says
The *Sinclair User* piece is short and worth having in full, because it is one
of the few contemporary accounts of how someone arrived at this work:

- He left school at **16** and had been involved in programming in one form or another since.
- He started as a **computer operator**, across several firms, picking up ideas along the way.
- He came to the Spectrum after owning a **TRS-80** and a **BBC Micro**.
- He had already written the Spectrum version of ***Rampage***.

No formal training, no games-industry entry route, and a job title that in 1988
meant loading tapes and minding a machine room. That is the ordinary shape of a
career in this period, and it is worth recording precisely because the
extraordinary result at the end of it invites a more romantic story.

## R-Type
The conversion reviewed at **92%** in *CRASH* (December 1988), 93% in *C&VG*,
90% in *Sinclair User* and *The Games Machine*, and 9/10 in *Your Sinclair*.
*Your Sinclair* later scored it **98%** in retrospectives in March 1990 and
January 1991 — above the mark its own reviewer gave at launch, which is a rare
direction for a game's reputation to travel.

## Rampage
The earlier conversion did not fare as well: **69%** from *CRASH* in February
1988, 6/10 from both *Sinclair User* and *Your Sinclair*. The gap between
*Rampage* and *R-Type* inside a single year is the interesting thing about it —
the same programmer, on the same machine, with a licence that suited the
hardware rather than fighting it.
