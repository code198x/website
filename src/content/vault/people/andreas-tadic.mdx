Andreas Tadic wrote the code for most of [Team17](/vault/companies/team17)'s first hits, and he did it from Sweden. In July 1992 *Amiga Power* introduced him and the artist Rico Holmes with a line about geography: "Sweden is a long way from Croydon, or Wakefield for that matter, but that's where you'll find the second of Team 17's new development duos." By then the pair had made *[Alien Breed](/vault/games/alien-breed)* and *Project-X*, and Team17's first full-price games had gone to number 2 and number 1 in the Gallup Amiga chart.

## Fast facts

- **From:** Sweden, and still working there while writing for Team17.
- **Games:** *Full Contact* (1991), *Alien Breed* (1991), *Project-X* (1992), *Alien Breed Special Edition 92* (1992), *Superfrog* (1993) and *Alien Breed II: The Horror Continues* (1993), all for Team17; before those, *Miami Chase* for Codemasters.
- **Worked with:** Peter Tuleby, another Swedish programmer, the artist Rico Holmes and the musician Allister Brimble.
- **Before games:** music software for the Amiga, sold through 17-Bit Software's public-domain library.

## From 17-Bit's music disks to Team 7

Tadic's name reached British Amiga owners before any of his games did. In 1989 17-Bit Software, the Wakefield public-domain library that later became half of Team17, advertised a "music creation pack featuring music composing utilities by Brian Postma and Andreas Tadic", both of which, the advert claimed, rivalled *SoundTracker*. In June 1990 *Amiga Computing*'s public-domain column rated "Andreas Tadic's Game Music Creator" as "light-years better than the SoundTracker guru-alikes". It could take notes from a MIDI keyboard, or turn the Amiga's own keyboard into a two-octave piano.

Simon Parkin's 2016 history of Team17 tells how Martyn Brown of 17-Bit put Tadic in touch with Rico Holmes, whose artwork 17-Bit was already distributing. With Peter Tuleby they called themselves Team 7. *CU Amiga* described Tadic and Tuleby in 1991 as "Swedish coders" who "first worked together on Codemaster's Miami Chase", a budget driving game, before *Full Contact*. When 17-Bit and Team 7 joined up in December 1990, Team 7's three members were part of the new company from the start.

## Full Contact and Alien Breed

Team17's first game, the £9.99 beat-'em-up *Full Contact*, carried Tadic's name as its programmer when *Amiga Action* reviewed it in August 1991. The same month *Amiga Computing* printed a publicity photograph of the development team on the bonnet of a sports car: "Andreas Tadic (programmer), Rico Holmes (artist), Peter Tuleby (programmer) and Martyn Brown (Team 17 leader)". Team17 apologised in the caption for the sunglasses.

*CU Amiga* reported that Tadic and Tuleby had been "hard at work on Alien Breed since August, 1990", with in-game graphics by Holmes, sound by Brimble and an intro by the German public-domain artist Tobias Richter. *Amiga Power*'s review in December 1991 named Tadic and Tuleby as the authors, gave the game 82%, and called it "a very sophisticated game exploiting the ability of the Amiga to its fullest". It entered the Gallup chart at number 2. In January 1992 *Amiga Power* profiled Team17 as a company of ten full-time staff, and named Tadic and Holmes as the best known of them.

## Project-X

*Project-X*, a horizontally scrolling shoot-'em-up for Amigas with a megabyte of memory, came out in the spring of 1992 at £25.99, with Tadic credited as author. *Amiga Power*'s reviewer admired it, "it looks and sounds undeniably beautiful", but found its "insane, unfair difficulty level" hard to forgive. The buyers disagreed. The next issue's chart page reported that *Project-X* had gone to number 2 after two days on sale and to number 1 after eight, and marvelled that a game "from an almost-unknown (and very new) programming/publishing set-up" had done it with word of mouth instead of a big advertising campaign. *The One*'s round-up the same summer called it "THE best shoot'em-up on the Amiga".

## Superfrog and Alien Breed II

Tadic and Holmes then made something softer. *Superfrog*, a platform game, was started in the middle of 1991, according to *The One*'s preview in December 1992, which described its makers as "the same Sweden-based duo that produced Alien Breed and Project-X", with Martyn Brown as project manager and Brimble on sound. Tadic was already thinking further ahead: "Super Frog 2 on the A1200 is going to be amazing," he told *CU Amiga* in February 1993. No sequel appeared. *Amiga Power* reviewed *Superfrog* in 1993, crediting Tadic, Holmes and Brimble, and gave it 78%.

Their next job was *Alien Breed II: The Horror Continues*, made in public. From June 1993 *The One* ran a monthly Team 17 diary on the game's development, and Tadic appeared in it every month: writing a new map editor, being taken off it to rework *Project-X* for a budget re-release, then pulling the whole game together in time for its deadline. *Amiga Power* gave *Alien Breed II* 81% in December 1993.

## After the Breed games

Tadic and Holmes did not write the next *Breed* game. *CU Amiga* reported in August 1994 that they had "taken a backseat" on *Alien Breed: Tower Assault*, and Martyn Brown later told *Amiga Action* that "none of the original team worked on it". Instead the pair started *King of Thieves*, which *The One* covered in a Work in Progress feature in the autumn of 1994 with a planned release at Easter 1995. It combined arcade sections with the conquest of a map; Team17's Alan Bunker described it to *Amiga Power* as "put together by the same people who did Alien Breed".

*King of Thieves* was never released. *Amiga Action* reported in March 1995 that it had been dropped and that Holmes and Tadic were due to start a new, secret project. In November 1996 *CU Amiga* asked Brown why little had been seen from the two after their early hits. "Sadly, the next project after AB2, King Of Thieves, didn't work out," he said, putting it down to complacency after two very successful years, "even though they are both still very capable." Public sources say little about Tadic's work after that.

The Team17 he had worked for changed in the same years. From 1995 it was distributed by [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software), *[Worms](/vault/games/worms)* went out on many formats, and it had a long public row with *Amiga Power*, told in [Amiga Power versus Team17](/vault/culture/amiga-power-versus-team17).
