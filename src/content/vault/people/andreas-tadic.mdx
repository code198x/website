## Overview

Andreas Tadic wrote *Alien Breed*, *Project X* and *Superfrog* with Rico Holmes for **[Team17](/vault/companies/team17)** — three of the games that defined what an Amiga action game looked like in the early nineties. *Amiga Power* introduced the pair by pointing at the map:

> **Sweden is a long way from Croydon, or Wakefield for that matter, but that's where you find the second of Team 17's new development duos.** *Superfrog*, as their game is known, is being developed by **Andreas Tadic and Rico Holmes**, both of *Alien Breed* and *Project X* fame.

## Fast facts

- **Games:** *Alien Breed*, *Project X*, *Superfrog*.
- **With:** Rico Holmes, as a two-person team.
- **For:** Team17, of Wakefield.
- **Based:** Sweden.

## A British studio that was not all British

Team17 is remembered as a Yorkshire company, and it was — but *Amiga Power*'s framing gives the real shape: a Wakefield publisher running **development duos**, one of which lived in Sweden.

That worked because of what an Amiga game needed. Two people, a shared understanding of the hardware, and disks in the post. There was no need for the programmer to be in the building, and by 1992 there was no need for him to be in the country.

Team17 itself came out of the same continental connection — the company was formed from a merger involving Swedish demo-scene and software people, and its house style shows it. **Fast, technically showy, sprite-heavy Amiga games with a demo-scene sense of what the hardware can be made to do** is not a British tradition; it is a Scandinavian one, arriving in Yorkshire.

## What Alien Breed and Project X were for

Both games are demonstrations as much as games. *Project X* is a horizontally scrolling shooter of the kind the Amiga was supposedly bad at, done at a speed and a sprite count that argued otherwise. *Alien Breed* is a top-down two-player shooter that looks and sounds like a film licence Team17 did not have to pay for.

The common thread is **using an Amiga as hard as it can be used** and making that the selling point — the demo scene's value system applied to commercial games, which is a fair description of Team17's whole catalogue.
