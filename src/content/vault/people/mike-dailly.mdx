Mike Dailly was there at the start of both of [DMA Design](/vault/companies/dma-design)'s famous games. By his own account, *[Lemmings](/vault/games/lemmings)* began with a few seconds of animation he made to win an argument about how small a sprite could be. In the mid-1990s he wrote the prototype city engines that grew into *[Grand Theft Auto](/vault/games/grand-theft-auto)*. Since then he has worked on game-making tools, and in recent years he has become one of the most active developers for the [ZX Spectrum Next](/vault/systems/zx-spectrum-next), writing its best-known emulator and a new version of *Lemmings* for it.

## Fast facts

- **Start:** one of DMA Design's first employees, from Dundee's Kingsway Amateur Computer Club.
- **At DMA:** programmer on *Lemmings*, chief Super NES programmer on *Lemmings 2*, lead programmer on the early *Grand Theft Auto* prototypes. He left in 1999.
- **Later:** Visual Sciences, Realtime Worlds, and YoYo Games, where he was lead developer of *[GameMaker](/vault/tools/game-maker): Studio*.
- **Spectrum Next:** the CSpect emulator; *Lemmings*; ports including *Super Crate Box* and *Mercenary*.
- **Award:** lifetime achievement at the Scottish Games Awards, 2023.

## Dundee and DMA

Dave Jones founded DMA Design in Dundee, and its first staff were his friends from the Kingsway Amateur Computer Club: Russell Kay, Steve Hammond and Mike Dailly. By November 1990 *RAZE* listed Dailly as co-programmer, with [Dave Jones](/vault/people/dave-jones), of the Atari ST version of *Lemmings*. The same issue found him converting Psygnosis's *[Shadow of the Beast](/vault/games/shadow-of-the-beast)* to NEC's PC Engine CD-ROM for the Japanese market. He called the console "gorgeous" and its development system "tacky", but had still managed 17 layers of parallax scrolling on it.

## Lemmings

DMA's own accounts of where *Lemmings* came from do not agree, and the [Lemmings](/vault/games/lemmings) entry sets them side by side. The earliest, in *The One* in January 1991, credits a Deluxe Paint animation by Gary Timmons. Later accounts put Dailly at the centre. In *Edge* in 2001 the story was an argument between Dailly and Scott Johnston: Johnston said a character could go no smaller than 16 pixels, and Dailly thought he could manage eight.

Dailly told his version to *Retro Gamer* in 2007. The team was working on *Walker*, which "featured lots of running men", and he argued that they could be made smaller:

> I had maintained that they could be 8 pixels tall (rather than 16). So I created a small animation that demonstrated the new size and showed it to the rest of the guys. However, as luck would have it I had created them walking over a landscape and had added a couple of deaths for them. This struck them all as pretty funny, and from then on the ideas on how to use them came thick and fast.

Gary Timmons then reworked the walk and animated the other actions. Dailly explained that the lemmings' colours came from the weakest of the target machines. The team wanted the game to look the same everywhere, and the PC's EGA graphics limited the choice to green hair with blue clothes or blue hair with green clothes. They preferred the green hair. He started the ST conversion, which Brian Watson finished.

For *Lemmings 2: The Tribes* Dailly was DMA's chief Super NES programmer. He began the conversion early in 1992, and *SNES Force* published his diary of it in January 1994. The Super NES's hardware could not help with the core of the game, lemmings walking over a landscape that they changed as they went. "This was one of the hardest sections to do as it can't use any of the nice SNES hardware," he wrote. "The processor is put through hell."

## Grand Theft Auto

By the mid-1990s Dailly was lead programmer at DMA. According to *Retro Gamer*'s 2008 history of the series, he had written an engine called Rotation that drew isometric cityscapes from a camera the player could turn. It became the first *Grand Theft Auto* prototype. Then Bullfrog released *Syndicate Wars*, and Dailly decided his city looked too similar. *Edge*'s 2006 account adds that he had been impressed by the perspective in Sega's *Clockwork Knight*, and a chance remark about top-down racing games gave him the next idea: "It occurred to me that although I had a side-on engine, all I needed to do was add a floor, and it could be an above engine." The result was a top-down city of simple block buildings. Gary Penn, who joined DMA in 1996, remembered Dailly as "constantly creating these interesting technology showcases, one of which was this top-down view of a world full of simple boxy polygon structures."

The game was pitched to BMG as *Race'n'Chase* and came out in 1997 as *Grand Theft Auto*. Eighteen months into development the whole city was rebuilt. "Mike Dailly came up with a new technique to draw 24bit colour tiles instead of the low-res ones we were using," one of the team told *Edge*, and every tile and graphic in the game had to be redrawn.

Dailly left DMA in 1999, before Take-Two bought the studio and moved it to Edinburgh, where it became [Rockstar North](/vault/companies/rockstar-north).

## Game-making tools

After DMA, Dailly worked at Visual Sciences, the studio set up by his DMA colleague Russell Kay, and at Realtime Worlds, which Dave Jones founded in 2002. From 2010 to 2018 he was lead developer of *GameMaker: Studio* at YoYo Games, the company that makes the [GameMaker](/vault/tools/game-maker) engine. In November 2023 he received the lifetime achievement award at the Scottish Games Awards.

## The Spectrum Next

Dailly wrote CSpect, an emulator of the [ZX Spectrum Next](/vault/systems/zx-spectrum-next), to develop his own Next software, and other developers adopted it. *Next Magazine* called it the "go-to emulator". He withdrew it from public use in late 2025, then made it available again after the community reacted. CSpect has also been used as an engine to put Next games on Steam. "I don't like Z80; it's clunky and annoying," he told *Next Magazine* in 2025, "yet the machine is such fun to write for."

His Next software includes a new *Lemmings*, written from scratch for the machine rather than converted. Its code is on GitHub, copyright Dailly 2017–2020, with permission to "use any and all code in whatever manner you wish". The original game's graphics and levels are excluded, and a converter lets owners of the Windows version use their own copies. He has also written *Kingpin*, a first-person shooter in the style of *Wolfenstein 3D*, and he was working on a Next port of *Super Crate Box* at the end of 2025. Over Christmas 2025 he took apart the Spectrum version of *Mercenary* with SkoolKit, a set of tools for disassembling Z80 programs, and rebuilt it as *Mercenary Enhanced* for the Next. "I've always felt the Spectrum version should have been the best version out there," he told the magazine.
