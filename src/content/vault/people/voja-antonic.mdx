**Voja Antonić** designed the [Galaksija](/vault/systems/galaksija), the computer a Belgrade magazine taught its readers to build in January 1984. He was not an engineer by trade. When *Svet kompjutera* introduced him to its readers in October 1984, it gave his profession as film editor, with ten years at the mixing desk in TV Belgrade's editing rooms, and added that he was "much better known as the designer of the first domestic computer to enter people's homes". The Galaksija, it said, "grew out of a hobby".

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1952 (he was 32 in October 1984).
- **Day job in 1984:** film editor at TV Belgrade.
- **Known for:** the [Galaksija](/vault/systems/galaksija), published in *Računari u vašoj kući*, January 1984.
- **Wrote for:** [*Računari*](/vault/magazines/racunari) and *Svet kompjutera*, where he ran the hardware column "Zlatne ruke" ("Golden hands").
- **Later:** designer of Hackaday conference badges, 2016 to 2022.

## A hobby since 1975

*Svet kompjutera* said Antonić had been studying microprocessors since 1975, and that the knowledge let him design complex automatic data-processing systems on his own as well as microcomputers. Later accounts add that he studied at Belgrade's Faculty of Dramatic Arts in the late 1970s, and that one of his first microprocessor projects was a machine that played Conway's Game of Life on a 16×16 grid of red LEDs.

## The Galaksija

In the summer of 1983 the popular-science magazine *Galaksija* commissioned Dejan Ristanović to write a special edition about home computers. Ristanović later recalled that by the end of August the chance arose to add a computer readers could build themselves, "designed by Voja Antonić". The edition, *Računari u vašoj kući* ("Computers in Your Home"), reached kiosks in the last days of December 1983, dated January 1984. Its editorial said the Galaksija had been designed by "one man, Voja Antonić, in a little over a month", and that barely more than six months had passed between the first idea and the first machines appearing.

The design used a Zilog Z80A and a handful of common chips, and had no video chip: the processor drew the 32×16 text screen itself, as Sinclair's [ZX81](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx81) did. Antonić's own assembly listing of the ROM, version 28, is dated 3 January 1984. His build article set out the schematic, the board and a network of Yugoslav and Austrian suppliers for the parts, and it was translated into English in 2018 for *PoC||GTFO* Volume 2.

Readers took him up on it. By October 1984 *Svet kompjutera* reported nearly 6,000 Galaksijas in Yugoslav homes. Ristanović's own later count is that more than 8,000 readers built one. The [Galaksija entry](/vault/systems/galaksija) tells the machine's story in full.

## Writing about hardware

Antonić was one of the group Ristanović names as starting *Svet kompjutera* in the autumn of 1984, and its first issue, in October, introduced him alongside Stanko Popović and Andrija Kolundžić on its "who's who" page. His name appears on its masthead as a specialist contributor through the late 1980s. He wrote a Galaksija machine-code monitor for the magazine in April 1985, and a run of hardware articles on logic chips, memory maps and the Z80's input and output. By the end of 1986 they appeared under the heading "Zlatne ruke" ("Golden hands"), a do-it-yourself hardware column. In December 1986 it built an EPROM emulator, and in June 1987 the magazine announced that "after a short break, Voja Antonić again brings you Zlatne ruke".

## Badges and later work

Antonić kept designing small, programmable hardware. He designed the badge for the Hackaday Belgrade conference in 2016, and then the badge for that year's Hackaday Superconference: a PIC18 microcontroller driving a matrix of 128 surface-mounted LEDs, which he described himself in a Hackaday article. He designed the retro-computing badge for Hackaday Belgrade 2018, and the virtual 4-bit machine inside the 2022 Superconference badge, a computer whose lights show its registers and memory as it runs.

He also went back to the Galaksija. He released a revised two-layer circuit board with modern key-switch connectors and an extra 5 nF capacitor on the Z80's refresh line, which the picture circuit depends on. He has also written books, among them a guide to critical thinking.
