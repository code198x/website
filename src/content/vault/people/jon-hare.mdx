Jon Hare wanted to be a pop star. In 1994 *[AMIGA POWER](/vault/magazines/amiga-power)* reported that his "ultimate ambition is to cut a single and get it into the Gallup Top 10", and he had started out playing in bands with his school friend Chris Yates. The two of them became [Sensible Software](/vault/companies/sensible-software) instead, with Hare as the artist and designer and Yates as the programmer. *[ZZAP!](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* called them "'jovial' Jon Hare (graphic genius) and 'cuddly' Chris Yates (programming prodigy)". Hare designed *[Sensible Soccer](/vault/games/sensible-soccer)* and *[Cannon Fodder](/vault/games/cannon-fodder)*, and he still got a song out: "War Has Never Been So Much Fun", *Cannon Fodder*'s theme.

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1966, according to public sources.
- **Known as:** "Jops", or "Jovial Jops", in the magazines.
- **Company:** co-founder of [Sensible Software](/vault/companies/sensible-software) (1986–1999), later Tower Studios.
- **Key games:** *[Wizball](/vault/games/wizball)*, *MicroProse Soccer*, *Mega-lo-Mania*, *[Sensible Soccer](/vault/games/sensible-soccer)*, *[Cannon Fodder](/vault/games/cannon-fodder)*, *Sensible World of Soccer*, *Sociable Soccer*.

## Sensible Software

Hare and Yates met at school in Essex and formed a band, which Hare said went through the names Zeus, Hamsterfish, Dark Goose and Touchstone. Yates found work as a Spectrum programmer at LT Software in Basildon, and Hare was brought in to do the graphics. In March 1986 they set up Sensible Software with help from a government enterprise scheme. Hare later counted the start among his best moments: coming back from Ocean in Manchester "with a £1,000 cheque after just signing up Parallax on C64, our first ever game as Sensible Software".

On the Commodore 64 he drew and co-designed *Parallax* and *[Wizball](/vault/games/wizball)* for [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software). For *Wizball*, he and Yates played guitar and bass lines to [Martin Galway](/vault/people/martin-galway), who turned them into the game's music. *MicroProse Soccer* followed, and on the Amiga *International 3D Tennis*, *Mega-lo-Mania* and *Wizkid*.

Football was his subject. In October 1991 he told *[ACE](/vault/magazines/ace-magazine)* what was wrong with *Kick Off 2*: "When you play the game you keep thinking that it would be so much better if certain things didn't happen and other things did." His answer was *Sensible Soccer* (1992), in which the computer runs the nearest player to the ball so that the person holding the joystick only has to decide what to do with it. In the same interview he described the management game, with real players, transfers and national teams, that became *Sensible World of Soccer* in 1994.

*[Cannon Fodder](/vault/games/cannon-fodder)* (1993) was his design too. He described its starting point to *[Edge](/vault/magazines/edge)*: "You take a bit of what Sensible stuff looked like, take Gauntlet, take explosive hardware." He wrote the riff and the singing for its theme song and took them to the musician Richard Joseph to finish. When the poppy on its packaging caused a row, he told *[Amiga Computing](/vault/magazines/amiga-computing)* that Sensible had chosen it "because the poppy symbolised people dying in war and a lot of people die in Cannon Fodder so it seemed appropriate". His next project, *Sex 'n' Drugs 'n' Rock 'n' Roll*, was "a labour of love", *Edge* reported, but after Time Warner dropped it as too risqué no other publisher made an acceptable offer, and it was never released.

## After Sensible

[Codemasters](/vault/companies/codemasters) bought Sensible Software in 1999, and Hare went with it to lead new projects. Looking back in *Edge* in 2000, he said that he and Yates had come through thirteen years, "the last two of which had been living hell", "without ever having had a single argument".

By 2005 he was running Tower Studios, which *Retro Gamer* reported he ran with John Phillips and Mike Montgomery of the [Bitmap Brothers](/vault/companies/bitmap-brothers). Its first work was a set of Java versions of *Sensible Soccer* and *Cannon Fodder* for mobile phones, published by Kuju Wireless. Hare said the deal gave them "the unique opportunity to re-vamp some of our classic old games for a younger audience". The same year he explained to *Edge* what he thought made his football game different: "Sensi recreates football psychologically – the pace makes it more akin to football as a mental activity, as opposed to Pro Evo and FIFA which show you what it looks like on the television." Codemasters then brought him back for a new *Sensible Soccer*, released in April 2006.

He kept playing music too. *Retro Gamer* reported him on stage with period game musicians, playing bass at a 2004 concert and guitar alongside Ben Daglish, Mark Knight and Chris Abbott in 2005.

Public sources record his later work outside the magazines. He worked on a high-definition remake of the Bitmap Brothers' *Speedball 2* (2013), and ran a Kickstarter in 2015 for *Sociable Soccer*, an overhead football game in the *Sensible Soccer* tradition. The campaign did not reach its target, but the game was developed anyway, with the Finnish studio Combo Breaker, reaching Steam Early Access in 2017 and Apple Arcade in 2019. *Sociable Soccer 25*, made by Amuzo Games with Tower Studios, was released on PC in November 2024 and on consoles in 2024 and 2025.
