## Overview

**David Lawson** is the thread running through Liverpool's games industry in the 1980s. He
was senior staff at [Bug-Byte](/vault/companies/bug-byte), a founder of
[Imagine Software](/vault/companies/imagine-software), and a founder of
[Psygnosis](/vault/companies/psygnosis) — three companies in six years, each begun by leaving
the last.

## The sequence

**January 1983 — out of Bug-Byte.** *ACE*, looking back from 1990, records Lawson leaving
Bug-Byte alongside its head programmer Eugene Evans and Mark Butler to form Imagine. *Big K*'s
account has the departures staggered rather than simultaneous, with Butler following a couple
of months later.

**1984 — out of Imagine.** Imagine collapsed in July 1984, publicly and expensively, with the
BBC filming as the bailiffs arrived. By the end of 1987 *ACE* was describing Lawson and
[Ian Hetherington](/vault/people/ian-hetherington) as former Imagine directors now running
Psygnosis.

Both moves follow the same shape: senior people leave a Liverpool company and start a larger
one a few streets away. The [Bug-Byte](/vault/companies/bug-byte) entry records the same
pattern happening twice more — Software Projects took Matthew Smith and *Manic Miner* out of
the same building a year after Imagine did.

## What he is not

⚠ Lawson's name is frequently confused in secondary accounts with other Imagine figures,
because the company's collapse produced far more coverage than its output did. What the
period press states is: senior at Bug-Byte, a founder of Imagine, a founder of Psygnosis.
Specific game credits are not established.

## Why he matters

Liverpool's 1980s games industry is usually told as three separate stories — the pioneer that
failed, the disaster, and the Amiga success. Lawson is evidence that it is one story with the
same people in it.

That continuity is the reason the [Psygnosis](/vault/companies/psygnosis) entry can trace an
unbroken line from Bug-Byte in 1980 to Sony's Studio Liverpool closing in 2012. The line runs
through individuals, not institutions — the companies kept failing, and the people kept
starting new ones within walking distance.
