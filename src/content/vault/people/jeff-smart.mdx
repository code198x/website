In October 1988 *Commodore User* printed the start of a letter from a reader who knew what the editors would think of him. "I guess you are shaking your head while reading the top of this letter, moaning: 'Damn it. Yet another stupid pirate going on my nerves!'" He asked them not to throw it "into your already overfilled dustbin", and signed it "Jeff Smart/TRIAD, West Germany". The magazine printed those few lines and cut the rest. Its reply was two words: "Too late."

Four months earlier the same magazine had written about Smart's work without naming him. He edited *[Illegal](/vault/magazines/illegal)*, a photocopied paper fanzine about the Commodore 64 cracking scene, and for most of its run it was one of the few places where that scene wrote about itself on paper.

## Fast facts

- **Scene name:** Jeff Smart. The scene database CSDb says he took it from the German title of a comic, *Clever & Smart*, whose two heroes are Fred Clever and Jeff Smart.
- **Born:** 1968, according to Triad's member page.
- **Based in:** West Germany.
- **Known for:** Editing *[Illegal](/vault/magazines/illegal)*, January 1986 to December 1991.
- **Scene roles:** Editor, swapper and cracker.
- **Groups:** Listed as a member of [Triad](/vault/groups/triad) from January to October 1988, then the Scouse Cracking Group; the last issue of *Illegal* came out with the group Elite.

## Illegal

Smart started *Illegal* in January 1986 with two pages. By its 26th issue, dated 7 March 1988, it was selling about 400 copies at 1.60 Deutschmarks each, with news of the latest cracks, gossip, interviews and a monthly chart of the cracking groups voted by readers. That February's "Cracker of the Month" placed Fusion first, [Fairlight](/vault/groups/fairlight) second and Triad third. Triad's own profile of Smart says *Illegal* was one of the first magazines to publish charts like these, and singles out a column called "The Story of System 4" as "a causerie of the Scene".

In June 1988 *Commodore User*'s piracy feature, by Mike Pattenden, told British readers about the fanzine. It listed the April issue's contents, "news, interviews with hackers, a report on the Venlo copy party, a review of *Rolling Thunder*, a debate on the relative merits of 64 and Amiga, classified ads, news of the latest cracked games and charts", and said the fanzine "revels in its downright naughtiness". It called *Illegal* "the magazine published by Germany's TRIAD group". Triad was Swedish; the German connection was Smart himself.

## Triad, and after

Smart's time in Triad was short. The scene database CSDb lists him as a member from January to October 1988, and credits issue 28, dated 21 May 1988, to "Jeff Smart and Triad". That autumn's letter to *Commodore User* was signed from Triad. According to Triad's member page, he moved to the Scouse Cracking Group in late September 1988.

The run ended with issue 38, dated 14 December 1991 and subtitled "Judgement Day". Smart produced it with the group Elite after 18 months without an issue, and called it "the final and last issue of ILLEGAL". Its charts named [Ikari](/vault/groups/ikari) the world's best cracking group. The [Illegal](/vault/magazines/illegal) entry has more on the fanzine's contents and what survives of it.

Almost everything known about Smart comes from *Illegal* itself, from *Commodore User*'s two mentions in 1988 and from scene websites and databases compiled later. Those later sources are scene members' own records, not independent reporting.
