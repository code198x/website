## Overview
Tim Gilberts owned and ran [Gilsoft](/vault/companies/gilsoft), the South Wales
company behind [The Quill](/vault/tools/the-quill) and
[PAW](/vault/tools/paw) — between them the tools that most British text
adventures of the 1980s were built with.

*Sinclair User* profiled him in April 1985 and described "a soft spoken
twenty-year old Welshman", a product of the whizz-kid era who had left school
early to get into computing.

## In his own words, 1985
> Gilsoft started mainly as a pocket money venture. I bought myself a Spectrum
> when it came on the market and pretty soon had a full-scale business on my
> hands.

He added that he could have gone to university and ended up working for a large
company, and had no regrets about not doing so.

## What he made
- **Games first.** *CRASH* reviewed a short line of 16K Spectrum games at £5.95 credited to T Gilberts through 1984, including *Styx*.
- **[The Quill](/vault/tools/the-quill)** (1983), written by [Graeme Yeandle](/vault/people/graeme-yeandle) and published by Gilsoft, with conversions to the Commodore 64 and Amstrad.
- **[PAW](/vault/tools/paw)** (1987). *CRASH* credits the Professional Adventure Writer to Tim Gilberts, Graeme Yeandle, Phil Wade, Alex Williams, Kevin Maddocks and Dicon Peeke, and elsewhere in the same issue refers to what "Tim Gilberts' team acquired". It scored **97%**.
- **S.W.A.N.** — *Sinclair User* reported in August 1988 on a further adventure writing system, the System Without A Name, developed by Gilberts and Yeandle among others.

Howard Gilberts also appears in the press for Gilsoft, demonstrating PAW for
*Sinclair User* in December 1986.

## The parser argument he lost to himself
The 1985 profile records Gilberts as unenthusiastic about elaborate parsers. He
thought complex interpreters could "actually limit communication", because the
player ends up working out which phrase the author implemented before a
character will respond at all.

Two years later his own team shipped PAW, whose headline features are
adjectives, adverbs, prepositions and pronoun resolution. The objection was
never that parsers were bad; it was that a half-built parser is worse than an
honest verb-noun one. PAW is what agreeing with both halves of that looks like.

## Still going
Gilberts remains active in the Spectrum scene and works on the **ZX Spectrum
Next**, the modern FPGA-based Spectrum successor — some four decades after the
pocket-money venture.
