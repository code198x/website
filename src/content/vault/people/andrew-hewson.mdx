Andrew Hewson ran [Hewson Consultants](/vault/companies/hewson-consultants), the
British publisher with the strongest claim to having treated programmers as
authors — their names went on the cassette inlay when most publishers kept them
anonymous. The roster it attracted is the argument for the policy:
[Steve Turner](/vault/people/steve-turner),
[Andrew Braybrook](/vault/people/andrew-braybrook),
[Raffaele Cecco](/vault/people/raffaele-cecco).

He appears on **178 pages** of the Spectrum press held here, and the company on
232 — but the number that says most about him is a different one.

## Hewson's Helpline
For years he wrote a monthly technical column in *Sinclair User* under his own
name. Not a marketing page: **Hewson's Helpline** answers reader questions about
the machine, and the answers are specific. February 1986 is about the printer
buffer, and gives the addresses — RAM from 23296 to 23551 inclusive. March 1986
is about using the Spectrum's internal clock.

A publisher's managing director writing memory maps for readers every month is
not a normal arrangement, and it explains a good deal about why programmers took
his calls.

## Against budget software
He was one of the few people in the industry willing to argue publicly against
the £1.99 model. *Sinclair User* asked him in August 1985 and printed the answer:

> We're not interested in the budget range because of the aura associated with
> it.

His position was that a good game deserved a reasonable price. Set against the
same feature's costing — a full-price £6.95 game returning the **author 40p** —
the argument reads less like snobbery and more like someone defending what his
programmers earned. See [budget games](/vault/distribution/budget-games) for the
figures he was arguing about.

## Technician Ted, and being in the room
*CRASH* interviewed him with Steve Marsden in February 1985 about *Technician
Ted*, written by Marsden and Dave Cooke and a CRASH Smash that month. He also
turns up in *CRASH*'s playing-tips pages supplying solutions — the magazine
credits "Andrew Hewson of Hewson Consultants, who supplied the entire solution"
for one game. A publisher who both wrote the technical column and sent in the
hints.

## After 1991, and again after 2013
Hewson Consultants closed in 1991. Andrew Hewson and the management team founded
**21st Century Entertainment**, which published *Pinball Dreams* and *Pinball
Fantasies* with Digital Illusions — later DICE.

In 2013 he reformed Hewson Consultants Ltd to write and publish
***Hints & Tips for Videogame Pioneers***, an account of the era co-written with
his son [Rob Hewson](/vault/people/rob-hewson) and funded on Kickstarter. Its
contributors include more than twenty people from the period — Steve Turner,
Raffaele Cecco, Jon Hare, the founders of EA DICE — which makes it an unusual
document: a publisher's memoir annotated by the people he published.

The campaign's success brought the games division back with it.
