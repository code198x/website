Kevin Toms wrote *[Football Manager](/vault/games/football-manager-1982)*, the 1982 game that put players in the manager's office instead of on the pitch, and ran [Addictive Games](/vault/companies/addictive-games), the company that sold it. The idea was older than the computer. In 1985 he told *CRASH* that it went back eighteen years, to a board game he had made on a cornflakes packet. By then the computer version was on nine machines and had sold more than 100,000 copies, and Addictive marked its third year with a dinner at the Dorchester. *CRASH* printed a photograph of the football manager Lawrie McMenemy discussing tactics with him.

## Fast facts

- **Known for:** *[Football Manager](/vault/games/football-manager-1982)* (1982), *Football Manager 2* (1988) and *Football Manager World Cup Edition* (1990).
- **Company:** [Addictive Games](/vault/companies/addictive-games), which he ran; its advertisements credited the game "Designed by Kevin Toms".
- **Sales:** 100,000 copies by June 1985, according to *CRASH*; half a million in six years, according to Addictive's 1990 advertising.
- **Later games:** *Kevin Toms Football Star Manager*, for mobile devices in 2016 and on Steam in 2025.

## Football Manager

Addictive first advertised *Football Manager* in February 1982, for the [ZX81](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx81), ZX80, TRS-80 and Video Genie at £9.95 by mail order from Milton Keynes. The [Spectrum](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum) 48K version followed that September. On the ZX81 there was nothing to watch: you picked the side, played the match and read the result. From the end of 1983 the advertisements listed "Matches in 3D graphics", the little figures in front of a goal that many players remember, and added in capitals that the 3D graphics were not included in the ZX81 version.

The game was written in BASIC. *Your Sinclair* recalled in 1990 that it was "Written in 100% Basic" and had a "unique 'customising' feature", which meant players could break into the program and change it. What held people was the season. Toms explained the game's success to *CRASH* as "the burst of activity involved in picking a side and arranging transfers, followed by the suspense of awaiting the results", and said he now saw it as a perennial, like *Monopoly*. *C&VG* readers voted it second only to *The Hobbit* as Best Strategy Game of 1983, and it was the fourth best-selling Spectrum game of 1984.

## The face on the box

*Football Manager* sold its author along with the game. The advertisements read "Designed by Kevin Toms", and the packaging carried his photograph: *Retro Gamer* later summed him up as "a bearded man who liked putting his picture on the box of his games". The magazines teased him about it for years. *Your Sinclair* remembered *Football Manager* in 1990 as "the one with mugshots of that cheery bearded bloke all over it". *Amiga Format* docked *Football Manager World Cup Edition* points for "the silly picture of Kevin Toms which appears a few times on the box, on the adverts, and even on the flippin' Wall Chart". *Your Sinclair*'s reviewer called him "Egobeard": "Not content with having his face all over the box and his name all over the instructions", Toms was offering an autographed football as a prize. The prizes in another competition included "getting your picture up alongside Kevin Toms on the packaging" of *Football Manager 3*.

## Running Addictive

In November 1984 *CRASH* reported that Toms was handing day-to-day responsibility for Addictive to its finance director, John Croft, "so that he could concentrate more on the creative side". At the same time he launched Silicon Joy, a label that its advertisements described as a division of Addictive Games. Its first releases were other people's programs, which Toms introduced in the advertisements: "many people have sent me the best software that they have written and asked me to publish it for them". They included *Grand Prix Manager*, a boxing game and *Trio*, three games on one cassette.

He stayed at the head of the company. *Dragon User* described him in 1985 as the man "who runs Addictive" when he converted *Football Manager* to the Dragon himself. *CRASH* was still calling him "Managing Director Kevin Toms" in December 1986, when it noticed that the quote praising Addictive's American football game *Headcoach* in its advertisements was his own. His strategy game *President* followed in 1987.

## The sequels

*Football Manager 2* came out in 1988. Its advertisements billed Toms as "inventor of the world's best selling football game" and quoted him saying the sequel "beats my best seller — hands down". *Football Manager World Cup Edition* followed in 1990, with advertisements that summed up the story so far: "In 1982, Kevin Toms wrote Football Manager, six years (and half a million copies sold) later, Kevin went one better and produced Football Manager 2." *Football Manager 3* (1991) was made without him, *Retro Gamer* noted, and quoted a lower figure for the first game's sales: "reportedly selling over 400,000 copies".

## Later work

Toms kept making football management games. In April 2001 *PC Zone* reported that he was "back with a new game, this time online", after a decade out of the public eye, and *Retro Gamer* found him still working on an online management game in 2004. That year the name *Football Manager* went to Sports Interactive, whose [series](/vault/games/football-manager) is not descended from Toms's game. His own later games carry his name instead: *Kevin Toms Football Star Manager* appeared for mobile devices in 2016, and a version reached Steam in August 2025.
