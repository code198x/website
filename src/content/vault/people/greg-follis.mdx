**Greg Follis** co-founded [Gargoyle Games](/vault/companies/gargoyle-games) with
[Roy Carter](/vault/people/roy-carter). *Amstrad Action* visited them in Dudley in December
1985 and described the operation as a **triumvirate** — Follis, Carter and **Ted Heathcote**
— asking how only three men were producing games of that ambition.

Follis is the one who talks in the interviews. Where Carter is described doing the technical
work, Follis explains what the company is for.

## Not teenage whizzkids

*Amtix!* made the point directly in January 1986, sending Robin Candy and Roger Kean up the
M5 to investigate a company whose success had stayed oddly out of the spotlight — because,
the magazine suggested, they were not teenage whizzkids.

That is worth recording, because the [bedroom coder](/vault/phenomena/bedroom-coder) is the
era's defining image and Gargoyle is a direct counter-example. Follis and Carter had known
each other for years and set up together after concluding there was a limit to how far you
could go working for somebody else.

## What he thought games would become

The most striking thing Follis said to *Amstrad Action* was about the future, and it is not
the prediction anyone else in 1985 was making. Asked where Gargoyle might be in ten years, he
suggested the future lay in the **large-scale public presentation of computer entertainment**
— something between playing a game and going to the cinema. If that happened, he said, they
would be writing software for it.

The magazine treated it as an amiable eccentricity. Read from here it is a reasonable
description of what the industry became.
