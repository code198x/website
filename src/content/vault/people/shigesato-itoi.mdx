When *Nintendo Power* introduced the author of *[EarthBound](/vault/games/earthbound)* to American players in June 1995, it had to explain who he was. "You may not have heard the name Shigesato Itoi (pronounced E-Toy) before," it began, "but his face is familiar to millions of Japanese TV viewers." Itoi was not a game developer. He was an advertising copywriter, a magazine columnist and a television presenter who had fallen in love with *Super Mario Bros.* and decided to write a role-playing game set in ordinary towns rather than a fantasy kingdom.

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1948, in Gunma Prefecture, Japan.
- **Careers:** advertising copywriter, essayist and columnist, television presenter, game designer; since 1998, head of *Hobo Nikkan Itoi Shinbun* ("Almost Daily Itoi Newspaper").
- **Games:** *Mother* (Famicom, 1989), *Mother 2* (Super Famicom, 1994; *EarthBound* in America, 1995), *Mother 3* (Game Boy Advance, 2006).
- **Developers:** Ape in Tokyo, with [HAL Laboratory](/vault/companies/hal-laboratory) on *Mother 2* and *Mother 3*.

## Copywriter and presenter

Itoi began as a copywriter for Seibu, one of Japan's big department store groups. His television advertisements won awards, and one of the best known starred Woody Allen in a non-speaking role. *Nintendo Power* compared his magazine column to the American humorist Dave Barry. On television he hosted a sports news programme, and as a devoted fan of the Giants baseball team he promised to shave his head if they lost the Japan Series. They lost, and he shaved it on air. He also led a televised hunt for treasure in the old gold mines of Mount Akagi. He found nothing, but the mine turned up again in *EarthBound*, in the Dusty Dunes Desert.

## How Mother began

Itoi told *Nintendo Power* that his interest in games began when he fell for *Super Mario Bros.*, though he admitted he was not very good at it. He preferred role-playing games, he joked, because they were "the only kind of game you can put down when you want to get something to eat or go to the bathroom. You can even drop it for months, then come back to the game and keep going."

He visited Nintendo in Kyoto, met [Shigeru Miyamoto](/vault/people/shigeru-miyamoto), and proposed an RPG that did not rely on fantasy or science fiction. "I wanted to create a game with real characters — characters whom players would recognize in the people around them." Nintendo's president, Hiroshi Yamauchi, approved the project, and Itoi took it to [Ape](/vault/companies/ape-inc), a Tokyo company best known for books about video games. "I didn't want to create a game by the numbers," he said. "Instead of putting the game in a fantasy setting, I wanted to use ordinary places." The first *Mother* came out on the Famicom in 1989. An English version was prepared but never released at the time.

## Mother 2 and EarthBound

The sequel stalled. Itoi later recalled asking [Satoru Iwata](/vault/people/satoru-iwata), then at HAL Laboratory, to look at it: "If we use what you've got now, it'll take two years to fix. If we start from scratch, we can get this done in six months." They started again, rebuilt it in about six months and spent six more polishing it. *Mother 2* came out on the Super Famicom in 1994, and Nintendo released it in America as *EarthBound* in 1995, with a player's guide containing scratch-and-sniff cards.

Its America was borrowed from television. Growing up, Itoi said, his family watched American shows such as *Father Knows Best* and *Leave It to Beaver*: "That was our picture of America... I love to think of America as a place like that." Some of it was closer to home. The hero, Ness, regularly phones his mother and a father he never sees, a detail *Edge* reported Itoi took from his own childhood.

## Mother 3

*Mother 3* was announced for the Nintendo 64 and its 64DD disk drive, made by HAL with Itoi's team. In 1997 *N64 Magazine* described a game of twelve chapters, set no longer in small-town America but across medieval, fantasy and science-fiction worlds, and called Itoi "the archetypal mad genius". Two years later *Edge* reported it only half finished. In August 2000, in a discussion published on Itoi's website, Itoi, Iwata and Miyamoto announced that development had been stopped. Iwata explained that the project had run for six years across the Super Famicom, the 64DD and the Nintendo 64, and that finishing it would take too much from Nintendo's other games.

It came back on the Game Boy Advance, in 2D, developed by Brownie Brown with HAL, and was released in Japan on 20 April 2006, with a limited-edition Game Boy Micro to go with it. Neither *Mother 3* nor the 2003 *Mother 1+2* compilation was released in the West at the time, although *Edge* noted that English scripts for the first two games already existed.

## Hobonichi

In June 1998 Itoi started a website, *Hobo Nikkan Itoi Shinbun*, which he has updated every day since and which grew into a company, Hobonichi, best known for its Hobonichi Techo diaries. In 2019 Hobonichi collected Iwata's interviews and writings in a book, published in English in 2021 as *Ask Iwata*.
