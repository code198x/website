Jonathan Smith signed his first Spectrum game backwards. The title screen of *Pud Pud*, which [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software) published and *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* reviewed in April 1985, credited "s'htimS nahtanoJ", and the magazine's reviewer, baffled by a game about a creature eating puddings in Weird World, decided that its author "must be a very abnormal person, or even a relative of Matthew Smith (no offence intended)!" Within two years the same programmer, known as Joffa, had written *Hyper Sports*, *Mikie*, *Green Beret* and *[Cobra](/vault/games/cobra)*, and *CRASH*'s readers had voted him the third-best programmer of 1986. By the end of 1988 other programmers were naming him as the one they most admired.

## Fast facts

- **Known as:** Joffa. *[Your Sinclair](/vault/magazines/your-sinclair)* introduced him as "Jonathan 'Joffa' Smith".
- **Born:** 1967. **Died:** 26 June 2010, aged 43 (secondary sources).
- **At Ocean and Imagine:** *Pud Pud* (reviewed April 1985), *Daley Thompson's Super-Test* (1985, with P. Owens), *[Hyper Sports](/vault/games/hypersports)* (1985), *Mikie* (1986), *Green Beret* (1986), *[Cobra](/vault/games/cobra)* (1986), *Terra Cresta* (1987).
- **At Special FX:** *Firefly* (1988) and *Batman: The Caped Crusader* (1988); the studio's first game was *Hysteria* (1987).
- **Readers' vote:** third in *CRASH*'s Best Programmer award for 1986, with 7%.

## Arcade games for Ocean and Imagine

Most of Smith's Ocean work was conversion: taking a coin-op or a sports game and fitting it into a 48K Spectrum. After *Pud Pud* he co-wrote *Daley Thompson's Super-Test*, the follow-up to Ocean's *Decathlon*, credited in *CRASH* to "P Owens and J Smith". Then came a run of Konami arcade conversions on Ocean's Imagine label. *CRASH* gave *Hyper Sports* 92% and a Smash, *Mikie*, about a schoolboy dodging his teachers to deliver a message to his girlfriend, 93%, and *Green Beret* 88%.

*Cobra*, the game of the Sylvester Stallone film, followed at the end of 1986. *CRASH* made it a Smash with 93%, and its reviewers singled out the scrolling ("very smooth and fast") and the sound, "the best on the Spectrum for years". *CRASH*'s readers voted it into their Hotline chart, where it spent three months at number 2 in 1987. The [Cobra](/vault/games/cobra) entry has the details. *Terra Cresta*, another Imagine coin-op conversion, took 81% in February 1987.

In the *CRASH* Readers' Awards for 1986 he came third as Best Programmer, behind Stephen Crow and Keith Burkhill. *Your Sinclair* made him the castaway in its desert-island feature, asking for his eight favourite games: "programmer of such classics as *Green Beret* and *Mikie*. He's currently furthering the cause of world peace by working on *Cobra* and *Terra Cresta* for Ocean." His list began with Ultimate's *Lunar Jetman*: "You want a short snappy answer? This is a short and snappy game."

## Special FX

In September 1987 Paul Finnegan left Ocean to found Special FX, a development studio in Liverpool, and Smith went with him. Special FX's first game, *Hysteria*, was published by Software Projects and took 78% in *CRASH*. Then the studio signed with Ocean. *CRASH* reported the arrangement in March 1988:

> Special FX has signed a deal with Ocean to handle the business side of its releases, and this month's Smash *Firefly* is the first under that arrangement. It follows a fling with Software Projects, which published Special FX's *Hysteria*. And the Liverpool-based development house's boss Paul Finnegan and *Firefly* programmer Jonathan Smith are both ex-Ocean men.

*Firefly* took 92%. Smith's next game was *Batman: The Caped Crusader*, with graphics by Charles Davies, which *CRASH* reviewed in January 1989 as "a finely-honed arcade adventure which is surely the best comic licence ever", giving it 93%. Special FX also developed games by other programmers for Ocean, among them [Jim Bagley](/vault/people/jim-bagley) and Charles Davies' *Gutz* (1988).

## The programmers' programmer

In December 1988 *CRASH*'s Phil King asked a group of Spectrum programmers to name the classic games and the programmers they most admired, and added up the answers. Smith came third in the programmers' top five, behind the Ultimate team and Jon Ritman. [Andrew Deakin](/vault/people/andrew-deakin) put *Cobra* at the top of his games and named Smith as his most admired programmer: "he's consistently produced playable games with excellent scrolling." *CRASH* added that Smith was "currently working on the second Batman game". [Mike Lamb](/vault/people/mike-lamb) named him alongside William Wray and the Ultimate team, and another programmer chose him because "he's so competent technically".

## Later work

Smith carried on after the 8-bit years, according to later secondary accounts. He wrote *Recoil* for the Atari ST (1989) and worked on *Hudson Hawk* (1991), then on console games with Software Creations, including *Spider-Man/X-Men: Arcade's Revenge* (1992), and with Warthog on *Looney Tunes: Back in Action* (2003). He also produced the computer displays for the television comedy *Red Dwarf* on an Atari ST. He kept the habit *Pud Pud* had shown: he often wrote his name into his games as a mirror image, with "Jonathan" and "Smith" spelt in various ways.

Smith died on 26 June 2010, aged 43. Former Ocean colleagues and friends collected tributes to him on the World of Spectrum website.
