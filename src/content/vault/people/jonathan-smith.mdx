## Overview

Jonathan Smith wrote arcade conversions for [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software) and Imagine, then left with colleagues to form **Special FX** in Liverpool — a studio that promptly signed a deal to keep making Ocean's games. *CRASH* laid the whole arrangement out when *Firefly* came out:

> Special FX has signed a deal with Ocean to handle the business side of its releases, and this month's Smash *Firefly* is the first under that arrangement… the Liverpool-based development house's boss **Paul Finnegan** and Firefly programmer **Jonathan Smith** are both **ex-Ocean men**. Smith's previous Ocean/Imagine credits include ***Green Beret*** and Smashes ***Cobra*** and ***Hypersports***.

## Fast facts

- **At Ocean/Imagine:** *Green Beret*, *Cobra*, *Hypersports*, *Terra Cresta*, *Batman*.
- **At Special FX:** *Firefly* (a *CRASH* Smash), *Hysteria*, and work on the *RoboCop* licence.
- **Colleagues there:** Paul Finnegan running it, **Tony Pomfrit** (*Rambo*, *Daley Thompson's Decathlon*) also ex-Ocean, and [Jim Bagley](/vault/people/jim-bagley).

## Leaving, and not leaving

The Special FX story is the most instructive thing here, because it is a pattern, not an anecdote.

A publisher's best programmers leave. They do not leave the publisher's work — they set up a studio a few miles away and **contract back to it**, with the publisher handling "the business side". Everybody gets something: the developers get independence, ownership and better terms; the publisher keeps the people and loses the payroll, the management and the risk.

That arrangement is now how most of the games industry is organised, and here it is being invented in Liverpool in 1988 and explained to readers as news. *CRASH*'s phrasing — "a fling with Software Projects", "future projects for Ocean include the *RoboCop* film licence" — treats a studio's publisher relationships as the interesting fact about it, which by then they were.

## Who wrote Gutz

Smith also resolves a small puzzle raised in the [Jim Bagley](/vault/people/jim-bagley) entry.

*CRASH*'s review box credits *Gutz* to "Ocean/Special FX… Authors: Jim Bagley and Chas Davies". A reader's letter to the same magazine lists *Gutz* among Jonathan Smith's games. Both looked like sources in conflict.

They are not. **Bagley and Smith were at the same studio**, working for the same publisher, on adjacent projects — so a fan naming the people behind a Special FX game and a credit box naming the two who wrote that particular one can both be right about the same cassette. The conflict was an artefact of reading each source as if it were a complete record.

## Why he matters

Smith is the entry for **the studio breaking away**, the structural change that turned publishers into financiers and put the making of games into independent hands.

He also carries a caution about this Vault's method. "Jonathan Smith" is a common name, and the prose scan counts him in issues where the Jonathan Smith on the page is a **reader in a high-score table** in Newport. The 29-issue figure that first surfaced him is inflated; the evidence that stands up is the *CRASH* news piece naming his employer, his colleagues and four of his games.
