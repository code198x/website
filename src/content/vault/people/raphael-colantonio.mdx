Raphaël Colantonio set up [Arkane Studios](/vault/companies/arkane-studios) to make a sequel. He and his team, then four people, wanted to make "an actual U3", a third *Ultima Underworld*, and went to Paul Neurath, who had founded [Looking Glass](/vault/companies/looking-glass), to ask for it. "Paul was very excited about it — but he had the IP rights, not the distribution rights," Colantonio told *Edge*. "So then we met with EA... and that's where that story ends." Arkane made its own game instead, and over the next 18 years Colantonio built it into the studio behind *[Dishonored](/vault/games/dishonored)* and *Prey*, games that kept Looking Glass's kind of first-person game alive for a big audience.

## Fast facts

- **Nationality:** French.
- **Founded:** Arkane Studios, Lyon, 1999; president until June 2017.
- **Games at Arkane:** *Arx Fatalis* (2002), *Dark Messiah of Might and Magic* (2006), *Dishonored* (2012, co-creative director with Harvey Smith), *Prey* (2017, creative director).
- **Since 2019:** WolfEye Studios, with Julien Roby; *Weird West* (2022).

## From Ultima fan to EA

Colantonio fell in love with *Ultima* when he was nine, *Edge* reported in 2006, and he was always "more interested in American RPGs than French games". That taste got him his first job. According to *Gamasutra*'s 2009 profile of the studio, he entered a magazine contest by answering questions about *Ultima*, around the time of his compulsory military service, and ended up as the eighth employee of Electronic Arts in France, setting up its IT and customer support. He left EA, by then working for its British arm, in 1997 to join a small French studio, and two years later started his own.

## Arkane

Arkane began in Lyon in 1999, "when all the RPG developers were folding, or about to fold", as Colantonio put it. Its first game, *Arx Fatalis* (2002), was a first-person RPG in a frozen world forced underground, made by nine people. It was rough, and Colantonio admitted to "a lot of rough edges and a lot of bugs", but it found a following. "I think it was the most true game that we've worked on and done," he told *Gamasutra* in 2009. "It was exactly what I wanted to do." The [Arkane Studios](/vault/companies/arkane-studios) entry follows the studio from there: a meeting with [Valve](/vault/companies/valve) at E3, the Source engine, and *Dark Messiah of Might and Magic* for [Ubisoft](/vault/companies/ubisoft).

By the time of *Dark Messiah* Arkane had about 30 people, and Colantonio had become good at recruiting. He brought in the *Thief* designer Randy Smith and *Half-Life 2*'s art director Viktor Antonov as consultants, and persuaded a well-known author of *Thief* fan missions to give up his accounting job and move his family from the United States to Lyon for a year. "You just have to ask," he told *Edge*. He also explained what he was aiming for:

> My ideal RPG would be almost like dreaming — I'm grabbing a sword, making my decisions, the world is responding to what I do — not saying: 'OK, I'm trying to open the door, so throw your dice and tell me if it opens'.

"If the player can find some way to surprise us," he added, "then we've won."

## Lyon and Austin

In 2005 Arkane opened a second studio in Austin, Texas. By 2010 Colantonio, as chief executive and game director, was co-directing Arkane's next game with Harvey Smith, the lead designer of *[Deus Ex](/vault/games/deus-ex)*. The two offices were linked by video-conferencing carts left switched on beside people's desks all day. Smith told *Edge* in 2010 that he and Colantonio had both been in games for about 16 years, that *Ultima Underworld* was their favourite game, and that *Thief*, *System Shock* and *Deus Ex* had formed their values. "We hire people that share this religion!" Colantonio said.

ZeniMax Media, the owner of [Bethesda](/vault/companies/bethesda), bought Arkane in 2010. *Dishonored* (2012), co-directed by Colantonio and Smith, was set in the plague-ridden whaling city of Dunwall and let the player reach every target by several routes, with or without killing. *Edge* gave it nine out of ten. Colantonio explained its Chaos system: the more the player kills, the darker the story becomes. "If things are more chaotic, it makes you think like life is cheap, there are more betrayals and more bad outcomes." Making the game work for both violent and peaceful players, he said, was like making "two or three" games at once.

At Arkane Austin, Colantonio was creative director of *Prey* (2017), with Ricardo Bare as lead designer. It was his last game at the studio.

## WolfEye

In June 2017 Colantonio stepped down as Arkane's president after 18 years. "It is time for me to step out to spend some time with my son and reflect on what is important to me and my future," he said, and called himself "Arkane Studios' biggest champion".

In November 2019 he and Julien Roby, who had worked with him at Arkane for just over a decade, announced WolfEye Studios. It had no central office and would make smaller independent games rather than big-budget ones. "The world has changed since 1999 when I started Arkane Studios," Colantonio said. "The market has changed, our tastes and desires too. I'm not the same person that I was back then, but there is one thing that hasn't changed: I want to create a game that is very dear to me and my friends at WolfEye."

That game was *Weird West* (2022), published by Devolver Digital: a top-down action RPG set in a supernatural American frontier, played as five different characters in turn, with several ways through each fight: by stealth, with a posse of hired guns, or by using the scenery. Colantonio also made music for it. With the singer Ava Gore, as Weird Wolves, he wrote songs for the soundtrack, which *Wireframe* called "the perfect musical accompaniment for the game's pulpy, eerie vibes". The same review was hard on the rest of the game, though: it found the combat crude and the side quests repetitive, and gave it 55%.
