Katsuhiro Harada's parents cried when he told them he had joined a games company. He had grown up forbidden a home console, sneaking into arcades until the local Parent Teacher Association dragged him out, and his first job at Namco was not making games at all but promoting them in one of its arcades. Within a year he had broken the arcade's sales record two months running and won a commendation from Namco's president, and he used it to ask for a move into development. Namco put him on the first *[Tekken](/vault/games/tekken)*. He stayed with Namco and the series for more than 30 years and became, in *Edge*'s words, "the creative face of Tekken".

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1970 or 1971, in Osaka; public sources differ on the year.
- **Education:** a degree in psychology.
- **Joined:** [Namco](/vault/companies/namco), as an arcade promoter, in the early 1990s.
- **First game:** *Tekken* (1994), balancing the game and working on its timing and frame data.
- **Tekken 3 (1997):** one of the game's two game designers, with Masahiro Kimoto.
- **Later roles:** director, producer and executive producer of the *Tekken* series.
- **Left Bandai Namco:** at the end of 2025.
- **Since 2026:** representative director and CEO of VS Studio SNK, a new studio of [SNK](/vault/companies/snk).

## From the arcade floor to the first Tekken

Harada told *Edge* in 2013 that he had once tried to write a game himself, copying code out of a magazine in middle school, but "the results were no good. So I figured I'd find a job that allowed me to simply play games for a living instead." At Namco he planned events and tournaments for *Street Fighter* and other games, and his psychology degree made him curious about the players: he moved cabinets around the arcade to see if they earned more in different spots, and tried to find out whether certain drinks made people play for longer. He spent a year, he said, "chatting to customers, finding out exactly what they liked or disliked".

Towards the end of that first year he joined the *Tekken* team. The game's director and lead designer was Seiichi Ishii, who had come from Sega's *[Virtua Fighter](/vault/games/virtua-fighter)*. Harada's job was "to balance the game, work on timing and frame data". He was frank about how well that went: "there were certain moves that couldn't be blocked. In truth, it wasn't very well balanced at all." He also lent the early games his voice. In 2024 he said he had voiced Marshall Law up to about *Tekken 6*, and that Yoshimitsu's voice was a mixture of his own, the sound team's and others'.

## Tekken 3 and after

Ishii left Namco after *Tekken 2*. When *Computer and Video Games* visited the *Tekken 3* team in 1998, Harada was one of its two game designers, with Masahiro Kimoto, under the producer Hajime Nakatani. Asked how to become a great player, he said: "First of all, practice each move 100 times before going to sleep. Also, always play with someone who is better than you." Later credit lists also name him among the game's directors.

From then on his title changed from game to game, but the series stayed his. *Edge* talked to "Tekken masters" Harada, Kimoto and Yuichi Yonemori about *Tekken 4* in 2001, and in 2006 called him the "veteran Tekken director" when he explained why the PSP's *Tekken: Dark Resurrection* had been rebuilt from scratch: "Had we called it Tekken Portable, it would be seen as a downsized Tekken." In January 2010 *Edge* listed him as executive producer of *Tekken 6*. "I've been making Tekken for 15 years," he said then. In 2011 he was producing *Tekken Tag Tournament 2*, and toured the world with Capcom's Yoshinori Ono to promote the crossover *Street Fighter X Tekken*. *Edge* reported in 2013 that the series had sold some 44 million copies.

## The public face

By 2013 *Edge* described him as a man who "straddles an awkward line between secretive developer and Twitter community manager, fielding requests and queries from players in (mostly) good spirits". He did not always tell fans what they wanted to hear. In 2012, answering players who demanded the original voice actors, he said that "before whining, and complaining about everything, you need practice at taking a step back and analysing things objectively. And also at being an adult." He also experimented with the business: *Tekken Revolution*, released for the PlayStation 3 in 2013, was free to play, and Harada led Namco Bandai's wider trials of the model.

As director of *Tekken 7* (2015) he brought in guest fighters from well beyond Namco. Capcom's Akuma, SNK's Geese Howard and *Final Fantasy XV*'s Noctis were followed in 2018 by Negan from *The Walking Dead*, whom Harada and the designer Michael Murray announced at the EVO tournament that August. He also ran a YouTube channel, *Harada's Bar*.

## Leaving Bandai Namco

On 8 December 2025 Harada announced that he would leave Bandai Namco at the end of the year, having handed his responsibilities to colleagues over the preceding years. *Game Developer* described him as the series' executive game director and chief producer, and noted his other work at the company, including *Pokkén Tournament*, *SoulCalibur* and the VR title *Summer Lesson*. He said he would "share more about my next steps at a later date". In May 2026 SNK announced that it had set up a new studio in Tokyo, VS Studio SNK, on 1 May, with Harada as its representative director and CEO. "From a free, open, and spacious environment," he said, "we will generate new ideas and create memorable games."
