**Sandy White** wrote *[Ant Attack](/vault/games/ant-attack)*, the game that put
isometric 3D on the [ZX Spectrum](/vault/systems/zx-spectrum) in 1983 — a year
before *[Knight Lore](/vault/games/knight-lore)*, which usually gets the credit.

He is one of the clearest cases in the British 8-bit era of somebody arriving at
games from somewhere else entirely, and it shows in the work.

## How he got there

*Computer & Video Games* made him **Designer of the Month** in May 1984 and
printed the route.

Born in **Edinburgh in 1961**. Art-college degree, then a solo show of his own
sculpture in **Aberdeen**. Two years after that he wrote *Ant Attack*.

The computing came out of the sculpture rather than alongside it. His interest
went back six years to a **Sinclair MK14** — the kit micro from Science of
Cambridge, Sinclair's pre-[Sinclair Research](/vault/companies/sinclair-research)
company — bought for a specific job. In his own words: "**I built my own computer
to operate one of my working sculptures — the ice cream van — which is a protest
against nuclear war.**"

So: a man who taught himself machine code to animate an anti-nuclear ice cream van, and who then applied a sculptor's understanding of three-dimensional
construction to a machine that could not do perspective.

*CRASH* saw the connection immediately. Reviewing *Ant Attack* in its first issue,
February 1984: "**Sandy White is a quiet Scot and a sculptor by trade. His
understanding of three dimensional construction is evident in his game, Ant
Attack.**"

## What he was worried about

The *C&VG* profile is unusual for the period in that the subject is visibly
uncomfortable with the machinery around him.

He would not discuss money. "**Quicksilva warned me that people would ask how much
I've earned**," he told the magazine, and left it there — *Ant Attack* was on its
way to 50,000 copies at the time.

And on the future, in May 1984, with one hit behind him and a second game agreed:
he was concerned that **business pressures might at some stage threaten his
artistic integrity**.

That is a strikingly clear-eyed thing for a 23-year-old to say in the middle of
the British software boom, and it is worth setting beside the more familiar
bedroom-coder story, in which the money arriving is the happy ending.

Sculpture remained the important thing. He was still exhibiting; the games were
what the computer turned out to also be good for.

## Fast facts

- **Born:** Edinburgh, 1961
- **Trained as:** a sculptor; art-college degree, solo show in Aberdeen
- **First computer:** a Sinclair MK14, bought to drive a sculpture
- **Known for:** *[Ant Attack](/vault/games/ant-attack)* (Quicksilva, 1983), with **Angela Sutherland** —
  the two of them received a "gold cassette" for 50,000 sales in December 1984
- **Ambition, as given to *C&VG* in 1984:** to travel in the Space Shuttle
