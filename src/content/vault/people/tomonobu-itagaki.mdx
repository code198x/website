When the *Official Xbox Magazine* reviewed *Dead or Alive 3* for its first issue in 2001, it had to fly to Japan to play it, because Tomonobu Itagaki was "famously and rightfully protective of his baby". He was by then an executive at [Tecmo](/vault/companies/tecmo) and head of its [Team Ninja](/vault/companies/team-ninja), the group whose fighting game *[Dead or Alive](/vault/games/dead-or-alive)* had helped pull the company back from financial trouble. Over the next few years he became one of the best-known figures in Japanese games: the man who brought *[Ninja Gaiden](/vault/games/ninja-gaiden)* back as one of the hardest action games of its time, who made a beach-volleyball game for his fighters, and who said exactly what he thought of everyone else.

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1967, in Tokyo.
- **Tecmo:** 1992 to 2008, for 16 years; head of Team Ninja.
- **Games:** *[Dead or Alive](/vault/games/dead-or-alive)* (1996) and its sequels to *Dead or Alive 4* (2005); *Dead or Alive Xtreme Beach Volleyball* (2003); *[Ninja Gaiden](/vault/games/ninja-gaiden)* (2004), *Ninja Gaiden Black* (2005), *Ninja Gaiden: Dragon Sword* (2008) and *Ninja Gaiden II* (2008).
- **Later:** Valhalla Game Studios and *Devil's Third* (2015); Itagaki Games from 2021.
- **Died:** October 2025, aged 58.

## Tecmo and Dead or Alive

Itagaki joined Tecmo in 1992. His first game, the *Official Xbox Magazine* reported, was the final version of the company's American football series, *Tecmo Bowl*. He told *Edge* that he had been "working in the 3D environment from the very beginning", on a game that was, in his words, "my famous setback".

By the mid-1990s Tecmo was struggling, and *Wireframe*'s history of the company credits its recovery to a design group headed by Itagaki, "handed the task of making a rival to Sega's *Virtua Fighter*". The result, *Dead or Alive*, reached the arcades in 1996 on Sega's own Model 2 board. Its central idea was the Hold button, which caught an incoming blow and turned it into a counter: Itagaki wanted "to recreate the thrill of a Kung-Fu movie, where you see fighters repeatedly countering hundreds of moves", *Computer and Video Games* reported. The [Dead or Alive](/vault/games/dead-or-alive) entry follows the series.

Itagaki moved the series to Microsoft's new console for *Dead or Alive 3*, a launch game for the [Xbox](/vault/systems/microsoft-xbox) in 2001. The *Official Xbox Magazine* introduced him as "Executive Officer & General Manager, Creative Division" and found that "Itagaki's claim that it was both easy and deep holds true." He defended the choice of hardware: "I admit I love machine power but I don't want to waste it. You need it to deliver your vision. That is why we went on Xbox while many were criticising it."

## The beach and the fighters

The series' women fighters drew attention of another kind, and Itagaki leaned into it. In 2003 Team Ninja released *Dead or Alive Xtreme Beach Volleyball*, in which the fighters spend two weeks on an island playing volleyball, gambling and buying swimsuits. When *Edge* asked him in 2002 whether he planned any male characters, he answered: "No, female characters only. I mean this is obvious." *Edge*'s review judged the game's "treatment of the female form" "overtly sexualised and, some might say, clichéd, but it's certainly not derogatory".

## Ninja Gaiden

Team Ninja had begun a new *Ninja Gaiden* in 1999, at first on Sega's NAOMI arcade board, and moved it to the Xbox. Itagaki told *Edge* why he was bringing the series back: "In my view, it was the 'violence' of the action that made Ninja Gaiden so good. So you might consider this 'sequel' as a very new Ninja Gaiden — a new start." It was, he said, "made very much for the west... quite hard, with many functions and a higher level of difficulty."

The 2004 game was hard enough that *Edge* warned of "an impassable difficulty spike" at the first boss, while giving it nine out of ten. Itagaki, its director, told *Electronic Gaming Monthly* he did not think it too tough. *Ninja Gaiden Black* (2005) added harder settings, one of them so difficult that, *Hyper* reported, Itagaki thought very few players in the US would be able to finish it. When Team Ninja made *Ninja Gaiden: Dragon Sword* for the Nintendo DS, Itagaki explained in a keynote speech that the team had picked *Ninja Gaiden* over *Dead or Alive*; *Edge* put the reason as its "excessively masculine hardcore connotations". *Ninja Gaiden II* followed on the Xbox 360 in 2008. By then he had a reputation for provocation: *Edge* recalled that he had "railed against the makers of *Tekken*" and dismissed weaker players as "ninja dogs".

## Leaving Tecmo

Itagaki left Tecmo in 2008 after 16 years and sued the company for unpaid bonuses. *Edge* reported that the dispute was over unpaid completion bonuses for *Dead or Alive 4* and over what the claim called "unreasonable and disingenuous" statements by Tecmo's president, Yoshimi Yasuda. *Kotaku* put the claim at 148 million yen. The two sides settled out of court in 2010, "after just under two years in the legal system", *Edge* reported. *Wireframe* later wrote that his lawsuit "prompted 300 other Tecmo employees to file a similar high-profile case of their own".

## Valhalla and after

By 2010 Itagaki was running development at Valhalla Game Studios, about 50 people, many of them from Team Ninja, with another former Tecmo man, Satoshi Kanematsu, running the company. He talked to *Edge* that year about its first game, *Devil's Third*, and about Japan: "Japan failed to import capitalism, or rather, it failed to understand the lessons of capitalism." He said he had been offered the chance to stand for political office, and that he would probably stay in games "until I die". *Devil's Third* was first announced with THQ for the Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3, and eventually came out on Nintendo's Wii U in 2015.

Valhalla closed in 2021, and Itagaki founded a new company, Itagaki Games, that year. It had released nothing when he died in October 2025, aged 58. His family announced his death on his Facebook account with a message he had written in advance: "Only one thing weighs on me — I'm deeply sorry to all my fans that I couldn't bring you a new work."
