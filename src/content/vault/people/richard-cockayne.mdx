## Overview

**Richard Cockayne** co-founded [PSS](/vault/companies/pss) with
[Gary Mays](/vault/people/gary-mays), running a Coventry house whose catalogue was built on
something almost nobody else was selling to home-computer owners: **wargames**.

*Amstrad Action* photographed the pair together in March 1986 and interviewed them about a
release schedule that ran to *Tobruk* in March and *Bismarck* in April, each offering the
player either side of a real Second World War campaign.

## The problem he named

Cockayne's contribution to that interview is a diagnosis rather than a boast. His observation
was that buyers had not yet learned to **tell arcade from strategy** — and for a publisher
selling the second thing into a market conditioned by the first, that is the whole commercial
difficulty in one sentence.

It explains PSS's positioning. A wargame competing on a shelf against *Commando* is not
competing on quality; it is competing for a customer who may not know the category exists.

## Why it matters here

PSS is one of the few British houses of the period whose output assumed an adult buying a
game to think about rather than to react to. That the company's own founder identified
audience education — not development, not distribution — as the binding constraint is a
useful corrective to the assumption that eighties publishing problems were all technical or
financial.
