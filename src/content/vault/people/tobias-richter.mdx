In April 1990 the German magazine *[Amiga Joker](/vault/magazines/amiga-joker)* introduced its readers to "Trekking Tobi". He was Tobias Richter, a 22-year-old computer-science student from Darmstadt, and his two-disk *Star Trek* game was, the magazine said, the brightest star in the public-domain games sky. He had been a *Star Trek* fan for about ten years and had owned an Amiga for about three. Anyone could have a copy for 13 marks by post. In Britain the game and his animations of the starship *Enterprise* went round the public-domain libraries, the mail-order businesses that copied free software onto disks for the price of the disk, and the libraries' adverts sold his work by name.

## Fast facts

- **Based:** Darmstadt in 1990; Cologne by the mid-1990s.
- **Known for:** the public-domain *Star Trek* game (finished around the end of 1989); the Agatron disks of animations and ray-traced pictures.
- **Commercial work on the Amiga:** all the graphics for *Oil Imperium* (1989); graphics for its follow-up *Transatlantic*; the intro to Team17's *[Alien Breed](/vault/games/alien-breed)* (1991); animations for the German satirical puppet show *Hurra Deutschland*.
- **Tools named in the press:** Videoscape 3D, the German ray tracer Reflections, Maxon's Cinema 4D.
- **Later:** *The Light Works* CD-ROM collection of his 3D work (1995); The Light Works, his computer-graphics company in Cologne.

## Trekking Tobi's Star Trek

Richter told *Amiga Joker* that he had first tried to write the game on an Apple, which "went pretty badly wrong", and then in BASIC on the Amiga, where animation was hard to come by and 512K of memory was too little to compile the program. After buying more memory he started again, and he had finished it about four months before the interview. The player commands the *Enterprise*, at first carrying objects from planet to planet or to a space station, then intercepting rebel ships and fighting Klingons and Romulans. He wanted the player to have a great deal of freedom and the game to be as true to life as he could make it: the speeds obeyed the laws of physics, so that travelling at warp 1 took about 1,700 times as long as at warp 12. He had planned landings on planets, but stopped when the program grew too big.

British magazines rated it highly. *Raze* wrote in November 1990 that "the best Star Trek game of all must undoubtedly be Tobias Richter's two disk Star Trek extravaganza". Libraries advertised "Tobias Richter's Star Trek (2)" in *[Amiga Format](/vault/magazines/amiga-format)* through 1991. *Retro Gamer* looked back in 2012: the game was "less Kirk and more Chekov", but there was "something quite addictive about looking out of that view screen and clicking around the Enterprise's controls", and "it seemed like practically everyone with an Amiga had this disk at some point".

## Agatron and the PD libraries

The game was only part of his output. Richter edited a public-domain series of his own, "AGAtron", made up mainly of computer animations. In 1989 the Wakefield library 17 Bit Software put "a special compilation of Star Trek Animations for ALL Amiga machines, animations by Tobias Richter" into its *Amiga Computing* pack. *Amiga Format*'s PD column explained in March 1990 that "the Agatron collection is put together by Tobias Richter of West Germany" and promised Star Trek fans "beautiful detailed shots of the Enterprise". *The One* picked out "a bunch of wicked Videoscape animations from the prolific Tobias Richter, with subjects including Star Trek, Star Trek and sometimes Star Trek", and in May 1990 a new Star Trek demo made with "an unreleased ray-tracing program called Reflections". *Amiga Format*, explaining 3D software to its readers in 1991, used "Tobias Richter's famous Star Trek demos" as the example of the modern science-fiction work programs like Videoscape were suited to.

The libraries' catalogue lines show how well known he was. In 1991 *Amiga Format*'s advertisers sold "Agatron 8: Fab Tobias Richter pics!", "Agatron 6: Stunning ray traced slides!", "Tobias Richter slideshow" and "AG.03 Star Trek (Agatron)... Tobias Richter". In lists made up mostly of disk numbers and titles, his was one of the few artists' names.

## Commercial games

Richter told *Amiga Joker* that his two most important jobs so far were the computer animations for *Hurra Deutschland*, a satirical puppet show on German television, "and of course all the graphics for *Oil Imperium*", the oil-business strategy game published by reLINE in 1989. When *Amiga Joker* previewed its follow-up, *Transatlantic*, a game about running a shipping line, it noted that the graphics were again by Richter, "only this time all the pictures are animated".

He was also close to the British demo scene that became [Team17](/vault/companies/team17). When *Amiga Format* announced in March 1991 that "some of the best demo writers in Europe have teamed-up to form a brand new software house: Team 17", it named Richter alongside Rico Holmes among the people around 17 Bit. Team17's *Alien Breed* opened that autumn with an introduction by him. *The One* called it "short but superb (as you'd expect from legendary demo programmer Tobias Richter)", and *ACE* praised the whole presentation "from the Tobias Richter intro to the fast full-screen scrolling".

## Cologne and The Light Works

By the mid-1990s Richter was in Cologne and was a fixture of German Amiga software stands. At a German Amiga show reported early in 1994 he was on the stand of IFD, the publisher of the ray tracer Reflections, a program *Amazing Computing* expected to be "familiar to anyone who is aware of the work of Tobias Richter". At the Computer '95 show in November 1995 *CU Amiga* found "the famous Amiga animator, Tobias Richter" drawing large crowds at Maxon's stand by showing what could be done with Cinema 4D. "You may not recognise this man, but you know his work," *Amiga Format* told its readers in 1996.

His models and scenes were collected on a CD-ROM, *The Light Works*, published in Germany by Schatztruhe of Essen and sold in Britain by Almathera in 1995, with objects for Cinema 4D, Reflections and Imagine. *Amiga Computing* found its best models and scenes were Richter's, by "a man who's made quite a name for himself in the world of computer animation", and *Amiga Report*'s reviewer described him as "famous for his Star Trek renders and his highly playable Shareware Star Trek game". In 1999 *Amiga Format*'s gallery editor could still compare a reader's picture with "Tobias Richter's later work on the Amiga".

The Light Works became the name of his computer-graphics company in Cologne, which offers visual effects, animation and visualisation. He has kept making starships. In 2011 the *Star Trek* novelist David Mack described a starbase fly-by by Richter of The Light Works, which he noted did the effects for the fan-produced series *Star Trek: Phase II*, and in 2024 a fan project recreating scenes from *Space: 1999* credited "Visual Effects Supervisor Tobias Richter at The Light Works in Cologne, Germany" with its Eagle and Hawk spacecraft.
