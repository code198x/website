## Overview

Dino Dini wrote *Kick Off* for [Anco](/vault/companies/anco) in 1989. It is viewed from above, it is fast, and its central decision — **the ball is a separate object from the player** — produced the deepest football game of its generation and an argument that ran for years.

## Fast facts

- **Wrote:** *Kick Off* (1989), *Kick Off 2*, *Player Manager*.
- **Then:** signed to **Virgin Games**, where he continued making football games under his own name.
- ***Zeta*** (Italy, 1997) opens a review by dating "the saga begun by Dino Dini in 1989 with the first *Kick Off*", and calls it **"il più insigne gioco di calcio di tutti i tempi"** — the most distinguished football game of all time, eight years on.

## One decision

Every other football game glued the ball to the player's boot. Yours until you passed it, controllable, obedient.

Dini's does not. Run with the ball and it travels ahead of you; you are chasing it as much as carrying it. Trapping, turning and shielding become skills you have to acquire, not buttons you press, and a loose ball in a crowded box becomes genuine chaos.

Everything the series is known for follows from that: the speed, the steep learning curve, the ten-year competitive scene, and the complaint. Critics called the result **pinball**. Players called it the point. Both were describing the same property.

## The natural experiment

Design claims about authorship are usually unfalsifiable. This one is not, because the series carried on without him and the press recorded what changed.

*Kick Off 3* was written by [Steve Screech](/vault/people/steve-screech) after Dini had gone to Virgin. *Amiga Power*'s verdict, at 69%:

> At least they've tried to be different: **the absence of Dino Dini has also spelt the end of the pinbally feel to this series.** But it's all a bit of a mess.

That sentence is doing something unusual. A magazine is naming a **person's absence** as the cause of a **design property's disappearance** — and treating the loss as a loss, in the same breath as noting the new game had tried to be different.

The thing critics had complained about for four years turned out to be the thing holding the series up, and it was identifiably one man's.

## Not a British story

*Kick Off*'s competitive community had its centre in **Italy and the Netherlands**, and outlasted the Amiga. This library's Italian material shows it plainly: *Zeta* is still measuring new football games against Dini's in 1997, and the [Krisalis](/vault/companies/krisalis) entry records a Greek magazine using *Kick Off* as the benchmark a British game had to be judged against.

A history assembled from British sources alone records the game and misses that its centre of gravity moved abroad and stayed there.

## Why he matters

Dini is the entry for **a single mechanical decision as authorship** — the clearest case in this Vault of one property, chosen deliberately and defended against criticism, carrying an entire series.

He is also the reason the [Anco](/vault/companies/anco) entry is about a design and not a company. Anco published *Kick Off*; Dini is what made it *Kick Off*, and the sequel made without him is the evidence.
