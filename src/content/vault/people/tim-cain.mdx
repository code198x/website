In early 1994 Tim Cain, a programmer at [Interplay](/vault/companies/interplay), started building a role-playing game prototype out of graphics borrowed from the company's other games. Three and a half years later it came out as *[Fallout](/vault/games/fallout)*, and *Computer Gaming World* called it "easily the best role-playing game released in the past year". Cain led it the whole way, as project leader, producer and lead programmer. Then, early in 1998, while work on the sequel was starting, he left. He and two colleagues set up their own studio, Troika Games, which made three ambitious, famously buggy role-playing games in six years and then closed. More than twenty years after *Fallout*, he was co-directing *The Outer Worlds* for Obsidian, and in 2023 he began telling the stories behind all of it on YouTube.

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1965, according to public reference sources.
- **Interplay:** programmer; project leader, producer and lead programmer of *[Fallout](/vault/games/fallout)* (1997). Left in early 1998.
- **Troika Games:** co-founded in 1998 with Leonard Boyarsky and Jason Anderson; *[Arcanum](/vault/games/arcanum)* (2001), *The Temple of Elemental Evil* (2003), *[Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines](/vault/games/vampire-the-masquerade-bloodlines)* (2004). Closed in 2005.
- **Later:** Carbine Studios; [Obsidian Entertainment](/vault/companies/obsidian-entertainment) from 2011, where he co-directed *The Outer Worlds* (2019).
- **YouTube:** regular videos about game development since 2023.

## Fallout

*Fallout* began, *Wireframe* wrote, in early 1994, "when then-Interplay employee Tim Cain started developing a prototype using some pre-existing assets from other Interplay games". It was pitched as a spiritual successor to Brian Fargo's *[Wasteland](/vault/games/wasteland)*, with the same post-apocalyptic setting and the same freedom to be as good or as bad as the player liked. According to *Edge*, the project survived two near-cancellations before Fargo took a build home for a weekend, gave the game his blessing and told Cain: "You should call this Fallout."

For most of its development *Fallout* was meant to use GURPS, Steve Jackson Games' tabletop role-playing rules. Early in 1997, after Jackson began vetoing design decisions, Interplay dropped the licence over "creative differences" and wrote its own rules, which became SPECIAL. The decision was not Cain's idea. "I was the one who wanted GURPS in the first place," he told *Computer Gaming World*. "I set out to make a GURPS game, so I don't want people to get the idea that I want to toss GURPS out."

The game came out in October 1997. *Computer Gaming World* credited its design to Cain, Chris Taylor, Chris Jones and Leonard Boyarsky, and in 2000 called it "brilliantly conceived and executed by Tim Cain, Leonard Boyarsky, Jason Anderson, and the rest of the team", "a minor miracle, and easily one of the best games of the 90's".

Cain did not stay for the sequel. In April 1998 *PC Zone* reported that "Tim Cain, producer of Interplay's surprisingly good RPG Fallout, has quit working on the sequel and left to pursue his own interests", adding that Interplay insisted the split was amicable. *Computer Gaming World* later referred to his "abrupt departure". *Fallout 2* came out later in 1998 under Interplay's Black Isle label.

## Troika Games

Troika Games was founded in April 1998 by Cain, Boyarsky and Jason Anderson, who had both worked on *Fallout*'s art. Cain spent six months looking for a publisher and chose Sierra, he said, "for one reason—trust."

That game was *[Arcanum: Of Steamworks and Magick Obscura](/vault/games/arcanum)* (2001), a fantasy world in the middle of an industrial revolution, where magic and machinery work against each other. *PC Zone* called it "Jules Verne meets JRR Tolkien" and introduced Cain as "the lead programmer/designer of Fallout". *Computer Gaming World*'s preview recognised the *Fallout* team's hand at once, from the view from above to the row of action-point bubbles in turn-based fights. Asked how many hours had gone into the game, Cain said: "A back of the envelope calculation revealed 100,000 man-hours. Oh my god."

Troika followed it with *The Temple of Elemental Evil* (2003), an adaptation of a classic *Dungeons & Dragons* module published by Atari, which Cain led, and *[Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines](/vault/games/vampire-the-masquerade-bloodlines)* (2004), published by Activision and built on Valve's Source engine, on which Cain was one of the programmers. Both shipped with serious bugs. *Computer Gaming World* complained that *Bloodlines* had "so many rough edges", from slow loading to broken enemy behaviour, while praising the "nightmarish" Los Angeles it created. Troika could not find a publisher for another game and closed in early 2005. *Retro Gamer* later recorded that the studio had closed "after going bust".

## Obsidian and The Outer Worlds

Cain moved into online games, as design director at Carbine Studios, which was making the online role-playing game *WildStar*. In 2011 he joined [Obsidian Entertainment](/vault/companies/obsidian-entertainment), the studio founded by former Black Isle staff, first as a programmer; he worked on its crowdfunded *[Pillars of Eternity](/vault/games/pillars-of-eternity)*. With Boyarsky he then co-directed *The Outer Worlds* (2019), a science-fiction role-playing game about colonists living under corporate rule. *Wireframe* noted that it was "directed by Tim Cain and Leonard Boyarsky, two developers who played a key role in making the original Fallout", and Obsidian's Brian Patel told the magazine that the two were its "game directors for this". From mid-2020 Cain worked for Obsidian on contract rather than as a full-time employee, including on *The Outer Worlds 2*. In December 2025 he announced that he had returned to the studio full time.

## Cain on Games

On 27 April 2023 Cain posted the first video of a series he called *Cain on Games*, and then posted almost daily. He explained the timing: "I'm now 57 years and 9 months old. That was the exact age my father was when he passed away." He wanted his stories "documented somewhere". The videos cover his time at Interplay, Troika and Obsidian, including why he left during *Fallout 2*, what it has been like to be a gay developer in the industry, making games with full colour blindness, and Troika's unmade projects.
