Ken Levine came to games from the theatre and from Hollywood, and he has spent the thirty years since making games that carry an argument. His *[BioShock](/vault/games/bioshock)* (2007) put a shooter in an underwater city built on Ayn Rand's ideas and then turned its most ordinary instruction, "would you kindly", against the player. Before that he had written the first designs for *[Thief](/vault/games/thief)* and led *[System Shock 2](/vault/games/system-shock-2)* from a studio that began in his living room.

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1966, in New York.
- **Before games:** drama at Vassar College, then screenwriting in Los Angeles.
- **[Looking Glass](/vault/companies/looking-glass), 1995–1997:** early story and designs for what became *Thief: The Dark Project* (1998).
- **[Irrational Games](/vault/companies/irrational-games), 1997–2014:** co-founder with Jonathan Chey and Rob Fermier; lead designer of *System Shock 2* (1999); creative director of *BioShock* (2007) and *BioShock Infinite* (2013).
- **Ghost Story Games, 2017–:** president and creative director; *Judas*, announced December 2022.

## From plays to Looking Glass

Levine studied drama at Vassar, where he wrote and staged plays, and then went to Los Angeles to write for films. He told his audience at the PAX games festival in 2008 that the high point was a commission for a romantic comedy, which did not turn out well. "I was a decent writer," he told *Boston Magazine* in 2013. "But I knew nothing about storytelling." In 1995, aged 29, he answered a job advert in a games magazine and became a designer at Looking Glass Studios in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

He spent two years there, in *Edge*'s words "developing the story concepts and game designs that would eventually become Thief". *Retro Gamer*'s history of *Thief* calls him the project's starter: before it became *Dark Camelot* in the spring of 1996, he had written design documents for games called *School of Wizards*, *Dark Elves Must Die* and a "communist zombie game", *Better Red Than Undead*. The designer Randy Smith said that Levine "established some of the earliest thematic elements, factions and highest-level plot trajectory". Levine left before *Thief* was finished, but *Edge* was still calling him "the writer of Thief" in 2006. Of working with Doug Church at Looking Glass, he said it "was like going to college and studying with Einstein".

## Irrational Games and System Shock 2

In 1997 Levine, Jonathan Chey and Rob Fermier left Looking Glass and started Irrational Games in Levine's living room. Their first contract, the single-player part of Multitude's shooter *FireTeam*, was cancelled after three weeks. "I'm living in my apartment in Cambridge without a dime to my name, we didn't have any start-up money," Levine told *Edge*, and the three thought the company was finished. The next week Looking Glass's Paul Neurath rang to offer them its *Thief* engine. The three of them and a contract artist built a prototype of a science-fiction role-playing game called *Shock* in two months; Electronic Arts, which part-owned the *System Shock* name, signed it, and it became *System Shock 2*.

Levine was its lead designer and writer. Talking to *PC Zone* before release, he named an unexpected source: "We were very influenced by an old paper game called Traveller. It was serious sci-fi - not fantasy sci-fi". He wanted "suspense and terror" rather than "gross-out gore horror". The rogue computer SHODAN, who returns in it, came from Looking Glass's *[System Shock](/vault/games/system-shock)* of 1994. *System Shock 2* came out in August 1999, made "in about a year", as he put it, with "no time to polish and balance". Critics loved it and sales were modest; the [System Shock 2](/vault/games/system-shock-2) entry tells that story.

The studio nearly ended there. "When we finished Shock 2, both my business partners said: 'This was great, we're exhausted, see you later.' And I was running the company by myself with no staff," Levine told *Edge* in 2005. He kept it going with the superhero game he had "always wanted to do", *Freedom Force* (2002), built in Boston and at a new studio in Canberra that Chey set up in 2000 after going home to Australia. A survival-horror retelling of Dante's *Inferno* for the PlayStation 2, *The Lost*, was never released. By 2005 Irrational had made *Tribes: Vengeance* and was finishing *SWAT 4*.

## BioShock

Irrational kept coming back to the idea of another *Shock* game. *Edge*'s history of *BioShock* describes a long start: by 2002 the team had only a basic system of three groups, "protectors, harvesters and drones carrying a valuable resource", which grew into the game's Big Daddies, Little Sisters and splicers. Pitches set in an abandoned Nazi genetics laboratory or a science-fiction setting were turned down by Codemasters, Atari and EA before 2K Games, part of Take-Two, signed the project in 2004. Take-Two then bought Irrational, whose studios became 2K Boston and 2K Australia, and the money turned a small role-playing game made by a team of 30 into a shooter made by a core team of 90.

The setting came from New York. Walking around the Rockefeller Center, with its art deco towers and statue of Atlas, Levine began to imagine a city in that style, and the story of Rockefeller finishing the project alone after the Depression fed into *BioShock*'s Andrew Ryan. Levine was not sure it would work. In February 2007 he watched a focus group mock the game from behind a one-way mirror: "It was pretty depressing." It came out that August. *Edge*'s review found in Ryan influences "from Orwell to Ayn Rand (note the initials)", and the game's late revelation turns on the phrase "would you kindly", with which another character had been steering the player all along. Levine was frank about his taste in stories: "I'm a pretentious jerk, so I write things that pretentious jerks like myself would come up with. Sometimes it works and sometimes it doesn't."

*BioShock Infinite* (2013) moved the series from Rapture to Columbia, a city floating in the sky, and gave the player a companion, Elizabeth. Levine told *Edge* that the team had begun with a prototype it was unhappy with, and had ruled out another *BioShock* until someone asked, "What if it wasn't in Rapture?"

## Ghost Story Games and Judas

On 18 February 2014, less than a year after *Infinite*'s release, Levine announced that he was "winding down Irrational Games as you know it", keeping "about fifteen members of the Irrational team" for "a smaller, more entrepreneurial endeavor at Take-Two" that would "make narrative-driven games for the core gamer that are highly replayable", sold only as downloads. He handed the *BioShock* series to 2K. In February 2017 the small studio took a new name, Ghost Story Games, with twelve founding staff and Levine as president and creative director.

Its first game, *Judas*, was announced at The Game Awards on 8 December 2022 for PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S and PC: a first-person game set on "a disintegrating starship", in which the player has "to make or break alliances with your worst enemies". Development had been long. In January 2022 *Wireframe*, following a Bloomberg report, noted that nothing of the game had been shown since the team formed in 2014. Previewing it in January 2026, *Game Informer* found the release date still to be announced, and Levine described it as "our first game where you truly inhabit a character in a way you didn't in … say BioShock", with characters built to respond to "the smallest details" of what the player does.
