*Amiga Power*'s review header for *Kick Off 3* is short: "Publisher: [Anco](/vault/companies/anco). **Author: Steve Screech.** Runs on: A1200. Price: £26." Screech took over the most argued-about football series on the Amiga after [Dino Dini](/vault/people/dino-dini) had left it, and the reception is the most instructive part of his entry.

## Fast facts

- **Programmed:** *Kick Off 3* (1994) for Anco, on A1200, at **£26**.
- **Named in:** *[Amiga Power](/vault/magazines/amiga-power)*, and in the Italian press, which followed the series closely.
- **The Italian magazine *Zeta*** notes he took up the routines that made the earlier games successful.

## Inheriting a franchise

*Kick Off*'s defining feature was that **the ball is not stuck to the player's feet**. That made it hard, fast and deep, and its critics called the result pinball.

*Kick Off 3* moved to a side-on view and fixed that. *Amiga Power*'s verdict is precise about what happened: the game "**successfully defeats the 'pinball' feel of the previous games**" — and the magazine then recommended a competitor instead.

The trap is a general one and worth naming. A franchise's most-criticised characteristic is often the same thing as its most-loved one, seen from outside. The people who complain are usually not the people who buy it. **Fixing the complaint can remove the reason anyone was there**, and a programmer inheriting somebody else's series is the person most likely to be told to do exactly that.

## Reviewed in one page

*Amiga Power* also made its opinion clear structurally before saying a word. The review runs to **one page**, and the magazine says so: that is "an excellent pointer to the importance we attach to the game", adding that it "was hardly awaited with" enthusiasm.

There is a piece of publisher behaviour recorded alongside it. *AP* reports that Anco held review copies back, having "decided that the computer press couldn't be trusted not to copy the game and distribute it to their piratey friends" — a delay the magazine treated with open sarcasm.

That is worth keeping as evidence about the period's press-publisher relationship. A magazine confident enough to print that, about a publisher it depended on for advertising, is a magazine whose scores meant something.

## Continental attention

The Italian magazine *Zeta* reviewed the PC version in 1997, eight years after the first *Kick Off*, still treating the series as the benchmark and noting that Screech had built on the routines behind the original's success.

That is the second time the [Anco](/vault/companies/anco) story leaves Britain. The *Kick Off* community's centre of gravity was in Italy and the Netherlands, and its judgement of the sequel was recorded there long after the British press had lost interest.

## Why he matters

Screech drew **inheriting a franchise**, one of the least sympathetic jobs in games: the previous author's decisions are the thing people love, changing them is what you were hired to do, and either way the comparison is not with your work.
