The man who gave the world *[Resident Evil](/vault/games/resident-evil)* does not, by his own account, enjoy being frightened. When his boss at [Capcom](/vault/companies/capcom), [Tokuro Fujiwara](/vault/people/tokuro-fujiwara), handed him a horror game to direct in 1993, *Wireframe* reported, Shinji Mikami was unenthusiastic: he had no experience of horror and "didn't even like being scared by entertainment products". He made it anyway. *Resident Evil* was his debut as a director, its team was mostly new to Capcom, and a consultant's report recommended cancelling it. It sold millions, and Capcom's marketing phrase for it, "survival horror", became the name of a genre. Nine years later he took the series apart and rebuilt it as *Resident Evil 4*, a game that *Edge* credited with "inspiring a new generation of action titles".

## Fast facts

- **Born:** 1965, in Yamaguchi prefecture, Japan.
- **Capcom (1990–2006):** planner on Disney games for the Super Nintendo; director of *Resident Evil* (1996), the GameCube *Resident Evil* (2002), *P.N.03* (2003) and *Resident Evil 4* (2005); producer of *Resident Evil 2* (1998) and *Dino Crisis* (1999); director of *God Hand* (2006) at Clover Studio.
- **Platinum Games (2007–2010):** a founding member; director of *Vanquish* (2010).
- **Tango Gameworks (2010–2023):** founder; director of *The Evil Within* (2014).
- **Since 2023:** his own company, KAMUY.

## From quiz games to Disney

Mikami was born in Yamaguchi prefecture, dreamed of being a Formula One driver, and studied at Doshisha University, where, *Wireframe* noted, his subject was "the study of merchandise". He did not start playing video games seriously until he was 20. Capcom hired him about halfway through 1990, a week or two after telling him he was not getting a job there. His first game, made in three months, was a Game Boy quiz, *Capcom Quiz: Hatena? no Daibouken*, in which Capcom characters such as Firebrand and Dr Wily try to stop the player answering correctly.

Disney licences followed. Mikami worked on *Who Framed Roger Rabbit* for the Game Boy, and on the Super Nintendo he was the lead designer of *Goof Troop* and a planner on Capcom's *Aladdin* (both 1993). *Retro Gamer* found that *Goof Troop* "borrows heavily from A Link To The Past, but that only assures its quality".

## Resident Evil

In 1993 Mikami was asked to head a horror project built on the system of *Sweet Home*, Fujiwara's Famicom game of 1989. "Biohazard's starting point came when my boss at the time — Fujiwara-san — told me to make a horror title using the game system of Sweet Home," he told *Retro Gamer*. He started planning in January 1994, dropped his first idea of a game full of ghosts, and settled on zombies after remembering *Dawn of the Dead*. For the first six months, he said, "I was the 'team'"; by the end more than 50 people were working on it.

He placed the fixed cameras to hide the enemy. "Even if you knew before looking round a corner that a zombie was going to be there, we set up blind spots so that players wouldn't be able to see the zombies [immediately] and that in turn produced an uneasy feeling that caused players to feel afraid," he said. "I remember thinking if the enemies were only scary because of the possibility of 'game over', it wouldn't be sufficient for me to reach my goal with Biohazard."

The pressure was real. "I knew that if Biohazard wasn't a big success there was a danger that Capcom would face bankruptcy," Mikami told *Retro Gamer*, and when it was finished he "seemed to be the only one who was completely burnt out". He still dislikes the Western title, which Capcom's American office chose without him: it is, he said, "stupid". The full story of the game, from the Saturn version to the "Jill sandwich", is in the [Resident Evil](/vault/games/resident-evil) entry.

## Producer and general manager

Success moved Mikami out of the director's chair. On *Resident Evil 2* (1998) he was producer and Hideki Kamiya directed for the first time; Mikami, *Retro Gamer* wrote, "simply oversaw Kamiya's work and made changes wherever necessary". By the summer of 1999 *Edge* counted four million *Resident Evil* games sold and described Mikami as "instrumental in restructuring Capcom from its 2D beat 'em up past". He was producing *Dino Crisis*, a survival horror game with dinosaurs in real-time 3D rooms, and *Resident Evil 3 Nemesis*, and overseeing *Code: Veronica* for the Dreamcast. "People call me on my mobile phone when they need me. That's why I've lost so much weight," he joked. *Edge* and the *Official U.S. PlayStation Magazine* credited him as producer of *Dino Crisis*; later profiles, including *Wireframe*'s, list it among the games he directed. *Wireframe* puts its sales at 2.4 million.

He explained the difference between the two series to the *Official U.S. PlayStation Magazine*: in *Dino Crisis* "you are constantly chased by huge, agile, overwhelmingly powerful creatures. It's a panicking horror. In Resident Evil, you guard yourself against slow-crawling zombies."

*Devil May Cry* (2001) began, the Australian magazine *PlayStation 2* reported, as Mikami's attempt to reinvent *Resident Evil* for the PlayStation 2, and grew into something else. Kamiya directed it, with Mikami, in the words of New Zealand's *Official PlayStation Magazine*, "at the helm". Looking back in 2010, *Edge* summed up this side of his career as "producer on countless titles from Steel Battalion to Viewtiful Joe".

## The GameCube and Resident Evil 4

In 2001 Mikami took the series to Nintendo. *Edge* reported Shigeru Miyamoto appearing in person "to endorse Shinji Mikami's decision to port Biohazard to GameCube", and Mikami directed a near-complete remake of the first game for it in 2002. His studio, Production Studio 4, also promised Nintendo five GameCube games, later called the Capcom Five: *P.N.03*, which Mikami directed, *Viewtiful Joe*, *Killer7*, *Resident Evil 4* and *Dead Phoenix*, which was cancelled. *Edge* called Mikami "widely seen to be the catalyst" for the deal. He was sure of it. "Resident Evil 4 will definitely release only on GameCube," he said in 2002. "If it [comes to another console], I will cut my head off." When Capcom announced a PlayStation 2 version at the end of 2004, *Electronic Gaming Monthly* quoted him and added: "Time to sharpen the axe."

*Resident Evil 4* took years to find. Capcom's producer Hiroyuki Kobayashi told *Edge* that the finished game was "actually the fourth version", and Mikami was asked to return as its director. He moved the camera over Leon Kennedy's shoulder and set the game in a European village, and, Kobayashi said, it was "Mikami's idea!" that the villagers should speak Spanish, so that Leon and the player would face "a language they could not understand". *Edge* found that it "unmistakably feels like a Mikami game - aggressive" and, looking back in 2012, called it "Mikami's bold reinvention of the series", which "swept away inertia, reinvigorating the survival horror genre while inspiring a new generation of action titles". The control scheme it kept from the older games is explained in [tank controls](/vault/techniques/tank-controls).

## Clover and Platinum

In 2004 the producer Atsushi Inaba set up Clover Studio inside Capcom. Asked by *Edge* what roles Kamiya and Mikami would take there, he said they were "simply" directors. Mikami's game there was *God Hand* (2006), a beat 'em up that, *Edge* thought, "looks for the ridiculous in the action genre and embraces it", and that although Mikami had not "gone soft", "he's certainly lightened up a little". Capcom dissolved Clover in October 2006. A year later Inaba, Kamiya and Mikami's holding company, Seeds, merged with another to form Platinum Games.

At Platinum Mikami directed *Vanquish* (2010), a fast third-person shooter in which an armoured soldier boosts across the battlefield against an army made entirely of robots. At its launch in Tokyo he mocked a US website for its review of *God Hand* and told the audience that "this is not P.N.03". *Edge* interviewed him again that year about *Shadows of the Damned*, which he made with Goichi Suda's Grasshopper Manufacture and which came out in 2011. Suda, he said, had the last word; Mikami was "in charge of the game's balance, systems and mechanics". Working on it while finishing *Vanquish* and founding a studio of his own "was hell on Earth! You have no idea."

## Tango Gameworks

Mikami left Platinum after *Vanquish* to set up his own studio, Tango Gameworks. "Mr Mikami always wanted to be his own man, and his own developer," Platinum's chief executive told *Edge*. Tango became part of ZeniMax, the owner of Bethesda, as its only studio in Japan, and its first game was *The Evil Within* (2014), which Mikami directed: his return to the genre his first game had named. "Not much has changed when it comes to instilling terror in the player," he told *Edge* in 2013. "But people have got used to the tropes of horror and they know what's coming next, so in that sense it is harder to make them afraid." His answer was enemies that bullets could not stop: "Even if you shoot them they won't die, so you'll have to run or use a trap to beat them. That should be fun — and also scary."

Tango, he said, was also a school. He named three staff he hoped would become directors and said it might take five to ten years to build a really strong team. After *The Evil Within* he produced rather than directed: Tango's later games were *The Evil Within 2* (2017), *Ghostwire: Tokyo* (2022) and the rhythm-action game *Hi-Fi Rush* (2023). In February 2023 Bethesda announced that Mikami would leave Tango "in the coming months", thanking him as "a creative leader and supportive mentor to young developers". A year later Microsoft, which had bought ZeniMax, closed the studio, and in August 2024 the Korean publisher Krafton bought it and the *Hi-Fi Rush* series.

By then Mikami had a new company. In March 2024 *Gematsu* reported that he had set up KAMUY, registered in Tokyo in October 2023.
