The YM2612 brought FM synthesis to the Sega Mega Drive, providing six channels of Yamaha FM sound plus one channel for digitised samples. Its metallic, distinctive timbres defined an era of gaming audio—bright leads, punchy bass, and that unmistakable Genesis sound. Composers learned to exploit its strengths while working around its limitations.

## Fast facts

- **Manufacturer:** Yamaha.
- **Technology:** FM (frequency modulation) synthesis.
- **Channels:** 6 FM. The DAC replaces channel 6 rather than adding to it.
- **Operators:** 4 per channel; channels 3 and 6 can give each operator its own frequency.
- **Algorithms:** 8 FM operator configurations.
- **DAC:** 8-bit sample playback, timed entirely in software.
- **Stereo:** any FM or DAC voice can go left, right or both — though on the Mega Drive itself only the headphone jack carries it.

## Technical specifications

YM2612 capabilities:
- **Operators:** 4 sine wave oscillators per channel.
- **Algorithms:** different operator connections.
- **LFO:** one low-frequency oscillator for vibrato/tremolo; each channel picks how far it is affected, if at all.
- **Timers:** two, and they do not raise interrupts — software has to poll them to find out whether they have finished counting.

### Channels 3 and 6 are the odd ones

The six channels come as two sets of three, and the last of each set can give
every operator its own frequency instead of an integer multiple of the channel's.
Sega's manual offers percussion as the reason, and does not pretend to certainty
about it: the technique "works well (we believe) for percussion instruments,
which have harmonics at odd multiples such as 1.4 or 1.7 of the fundamental".

## The Genesis sound

Distinctive characteristics:
- **Metallic timbres:** FM synthesis signature.
- **Punchy bass:** tight, defined low end.
- **Bright leads:** cutting high-frequency content.
- **DAC samples:** used for drums, voices.

The DAC is also the source of the platform's hardest audio constraint. It does
not add a seventh voice — it replaces FM channel 6 — and because every sample
byte has to be clocked out by software, Sega's own manual warns that "unless the
software is very cleverly constructed, it is impossible to use any of the FM
channels at the same time as the DAC". Getting sampled drums *and* a full FM
arrangement out of a Mega Drive is a programming achievement, not a setting.
