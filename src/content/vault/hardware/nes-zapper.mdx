The **NES Zapper** was Nintendo's light gun controller for the Nintendo Entertainment System, bundled with Duck Hunt in millions of console packages. Its distinctive orange design (grey in Japan and early US models) became iconic, and for many players it was their first experience with light gun gaming.

The Zapper worked only with CRT televisions, and for two separate reasons: its light sensor is filtered to the CRT scanline frequency, and light gun games assume a display with no lag. Flat panels broke both assumptions, which is what made it obsolete.

- **Colour:** Grey (early), orange (later, required by US law)

## How it worked

**The Zapper does not report where it is pointing.** It reports one bit — light,
or no light, right now. Everything else is the game's doing.

1. The game waits for the trigger.
2. During vertical blank it checks that the gun is *not* seeing light. If it is,
   the player is aiming at a lamp; if bit 3 is false, there is probably no
   Zapper plugged in at all.
3. The game then draws a black screen with a single white box on **one** target,
   and watches the light-sense bit for that whole frame.
4. Hit or miss recorded, it moves to the next target and repeats.

So a duck is identified by elimination, one frame per candidate, not by the gun
measuring an angle. Scanline timing appears only as an optional shortcut — flash
everything white first to learn roughly how far down the screen the gun is
aimed, and skip the targets that cannot match.

This works only on a CRT. The photodiode is filtered to roughly 15 kHz, the CRT
scanline frequency, which rejects room lighting but also rejects most of the
signal from an LCD; and the whole scheme assumes the television draws the frame
with no delay.

### The trigger has three states

Not a switch. The official Zapper returns 0 with the trigger fully open, 1 with
it halfway in, and 0 again once it is "pulled all the way to where it goes
clunk". A 10 µF capacitor inside the gun against a 10 kΩ pull-up in the console
means about 100 ms to fall back to released. Clone guns replace this with a
plain switch, and mostly get away with it because games fire on the 1→0
transition either way.

The Zapper supported several titles:

## Design evolution

The Zapper changed appearance:

- **1985:** Grey (resembled realistic firearm)
- **1989:** Orange (US toy safety regulations)
- **Famicom version:** Different styling

The orange colour became so iconic that it's now synonymous with the accessory.

## Legacy

The Zapper established conventions:

- Light guns as bundled accessories
- Shooting galleries as casual games
- The "point at TV and shoot" experience

Its technology limitations (CRT-only) eventually ended the light gun era on consoles.
