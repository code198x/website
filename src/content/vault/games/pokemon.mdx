"It can be tempting to overlook that Pokémon Red and Blue were indie underdogs once," *Edge* wrote in 2010, "made by a tiny, almost-bankrupt team for a technologically limited handheld seemingly at the end of its lifecycle." [Satoshi Tajiri](/vault/people/satoshi-tajiri)'s company, [Game Freak](/vault/companies/game-freak), spent about six years on a Game Boy role-playing game about catching monsters, and Nintendo, Tajiri later said, "couldn't really understand the concept of the game". It came out in Japan in February 1996 with little fuss. Thirty years later the series it started is one of the largest in games: by the end of March 2026, The Pokémon Company counted more than 515 million units of Pokémon software shipped, more than 85 billion trading cards printed and an animated series shown in over 190 countries and regions.

This entry covers the series. The first games have their own entry, [Pokémon Red & Blue](/vault/games/pokemon-red-blue), and the craze that followed them is told in [the Pokémon phenomenon](/vault/phenomena/pokemon-phenomenon).

## Fast facts

- **Creator:** [Satoshi Tajiri](/vault/people/satoshi-tajiri).
- **Developer:** [Game Freak](/vault/companies/game-freak), which has made every main-series game.
- **Publisher:** [Nintendo](/vault/companies/nintendo). The rights are shared by Nintendo, [Creatures](/vault/companies/creatures-inc) and Game Freak, which together set up the company renamed The Pokémon Company in 2000.
- **First release:** *Pocket Monsters Red* and *Green*, Game Boy, Japan, 27 February 1996; *Red* and *Blue* in America in September 1998 and in Britain in October 1999.
- **Sales:** over 143 million games by 2006 (*Retro Gamer*); more than 515 million units of Pokémon software by March 2026 (The Pokémon Company).
- **Spin-offs:** the trading card game (1996), the anime (1997), films (from 1998) and *Pokémon GO* (2016).

## How the games work

The idea came from two places. Tajiri had collected insects as a boy, and he saw a use for the Game Boy's [link cable](/vault/hardware/link-cable) that nobody else had: "I thought of actual living organisms moving back and forth across the cable," he told *TIME* in 1999. "But everyone was using it to compete. The idea I had was for information to go back and forth."

The main games have kept the shape of the first. The player sets out from a small town with one monster, catches wild Pokémon by weakening them in turn-based battles and throwing a ball, and works through a region's Gym Leaders towards a final group of champions. Battles turn on types: water beats fire, electric beats water, and so on. A Pokémon that loses faints rather than dies. The player carries a team of six. And each generation comes as a pair of cartridges, each holding Pokémon the other lacks, so that completing the collection means trading with a friend.

*Edge*, reviewing *Ruby* and *Sapphire* in 2003, thought the slogan oversold it. "Pokémon is built on a lie. You haven't 'gotta catch 'em all'. Very few people do, relying instead on a SWAT team of their favourite six." What kept players going was depth beneath a plain surface: in 2010 the same magazine argued that rivals "have always failed to understand that, ultimately, it's the complexity that makes Pokémon so consuming", and that the basic presentation "leaves room for the imagination".

## The generations

Each new pair of main games brought a new region and new species, and usually a new Nintendo handheld. The Japanese release dates are The Pokémon Company's.

*Edge* described Game Freak's approach to change bluntly in 2003: each generation "inherits all the elements of its predecessor, and develops its own elaborations". *Ruby* and *Sapphire* added beauty contests for Pokémon, two-on-two battles and secret bases to decorate, and *Edge* called the result "probably the most intricate and detailed console RPG available", giving it eight out of ten. *X* and *Y* in 2013 were released around the world on the same day. Alongside the pairs came other kinds of Pokémon game, such as *Pokémon Legends: Arceus* (2022) and *Pokémon Legends: Z-A* (2025).

## Beyond the games

The games were only part of it from early on. The trading card game appeared in Japan in October 1996, and the animated series began on TV Tokyo in April 1997; the first film followed in July 1998. American players met all of it at once in 1998 and 1999. Competitive play grew up around the battles: in 2000 Britain's *Nintendo Official Magazine* reported a Pokémon World Championships in Sydney, held during the Olympics, and The Pokémon Company records holding the World Championships in 2009.

In July 2016 Pokémon reached mobile phones with *Pokémon GO*, developed by Niantic. In 2019 a live-action film, *Pokémon Detective Pikachu*, reached cinemas.
