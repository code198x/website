*Flashback* was meant to be *The Godfather*. In 1991 US Gold's chief executive, Geoff Brown, had bought the licence and asked Delphine Software in Paris to make the game. Paul Cuisset, who had just finished *Cruise for a Corpse*, did not want another enclosed, old-fashioned setting, so he suggested moving the plot into the future. Brown agreed, and by the time the first level reached him eight months later Michael Corleone had become Conrad B. Hart, an agent who wakes in a jungle on Saturn's moon Titan with his memory wiped. Nobody at US Gold asked for the Mafia back. Brown suggested the title instead, *Flashback: The Quest for Identity*.

What the players of 1993 noticed first was the movement. Conrad runs, jumps, hangs from ledges, rolls and draws his pistol in animation traced from film of a real person, and *The One* called the game "the closest we've come yet to actually playing the movie".

## Fast facts

- **Developer:** [Delphine Software](/vault/companies/delphine-software), Paris.
- **Designer:** Paul Cuisset, who also wrote the story; programmers included Cuisset, Philippe Chastel and Frédéric Savoir.
- **Artists:** Denis Mercier, Christian Robert and Thierry Perreau, later joined by Thierry Levastre, Fabrice Visserot and Patrick Daher.
- **Publisher:** [US Gold](/vault/companies/us-gold).
- **First release:** Amiga, in France, late 1992. Amiga, Mega Drive and PC versions followed in Britain and America in 1993.
- **Later versions:** Super Nintendo, Acorn Archimedes, Mega-CD, PC CD-ROM, CD-i, 3DO, Jaguar and Macintosh; many of them converted by [Tiertex](/vault/companies/tiertex).

## From The Godfather to Titan

*Edge*'s account of the game's making, published in 2006, tells how far the project drifted from its licence. Cuisset and Chastel built a new system that let them try ideas and play them straight away, and Delphine decided early to release the game on the Mega Drive as well as the Amiga, a new market for a company that had only made computer games. Cuisset "had been promised that the console market could bring millions of sales". When Brown played the first level, the game "had next to nothing to do with the licence", but he liked it and told the team to carry on.

The finished story borrows openly from the cinema. *The One*'s David Upchurch, visiting Delphine in September 1992, listed *They Live*, *Total Recall*, *Alien* and *Terminator 2* among its influences. Conrad has discovered, with a pair of glasses that measure molecular density, that some of the people running Earth are shape-shifting aliens, the Morphs. They capture him and wipe his memory; he escapes on a stolen hoverbike, is shot down over Titan, and finds a holocube recording of himself telling him what to do next. From there the game crosses a jungle, the city of New Washington, a televised death-match in the Death Tower, Earth and finally the Morphs' own planet, across seven levels.

## Animation from traced video

The team made Conrad move the way [Jordan Mechner](/vault/people/jordan-mechner) had made the prince move in *[Prince of Persia](/vault/games/prince-of-persia)*, a game *Edge* says strongly influenced Cuisset. They filmed one of their number in front of a white wall, ran the footage frame by frame on a television, traced the figure onto a transparency taped to the screen, then taped the transparency to a computer monitor and traced it again in Deluxe Paint. Chastel remembered 1,500 frames, 800 of them for Conrad; Cuisset remembered "crates of transparencies". The [rotoscoping](/vault/techniques/rotoscoping) entry tells the story in more detail, including the visit from Mechner that kept the tired artists going.

In 1992 Cuisset was less forthcoming. The characters, he told *The One*, "were animated using Rotoscoping, our Top Secret programming methods and our amazingly talented artists!" He did explain how the frames fitted in memory: all the bitmapped graphics were stored compressed, and "all the sprites use a unique high-speed decompression routine to get them from memory and onto the screen as quickly as possible". The cut-scenes between the action were drawn in polygons, like those of *[Another World](/vault/games/another-world)*, because polygon animation took far less memory than bitmaps; the Amiga version, he said, had "at least fifty such sequences", more than a cartridge could hold. Cuisset added that the graphics system was not Delphine's Cinématique engine from *Future Wars* but "a new system that's been specially designed for Flashback which is much more orientated towards doing animation."

Delphine and US Gold made the frame rate the selling point. *Amiga Format* reported Delphine's claim of "a main character animated at a rate of 24 frames per second", and a US Gold advertisement in *Sega Visions* in April 1993 called *Flashback* "the first game ever to pump awesome high-octane CD ROM performance from a cartridge".

## What it was like to play

*Flashback* looks like a platform game and plays more slowly and deliberately. Every move takes the time its animation takes, so the player plans a route screen by screen: hang from a ledge to see what is below, roll past a droid, draw the gun before landing, not after. *Edge* noted that the delay between command and movement suited Cuisset, who wanted "a slower, deeper game". Conrad carries a pistol with unlimited ammunition and a shield that each hit wears down. Between fights he talks to people, picks up and uses objects, earns credits for the fare home, and carries a teleporter he can throw ahead of himself.

It was much longer than *Another World*. A US Gold tester told *The One* in 1992 that he had finished *Another World* in five hours but had played *Flashback* for nearly fifty without reaching the halfway point, and Delphine claimed the game was six times the size.

Most versions played the same game. *3DO Magazine* noted that the 3DO version "adds no new levels or radically reworked graphics", and *ST Format*, reviewing an early Jaguar version in June 1994, found its graphics smaller than on other machines but the colours richer and the animation more fluid; "The Jaguar game makes this SNES version look ill." The history of US Gold also lists FM Towns and PC-98 versions for Japanese computers. A version for the iPhone followed in 2009.

## Reception

The British Amiga press gave it its highest praise. Simon Byron in *The One* wrote in March 1993: "Even though it's only March and there's over two-thirds of the year left, I can honestly state that Flashback is the game of the year." *Amiga Format* gave it 93 per cent the following month and called it "one of the slickest, best-looking, most wonderful-sounding, atmosphere-packed, futuristic arcade adventures ever seen", adding that it fitted on four disks "with hardly any swapping at all". *Amiga Power* chose it among its games of 1993.

The console and PC reviews agreed. *SNES Force* found the Super Nintendo conversion kept everything that had "blown away" its reviewer on the Amiga, and *Computer Gaming World* praised the "impressive animation derived from 24 frames-per-second rotoscoping — a truly uncanny thing to watch!" In August 1993 *Computer and Video Games*' charts had the Mega Drive version at number two and the Amiga version at number five, and *The One* had it third in the Amiga full-price chart.

No reliable sales figure was published at the time. The history of US Gold says only that *Flashback* on the Genesis was "a huge hit" in America in 1993. France's Centre national du cinéma called it the best-selling French video game in the world in 2021, without giving a number.

## Later history

Delphine followed it in 1995 with *Fade to Black*, a 3D sequel for the PC and PlayStation that brought Conrad back but was, in *Retro Gamer*'s view, "nowhere near as well received". By then [Éric Chahi](/vault/people/eric-chahi) had left Delphine and taken key members of the *Flashback* team, among them the programmer Frédéric Savoir, to form Amazing Studio.

Cuisset returned to the game twice. In 2013 his studio VectorCell, working with members of the original team, remade *Flashback* for Ubisoft on the Xbox 360, PlayStation 3 and PC, with new graphics and audio; *Edge* felt it lacked "the 1992 game's rotoscoped charm", and reviews were mixed. In 2018 Microids published *Flashback 25th Anniversary*, the original game with a choice between the 1993 version and a modern mode with graphic filters, remastered sound and a rewind feature, first on the Nintendo Switch in June 2018 and then on other machines.

A full sequel, *Flashback 2*, followed on 16 November 2023 for the PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S and PC, developed by Microids' studios in Lyon and Paris with Cuisset leading the design. Critics were unkind: Metacritic's averages sat in the mid-thirties out of a hundred, with reviewers citing bugs and flat, linear design.
