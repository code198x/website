In a Persona game the monsters can be talked to. The first one, *Revelations: Persona* (1996), sent a group of Japanese high-school students against demons from another dimension, and gave them a list of things to try before fighting: *GamePro*'s strategy guide advised staring at some, being sarcastic to others, and dancing for one called Macabre. Talk your way through and a demon might hand over a spell card instead of a fight. The idea came from [Atlus](/vault/companies/atlus)'s *[Megami Tensei](/vault/games/shin-megami-tensei)* games. What Persona added was the teenagers, and the Personas themselves: figures summoned from the characters' own minds, an idea *Edge* called "a nod to Jungian psychology".

Ten years later *Persona 3* gave the series the shape it has kept. By day the player goes to school, joins clubs and makes friends; by night those friendships make the Personas stronger in a monster-filled tower. *Persona 5* and its expanded edition had sold more than ten million copies by 2025.

## Fast facts

- **Developer and publisher:** [Atlus](/vault/companies/atlus).
- **Parent series:** *[Megami Tensei](/vault/games/shin-megami-tensei)*.
- **Main games:** *Revelations: Persona* (1996), *Persona 2: Innocent Sin* (1999) and *Eternal Punishment* (2000), *Persona 3* (2006), *Persona 4* (2008), *Persona 5* (2016).
- **Art:** Kazuma Kaneko for the early games; Shigenori Soejima from *Persona 3*.
- **Sales:** *Persona 5* and *Persona 5 Royal*, 10.46 million together by the end of August 2025.

## Revelations: Persona

*Megami Ibunroku Persona* came out on the PlayStation in Japan in September 1996, and Atlus brought it to America as *Revelations: Persona* that December. It was the first *Megami Tensei* game released there, and the advertisement said so: "first time to hit US shores". It promised "over 300 different monsters", "100+ hours of pulse pounding gameplay", several endings, and the chance to "morph any member of your party into a more powerful source known as 'Persona'". *GamePro*'s reviewer, in February 1997, thought it might "even challenge the upcoming Final Fantasy VII as the season's best RPG".

The American version was not a straight translation. Characters and places were renamed, the hero's hairstyle and some characters' ethnic origins were changed, and one whole storyline, the Snow Queen quest, was cut. When the game was remade for the PSP in 2009 as *Shin Megami Tensei: Persona*, those changes were dropped and the Snow Queen quest restored. In 2018 Sony included the original on its PlayStation Classic.

## Persona 2 and rumours

*Persona 2* came in two halves. *Innocent Sin* (1999) began, the *Official US PlayStation Magazine* reported, with rumours that a local high school's insignia was cursed, and it was fifth in the Japanese PlayStation sales chart for July 1999. Its sequel, *Eternal Punishment* (2000), followed a magazine reporter, Maya Amano, investigating murders by a figure called the Joker. Rumours were the mechanic as well as the plot: in a bar the player picks up a rumour, spreads it around town and watches the effects. *Video Games* gave the examples of a model zeppelin that cannot fly and a fortune teller who cannot tell fortunes. *Eternal Punishment* was released in English, and Germany's *Video Games*, reviewing the American import, called the rumour system a very interesting feature and the game one about reality "and the masks that each of us wears".

## Persona 3: school by day

*Persona 3* (2006) was a new start. Its director, Katsura Hashino, wrote much of the scenario, and Kaneko's assistant Shigenori Soejima took over the art with modern-looking characters. *Edge*'s review explained the premise: "there are, in fact, 25 hours to a day". During the extra "dark hour" most people are sealed in coffins while monsters roam, and a tower called Tartarus rises from the school grounds, its floors rearranged on every visit.

The rest of the day is school. Classes, exams, clubs and part-time jobs raise the hero's academics, charm and courage, and each friendship, or Social Link, is tied to a type of Persona: the closer the friendship, the stronger the Personas the player can fuse. *Edge* thought the scheme lifted the game "out of the firm grip of its JRPG roots". *Retro Gamer* later called it "against the tide of pretty much everything in its own genre" and concluded: "Only Atlus is making games like this." The way characters summon their Personas, with a gun-shaped "Evoker" held to the head, caused controversy as well as attention.

## Persona 4 and Persona 5

*Persona 4* (2008) moved the school year to the sleepy town of Inaba, where people go missing and often turn up dead, and where a rumoured Midnight Channel shows whoever has vanished most recently. The hero can reach into a television and climb into a world where the missing are trapped inside dungeons shaped by their own minds; rescuing them means getting them to face their shadow selves, the parts of themselves they would rather hide. *Edge*, reviewing the Vita version, *Persona 4 Golden*, in 2013, saw the shadows as Jungian too, and summed up the game's message: "being nice makes you stronger. Making new friends — and better friends of the ones you already have — makes you stronger still." It called the four-year-old game "by some distance, Vita's best game to date".

*Persona 5* (2016 in Japan, 2017 in English) cast its students as the Phantom Thieves, who steal the hearts of corrupt adults in Tokyo to make them change their ways. *Wireframe* summed up the series' setting as "kids in school with 'kids in school' problems given a fantastical twist". By then Atlus belonged to Sega, and *Persona 5* became the series' biggest seller.

## Beyond the main games

The characters have turned up in other kinds of game: the fighting game *Persona 4 Arena* (2012), made with Arc System Works, rhythm games, and *Persona 5 Strikers*, an action game made with Koei Tecmo's Omega Force. "Ironically for a series that began as a spin-off," *Wireframe* noted, "Persona itself is no stranger to spin-offs." The series has also had several animated adaptations. In 2024 Atlus released *Persona 3 Reload*, a remake of *Persona 3*.
