## Overview
*Laser Squad* (1988) is [Julian Gollop](/vault/people/julian-gollop)'s turn-based
tactical game: small squads, mission-based scenarios, and a system of action
points that makes every step a decision about what you are giving up. It is the
game *X-COM* grew out of, and it was written for the Spectrum first.

## Fast facts
- **Author:** Julian Gollop, credited by name in *CRASH*'s review.
- **Publisher:** Target Games — Gollop's own label, founded in March 1988.
- **Lineage:** *Rebelstar Raiders*, then *Rebel Star*, then this.
- **Two-player:** hot-seat tactical play, not an afterthought.

*CRASH*'s reviewer works through the equipment tables and reports that even
level four armour offers scant protection against a rocket launcher — a
complaint that is a compliment in disguise, since the game's whole argument is that
cover and positioning matter more than loadout.

## Key mechanics
- **Action points:** every unit spends from one pool for movement, turning, aiming and firing.
- **Line of sight:** visibility depends on facing and obstacles, so where a unit looks is itself a decision.
- **Opportunity fire:** points held back let a unit react during the opponent's turn.
- **Equipment:** loadout is chosen against a budget before the mission.
- **Two-player mode:** the tactical system holds up against a human.

## Scenarios
Missions vary the objective rather than the map alone — rescues, base assaults,
defensive holds and infiltrations, each with its own victory condition and its
own reason to spend points differently.

## Target Games and the lineage
*CRASH* announced Target Games in June 1988 as a company founded that March by
Gollop. Its December 1988 profile of him fills in what came before: the strategy
game *Rebelstar Raiders* had achieved cult status, and the revised version on
Firebird's Silver label — *Rebel Star* — earned a CRASH Smash at **93%**.

The same profile records his most admired programmer, and the answer is not
another Spectrum name. Gollop nominated **Ada Lovelace**, whom the magazine
described as having written the first book about computer programs in 1843 at
the age of 28. (The magazine's characterisation is generous — Lovelace's work
was a translation with appended notes rather than a book — but the nomination
is his, in print, in 1988.)

## Legacy
Gollop refined the system into *UFO: Enemy Unknown* — *X-COM* — in 1994. The
action-point economy, the opportunity fire and the squad-level scale all
survive the journey from a 48K Spectrum to the PC intact, which is the strongest
thing that can be said about a design.
