By 2003 a *Star Wars* game was no longer a safe bet. *Edge* opened its review of *Knights of the Old Republic* by recalling LucasArts' own admission that the name had stopped meaning quality, and *Computer Gaming World* confessed it had nearly fallen asleep during the game's demonstration at E3. Then [BioWare](/vault/companies/bioware), the Canadian studio behind *[Baldur's Gate](/vault/games/baldurs-gate)*, delivered a role-playing game that *Edge* thought captured "the defining features of the films more than almost any other 'Star Wars' title to date". Its trick was to go back about 4,000 years before the films, where nothing in them could get in the way, and to let the player decide whether their Jedi served the light side of the Force or the dark.

## Fast facts

- **Developer:** [BioWare](/vault/companies/bioware).
- **Publisher:** [LucasArts](/vault/companies/lucasarts).
- **Released:** [Xbox](/vault/systems/microsoft-xbox), North America, July 2003; Britain, 12 September 2003. PC, late 2003. Later on the Mac (2004), iOS (2013), Android (2014) and Nintendo Switch (2021).
- **Director:** Casey Hudson.
- **Setting:** about 4,000 years before the films, during a war between the Jedi and the Sith.
- **Rules:** based on Wizards of the Coast's d20 tabletop system, played out in real time with pause.

## A Star Wars role-playing game

The partnership was announced in July 2000, when LucasArts' president Simon Jeffery described a role-playing game set 4,000 years before *Episode I*. LucasArts was handing its most valuable property to outside developers, and *Edge* called the result "a stunning vindication" of the policy.

The game begins on the Outer Rim world of Taris and goes on to Tatooine, the Wookiee world of Kashyyyk and other planets, in a war against the Sith Lord Darth Malak, a "mechanically augmented villain", in *Edge*'s words, "who prefigures the eventual menace of Darth Vader". The player travels in a ship called the *Ebon Hawk*, which serves as a hub between missions and a home for the party, an idea *Wireframe* traced through later BioWare and Obsidian games. Party members include Carth Onasi, the Jedi Bastila Shan, Juhani and Jolee Bindo, and the droid HK-47.

## Light side and dark side

*Edge* thought the game's "most significant achievement" was that the choice between the light and dark sides changed what happened, not just a number on the character sheet. Its example: does the player take a bounty on an innocent dancing girl to raise some credits, try to free her from the contract, or leave her alone? A choice like that can make a quest easier or stop a character joining the party, and the ending depends on the path taken. Most problems have more than one answer: a character good with computers can hack a door that another would have to open some other way.

The combat came from *Baldur's Gate*. It follows a turn-based ruleset but plays out in real time, and the player can pause at any moment to give orders, or leave the other party members to their own scripted behaviour. For anyone who did not want the arithmetic, levelling up could be left to the game. Between missions there were swoop-bike races and a card game, Pazaak, which *Edge* likened to pontoon.

The story turns on a twist that both *Edge* and *Computer Gaming World* placed around the middle of the game. *CGW* called it "one of the most brutal and surprising plot twists ever seen in a videogame".

## Reception

The Xbox version sold 250,000 copies in its first four days, and *Computer Gaming World* reported it as the fastest-selling Xbox game yet. *Edge* gave it nine out of ten. The PC version followed with higher-resolution graphics and a small new area, and *Computer Gaming World* named it Game of the Year for 2003: "the RPG for non-RPG fans, the Star Wars game for Star Wars haters". *Edge*'s only real criticism was that the game became fairly easy towards the end, as the player's Force powers and lightsaber skills grew.

## Legacy

[Obsidian Entertainment](/vault/companies/obsidian-entertainment) made the sequel, *Knights of the Old Republic II: The Sith Lords*, released in December 2004 on the Xbox and in February 2005 on the PC. Aspyr converted the first game to the Mac in 2004. BioWare's Austin studio returned to the era with the online game *Star Wars: The Old Republic*, launched in 2011.

A remake was announced in 2021 with Aspyr as developer. Development later passed to Saber Interactive, and in March 2026 Saber's chief creative officer, Tim Willits, would say only that "it is still in development".
