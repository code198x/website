**Commander Keen** is the platform-game series that helped [id Software](/vault/companies/id-software) and [Apogee](/vault/companies/apogee-software) build their early audience. The original *Invasion of the Vorticons* trilogy appeared in December 1990. Its first episode could be copied and shared; players paid for the remaining adventures.

You control Keen through side-scrolling stages reached from a world map. The first episode’s help screen explains the essentials: jump, fire a charged raygun, and use the pogo stick once you have found it. Matching coloured keycards open doors. Completing a location lets you move farther across the map.

## A world that keeps moving

The scrolling mattered on an EGA-equipped PC. In its anniversary account, the publisher describes [John Carmack’s](/vault/people/john-carmack) smooth-scrolling work as the technology behind the project. It allowed the scene to travel with the player, supporting a kind of platform adventure strongly associated with consoles.

The same account credits **Tom Hall** with the story, **John Romero** with development alongside Hall and Carmack, and **Adrian Carmack** with artwork. They worked at [Softdisk](/vault/companies/softdisk) during the game’s development; the publisher dates id’s formal establishment to February 1991. The game therefore precedes the company’s formal founding.

## An episode is a complete invitation

Keen’s [shareware](/vault/distribution/shareware) structure let someone play a substantial part before deciding whether to order more. Copying the first episode helped it find its next player. Apogee supplied the route from that freely shared game to a paid continuation.

Later Keen games expanded the series, but their technology and publishing arrangements differ. The preserved source for *Keen Dreams*, for example, documents its own EGA and CGA versions. It supplies a view into that particular game; it does not describe every detail of the original trilogy.

[Wolfenstein 3D](/vault/games/wolfenstein-3d) would carry id and Apogee’s episodic distribution into a different kind of space: corridors seen through the player’s eyes.
