## What the press gave it

August 1985 was a clean sweep. *Sinclair User* and *Your Computer* both gave it **5/5**, *ZX Computing* **6/6**, and *Sinclair Programs* **96%** — four magazines, four top or near-top marks, in a single month.

## A city that goes about its business

**Gargoyle Games**' follow-up to *Tir Na Nog*, in which the Irish hero Cuchulainn searches a maze-city for his charioteer — and the city goes about its business regardless.

## A city with its own errands

The reason the game is remembered is that **Dun Darach does not wait for you**. Traders move stock, thieves work the streets and lift things from you, characters keep appointments, and the economy of favours and objects runs on its own clock.

Most games of 1985 are a set of rooms that hold still until entered. This one is closer to a simulation with a player inside it — and the effect on play is that you stop solving a puzzle and start **working out how a place operates**: who has what, who wants what, and who will take it off you if you stand still.

Doing that in 48K means the city cannot be stored as content. It has to be **rules plus a small cast**, which is the same economy [Mike Singleton](/vault/people/mike-singleton) applied to landscape and [Brian Howarth](/vault/people/brian-howarth) applied to adventures: describe the system, generate the situation.

## Gargoyle

*Tir Na Nog*, *Dun Darach*, *Marsport* and [*Heavy on the Magick*](/vault/games/heavy-on-the-magick) are a remarkably consistent body of work — dense, literate, slow, and drawn in a distinctive tall-character style that nobody else used.

A small house with a house style, charting where its audience was and nowhere else.
