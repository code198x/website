*Cobra*, the Sylvester Stallone film tie-in, programmed for [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software) by [Jonathan 'Joffa' Smith](/vault/people/jonathan-smith) — one of the games *Your Sinclair* named him as working on when it ran him as its castaway of the month.

- **Programmer:** Joffa Smith. **Publisher:** Ocean.

⭐ **This is what a licence looks like in a chart.** In at #3, and then down — 4, 10, 7, 14 — with no recovery. The game sells hardest the month it appears and then declines as the film recedes.

Set that beside [*Match Day*](/vault/games/match-day), which entered at **#9** and climbed to **#3** four months later, then kept returning there for a year.

Two games, one publisher, opposite shapes. *Cobra* is bought because people know the film; *Match Day* is bought because people know somebody who owns it. **A licence buys attention at launch and cannot renew it. Word of mouth is slower and compounds.**

That is the clearest argument in this data for recording chart runs and not peak positions — both games peak at #3, and they are nothing like each other.

## The programming

*Cobra* is remembered by Spectrum owners for how it moves. Joffa's reputation rests on getting arcade pace and colour out of hardware with no sprites, no hardware scrolling and one colour pair per 8×8 cell, and *Cobra* is one of the two games — with *Green Beret* — that people cite for it.

The licence bought the sales; the programming is why anyone still talks about it.
