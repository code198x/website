## Overview

*Jetpac* introduced the world to [Ultimate Play the Game](/vault/companies/ultimate). Its premise — assemble a rocket, fuel it, take off — carries more game than it looks like. Jetman's momentum, the responsive controls and the escalating waves made it an instant classic and set the standard Ultimate would be judged against.

📘 **It runs in 16K.** *CRASH* listed it as "Ultimate, 16K £5.50", which is the fact that explains the rest: every screen, every alien and the whole rocket-assembly loop fit in the smaller Spectrum's memory, at a time when 48K games were already common. Ultimate's reputation for getting more out of the machine than anyone else starts here, on the model with least to give.

## Fast facts

- **Developer and publisher:** [Ultimate Play the Game](/vault/companies/ultimate).
- **Programmer:** Chris Stamper, one of [the Stamper brothers](/vault/people/stamper-brothers) who founded Ultimate.
- **Release:** 1983 — reviewed across the British press that October, in *Sinclair User*, *C&VG*, *Home Computing Weekly*, *Personal Computer News*, *Popular Computing Weekly* and *Sinclair Programs* within a single month.
- **Format and price:** 16K, £5.50.
