**Ultima Underworld: The Stygian Abyss** is a 1992 first-person role-playing game, developed by Blue Sky Productions and published by [Origin](/vault/companies/origin-systems). The studio later became part of [Looking Glass](/vault/companies/looking-glass). Its dungeon is a place to explore, talk, trade and survive in, as well as fight through.

You can turn freely, look up and down, jump and swim. These movements share the screen with an inventory and commands for examining and using things. A torch makes the surroundings brighter; food and rest support the person moving through them.

## Different reasons to approach a creature

The player’s guide encourages conversation before attacking. Someone you meet may offer information or trade. Bartering gives objects a use beyond equipping or consuming them: something spare in your pack may be useful to another inhabitant.

Magic also has a physical vocabulary. You collect rune stones and combine them to prepare spells. Movement, inventory, skills and spells meet in the same dungeon, so understanding a rule can help with more than one obstacle.

That combination helps explain the game’s connection to the [immersive sim](/vault/genres/immersive-sim). The term describes games that give players interacting systems and room to choose an approach. Underworld’s first-person view matters because it puts those decisions into a continuous space.

## A team behind the view

In a 2022 interview, **Paul Neurath** recalled building the project around the possibility of a world that allowed unexpected player choices. He credited **Chris Green** and other team members with work on its textured 3D foundation, and described [Warren Spector’s](/vault/people/warren-spector) contribution as producer and collaborator on the story.

Neurath’s account also connects Origin’s involvement to the [Ultima](/vault/games/ultima) name: the project began as a new game before taking on that identity. Its later neighbours, including [System Shock](/vault/games/system-shock), continued exploring how a convincing place could emerge from rules, objects and the player’s decisions.
