## Overview

[Raffaele Cecco](/vault/people/raffaele-cecco)'s run-and-gun for [Hewson](/vault/companies/hewson-consultants), and one of the games that made the Spectrum look like better hardware than it was.

## Fast facts

- ***CRASH*:** **90%**, issue 43 — confirmed against the magazine's own index (issue 43, p14), a later re-citation in *CRASH* itself and the rating panel in issue 43 (August 1987), p15.
- **Author:** Raffaele Cecco. **Publisher:** Hewson Consultants.
- ***CRASH*** billed it on the author: "**Exolon author, Raffaele Cecco, produces his best game ever!**"
- **Followed by:** *Cybernoid — The Fighting Machine*, also Cecco's, also Hewson's.

⭐ **Two separate spells at number one.** Not a peak and a decline — a game that goes to the top, drops, and returns. Nothing in this library's chart data does that except *Exolon*.

The likely mechanism is the one *CRASH*'s own billing points at. When a magazine advertises a game as "**the *Exolon* author's** best ever", the buying signal is a **person**, and that signal fires again every time the next issue mentions him. A game sold on its licence decays with the licence; a game sold on its programmer gets a second push when the programmer is next in the news.

## Cecco at Hewson

*Exolon* and *Cybernoid* are the pair people cite, and the *CRASH* line that connects them — "*Exolon* author: Hewson's *Cybernoid*" — is the magazine using one game to sell the next through the man who wrote both.

[Hewson](/vault/companies/hewson-consultants) is the publisher this worked for. Its house identity was **technically excellent action games by named programmers** — Cecco, [Steve Turner](/vault/people/steve-turner), [Andrew Braybrook](/vault/people/andrew-braybrook) — at a moment when most publishers were selling licences instead.
