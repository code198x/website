## Overview

**Gargoyle Games**' adventure, in which you play the sorcerer Axil and negotiate with demons who have opinions about you — and the clearest single illustration of what a "chart position" leaves out.

⭐ **Number one three times, and never near the top of the chart that counted everything.** In the general Hotline chart the same game sits at **#16**.

[*Spellbound*](/vault/games/spellbound) shows the identical split — #3 in the Adventure chart, #21 in the general one, in the same month. Two games, two publishers, two price points, one pattern.

## What the split measures

A genre chart ranks a game against **the other things its buyers considered**. A general chart ranks it against **everything on the shelf**. Where those two disagree by fifteen places, the game has a devoted audience and a small one.

That was the adventure's position in 1986–87. It was still the form with the most committed players — the ones writing to [Mike Gerrard](/vault/people/mike-gerrard) and [Derek Brewster](/vault/people/derek-brewster) every month, mapping on graph paper, arguing in print — and it was losing the shop shelf to arcade conversions like [*Enduro Racer*](/vault/games/enduro-racer).

The 1988 reappearance says the same thing from the other end. A general chart has no memory; an adventure chart still had this game in it **two years later**, because its audience was not replacing it monthly.

## Gargoyle

*Heavy on the Magick* and *Dun Darach* were both Gargoyle's, and both hold the pattern — *Dun Darach* peaks at #5 in the Hotline chart in 1985 and is still in the Adventure Top 30 in early 1987.

A small Midlands house making dense, slow, literate games for the people who wanted them, charting exactly where you would expect and nowhere else.
