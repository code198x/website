Brutal Deluxe are the worst team in the Speedball league, and in *Speedball 2* they are yours. The game is a sport of the year 2100, played by armoured nine-a-side teams in a steel arena with no referee. You score by throwing a metal ball into the opposing goal, by lighting up stars along the walls, by rolling the ball up a spiral ramp to double your points, and by injuring opponents until robot medics stretcher them off. Each half lasts 90 seconds. Between matches you spend the prize money on training and new players, and try to drag Brutal Deluxe up through two divisions. [The Bitmap Brothers](/vault/companies/bitmap-brothers) released it at the end of 1990, and *[ZZAP!](/vault/magazines/zzap-64)* called it "without doubt the best two-player game since Kick Off II".

## Fast facts

- **Developer:** [The Bitmap Brothers](/vault/companies/bitmap-brothers). Designed by Eric Matthews, programmed by Rob Trevelyan, graphics by [Dan Malone](/vault/people/dan-malone), sound effects and music programming by Richard Joseph.
- **Publisher:** Image Works, [Mirrorsoft](/vault/companies/mirrorsoft)'s label; £24.99 on the Amiga and ST.
- **Released:** late 1990 on the [Atari ST](/vault/systems/atari-st) and [Amiga](/vault/systems/commodore-amiga); a PC version followed.
- **Music:** a theme by Nation 12, a group assembled through the Rhythm King record label, featuring John Foxx and Tim Simenon of Bomb the Bass.
- **Award:** best 16-bit soundtrack at the Golden Joystick Awards, April 1991.
- **Other versions:** [Commodore 64](/vault/systems/commodore-64) (1991), [Mega Drive](/vault/systems/sega-mega-drive), [Master System](/vault/systems/sega-master-system), [Game Boy](/vault/systems/game-boy), [Acorn Archimedes](/vault/systems/acorn-archimedes), Amiga CD32 (1995), [Game Boy Advance](/vault/systems/game-boy-advance); remade as *Speedball 2100* for the [PlayStation](/vault/systems/sony-playstation) (2000).

## A bigger game

The first *Speedball* (1988) had been a five-a-side game on a pitch three screens high. For the sequel the Bitmaps made nearly everything larger. *CU Amiga*'s Steve Merrett counted the new features: a much larger arena, nearly three times as many warp gates, a spiral channel that multiplied the value of goals, and pinball-style stars and bumpers along the walls. Teams grew to twelve players, nine on the pitch and three substitutes, and each had ratings for attributes such as aggression, attack, power and stamina that you could raise in the gym.

The scoring rewards variety. *ZZAP!* listed it: ten points for a goal, ten for injuring an opponent so badly that he is carried off, two for hitting a bounce dome, two for each star lit on the opposing wall, with a ten-point bonus for all five. A ball thrown up the spiral ramp twice doubles every point you then score, until your opponent cancels it by doing the same. Electrobounce pads charge the ball so that it knocks down the next player who touches it, and tokens freeze the other team, reverse their controls or give you the ball. The league has two divisions of eight teams and a 14-week season; there is also a four-round cup, a knockout mode and a two-player game.

The Bitmaps spent about 40 per cent of the development time on the computer opponents, *[ACE](/vault/magazines/ace-magazine)* reported, though its reviewer still thought the game was at its best with two people playing. *Retro Gamer* later singled out one fix: in the first *Speedball*, the team attacking down the screen almost always lost the ball at the restart. Mike Montgomery admitted it had been a bug in the random number routines, and it was corrected for the sequel.

## Sound and "Ice cream!"

Much of *Speedball 2*'s character is in the sound. The title theme came from Nation 12, a group put together through the Bitmaps' links with Rhythm King: *CU Amiga* named John Foxx of Ultravox, Tim Simenon of Bomb the Bass and Simon Rogers among its members, and reported that Rogers wrote the music. Richard Joseph programmed the tune for the Amiga and made the sound effects: crashing, scraping and grunting samples, and a sampled salesman in the crowd who, as *[Amiga Format](/vault/magazines/amiga-format)* put it, "shouts out 'ICE-CREAM!' at every possible opportunity". Montgomery credited the idea to Joseph, who found crowd noise alone "just boring"; later versions added hot dogs and popcorn. The Amiga version was always meant to sound better than the ST one, *CU Amiga* said, and at the Golden Joystick Awards in April 1991 *Speedball 2* won the prize for the best 16-bit soundtrack.

## Reviews

The ST version came first. *[The One](/vault/magazines/the-one)* reviewed it in November 1990 and called it "a tremendous improvement over its illustrious predecessor", adding that the Amiga version, with 32-colour graphics and better sound, would be "marginally more impressive". The Amiga reviews followed over the winter. *Amiga Action* thought it "the best I have ever seen" of all the sports games around. *Zero*'s David McCandless admitted defeat in his hope of one day being able to criticise the Bitmaps: "*Speedball 2* is typically Bitmap, i.e. brilliant." *[CU Amiga](/vault/magazines/cu-amiga)* called it "easily the Bitmap's best game – a genuine classic". *ZZAP!*, reviewing the Amiga game in March 1991, described the graphics as "a kind of neo-fascist brutalism" and gave it 96%. *Amiga Format*'s Maff Evans in the same month thought it "not as good as the original … it's miles better".

## Other versions

Carl Muller, a programmer from New Zealand, wrote the Commodore 64 version, which *[Commodore Format](/vault/magazines/commodore-format)* reviewed in September 1991 and called "utterly brilliant". *[Mean Machines](/vault/magazines/mean-machines)* found the Mega Drive version "the best future sport game available on the Megadrive" in February 1992, and [Virgin Games](/vault/companies/virgin-games) advertised Mega Drive and Master System versions later that year. Mindscape published the Game Boy version in the United States in January 1993, and [Krisalis](/vault/companies/krisalis) converted it to the Archimedes around the start of 1994.

The game stayed in print. By 1993 the Bitmaps' own label, [Renegade](/vault/companies/renegade), was selling it in a Bitmap Brothers compilation. A budget re-release in 1994 was the first to run on the A600 and A1200, and *[AMIGA POWER](/vault/magazines/amiga-power)* gave it 92%. Renegade's CD32 version followed in 1995; *AMIGA POWER*'s Paul Mellerick was pleased that the teams now wore completely different colours instead of different headbands.

## Later remakes

*Speedball 2100* (2000) rebuilt the game in 3D for the PlayStation, keeping camera angles close to the Amiga original. Montgomery said it was his favourite of the series, but *[Edge](/vault/magazines/edge)* gave it 4/10 and found its slowdown "almost unforgivable given the game's name". Crawfish Interactive built a Game Boy Advance version from "the Bitmaps' original Mega Drive and Amiga code", and Broadsword Interactive converted the game for Pocket PC in 2002. According to public sources, *Speedball 2 Tournament* (2007) remade it for the PC, *Speedball 2 Evolution* brought it to phones and tablets, and *Speedball 2 HD* followed in 2013. Rebellion, which bought the Bitmap Brothers' games in 2019, now owns it.
