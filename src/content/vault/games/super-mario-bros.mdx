*Super Mario Bros.* wasn't just a game—it was a rescue mission. When Nintendo launched the NES in North America in 1985, retailers were sceptical after the [1983 crash](/vault/phenomena/1983-crash). The pack-in game needed to prove that console gaming was worth another chance. [Shigeru Miyamoto](/vault/people/shigeru-miyamoto) delivered something that didn't just prove the case—it set the standard every platformer has followed since.

## Fast facts
- **Developer:** [Nintendo](/vault/companies/nintendo) R&D4.
- **Director/Designer:** [Shigeru Miyamoto](/vault/people/shigeru-miyamoto).
- **Release:** September 1985 (Japan), October 1985 (North America).
- **Sales:** over 40 million copies (pack-in + standalone).
- **Innovation:** horizontal scrolling through a persistent world; power-ups that changed gameplay.
- **Cultural impact:** Mario became more recognisable than Mickey Mouse.

## World 1-1
The first level is a masterclass in teaching through play:
- **Safe start:** ground to the left, no immediate threat.
- **First enemy:** a Goomba walks toward you. Jump or die. Lesson learned.
- **Mystery block:** hit it, get a mushroom. Now you're bigger.
- **Bigger means different:** you can break bricks now. New possibilities.
- **The pipe:** some are just decoration. Some take you underground.
- **The flagpole:** touch it to win. Simple, satisfying.

No manual needed. The level design *is* the tutorial.

## The formula
*Super Mario Bros.* codified platform game conventions:

**Momentum physics**
Mario accelerates and decelerates. Running and jumping are *physical*—you feel the weight.

**Power-up progression**
- Small Mario: one hit kills
- Super Mario: take a hit, shrink instead
- Fire Mario: shoot fireballs, plus the shrink buffer

Higher states mean more capability but more to lose.

**Secret worlds**
Warp zones. Hidden 1-ups. Vine blocks leading to coin heavens. Exploration is rewarded.

**Lives and continues**
Enough challenge to feel accomplishment, enough forgiveness to encourage persistence.

## Technical achievement
On NES hardware, *Super Mario Bros.* pushed limits:
- **Smooth scrolling:** horizontal movement at 60fps, unprecedented for home consoles.
- **Memory efficiency:** level data compressed cleverly; the entire game fit in 32KB.
- **Music:** Koji Kondo's theme became the most recognisable video game music ever written.

## Legacy
Every 2D platformer since 1985 exists in dialogue with *Super Mario Bros.* The physics feel, the power-up structure, the level design philosophy—it all traces back to this one game. When people say "gaming grammar," they mean the language Miyamoto invented here.
