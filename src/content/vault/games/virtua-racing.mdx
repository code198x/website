**Virtua Racing** is [Sega AM2’s](/vault/companies/sega-am2) 1992 polygon racing game for Model 1 arcade hardware. Cars, road and scenery occupy a three-dimensional space built from solid-coloured faces. Four viewpoint buttons let you choose how much of that space to see.

From close to the car, the next corner arrives quickly. A more distant view reveals more of the route and the vehicles around you. Switching views changes the information available to the driver without changing the road.

## More than the car

Sega’s historical account credits [Yu Suzuki](/vault/people/yu-suzuki) as director, **Takenobu Mitsuyoshi** for sound and [Toshihiro Nagoshi](/vault/people/toshihiro-nagoshi) as chief designer. It also recalls the moving pit crew and tyre marks as parts of the team’s exploration of polygon graphics.

The three courses offer increasing difficulty. Linked cabinets let people race one another, while the arcade presentation could show the event to spectators as well as drivers. The Twin manual describes a pair of communicating stations, with one designated master and the other slave in the original settings.

Operators had choices too. The manual gives a normal four-lap setting and a twenty-lap Grand Prix setting. Cabinet configuration therefore affects the length and character of a session; the short arcade race is not the only documented arrangement.

## A changing arcade

[Ridge Racer](/vault/games/ridge-racer) and [Daytona USA](/vault/games/daytona-usa) would make textured racing scenery a prominent part of the following generation. Virtua Racing’s visible polygon faces show a different moment in that transition. The shape of the scene carries much of the visual information.

Later versions revisit the game with different capabilities. Sega’s Switch release, for example, adds saved replays and eight-player split-screen play. Those additions belong to that conversion; the original multiplayer experience depended on the arcade’s connected machines.
