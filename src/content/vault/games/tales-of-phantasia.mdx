*Tales of Phantasia* opens with a sung theme on a cartridge. Reviewing the Japanese import in June 1996, *Super Play* reported that roughly 16 of its 48 megabits held voice samples. Characters called out their spells and attacks, and a sound mode let players listen outside the adventure. The extra space was audible.

Wolf Team developed the game for [Namco](/vault/companies/namco), which released it on the Super Famicom on 15 December 1995. It began the *Tales* series, with characters designed by Kōsuke Fujishima and battles in which the player controlled a fighter directly.

## A party across time

Cress returns to find his village destroyed and his family killed. The attack leads into a struggle with Dhaos, a sealed-away enemy released into a world losing its magic. Cress and the healer Mint travel into the past, gathering companions and searching for a way to confront him. The story moves between past, present and future.

The battles switch to a side-on field. You move the hero left and right and time attacks, while companions act under computer control. Orders let you influence their behaviour. *Super Play* found that allies left to attack freely could spend magic points too enthusiastically, and criticised the frequency of random encounters. Its enthusiasm for the presentation came with reservations about how often exploration stopped for another fight.

*Nintendo Power* was more effusive in its February 1996 preview, comparing the graphics favourably with *Chrono Trigger*. That July it reported that several people working on *Star Ocean* had also worked on *Phantasia*, and connected the two games' use of digitised voices.

## A long route into English

The original Super Famicom release remained in Japan. DeJap's English translation patch arrived in 2001, before an official English version. The group's account credits Dark Force with programming and makokichi and Chess Piece Face with translation. It describes extracting the script in 1997, translating much of it in 1999, and the work needed to fit the text back into the game.

This was a modification of the existing game. A patch changed its text and supporting routines; it was not a release of Namco's original source code.

## Remakes and later releases

Namco released a PlayStation remake in Japan in December 1998, followed by the Game Boy Advance version in August 2003. The GBA edition finally reached Western markets in 2006; Nintendo's European record gives 31 March. It retained direct control of Cress alongside independently acting companions.

The PSP's *Full Voice Edition* followed in Japan on 7 September 2006. Bandai Namco's announcement described spoken dialogue throughout the main story, larger battle characters and additions carried over from the PlayStation and GBA versions. An iOS edition also appeared; the publisher now marks its distribution as ended.

The series continued under Bandai Namco, which celebrated its thirtieth anniversary in 2025 and established a programme of remasters. In an anniversary interview, Fujishima recalled keeping his early designs simple to suit the graphics limitations. He singled out Cress as the first character he designed for the series, and one that helped determine its direction.
