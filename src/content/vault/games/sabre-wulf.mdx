In the summer of 1984 Ultimate put a new game in the shops at £9.95. Its previous games had cost £5.50, and most Spectrum games still cost under £6. *CRASH* got its review copy with two hours to spare before going to press. One reviewer complained that it took almost that long to load, ran through the jungle "like mad", and decided that it was better than *Atic Atac* and that the price rise was a pity. The magazine made *Sabre Wulf* a CRASH Smash and then declined to give it a percentage, the only Ultimate game in *CRASH*'s own table of reviews without one: "Let's just say 'unrateable!' and leave it."

## Four pieces of an amulet

The hero is a new character, Sabreman, in a pith helmet and armed with a sabre. He is lost in a jungle maze. The only instructions are a warning carved in stone and printed on the cassette insert: put together the four pieces of the ACG Amulet so that he can pass the guardian of the cave and escape. As with *[Atic Atac](/vault/games/atic-atac)*, working out what anything does is left to the player.

*Your Spectrum* counted 256 screens, arranged as a 16 by 16 maze bordered by mountains. Sabreman moves around one screen at a time, and when he walks off an edge the next screen is drawn in its place. Jungle creatures burst out of the ground a moment after he arrives: spiders, scorpions, snakes, hippos and rhinos, frogs and tribesmen. Some can be killed with the sabre; others can only be dodged. The Wulf itself lives in one small but vital part of the maze and cannot be fought.

Orchids grow in the clearings, blossoming and wilting quickly. Run over one in full bloom and something happens. *Your Spectrum* worked out five colours: cyan made Sabreman run faster, purple reversed the controls, red let him run through creatures unharmed, yellow put him to sleep for a few seconds, and white cured the effects of the others. *Personal Computer Games* found another clue: when a native lines up with a piece of the amulet horizontally or vertically, he turns slowly and a stern tune plays.

## How it was built

*Your Spectrum*'s hacker Dave Nicholls opened up the code and found that the 256 screens are built from only 48 room types, each assembled from blocks such as a lake that reappears around the jungle. He guessed there were more than a hundred of those blocks. *Personal Computer Games* named the team as Tim and Chris Stamper, Carol Ward and John Lathbury: "Chris and John program the structure of the game, while Tim and Carol concentrate on the graphics." They had worked on it "full-time and at weekends" for six months since finishing *Atic Atac*, and Ultimate gave that as the reason for the higher price.

In 1988 Tim Stamper gave *CRASH* another reason. "We were having a severe problem with the number of (illegal) copies. And I think going from £5.50 to £9.95 was a bold step we took." He also revealed that *[Knight Lore](/vault/games/knight-lore)* had already been finished, and was held back so that it would not stop *Sabre Wulf* selling.

## Reception

*Personal Computer Games* made it a PCG Hit and said "Sabre Wulf is Ultimate's best entertainment yet". *Computer & Video Games* scored it 9 for getting started, graphics, value and playability, and the reviewer wrote: "I have never seen graphics of such high quality on a Spectrum." *Sinclair User* gave it 8 out of 10, found the graphics "not entirely flicker-free" and thought the main criticism was the price.

It sold anyway. *CRASH* reported in 1988 that in Ultimate's heyday a game like *Sabre Wulf* sold more than 350,000 Spectrum copies, more than Activision's *Ghostbusters* sold on all formats together. [Firebird](/vault/companies/firebird) bought the rights to a Commodore 64 version, released in 1985 at £9.95, and there were versions for the BBC Micro and Amstrad CPC.

## Sabreman's return

*Personal Computer Games* was told, as a secret, that *Sabre Wulf* was to be the first of a four-part series. Sabreman came back in *Underwurlde* and *Knight Lore*, both released at the end of 1984, and again in *Pentagram* (1986). Rare returned to the name in 2004 with a *Sabre Wulf* for the Game Boy Advance.
