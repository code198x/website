*Super Tennis* on the Super Nintendo had used six buttons. When [Camelot Software Planning](/vault/companies/camelot-software) showed its Nintendo 64 tennis game at the E3 show in 2000, it used two, plus the analogue stick, and *N64 Magazine* reported that it was "widely regarded as the most playable game on show". *Mario Tennis* went on sale in Japan that July. It put Mario and his friends on grass, clay and hard courts, gave each of them a style of play, and brought in a new character, Waluigi, who has been part of Mario's world ever since.

## Fast facts

- **Developer:** [Camelot Software Planning](/vault/companies/camelot-software), the makers of *Mario Golf* (1999).
- **Publisher:** [Nintendo](/vault/companies/nintendo).
- **Released:** [Nintendo 64](/vault/systems/nintendo-64), Japan in July 2000, North America that summer, Britain in November 2000. A Game Boy Color version followed in Japan at the end of 2000.
- **Players:** one to four.
- **Characters:** 14 at the start and two to unlock, among them the new Waluigi.
- **Before it:** Nintendo's *Mario's Tennis* (1995) for the Virtual Boy.
- **After it:** *Mario Power Tennis* (GameCube), *Mario Tennis Open* (3DS, 2012) and more, still made by Camelot.

## Two buttons

Camelot kept the controls small. *Edge* explained them: A for a strike, B for slice, A then B for a lob, B then A for a drop shot, and both together for a powerful flat shot, which *Nintendo Official Magazine* advised using to smash a short lob away at the net. Pressing a button early made the player stop and wind up, "unable to move, but capable of striking the ball harder than before". Held for a second or two, *N64 Magazine* found, the racket "crackles with electricity". Dives and smashes happened by themselves if the right button went in at the right moment.

The depth was in the players. *N64 Magazine* sorted the 14 into five types. Mario and Luigi were the all-rounders. Peach, Daisy, Toad and Waluigi were fast but hit softly. Yoshi, Baby Mario and Birdo (Catherine in the Japanese version) were agile at the net. Donkey Kong, Bowser and Wario hit "huge groundstrokes and thunderous serving" but could not run. A flying Koopa and a Boo curled the ball. Winning tournaments unlocked two more: Donkey Kong Jr. and Shy Guy.

The courts mattered too. Grass, clay and indoor and outdoor hard courts each made the ball bounce differently, and *N64 Magazine* noticed the grass wearing out as a match went on. Three more courts, themed on Nintendo characters, opened up as the cups were won.

## Waluigi

*N64 Magazine* introduced him to its readers in June 2000 under the heading "Luigi's evil cousin?!": thinner than Luigi, "with a Wario-style red nose and spiky moustache". He was, in its words a month later, "an evil Dick Dastardly version of Luigi". He was the only new character in the game. *Nintendo Official Magazine* introduced him to British players as "Wario's skinny brother" and "Nintendo's new evil guy", who stamps the floor like a big baby when he loses a point.

## Beyond tennis

Besides exhibition matches and three tournament cups (Mushroom, Flower and Star), the game had three bonus modes. In one, Piranha Plants spat balls at the player like serving machines. In another, golden rings floated over the net and points were scored by hitting the ball through them, with the winner of the rally taking the lot. The third, on a tilting court in Bowser's castle, threw in the items from *[Mario Kart 64](/vault/games/mario-kart-64)*, red and green shells, bananas, mushrooms, stars and lightning bolts. "A game of doubles here really is a unique experience," *N64 Magazine* wrote.

## Reception

*Edge* compared it with *Sensible Soccer*: Camelot had taken the same approach, "scoffing at the ultra-realism of Virtua Tennis and sucking Wimbledon into candy-coloured Mario Land". It thought the single-player game could get repetitive, because the computer players' simple tactics repeated themselves, and gave it eight out of ten, calling it "exactly what you'd expect from Nintendo: exuberant, addictive". *N64 Magazine* gave the British release 91%, and *Nintendo Official Magazine* headed its review "Ball smashing brilliance".

The speed of its making impressed too. *N64 Magazine* reported that Camelot had written the game in less than eight months, when Nintendo's own teams and Rare took two to three years over a new game.

## The Game Boy Color version

The handheld *Mario Tennis* was a different game. The player began, *Hyper* explained, as a junior who has just joined the Royal Tennis Academy, exploring the school, practising on its machines, taking lessons and hanging out in the café with a roommate who becomes the doubles partner. Winning matches earned experience points to spend on speed, lobbing, placement, topspin and other skills, which made it "almost like a Tennis RPG if there could ever be such a thing". Mario and his friends were still there for exhibition matches, with mini-games for Mario, Yoshi and Bowser.

The two versions talked to each other through the N64's Transfer Pak. *N64 Magazine*, reporting on the Japanese release at the end of 2000, said the link unlocked four new characters in the console game; *IGN Unplugged* added that they would only be as good on the N64 as the player had made them on the Game Boy. It made one of the staff top-five lists in the same issue of *Hyper*, whose review summed it up: "top class, and we can't recommend it enough".

## Before and after

Mario had played tennis before. *Mario's Tennis* (1995), by Nintendo's R&D1, was a launch game for the Virtual Boy in Japan and came packed with the American machine, and *Retro Gamer* counts it as the first game in the *Mario Tennis* series.

Camelot made the sequels. *Mario Power Tennis* reached the GameCube in Britain in February 2005 with a special attack and a special defence for every character, which *Edge* thought diluted "the sport's spirit": "Those who loved the original will find Mario Tennis is a good work spoiled." *Mario Tennis Open* for the 3DS followed in May 2012, which *Edge* found "a step removed from past excesses". Camelot's own list of its games runs on through *Mario Tennis Aces* on the Switch to *Mario Tennis Fever*, released in February 2026.
