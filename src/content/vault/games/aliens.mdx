## Two games, one title

The most likely explanation is in the review data, and it is a warning about the source.

*CRASH* reviewed **two different Electric Dreams games called *Aliens***: one scoring **84%**, and one listed as "**Aliens U S Version**" scoring **45%**. They are separate products — the British and American takes on the same licence, with different designs and sharply different receptions.

⚠ **The chart rows say only "ALIENS".** Nothing in the data distinguishes which game is charting in 1988, and a title string cannot carry a version. The 1988 run is consistent with a budget re-release, with the second version reaching the shops, or with the film's video release pulling the licence back into demand — and this library **cannot choose between those** from what it holds.

That is worth stating plainly instead of picking the tidiest story. A chart position is evidence about a *name*, not about a *game*, and where a name covers more than one product the chart quietly stops meaning what it appears to mean.

## Why it is here

*Aliens* earns an entry less for itself than for what it shows about reading this data.

The other chart entries in this Vault draw conclusions from shape — [*Match Day*](/vault/games/match-day)'s slow climb as word of mouth, *Cobra*'s decay as a licence burning off. Those readings are sound because each title maps to one game. **This one does not**, and the anomaly is what exposes the assumption.

Any future work using the charts table should treat a title as a key that may collide, in the same way [publisher attributions](/vault/companies/players-software) can bleed between games sharing a printed page.
