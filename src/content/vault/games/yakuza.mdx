When *Edge* reviewed the English version of *Yakuza* in October 2006, it called it "the first truly Sega Sega game to appear on a home console since *Outrun 2*", a game "with a powerful story to tell and vibrant and amusing ideas to back it up — a game with a heart". Its hero, Kazuma Kiryu, is a gangster who slams thugs' faces into walls in one scene and, in the next, lets a young girl, Haruka, tag along to cheer him on and pick his next game at the casino. The series built on that mix for twenty years: a melodramatic crime story, brutal street fights, and a city full of side stories and things to do. In Japan it is *Ryu Ga Gotoku*, "like a dragon", and since 2022 that has been its name in the West too.

## Fast facts

- **Developer and publisher:** [Sega](/vault/companies/sega); the team became Ryu Ga Gotoku Studio.
- **Creator:** [Toshihiro Nagoshi](/vault/people/toshihiro-nagoshi).
- **First game:** *Ryu Ga Gotoku*, PlayStation 2, Japan, end of 2005; *Yakuza* in the West, 2006.
- **Heroes:** Kazuma Kiryu, the "Dragon of Dojima"; from *Yakuza: Like a Dragon* (2020), Ichiban Kasuga.
- **Setting:** Kamurocho, a fictional Tokyo entertainment district; *Yakuza: Like a Dragon* moved the main story to Yokohama.
- **Combat:** a street brawler, until *Yakuza: Like a Dragon* switched to turn-based battles.

## Ryu Ga Gotoku

Nagoshi had made his name with arcade racing games and *Super Monkey Ball*. His crime drama came out in Japan for the PlayStation 2 at the end of 2005, and *Edge* reviewed an import copy in February 2006. Kiryu returns to "a world he knows only too well", and the game follows him through the streets, clubs and back rooms of Kamurocho. *Edge* counted some 70 side events, "only around half of which" a player would blunder into without looking for them, and noted that the story had been "penned by a novelist, voiced by professional actors". The reviewer also found the game full of its creator. Details such as "an impromptu drinking contest, Kazuma's snakeskin shoes" made him wonder whether Nagoshi's column in *Edge* "was really a design diary"; the [Toshihiro Nagoshi](/vault/people/toshihiro-nagoshi) entry tells that story.

Sega released it in English as *Yakuza* in 2006, with an English voice cast. *Edge*'s review set it beside Sega's *[Shenmue](/vault/games/shenmue)*: both had open city streets full of people, a plot told in long cut-scenes and fights against crowds, but *Yakuza* was what *Shenmue* might have been if it had "stopped aiming to become the eighth wonder of the world, and instead had to condense itself into a proper videogame". Fights were "brutal and uncomplicated — it thuds, it crunches": "Faces get slammed into walls and stomped, and prone bodies have furniture crashed into them." Kiryu could be upgraded in special moves, new techniques and health. The reviewer liked the hunt for locker keys hidden around the city, each opening a numbered locker, because "there's some true curiosity as to what exactly your next find will be". The camera and the lock-on in boss fights were the main complaints.

## The series

Sequels followed on the PlayStation 3 and PlayStation 4, with Nagoshi credited as general director of *Yakuza 3*, *4* and *5* and executive director of *Yakuza 0* and *Yakuza 6*. Kamurocho stayed at the centre of them, and later games let players watch it change. *Yakuza 0* (2015), a prequel, is set in the bubble-economy Japan of the 1980s, with playable arcade machines "from Sega's glory days", as *Wireframe* put it, and its plot turns on a small, empty plot of land in Kamurocho which, by the time of the first game, has become the Millennium Tower. Kiryu spends part of the game running a property business.

In the West the series stayed a cult until *Yakuza 0*. It came out in English in January 2017, and Wikipedia's account of the game credits it with the series' rise in popularity outside Japan. By 2022 *Wireframe* was calling the franchise's "sudden breakout popularity in the west" one of Sega's biggest successes of recent years.

The studio also made spin-offs, among them *Judgment* (2019), a detective story set in Kamurocho with a disgraced lawyer as its hero. *Yakuza: Like a Dragon* (2020) went further: a new hero, Ichiban Kasuga, and turn-based battles of the kind found in Japanese role-playing games, with those games' conventions played out in a modern Japanese city, Yokohama. *Wireframe* gave it 90%. In 2022 Sega dropped the *Yakuza* name in the West: starting with *Like a Dragon: Ishin!*, "everything Yakuza-related will be named Like a Dragon", a closer translation of the Japanese title. *Like a Dragon: Infinite Wealth* followed in 2024.

Nagoshi left Sega in 2021, and Masayoshi Yokoyama became head of Ryu Ga Gotoku Studio.
