The second **Magic Knight** game, written by [David Jones](/vault/people/david-jones-magic-knight) and published by [Mastertronic Added Dimension](/vault/companies/mastertronic-added-dimension) at **£2.99** — an arcade adventure with an interface that full-price games were not attempting.

## Fast facts

- **Author:** David Jones — *not* the DMA Design founder; see [the disambiguation](/vault/people/dave-jones).
- **Series:** *Finders Keepers*, ***Spellbound***, *Knight Tyme*, *Stormbringer*.
- **Interface:** "Windimation" — pull-down menus over a running game.
- ***CRASH*** on the system: "a delight to use".

⭐ **The two January 1987 rows are the entry.** In the same month, in the same magazine, *Spellbound* is **#3 in the Adventure chart and #21 in the general one**.

Both are true and they measure different things. Among people buying adventures it is near the top; among everyone buying anything it is barely visible. A £2.99 game with a menu system beat the other adventures and lost to the arcade releases — which is the budget tier's position in one pair of numbers.

Note also the tail: still charting in **July 1988**, nearly three years after release. Budget games did not have release weeks; they had shelf lives, sitting in newsagents at pocket-money prices long after full-price titles had been cleared.

## Windows on a 48K machine

Pressing a key raises **pull-down menus** over the game — objects to take, people to talk to, spells to cast — drawn over a moving screen, dismissed without corrupting what is underneath, and driven from the keyboard fast enough not to irritate. On a display with [attribute colour constraints](/vault/techniques/colour-clash) that make overlapping graphics expensive.

Menu-driven interaction is usually traced through the Macintosh and then [SCUMM](/vault/techniques/scumm) on 16-bit machines at full price. It is also here, in 1985, on a cassette that cost less than a magazine.
