**Batman** was [Jon Ritman](/vault/people/jon-ritman) and
[Bernie Drummond](/vault/people/bernie-drummond)'s first isometric game for
[Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software), reviewed by
*[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* in **May 1986** at £7.95. Robin has been
kidnapped, the Batmobile is in pieces, and the Caped Crusader has to cross a mansion in
three-quarter view collecting the parts.

It is not the film tie-in. Ocean held the licence for years and made two entirely different
games under it; the [1989 one](/vault/games/batman-ocean) followed Tim Burton's film. This is
the earlier one, and it is the one that matters technically.

## Fast facts

- **Authors:** [Jon Ritman](/vault/people/jon-ritman) and [Bernie Drummond](/vault/people/bernie-drummond), credited by name in the review.
- **Publisher:** [Ocean](/vault/companies/ocean-software), £7.95.
- **Machines:** [Spectrum](/vault/systems/zx-spectrum), [Amstrad CPC](/vault/systems/amstrad-cpc), MSX.
- **CRASH:** 93% overall — graphics 95%, playability 94%, addictive qualities 93%.
- **Successor:** *[Head Over Heels](/vault/games/head-over-heels)*, on a developed version of the same engine.

A game that scores 91% at its weakest is being told it has no weak side. *CRASH* was handing
out marks in the nineties sparingly in 1986, and Batman got one without the reviewers
reaching for the licence as an excuse.

## Why it matters more than the licence

[Knight Lore](/vault/games/knight-lore) had shown in 1984 that the Spectrum could hold a
three-quarter-view room and a solid object in it. What Ritman added was **a game built for the
view rather than displayed in it**: rooms designed as puzzles about height and depth,
objects you carry between them, and controls that let you place a jump precisely enough for
that to be fair.

The engine went on to *[Head Over Heels](/vault/games/head-over-heels)* the following year,
where the two-character mechanic gave it something to be about. The period press made the
connection at the time rather than in retrospect — *CRASH*, *Amstrad Action* and *ACE* all
introduce *Head Over Heels* by way of *Batman* through 1987 and 1988.

That is the honest shape of this game's importance. It is a very good licensed platformer
that reviewed at 93%, and it is the working prototype for a better game.
