## Overview
*Driller* is the game [Freescape](/vault/techniques/freescape) was built for:
a solid three-dimensional world on 8-bit hardware that you move through freely,
looking up and down, flying over structures and standing beneath them.

The task is methodical rather than frantic. The moon Mitral is riddled with gas
pockets that will ignite; you tour eighteen sectors, site a drilling rig in each,
and work out how to reach the sectors that refuse to let you in.

97% puts it alongside [*Head Over Heels*](/vault/games/head-over-heels) and
[PAW](/vault/tools/paw) at the top of the *CRASH* marks recorded in this Vault.
*CRASH* was still citing the score four years later when reviewing
[3D Construction Kit](/vault/tools/3d-construction-kit): "Driller (97%, Issue
47)".

The game had a long tail. *Your Sinclair* returned to it at 90% in September
1989 and 97% in May 1991; *CRASH* re-reviewed budget re-releases at 86% and 87%
in 1991. A 1991 *Sinclair User* re-review at 60% is the outlier, and by then it
was four years old and being judged against 1991.

## Why the score
Read the review dates against what else was shipping. In December 1987 the
Spectrum's showcase games were arcade conversions and isometric adventures.
*Driller* offered filled polygons and an unconstrained camera on the same 48K.

The frame rate is low — a few frames per second — and that was accepted as the
cost of the thing existing at all. Freescape hands the trade-off to the player
through a resizable viewport: shrink the window onto the world and it moves
faster.

## What followed
*Driller* was the first of four Freescape games — *Dark Side* (95%),
*Total Eclipse* (93%) and *Castle Master* (85%) — after which
[Incentive](/vault/companies/incentive-software) sold the engine itself as
[3D Construction Kit](/vault/tools/3d-construction-kit).
