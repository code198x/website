In *Jackal*, clearing a path through enemy territory is only part of the job. Prisoners are waiting in buildings along the route. Freeing them means approaching under fire, collecting passengers and reaching a helicopter pickup point.

[Konami](/vault/companies/konami) released the arcade game in 1986. Its armoured jeeps put the player between two competing tasks: shooting through the battlefield and stopping long enough to rescue people. Two players can take on the mission together.

## Driving and shooting

The arcade game's weapons aim differently. The machine gun fires towards the top of the screen; grenades and later missiles follow the direction the jeep faces. Turning away from an enemy therefore changes where one weapon goes without changing the other. Driving, aiming and avoiding incoming fire are related decisions, rather than a single action.

Rescuing particular prisoners upgrades the secondary weapon. *Retro Gamer*'s account describes increasingly powerful missiles and a limit of eight passengers, making room in the jeep another consideration. These details belong to the arcade game; they should not be assumed to describe every conversion.

## The NES campaign

The NES manual divides the journey into six territories, ending at Checkpoint Zulu. It assigns the machine gun to B and grenades or the upgraded bazooka to A, with eight-direction movement on the control pad.

The rescue sequence is explicit: destroy a barracks, pull up beside the freed prisoners, then take them to a heliport. Rescues contribute to weapon improvements and extra lives. This gives the detours a practical value as well as advancing the mission's premise.

The NES version also supports two simultaneous players. Its six-part campaign differs from the arcade game's continuous journey, a distinction highlighted in *Retro Gamer*'s comparison. Describing it merely as the same game with reduced graphics misses that structural change.

## Other versions

Home conversions did not receive a uniform welcome. *CRASH* reviewed the Spectrum release in January 1988 and awarded it 47%. Its reviewers criticised the scrolling, graphics and colour clashes. That verdict concerns this conversion, rather than the arcade original or NES game.

The arcade version returned in Hamster's licensed *Arcade Archives* series for Nintendo Switch and PlayStation 4 on 27 June 2024. That release offers another route to the original game, alongside the distinct adaptations made for earlier home machines.
