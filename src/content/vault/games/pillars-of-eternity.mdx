*PC Gamer*'s reviewer spent almost an hour on the character creation screen before he had played a minute of *Pillars of Eternity*, and took that as a good sign. Obsidian Entertainment had promised its backers a game like the ones its founders had made at Black Isle Studios fifteen years earlier, *[Baldur's Gate](/vault/games/baldurs-gate)*, *Icewind Dale* and *[Planescape: Torment](/vault/games/planescape-torment)*, and in March 2015 it delivered one: a party of adventurers seen from above, fights that could be paused at any moment to give orders, and "page upon page of superbly written, vivid, descriptive text". It was also a world of Obsidian's own. Apart from the spy game *Alpha Protocol*, the studio had spent more than a decade making games in other people's settings, from *Star Wars* and Dungeons & Dragons to *Fallout*.

## Fast facts

- **Developer:** [Obsidian Entertainment](/vault/companies/obsidian-entertainment), Irvine, California.
- **Publisher:** Paradox Interactive.
- **Director:** Josh Sawyer (J. E. Sawyer), formerly lead designer of Black Isle's *Icewind Dale* games.
- **Kickstarter:** September to October 2012, as *Project Eternity*; $3,986,929 from 73,986 backers against a $1.1 million goal, and $4,163,208 including PayPal pledges.
- **Engine:** built in Unity, with 3D characters over pre-rendered 2D backgrounds.
- **Released:** 26 March 2015 for Windows, OS X and Linux; on PlayStation 4 and Xbox One in August 2017 and on Switch in August 2019.
- **Expansion:** *The White March*, in two parts (August 2015 and February 2016).
- **Sequel:** *Pillars of Eternity II: Deadfire* (2018).

## The campaign

In September 2012 Obsidian asked players for $1.1 million to make *Project Eternity*, a game "inspired by Planescape: Torment, Baldur's Gate and Icewind Dale", as *Eurogamer* put it. *Eurogamer* noted that *Alpha Protocol*, the studio's one attempt at a setting of its own, had been "overlooked", and *Edge* saw the campaign as a chance "to build a world from scratch, without publisher input". The Kickstarter closed in October 2012 with $3,986,929 from 73,986 backers, the most any video game had raised there at that time, and PayPal pledges took the total to $4,163,208.

The money bought more game. *Eurogamer*'s summary of what the final total had paid for listed eleven classes, six races, a stronghold for the player to run, and a dungeon of fifteen levels. That mega-dungeon, the Endless Paths of Od Nua beneath the stronghold, became one of the game's attractions. Only its first level has to be explored; the rest is there for players who want more than the main quest. Obsidian also brought in a publisher late on: in March 2014 Paradox Interactive agreed to market and distribute the game.

## The world

Sawyer explained the setting to *PC World* in 2013. The game is set in the Dyrwood, a former colony, "more like a United States/Canada type of environment", whose settlers had "a rough past with the natives" and had recently won their independence. Its technology is "closer to the early Renaissance", with matchlock guns alongside swords and bows. People in this world know that souls are reborn in new bodies, and the newly independent Dyrwood has started to study them in a science called animancy. "Animancers are like the biologists of the soul," Sawyer said. Others think it is wrong, and that the gods will punish people for it.

When the player's character arrives, a curse is causing babies to be born without souls, and animancers say they can cure it. A supernatural storm turns the hero into a Watcher, someone who can see into other people's souls and past lives. In play, that power opens up short written vignettes of strangers' earlier lives, which *PC Gamer* measured at around 500 words each.

## How it plays

Characters come from six races, among them the small, furry orlans and the giant, semi-aquatic aumaua, and eleven classes. Two of the classes were unfamiliar: the cipher, who draws power from enemies' souls, and the chanter, a bard who builds spells out of songs and stories. Combat is real-time with pause, as in *Baldur's Gate*. "Think of the combat as like a real-time strategy game, but with the ability to pause at any time and plan your next move," *PC Gamer* advised newcomers. Characters earn no experience for killing monsters, only for finishing quests, exploring and actions such as disarming traps, so a party that sneaks or talks its way past a fight loses nothing by it. *PC Gamer* called that "a bold, brilliant idea" and noted that it was "already dividing the community".

## Reception

*PC Gamer* gave it 92 and called it "the most captivating, rewarding RPG I've played on a PC in years". Its reviewer took eleven hours to reach the first city, Defiance Bay, and another thirty to reach the second, and said Obsidian had made "not only their best game to date, but one of the best RPGs on PC". Obsidian and Paradox reported 500,000 copies sold by October 2015, and *Rock Paper Shotgun* reported more than 700,000 by February 2016, when the second part of the expansion came out.

## The series

*Pillars of Eternity II: Deadfire* (2018) was crowdfunded too, raising about $4.4 million on Fig in 2017. In November 2018 Microsoft bought Obsidian, and in February 2025 the studio released *Avowed*, a first-person role-playing game set in the same world. The [CRPG Renaissance](/vault/phenomena/crpg-renaissance) entry places *Pillars* among the crowdfunded games that brought the party-based role-playing game back.
