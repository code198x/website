Konami's 1985 coin-op *Gradius* let the player choose how to arm the ship. Shoot down certain enemies and they leave a capsule. Each capsule moves a highlight one step along a row of upgrades at the bottom of the screen, and a separate button buys whichever upgrade is lit, so every capsule means deciding whether to spend now or save for something better. In Europe the game was called *Nemesis*. Its Famicom conversion introduced the cheat that became known as the Konami Code.

## Fast facts

- **Developer and publisher:** [Konami](/vault/companies/konami).
- **Arcade release:** 29 May 1985 in Japan; *Nemesis* in Europe.
- **Home versions:** MSX cartridge; Famicom (25 April 1986) and NES; ZX Spectrum and Commodore 64, published by Konami in Britain in 1987.
- **The gauge:** Speed Up, Missile, Double, Laser, Option and a shield.
- **Options:** up to four in the arcade, two on the Famicom and NES.
- **The Konami Code:** added by Kazuhisa Hashimoto to the Famicom version.
- **Sequels:** *[Salamander](/vault/games/salamander)* (1986), then *Vulcan Venture* (*Gradius II*), the third in the series, in 1988.

## The gauge

*Computer Gamer*'s arcade reporters, Mike Roberts and Eric Doyle, explained the system to British readers in February 1986. Enemies sometimes left a "supply pod". The extras were "displayed on a list at the bottom of the screen. As you collect a pod, the highlighted item on the list increments. Pressing the select button will select that item for your ship." Then the highlight jumped back to the start of the list.

The six choices ran in a fixed order:

1. **Speed Up**: faster movement, which could be taken "five times for really fast moving".
2. **Missile**: air-to-ground missiles, fired with their own button.
3. **Double**: a second shot fired upwards as well as forwards, "useful for the gun batteries on the roof of the tunnel".
4. **Laser**: turned the gun into "a rapid firing laser", which "slices through aliens like a hot knife through butter".
5. **Option**, called **Multiple** on *Nemesis*: "an extra gun, up to four, every time it is selected".
6. **Shield**, shown on the Japanese gauge as "?": "forward shielding against everything", which "lulls you into a false sense of security as attacks from the rear still can get you".

Speed Up cost one capsule and an Option five, so a better weapon meant playing on without it for longer. Losing a life took everything away. *CRASH* spelled out the rule in its review: "When a ship is lost all the features that it has gathered have to be collected once again."

The Options gave *Gradius* its look. *MSX Magazine* described each one as "une ombre", a shadow of the ship that fires at the enemy alongside it. Of the same system in *Salamander*, *Computer Gamer* worked out that four of them gave "five times the fire power you started with".

## Volcanoes and stone heads

The arcade stages included volcanoes, Stonehenge and Moai. *CRASH*'s review of the Spectrum version listed a forest, "space islands, an alien graveyard and the interior of a Bacterion battle cruiser", with a mothership to destroy at the end of each. The Moai, stone heads modelled on the statues of Easter Island, are the best-known enemies. *ACE* described them as "missile-spitting", and a *Zzap!64* caption warned that "the 'statues' are alive and spitting".

## In the arcades

*Gradius* made a lot of money. *Retro Gamer*, looking back in 2008, says it "doubled the first-year sales of *[Scramble](/vault/games/scramble)* in just a six-week period". *Computer Gamer* in 1986 called it "an excellent game" that mixed *[Defender](/vault/games/defender)*, *Scramble* and "every other horizontal scrolling game you can ever think of", and expected "a lot of interest in this game" despite "its high purchase price for the operator". Two years later *The One* judged that *Nemesis* and *Salamander* were "arguably the first games" of the progressive shoot-'em-up type, in which the player builds up firepower as the game goes on.

## The Famicom version and the Konami Code

The Famicom *Gradius* went on sale in Japan on 25 April 1986. It was cut down to suit the hardware: two Options instead of four. When *ACE* reviewed the NES cartridge in May 1989, it called that "an intelligent conversion, tailoring the game to suit the console's capabilities (there are only two drones available, for instance). As a result, it's colourful, smooth and pretty much flicker-free." It rated the cartridge 874 out of 1,000 and called it "a definite must-buy".

The Famicom version is also where the Konami Code began. Kazuhisa Hashimoto, who programmed it, told *Retro Gamer* why: "The arcade version of Gradius is really difficult. I never played it that much, and there was no way I could finish the game, so I inserted the so-called Konami code." Entered while the game is paused, up, up, down, down, left, right, left, right, B, A gives the ship a full set of upgrades at once. In America the same sequence became famous through *[Contra](/vault/games/contra)*, where the first issue of *[Nintendo Power](/vault/magazines/nintendo-power)* printed it as a way to "start your game with over 30 extra men". The [Konami Code](/vault/culture/konami-code) entry follows it further.

## Nemesis on British computers

In 1987 Konami published *Nemesis* in Britain itself. Until then Ocean's Imagine label had, in *Zzap!64*'s words, "had the pick of the deals" for Konami's coin-ops, but "now Konami have decided to do it all themselves". The two main 8-bit versions got very different reviews.

On the Commodore 64 (£8.95), *Zzap!64* gave it 80%. One reviewer welcomed "the arcade game that destroyed a large proportion of my wage packet last summer", finding that "nearly all of the original features have been included", though "the sprites flicker quite badly when the screen gets busy".

The Spectrum version (£7.95) got 59% from *CRASH*. One reviewer complained that the ship was "very unresponsive to quick key presses", and another called the game "an overly difficult, overpriced and mostly unplayable shoot 'em up". Both versions later appeared on Imagine's *Konami Arcade Collection*.

On the MSX, Konami's own one-megabit cartridge won over *MSX Magazine* in France. The reviewers had expected "encore un banal jeu d'arcade" (another ordinary arcade game) and found "un jeu excellent".

## The series

*Salamander*, in 1986, gave each weapon its own pick-up instead of the gauge, added a two-player mode and alternated horizontally and vertically scrolling stages. *Vulcan Venture* (*Gradius II*), in 1988, went back to saving up capsules and let the player pick one of four weapon sets before starting, the first of them "identical to Nemesis".
