**Football Manager** was written by [Kevin Toms](/vault/people/kevin-toms) and sold by
[Addictive Games](/vault/companies/addictive-games) by mail order, at first from a house
number on Conniburrow Boulevard in Milton Keynes. *Your Computer* carried it in
**February 1982** as "a great new game", £9.95, for the [ZX81](/vault/systems/zx81), the
ZX80 and the [TRS-80](/vault/systems/trs-80). The
[Spectrum](/vault/systems/zx-spectrum) version followed in **September 1982**, announced in
the same magazine with "Now! For the SPECTRUM 48K!"

The advertisement is worth quoting, because it makes the pitch better than a summary can:

> This is NOT a mere simulation. This is an exciting and highly enjoyable game in which you
> play the part of a Football Manager coping with the problems and decisions involved in
> running your club. [...] included are form (top teams NORMALLY win), giant-killings, wage
> bills to pay, and you can even be sacked! [...] BUT BEWARE, this game is extremely
> addictive!

The pun was the company's whole identity, and it was doing real work. Nothing in the feature
list is a spectacle. Four divisions, promotion and relegation, an FA Cup, full league tables,
a transfer market, injuries, seven skill levels, a managerial rating, a save facility — and
"as many seasons as you like". The claim being made is not that the game looks good. It is
that you will not stop playing it.

## What the machine could carry

The ZX81 original held all of that in **16K**, and it held no football. There is no pitch, no
players, no movement — you pick a side, and results arrive as numbers.

Addictive were explicit about this once the game went multi-format. Their 1984 advertisement
lists the price for each machine and then adds, in capitals:
**"3D GRAPHICS ARE NOT INCLUDED IN THE ZX81 VERSION"** — the stick-figure match highlights
everyone remembers belong to the Spectrum release and the ports that followed it, not to the
game as first published.

That is the more interesting version of the achievement, not a smaller one. A management game
needs numbers, text and a little logic. No scrolling, no sprites, no attribute clash. On a
16K machine that is the difference between a game that can exist and one that cannot, and it
is why this genre started here rather than on anything more capable.

By then Addictive were advertising from **7a Richmond Hill, Bournemouth**, "available from
computer software stockists nationwide" — a mail-order operation that had reached the
shops.

## How it was received

*[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)*'s first issue, in February 1984, put it in the
company it thought it belonged in:

> Universally agreed as one of the best of Spectrum games of any type.

The same entry describes the appeal precisely: players losing strength and reserve value and
regaining it by resting a match, morale worsening with defeats, crowds declining, and gate
money falling with them. Every system in the game feeds the next one, which is why a screen
of tables holds attention that a screen of sprites would not.

It was nominated as **Strategy Game of the Year, 1983** at the Golden Joystick Awards.
Addictive printed the nomination on the advertisements that followed, alongside a line from
the footballer **Charlie Nicholas**, reprinted from *Big K*:

> The Highlight sequences are pure magic. And then you have to sit on the sidelines and sweat
> it out! Completely fantastic. I want one!

A footballer endorsing a game about not being a footballer is a fair measure of how far the
idea had travelled by 1984.

## What it started

Everything the [management game](/vault/genres/management-game) still does was in place at
the start. A squad with attributes. A transfer market where the interesting decision is what
to sell. A league that grinds on whether you are ready or not. Injuries that ruin a plan. The
pleasure is in the spreadsheet you cannot see, and in the fact that the world keeps score.

The genre it opened outlasted almost every other British 8-bit form and is still selling.
The name went with the genre rather than with its author: *Football Manager* today is
[Sports Interactive](/vault/companies/sports-interactive)'s
[series of the same name](/vault/games/football-manager), and when Toms returned to his own
game decades later he had to call it something else.
