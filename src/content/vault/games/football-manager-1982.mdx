**Football Manager** put you in charge of a football club and left the football almost entirely off the screen. [Kevin Toms](/vault/people/kevin-toms) wrote it, and [Addictive Games](/vault/companies/addictive-games) sold it by mail order, at first from an address on Conniburrow Boulevard in Milton Keynes. In **February 1982** the company advertised it in *Your Computer* and *Computer & Video Games* as "a great new game", £9.95, for the [ZX81](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx81), the [ZX80](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx80), the [TRS-80](/vault/systems/trs-80) and the Video Genie. The [Spectrum](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum) version followed in **September 1982**: "Now! For the SPECTRUM 48K!"

The first advertisement listed what the player got: four divisions, promotion and relegation, the FA Cup, full league tables, a transfer market to buy and sell players, a team to pick for each match "(but watch out for Injuries!)", "as many seasons as you like", seven levels of play "from Beginner to Genius", a managerial rating that "tells you how good you REALLY are", and a save-game facility, "all this and much much more packed tightly into 16K RAM". The September advertisement explained the idea:

> This is NOT a mere simulation. This is an exciting and highly enjoyable game in which you
> play the part of a Football Manager coping with the problems and decisions involved in
> running your club. [...] included are form (top teams NORMALLY win), giant-killings, wage
> bills to pay, and you can even be sacked! [...] BUT BEWARE, this game is extremely
> addictive!

## Fast facts

- **Designer and programmer:** [Kevin Toms](/vault/people/kevin-toms).
- **Publisher:** [Addictive Games](/vault/companies/addictive-games), Milton Keynes, later Bournemouth.
- **First advertised:** February 1982, ZX81, ZX80, TRS-80 and Video Genie, £9.95 by mail order.
- **Spectrum 48K:** September 1982, £7.95 by mail order; £6.95 by 1984.
- **Golden Joysticks:** second to *The Hobbit* for Best Strategy Game of 1983.
- **Sales:** by Addictive's own count in 1990, half a million copies in six years.

## Tables, not players

On the ZX81 there was nothing to watch. You chose a side, played the match and read the result. The match highlights that many players remember, little figures moving about in front of a goal, belonged to the later versions. Addictive's 1984 advertisements listed "Matches in 3D graphics" among the features, then added a warning in capitals: "3D GRAPHICS ARE NOT INCLUDED IN THE ZX81 VERSION".

*[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* reviewed the Spectrum game in its first issue, in February 1984, and explained how it worked. You chose a team from 64 players on offer and tried to work your way up from the Fourth Division. Players lost strength by playing and regained it by resting a match. Morale fell with every defeat, crowds dropped, and the gate money dropped with them. Once you had picked the team and "studied the opponent's dossier", you could "sit back and watch the fully animated, edited highlights of the match!" Each part of the simulation feeds the next, which is why a screen of tables held players for whole seasons. *CRASH* called it "an amazingly engrossing game" and began its entry:

> Universally agreed as one of the best of Spectrum games of any type.

## Awards and sales

In the first *Computer & Video Games* [Golden Joystick Awards](/vault/events/golden-joystick-awards), announced in March 1984, readers placed *Football Manager* second for **Best Strategy Game of 1983**, behind Melbourne House's *[The Hobbit](/vault/games/the-hobbit)*. Addictive put it on the advertisements straight away: "Strategy Game of the Year, 1983 (Nominated) Golden Joystick Awards". The same advertisements quoted the footballer Charlie Nicholas, "reprinted from *Big K*":

> The Highlight sequences are pure magic. And then you have to sit on the sidelines and sweat
> it out! Completely fantastic. I want one!

By August 1984 the company was advertising from **7a Richmond Hill, Bournemouth**, with versions for the BBC Model B (£7.95), Commodore 64 (£7.95), Spectrum 48K (£6.95) and ZX81 16K (£5.95), and the game was "available from computer software stockists" as well as by post. *Personal Computer Games*' list of the best-selling games of 1984 had *Football Manager* at number 4 on the Spectrum, behind *Jet Set Willy*, *Daley Thompson's Decathlon* and *Manic Miner*, and at number 7 on the BBC Micro. By spring 1985 Addictive listed versions for the Amstrad CPC 464, Electron, Oric, Dragon and VIC-20 as well, with an MSX version to come.

In 1990 Addictive's advertisements for *Football Manager World Cup Edition* summed up the first game: "In 1982, Kevin Toms wrote Football Manager, six years (and half a million copies sold) later, Kevin went one better and produced Football Manager 2."

## What it started

*Football Manager* is widely credited with starting the football management game. Its advertisements already describe most of the genre: a squad to pick, a transfer market, promotion and relegation, injuries, finances, and a league that carries on season after season. Toms has come back to his game more than once. *Kevin Toms Football Star Manager* came out for mobile devices in 2016, and a remake reached Steam in August 2025. The name *Football Manager* is now best known as the title of [Sports Interactive](/vault/companies/sports-interactive)'s [series](/vault/games/football-manager), which is not descended from Toms's game.
