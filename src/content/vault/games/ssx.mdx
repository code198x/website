*SSX* came from a man who had invented a sport. Steven Rechtschaffner, its executive producer, had been a competitive freestyle skier and a television producer before he joined EA Canada, and while filming a stunt show at Whistler Blackcomb in British Columbia he had dreamed up boardercross: a motocross-style race for snowboarders. The game, *Snowboard Supercross* or *SSX* for short, kept the race and threw away the realism, with jumps so long that the team compared take-off to "a plane taking off from the tarmac". *[Edge](/vault/magazines/edge)* was surprised to find an EA game "oddly laden with enthusiasm and personality, bereft of the corporate franchise feel that marks the interminable Madden and FIFA series".

## Fast facts

- **Developer:** EA Canada, Vancouver.
- **Publisher:** [Electronic Arts](/vault/companies/electronic-arts), as the first game on its [EA Sports](/vault/companies/ea-sports) Big label.
- **Platform:** [PlayStation 2](/vault/systems/sony-playstation-2) only; one of the console's launch games in America and Europe, late 2000.
- **Name:** *Snowboard Supercross*; earlier working titles included *Super Boardercross* and *King of the Hill*.
- **Players:** one, or two on a split screen.
- **Riders:** eight, among them Mac, Kaori, Elise and Zoe.

## Making it

Rechtschaffner first suggested a boardercross game when he arrived at EA Canada, but it did not go ahead. Instead he worked on *[FIFA](/vault/games/fifa)* and then helped launch EA's *Triple Play* baseball series. Its success earned him what the studio called a "silver bullet": a producer with a hit could make something new. He told *Wireframe* that if a new game failed, "you lost that silver bullet and you had to have another success before you could get another opportunity".

The game began with one designer, Adam MacKay-Smith, who had worked on *Need for Speed II*. "I ended up spending a year and a half on that game all by myself," he said. "The odds were against us – no one thought a snowboarding game was going to do good." He took ideas from the arcade games in EA Canada's lobby, among them Sega's *Top Skater* and Midway's *NFL Blitz*, but named Psygnosis's *[WipEout](/vault/games/wipeout)* as the biggest influence, on the game's feel, its rewards and its electronic music. The music budget, he recalled, was $100,000 for the whole soundtrack, "at the time, FIFA had, like, $100,000 [to license] one song". The team kept adjusting the physics, and producer Larry LaPierre described the result: "you hit the apex, there's this pause, and then all of a sudden you're dropping like an anchor." The game was delayed by six months for polish.

## Playing it

The player picks a rider, a board and a mountain, and races down it against the others. Winning races and performing tricks unlocks more riders, boards and courses. In the air, *Edge* explained, "tricks can be performed by using the D-pad for turns and the L and R buttons for grabs. The longer the grabs are held, the more points are accrued." Tricks also fill a boost meter, which the rider can spend on extra speed. The right analogue stick swings the rider's arms, so "well-timed punches can knock down opponents".

The courses were full of alternative routes and shortcuts. Tokyo Megaplex was modelled on the inside of a pinball machine. The last track, Untracked, came from MacKay-Smith's first idea for the game, a climb to "the mysterious powder track... where there were no rules".

*Edge* thought the graphics had "moments of splendour" but no more than that, with ice "still picked out in the same non-reflective pale blue that's been used to represent it since the 8bit era". The handling made up for it. It compared the analogue control with Nintendo's *1080° Snowboarding*, noted the impact of every landing, and found the two-player split screen ran with "no noticeable slow down at all".

## SSX Tricky and after

EA's sales and marketing staff wanted a sequel fast, and *SSX Tricky* followed in 2001, after MacKay-Smith had left. It added courses, a rivalry system in which knocking opponents over started feuds, celebrity voices including David Arquette, Lucy Liu and Macy Gray, and a new rider, Psymon Stark, who appeared in every *SSX* game afterwards. LaPierre called it "kind of SSX 1.5". *SSX 3* (2003) replaced separate courses with one continuous mountain, and was the first in the series to sell more than a million copies.

EA Sports Big added *NBA Street*, *Freekstyle* and *Sled Storm* among others, but Rechtschaffner believed the label suffered when EA tried to release its games every year: "I looked towards Nintendo and I said, 'I think we should bring out a new game every two to three years.'" When *SSX* returned in 2012, on the Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3, it moved to real mountains such as Patagonia and the Antarctic, with "deadly descents" that used wingsuits and headlamps, and a button to rewind time. *Edge* gave it five out of ten, finding that it had veered "away from some of the fun and fireworks of yesteryear".
