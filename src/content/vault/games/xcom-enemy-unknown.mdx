## Overview
*XCOM: Enemy Unknown* is Firaxis's 2012 reboot of
[*X-COM: UFO Defense*](/vault/games/x-com-ufo-defense). It keeps the shape of
the original — a strategic layer managing bases, research and funding above a
turn-based tactical layer where soldiers die permanently — and rebuilds almost
everything underneath it.

The dropped hyphen is not a typo. It is how the two eras are told apart: **X-COM**
for the 1990s MicroProse games, **XCOM** for the Firaxis series.

## What changed
| Original (1994) | Reboot (2012) |
|---|---|
| Time Units spent per action | Two actions per soldier per turn |
| Undifferentiated rookies | Class system with branching promotions |
| Freeform base placement worldwide | One base, viewed in cutaway |
| Inventory managed item by item | Restricted loadout slots |
| Cover implicit in terrain | Explicit cover states, shown in the interface |

The direction of every one of those changes is the same: fewer things to
manage, each with a clearer consequence. Whether that is a loss or a gain is the
long-running argument between the two audiences, and it is a genuine design
disagreement rather than a matter of one being unfinished.

## What stayed
- **Permadeath.** Soldiers do not come back, and the game encourages naming them.
- **The two layers feeding each other.** Research gates equipment; tactical results gate funding.
- **Fog of war and ambush.** Discovering a pod of aliens still hands them the first move.
- **Escalation.** The enemy roster grows harder faster than the squad improves.

## The series
- *XCOM: Enemy Unknown* (2012), expanded by *Enemy Within* (2013).
- *XCOM 2* (2016), which inverts the premise — the aliens won, and the player runs an insurgency — expanded by *War of the Chosen* (2017).
- *XCOM: Chimera Squad* (2020), a smaller spin-off.

## Why it matters here
The reboot is the reason the original's design vocabulary is still current.
Action economies, overwatch, permadeath and the strategic-tactical sandwich all
reached a mainstream audience through these games, and "XCOM-like" became a
usable genre label — which is a strange fate for a system first worked out on a
48K Spectrum in [*Laser Squad*](/vault/games/laser-squad).
