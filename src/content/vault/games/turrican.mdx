When Rainbow Arts showed *Turrican* to *ZZAP!* in the spring of 1990, its representative Nikki Hemming promised it was one of the best Commodore 64 games ever. The reviewer admitted the claim "almost had me laughing" when the game started with a little figure hopping over some hills against a bland background. Then came the waterfalls, the lightning weapon, a giant clenched fist that attacked at speed, a fish monster and a world of Giger-style alien architecture. *ZZAP!* gave it a Gold Medal and 97%, and said "all the whinging about the 64's demise is shown up as pathetic drivel." [Manfred Trenz](/vault/people/manfred-trenz) had written it for the C64, and the [Amiga](/vault/systems/commodore-amiga) and other versions were conversions of his game.

## Fast facts

- **Designer and programmer:** [Manfred Trenz](/vault/people/manfred-trenz).
- **Publisher:** [Rainbow Arts](/vault/companies/rainbow-arts), 1990.
- **Original version:** [Commodore 64](/vault/systems/commodore-64).
- **Amiga version:** [Factor 5](/vault/companies/factor-5), with music by [Chris Hülsbeck](/vault/people/chris-huelsbeck).
- **Other versions:** [Atari ST](/vault/systems/atari-st), [Amstrad CPC](/vault/systems/amstrad-cpc), [ZX Spectrum](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum) (by [Probe](/vault/companies/probe-software)) and, from [Accolade](/vault/companies/accolade), the [Mega Drive](/vault/systems/sega-mega-drive).
- **Size:** five worlds, 13 levels, about 1,300 screens.
- **Sequels:** *Turrican II* (1991), *Turrican 3* (1993) and console games including *Super Turrican* for the Super Nintendo.

## Morgul's kingdom

The plot is the usual kind. Morgul, a three-headed creature who is the source of mankind's nightmares, has escaped from the dimension where the hero Devolon banished him, and only one man in an armoured combat suit, the Turrican, is brave enough to fight him. The game is a platform shooter that scrolls in all directions. Its five worlds are split into 13 levels, which *ZZAP!* reckoned came to 1,300 screens, with hidden rooms full of diamonds and a boss at the end of each section. One level straps a jetpack to the hero and turns into a vertically scrolling shoot-'em-up.

The weapons are what reviewers remembered:

- **Laser or machine gun:** a quick press of the fire button. Power-ups give a triple shot or swap the gun for a laser.
- **Lightning:** hold the fire button and a beam comes out that the player can rotate around the hero. Power-ups extend it to the width of the screen. *ZZAP!* called it "one of the best weapons in any game".
- **Grenades, mines and energy lines:** limited stocks. Energy lines, fired with the space bar, act as a smart bomb.
- **Gyroscope:** pull down and press space and the hero becomes a spinning wheel that can't be hurt and fits through narrow gaps. *CRASH* noted that it could be used three times per life.

Collecting 300 diamonds earned an extra continue, so exploring the levels paid off.

## Borrowed ideas

In 2003 Factor 5's Julian Eggebrecht, who produced the game, told *Edge* where the ideas came from. The team started from a little-known Data East arcade game, *Psycho-Nics Oscar*, and added what they liked from elsewhere: "They took the character design and pacing from Psycho Nic, the searching from *[Metroid](/vault/games/metroid)* and the leaping and exploration from Mario." The title screen and logo came from the cover of Manowar's heavy metal album *Kings of Metal*, and the alien architecture from the work of H. R. Giger. Eggebrecht also named Thalamus's *Hawkeye*, then "the most advanced game on the C64", as an inspiration.

The game was previewed early in 1990 as *Hurrican* and renamed at Trenz's request. The voice in the introduction was a joke, Andreas Escher told *GO64!*: Markus Wiederstein recorded it, and Trenz distorted it and put it in the game.

## Four months for the Amiga

Trenz expected to finish the C64 version in March 1990. Then, as Eggebrecht tells it, the decision was taken to launch an Amiga version at the same time. "We had four months," Eggebrecht told *Edge*. "Nowadays, to do a port, you can re-use code. Then we couldn't. We had to do a complete rewrite." *The Games Machine* credited the Amiga game to Trenz and Holger Schmidt of Factor 5. Parallax scrolling was hard on the Amiga, and Eggebrecht said that "in the end we built the scrolling from sprites".

## What the magazines said

- **Commodore 64:** *ZZAP!* issue 61, May 1990: Gold Medal, 97% overall, 97% for graphics and 90% for sound. It called the game "an absolutely awesome experience, developed specifically for the C64".
- **Amiga:** *ZZAP!* gave it 94% in August 1990, calling it "an astounding testimony to the brilliance of the C64 game that a fairly straightforward conversion has resulted in one of the best Amiga games we've seen". *CU Amiga* gave 90%. *The Games Machine* was cooler: "Unfortunately not a great improvement over the C64", although it called the game "the best pure arcade blast released for the 16-bit Commodore so far this year". For *ACE*, "the overall feel of *Turrican* is pure coin-op".
- **ZX Spectrum:** *CRASH* gave Probe's conversion 93% in June 1990: "a no holds barred shoot-'em-up that no joystick mangling fan should miss."
- **Amstrad CPC:** *Amstrad Action* gave 90%.

The sound differed between machines. The C64 game had title music but none during play. The Amiga version had "a different tune per level" by Chris Hülsbeck, and *ZZAP!* thought the soundtrack "sounds just like something Konami might write".

The console versions came from outside. Rainbow Arts sold the console rights to Accolade, which published *Turrican* on the Mega Drive. Eggebrecht told *Edge* that Factor 5 had been "begging Rainbow Arts to let us do the console" and that "the Genesis version of Turrican actually looks worse than the Amiga version".

## Sequels

*Turrican II* (1991) reunited Trenz and Escher on the C64, with Katakis-style flying levels, and Factor 5 wrote the Amiga version, for which Hülsbeck built his seven-voice music system. Eggebrecht told *Edge* that at the pre-launch demonstration in Cologne two people were hurt in the rush for one of 900 copies. *Turrican 3* followed on the Amiga in 1993, developed by Factor 5 and published in Britain by [Renegade](/vault/companies/renegade) at £25.99; *The One* called it "definitely the best of the trilogy". Factor 5 also made *Super Turrican* for the Super Nintendo. A *Turrican 3D* by Trenz was announced for 1999 but never released.

Who deserved the credit became a sore point. Andreas Escher, Trenz's graphic artist, said in 2000 that Factor 5 had earned "a sack of money with the Amiga version and games console sequels of 'Turrican' without acknowledging that 'Turrican' is Manfred's product".
