**Ridge Racer** is [Namco’s](/vault/companies/namco) 1993 arcade driving game. You race through a coastal city, trying to finish before time runs out. Its textured scenery belongs to Namco’s System 22 hardware; the familiar [PlayStation](/vault/systems/sony-playstation) version followed in Japan in 1994.

## Learn the road

The arcade game offers four modes. Novice and Intermediate use the main course at different speeds. Advanced adds a technical branch, and Time Trial pits you against a single computer-controlled car at a higher speed. Those distinctions come from the original operators manual: selecting a harder mode changes the task, rather than adding opponents alone.

The clock makes mistakes expensive. The aim is to complete the required laps within the allowance; reaching zero ends the game. A fast stretch is only useful if you can carry that advantage through the next corner. Familiarity with the road becomes as valuable as a quick reaction.

The UK sit-down cabinet described in the manual supplies a steering wheel, pedals, clutch and gear lever. Automatic transmission is also available. Operators can change lap counts and the time allowance, so two machines need not offer exactly the same challenge.

## The arcade at home

The PlayStation conversion made Ridge Racer part of the new console’s early identity. It put a game already associated with arcade 3D onto a machine that could sit beneath a television. The conversion and the arcade original remain separate releases, with different hardware behind them.

[Virtua Racing](/vault/games/virtua-racing) and [Daytona USA](/vault/games/daytona-usa) offer useful neighbours: all ask you to read a three-dimensional road at speed. Ridge Racer gives that road a textured surface. [Texture mapping](/vault/techniques/texture-mapping) explains how an image can supply the road markings and building detail that would otherwise need more geometry.
