Pass across the ice and shoot before the receiver settles the puck. The **one-timer** made that quick exchange a central part of *NHL '94*. EA's contemporary advertising singled it out alongside the real players and the new Anaheim and Florida teams. The game arrived in 1993, despite the year in its title.

[EA Sports](/vault/companies/ea-sports) published versions for the [Mega Drive](/vault/systems/sega-mega-drive), Super NES and Sega CD. The Genesis manual credits Mark Lesser for programming, Michael Brook for production and adapting the design, Doug Wike for graphics, Rob Hubbard for music and sound, and Dieter Ruehle for organ music. Those credits describe that version, rather than assigning every conversion to one studio.

## A small set of controls

On the Mega Drive, B passes and C shoots. To attempt a one-timer, the player presses C after the pass leaves the first skater. The receiver strikes it immediately. Manual goalie control adds another decision: trust the computer in goal, or take over to meet an approaching shot.

The game also supports up to four players with an adapter, allowing teammates as well as opponents. Fighting and blood were removed from this instalment. Real teams and named players remain, with ratings based on their performance.

## Matches and playoffs

The console game's “Regular Season” option means a single regular-season match. It does **not** run a complete league schedule or a career. There are playoff modes, including best-of-seven series, and saved user records. NHL94.com's comparison of the early EA games places the full season mode in *NHL '95*.

The Sega CD edition made use of the different medium. *Sega Visions*' December 1993/January 1994 guide describes video clips, commentary by Ron Barr and organ music. These additions should not be confused with a different set of league-management features.

## Playing beyond 1994

The game's continued following is visible in what its players organise. NHL94.com provides guides for online play, leagues, tournaments and editing the Genesis and Super NES versions. Its enthusiasm is the community's judgement, rather than proof that every player prefers the old game to newer hockey games.

EA returned to it in 2020 with *NHL 94 Rewind*. The publisher described the combination as the older game's graphics and gameplay with contemporary teams and rosters. That release and the fan leagues are two different ways the original design has continued: an official update for newer consoles, and players keeping the earlier versions in competition.
