When Namco's *[Rolling Thunder](/vault/games/rolling-thunder)* did well in the arcades, *[Sinclair User](/vault/magazines/sinclair-user)* wrote in June 1988, "clones were only a matter of time. And Shinobi is probably the best of them." Sega's 1987 coin-op swapped Namco's secret agent for a ninja, Joe Musashi, and gave him an unlimited supply of throwing stars, a two-level street to leap about on, and a single spell per stage that wiped every enemy off the screen. One hit from an enemy's weapon still killed him. *Retro Gamer* called it "an improved variant of Namco's excellent Rolling Thunder", and judged that "while it was tough, it was never unfair". The game made Musashi the hero of a series that Sega was still adding to in 2025.

## Fast facts

- **Developer and publisher:** [Sega](/vault/companies/sega).
- **Arcade:** 1987, on Sega's System 16 board.
- **Hero:** Joe Musashi, a ninja.
- **Structure:** five missions of three or four stages, each mission ending with a boss.
- **Home versions:** [Master System](/vault/systems/sega-master-system), 1988; Spectrum, Commodore 64, Amstrad CPC, Atari ST and Amiga, 1989, published by [Virgin](/vault/companies/virgin-games) Mastertronic; also PC Engine, NES and PC.
- **Sequels:** *Shadow Dancer* (arcade) and *The Revenge of Shinobi* (Mega Drive), both 1989; *Shinobi III: Return of the Ninja Master* (1993); *Shinobi: Art of Vengeance* (2025).

## The arcade game

A crime syndicate called Zeed has kidnapped children, and *Retro Gamer* puts Musashi's task simply: "to rescue the kidnapped children of your clan". He has to find and touch every hostage in a stage before he can leave it. Some hostages give him a power-up, which, as Sega Retro describes it, turns his throwing stars into a gun and his punches and kicks into a sword. The streets have an upper and a lower level, and both Musashi and some of his enemies can jump between them.

*Sinclair User* named the five missions by their bosses. The first ends with Ken Oh, who spits fireballs in circular patterns. The second, on the waterfront, ends with the Black Turtle, a helicopter whose "glowing front nosecone" is the target. Then come Mandara, "really odd", and Lobster, "a bit more conventional, but harder", and finally the Masked Ninja, which the magazine found harder still. Sega Retro gives the settings as slums, a harbour, a weapons factory, an outpost and the enemy base.

The ninja magic acted, in *Retro Gamer*'s description, "like a smart bomb", and could be used once on each stage. It came in three forms: a burst of electricity, a hurricane, and "a strange spell where lots of clones sped around the screen taking out any foes in the vicinity". Finishing a stage without it earned a bonus. Between missions came a bonus stage seen from Musashi's own viewpoint, in which he threw shuriken at ninjas leaping towards him; hitting them all won an extra life.

*Sinclair User* thought its combat ideas conventional but its gameplay good enough to make it a winner, and gave it 9 for gameplay, 8 for sound and addictiveness, and 7 for graphics.

## On the Master System

The Master System version was, for *[The Games Machine](/vault/magazines/the-games-machine)* in January 1989, "another very close Sega conversion of its arcade big brother". It changed more than that suggests. The villains became the Ring of Five, a terrorist group holding the children of world leaders hostage. Musashi now had an energy bar instead of dying in one hit, and rescuing children became optional, earning weapons and extra health instead. The magic was won in the bonus round, and took new forms: one froze every enemy on the screen, one made Musashi invincible, and "flying squirrel magic" let him fly for a short time. *The Games Machine* called it "one of those games that does nothing for innovation but is well presented", and "almost worth buying a Sega console for".

## On home computers

In Britain, Virgin Mastertronic held the rights to convert Sega's arcade games, and Binary Design programmed *Shinobi* for it in 1989. *[Computer and Video Games](/vault/magazines/computer-and-video-games)* tried the versions together in August. "The best version of Shinobi is the C64," it concluded, with "excellent graphics, fast action, good tunes and faithful arcade gameplay". The Amstrad and Spectrum versions were "great fun". The Atari ST's graphics were "juddery".

The computer versions had a story of their own. *[CRASH](/vault/magazines/crash-magazine)* explained that Musashi is invited back to his old ninja school's graduation ceremony as a guest, only for a renegade graduate called Bwah Foo to carry off the new graduates and hold them to ransom. On the Spectrum (£9.99 on cassette, £14.99 on disk) its reviewers disagreed. One gave it 85% and expected it to "keep the Bruce Lee fans among you happy". Another, at 67%, thought it "basically Double Dragon with a few extra obstacles stuck on the ground to jump over". The overall rating was 76%. *Sinclair User* gave it 75% and described the difficulty: "the easy start rapidly becomes a tough middle and a virtually impossible end."

Other versions followed elsewhere. Asmik published one for NEC's PC Engine in Japan in 1989, which *Retro Gamer* thought "decent but still sorely lacking". [Tengen](/vault/companies/tengen) published an NES version in America, and there was a PC version too.

## Later history

Two sequels were developed together and released in 1989: *Shadow Dancer* for the arcades and *The Revenge of Shinobi* for the [Mega Drive](/vault/systems/sega-mega-drive). *Revenge* replaced the one-hit deaths with an energy bar, let players choose between four kinds of magic, and added a double jump. *Wireframe* picked out *Shinobi III: Return of the Ninja Master* (1993) as "perhaps" the series' high point. Musashi's grandson, also called Joe, starred in *The Cyber Shinobi* on the Master System in 1990, and the [Game Gear](/vault/systems/sega-game-gear) had its own *GG Shinobi*, which *Mean Machines* gave 90%.

*Shinobi* came back on the Wii's Virtual Console and on Xbox Live Arcade in 2009, and a version for SNK's Neo Geo appeared in December 2024. In 2025 Sega published *Shinobi: Art of Vengeance*, made by Lizardcube, the studio behind *Streets of Rage 4*. Joe Musashi is its hero again, now "the head of the Oboro Clan", fighting for revenge after his village is burned and his clan turned to stone.
