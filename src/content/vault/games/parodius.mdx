"In Parodius, Konami takes a sideswipe at the console shoot 'em up establishment," *Computer and Video Games* wrote in 1991, "and why not, seeing as they're responsible for most of it?" Konami made *[Gradius](/vault/games/gradius)*, the horizontal shooter with the power-up bar, and *Parodius* is the same game played for laughs. The ship can be an octopus or a penguin, the first boss is a pirate galleon shaped like a cat, and a megaphone mounted on the nose shouts Japanese phrases at the enemy. The name, as *Mean Machines* spelled it out, is "Parody + Gradius".

## Fast facts

- **Developer and publisher:** [Konami](/vault/companies/konami).
- **First game:** *Parodius: Tako wa Chikyū o Sukuu* ("the octopus saves the Earth"), [MSX](/vault/systems/msx), 1988, Japan only.
- **Arcade:** *Parodius Da!*, 1990, released in Europe as *Parodius*.
- **Conversions:** [NES](/vault/systems/nintendo-entertainment-system), [Game Boy](/vault/systems/game-boy), [PC Engine](/vault/systems/pc-engine), [Super Nintendo](/vault/systems/super-nintendo), and later PlayStation and Saturn.
- **Ships:** Vic Viper from *Gradius*, TwinBee from *[TwinBee](/vault/games/twinbee)*, Takosuke the octopus and Pentarou the penguin.

## From the MSX to the arcade

The series began on the [MSX](/vault/systems/msx), the home computer standard on which Konami published many of its 1980s games. *Edge* listed *Parodius* among the games that "made their first official appearances on MSX". Its penguin hero was older still: *Retro Gamer* points out that Pentarou first appeared in Konami's *Antarctic Adventure*, which explains the series' "inexplicable obsession with penguins".

The arcade game came in 1990. *Edge* later summed up its progress: "Starting life as a coin-op in 1990, Konami's superbly crafted parody of its classic Nemesis became one of the best shoot 'em ups on the PC Engine and SFC". (*Nemesis* was *Gradius*'s name in Europe.)

## How it plays

Underneath the jokes it is *Gradius*. Shot enemies leave power-up icons, and each one collected moves a highlight along a bar of upgrades at the bottom of the screen, from speed-ups to shields. Players can pick when to cash it in or let the game choose. Some icons start a roulette instead, flicking along the bar at speed; stop it on a weapon you already have and, *Mean Machines* warned, "you lose all your current power-ups". Each of the four ships has its own weapons: TwinBee a three-way shot and a "Rocket Punch", the octopus a "Ripple" of rings, Pentarou a spread of exploding bullets and a bubble shield.

The other half comes from *TwinBee*: bells that change colour when shot. A golden bell is worth points, a blue one gives a smart bomb, a red one adds miniature ships that fire lasers, and a green one inflates the ship like bubble-gum, making it invulnerable but unable to fire. The silver bell bolts on the megaphone, which, in *Mean Machines*' words, destroys enemies "by shouting a selection of withering Japanese phrases at them".

## The stages

*Nintendo Magazine System*'s guide to the Super Nintendo version describes what the player meets. There is a castle of sweets guarded by rows of grinning lips and teeth; a stage of sumo wrestlers who walk across the ceiling and whip the ship with their loincloths, ending with a sumo boss whose stomach is his weak spot; a mothership wearing shades and smoking a pipe, followed by a female Easter Island head that spits out statues; a pinball table; a dream of pink clouds where a giant girl blows bubbles full of imps; frozen waters full of begoggled penguins; and a bathroom where an octopus washes its head with soap. The final boss, a giant octopus, "doesn't move" and "doesn't fire". The music, *Computer and Video Games* noted of the Game Boy version, mixes *Gradius* themes with "pepped-up classical tunes".

## The conversions

*Computer and Video Games* reviewed the Game Boy version on import in September 1991, liked the "tremendous" bosses and called it "one of the best shoot 'em ups yet seen on the Game Boy. An essential purchase!" The Super Nintendo version followed in 1992. *Mean Machines* thought it "the best version of the game ever", with "pirate penguins and sumo-wrestling pigs" and some slowdown on the hardest settings, and one reviewer added that he had enjoyed it "on the NES, Gameboy and PC Engine" before. It reached number two in the magazine's Super Famicom chart.

The 32-bit consoles got the arcade games almost whole. *Edge* previewed the PlayStation *Gokujō Parodius* "Deluxe Pack" in January 1995, which included the original arcade game too, as a test of the new machine's 2D abilities. *Retro Gamer* later called the two arcade games "like playing Gradius programmed by lunatics — but still manage to be utterly brilliant", and described *Sexy Parodius* as playing "just as well as the other games in the series".
