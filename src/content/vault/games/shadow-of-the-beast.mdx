*Shadow of the Beast* was designed to sell Amigas. Its deep parallax scrolling, ethereal soundtrack by David Whittaker and alien landscapes made other systems look primitive. That the gameplay was brutally difficult and sometimes unfair mattered less than the technical showcase it provided.

## Fast facts

- **Developer:** Reflections — founded by **Cormac Batstone and Martin Edmondson**, described by *The One* in March 1990 as "the dynamic duo behind Reflections, the development team which debuted with Ballistix and Shadow Of The Beast". ("Reflections Interactive" is a later name for the studio.)
- **Publisher:** Psygnosis.
- **Programmer:** **Paul Howarth**, credited as such by *The Games Machine* in October 1989. **Design and art:** Martin Edmondson, who is often miscredited as the programmer.
- **Artist:** Roger Dean (cover), internal team.

## Technical showcase

| Feature | Achievement |
|---------|-------------|
| Parallax layers | **13**, per Psygnosis' own advertising — the October 1989 feature list reads "13 levels of parallax scrolling". ⚠ That is the publisher's count; later technical breakdowns reach 12, depending on what is counted as a layer |
| Colour palette | Up to 128 on screen |
| Scrolling | Advertised as "50 frames per second arcade quality scroll". ⚠ Publisher's claim; 50 Hz is the PAL display rate regardless, so the number proves less than it sounds |
| Memory | Fits a stock 512K Amiga. The same advertising quotes "2Mb of graphics compressed in two disks" and "900K of emotive music" — compressed sizes, not memory needed |
