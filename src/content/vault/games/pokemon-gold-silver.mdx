*Pokémon Gold* and *Silver* kept time. A clock inside the cartridge, kept running by its battery, tracked the real hour and day, and the game followed it: some Pokémon only came out at night, and some events happened only on certain days of the week. *Edge*'s columnist Steven Poole was struck by how tightly the game was tied to the real world. "Some species of monster are nocturnal," he wrote in 2000, "so that you will only see them if you are playing after dark." He called it "chronological totalitarianism", and offered the cynical view that it was "a fiendishly clever way to turn the world's children into sleep-deprived, schoolwork-shunning zombies".

The games took far longer than planned. In the summer of 1997, with the first games selling in Japan, *Nintendo Power* reported that Nintendo planned to release "Gold and Silver versions of Pocket Monsters 2" that autumn. They came out in November 1999, and sold 3.3 million copies in Japan by the end of the year.

## Fast facts

- **Developer:** [Game Freak](/vault/companies/game-freak). **Publisher:** [Nintendo](/vault/companies/nintendo).
- **Released:** Japan, November 1999; North America, October 2000; Europe, April 2001.
- **Platform:** Game Boy Color.
- **Pokémon:** 100 new species, 251 in all.
- **New:** a real-time clock, day and night, breeding, held items, the Dark and Steel types, a phone in the Pokégear.
- **Sales:** 3.3 million in Japan by December 1999.

## Space World 1999

When Nintendo finally showed the games at its Space World show at the Makuhari Messe in August 1999, it gave them more than a quarter of the floor. *Edge*'s reporter counted 288 monitors running "Pocket Monsters Gold and Silver (essentially Pocket Monsters 2)", and noted that to the untrained eye the new game "seemed to look absolutely the same as the original". The crowds of children did not mind. In Britain at that moment, the first games were only just going on sale.

## What changed

The world of *Gold* and *Silver* is Johto, a new region next to the first games' Kanto, with its own eight Gym Leaders. The new monsters arrived alongside new rules. The Dark type, *Retro Gamer* explained, answered the over-strong Psychic Pokémon of *Red* and *Blue*. Pokémon could now hold items, and two of them left at the Day Care could produce an egg. *Retro Gamer* called the night-and-day cycle "inspired", and the clock, it said in a list of the best Game Boy Color games, gave the games "a 'real life' quality missing from previous Pokémon titles". The Pokégear added a phone, so that trainers the player had met could call with tips. And some wild Pokémon appeared, very rarely, in different colours.

Two Game Boy Colors could also swap Mystery Gifts through the console's infrared port.

## Back to Kanto

The biggest surprise came after the end. Once the player has beaten the Elite Four, the game opens up Kanto, the whole region of the first games, with its own eight Gym Leaders to beat. A player with all 16 badges can climb Mt. Silver, where the last opponent waiting at the top is Red, the hero of the first games.

How Game Freak fitted two regions into one Game Boy cartridge has its own popular story. [Satoru Iwata](/vault/people/satoru-iwata), then at [HAL Laboratory](/vault/companies/hal-laboratory), helped Game Freak with its code: Tsunekazu Ishihara later remembered him writing "tools for compressing the Pokémon graphic code". It is often said that Iwata's compression made room for Kanto. A 2023 analysis of the games' code, reported by *Nintendo Life*, found otherwise: his routine made the games faster rather than much smaller.

## Abroad

The games reached America in October 2000. *N64 Magazine* reported in its Christmas issue that they were "storming off the shelves in the US", and *Edge* counted 48 million copies of *Red*, *Blue*, *Yellow*, *Gold* and *Silver* sold worldwide by May 2001. They went on sale in Britain in April 2001. Nintendo's *Pokémon Stadium 2* on the Nintendo 64 let players load their *Gold* and *Silver* teams through the Transfer Pak and battle them in 3D.

## Later versions

*Pokémon Crystal* followed, reworking the same adventure. In 2009 Game Freak remade the pair for the Nintendo DS as *Pokémon HeartGold* and *SoulSilver*, which had sold more than 2.6 million copies in Japan by the end of that year, and in September 2017 the originals were re-released on the Nintendo 3DS download store.
