*Cave Story* is a 2004 game that looks as if it was made in 1988. A small robot with no memory wakes in a cave, finds a village of rabbit-like creatures called Mimigas under threat from a scientist and his henchmen, and fights through a maze of caverns with a growing collection of guns. It is a Japanese freeware game, *Doukutsu Monogatari*, and one person made all of it: Daisuke Amaya, who works as Pixel. *Edge* picked it out as a free download in March 2005, praising a "level of polish and depth that could easily elevate it from freeware".

## One person, five years

Amaya made the game in his spare time over about five years. *Wireframe* pictured him "huddled up in front of his computer for about five years", emerging with a game that was "an homage to *[Metroid](/vault/games/metroid)* – before such a thing was commonplace". *Edge* described the result as "designed and developed solely" by Amaya, and he gave it away.

It was small. The German magazine *Lotek64* noted in 2005 that the whole game, with its manual in HTML, came to 865 KB compressed and ran on Windows 98 or later with DirectX 5. It was in Japanese, but the fan group Aeon Genesis translated it into English, which *Edge* said made it "an essential download".

## Playing it

The hero, Quote, explores a network of linked underground areas. *Edge* described it in 2010 as "*Metroid* or *Castlevania* filtered through *Spelunker*": capsules extend his health bar in the manner of *Metroid*'s energy tanks, and his weapons grow stronger with use, as in *[Castlevania](/vault/games/castlevania)*.

The weapons are the clever part. Defeated enemies drop energy crystals that level up the gun in Quote's hand, and every hit he takes drains experience back out of it, so a powerful weapon can drop a level in the middle of a fight. *Edge* called the result "a juggling routine, balancing the usefulness of charged weapons against the desire to strengthen others". Characters offer to swap or upgrade some weapons along the way, and holding on to a weaker one sometimes leads to something better later. The bosses, *Edge* thought, were "resolutely old-school": learn the pattern, dodge, and hit the weak spot.

The game has several endings, and seeing the best one means meeting what *Edge* called "a horrendously nasty sting in the tail". Its characters are part of its appeal, among them Curly Brace, a second robot, and Balrog, a boss shaped like a toaster who keeps coming back to fight Quote.

## From freeware to Nicalis

*Cave Story* spread among players of independent games. *Retro Gamer* noted in 2006 that "thanks to the phenomenal *Cave Story*", the *Metroid*-style exploration game was "de rigueur within the indie scene".

The American publisher Nicalis, whose co-founder Tyrone Rodriguez made a business of bringing PC freeware to WiiWare "in association with the original developers", released a new version for the Wii in 2010, credited to Nicalis and Studio Pixel. It cost 1,200 Wii points. *Edge* gave it 8 out of 10, liked the smoothed sprites and remastered music, the option to play with the original graphics, the new difficulty modes and the chance to play as Curly, and summed it up as "the great NES game lost for 20 years down the back of the sofa". A DSiWare version followed later in 2010. *Cave Story+* reached Steam on 22 November 2011 and the Nintendo Switch on 20 June 2017; *Cave Story 3D*, a remake with three-dimensional graphics made by Nicalis and published by NIS America, came out for the 3DS in November 2011.

Amaya went on making games on his own. At BitSummit in Kyoto in 2013, where *Edge* noted that *Cave Story* had been ported to "various PC, console and handheld platforms", he announced a new game for iOS, explaining that Android's variety of hardware made it impossible for one person to test.
