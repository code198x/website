By 1999 the *[Fatal Fury](/vault/games/fatal-fury)* series had been running for eight years, and its fighters were getting old. *Garou: Mark of the Wolves* jumped ten years ahead in the story and threw almost all of them out. Of the original cast only Terry Bogard came back, older, with a new look and an adopted son. The son was Rock Howard, the child of the series' great villain, Geese Howard, and the game was built around him and a generation of new fighters.

It was also very nearly the last thing the original SNK made. *Garou* reached arcades at the end of 1999. In January 2000 ownership of SNK passed to Aruze, a pachinko machine maker, and by October 2001 the company was bankrupt. A planned sequel was shelved, and the series stood still for a quarter of a century.

## Fast facts

- **Developer and publisher:** [SNK](/vault/companies/snk).
- **Hardware:** the [Neo Geo](/vault/systems/neo-geo), in arcades (MVS) and at home (AES).
- **Released:** arcades in late 1999; the home Neo Geo cartridge in 2000.
- **Later versions:** [Dreamcast](/vault/systems/sega-dreamcast) (2001; in North America as *Fatal Fury: Mark of the Wolves*) and PlayStation 2 (2005, Japan only).
- **Cast:** 14 playable fighters, with Terry Bogard the only returning one.
- **Sequel:** *Fatal Fury: City of the Wolves* (2025).

## A 2D game after all

SNK first meant the game to be 3D. *Edge* reported in January 2000, after SNK's private arcade show, that *Mark of the Wolves* "was originally designed as a 3D game, but SNK felt that the Hyper Neo-Geo 64 board couldn't deal with the level of graphics it hoped to create." SNK's 3D arcade board had already carried *Fatal Fury: Wild Ambition*. The new game went back to the ageing 2D Neo Geo instead, and *Retro Gamer* later counted its visuals among "the most polished" ever seen in a 2D fighter, "certainly on the Neo-Geo".

It also dropped the series' oldest trick. Earlier *Fatal Fury* games let fighters hop between a front and a back plane of the arena; *Garou* fought on a single plane, with four attack buttons, closer to SNK's *[The King of Fighters](/vault/games/king-of-fighters)*.

## A new generation

The game is set ten years after Geese Howard's death. Terry has adopted Rock, whose moves mix his father's style with Terry's. Many of the new fighters are children or pupils of older ones: Kim Dong Hwan and Kim Jae Hoon are the sons of the taekwondo master Kim Kaphwan, and Hokutomaru is a pupil of Terry's brother Andy. Others are new. B. Jenet is a runaway heiress who leads a band of pirates; Gato is a lone fighter hunting his own father; Hotaru Futaba is a young girl searching for her missing brother. The tournament's host, and the final boss, is Kain R. Heinlein, Rock's uncle, who wants to take Geese's place as the ruler of the city's underworld.

The change of cast was a decision about style. Nobuyuki Kuroki, one of the game's designers, recalled in a 2018 interview that the earlier games had been full of old men and macho characters. Around the time *Garou* was in development, he said, the Japanese word *moe*, used for cute, endearing characters, was coming into use; the team were not sure what it meant, asked colleagues who were, and the result was Hotaru.

## T.O.P. and Just Defend

Two new systems give *Garou* its character.

The **T.O.P.** (Tactical Offence Position) is a zone on the player's own health bar. Before a round, each player chooses whether it sits at the start, the middle or the end of the bar. While their health is inside that zone, their attacks hit harder, their health slowly recovers, and they can use a special T.O.P. attack. Players who like to start strongly put it at the front; those who want a comeback put it at the end.

**Just Defend** rewards blocking at the last possible moment. *Retro Gamer* described it as rewarding "a late tap of away to guard": done correctly, it gives back a small amount of health and recovers from the block quickly enough to counter-attack. The magazine compared it with the parry in Capcom's *Street Fighter III* and called Just Defend SNK's "safer" answer, lacking the parry's balance of risk and reward.

Each fighter also has two levels of super move, a "break" that cuts one special move short so it can be chained into a combo, and feints that start a special move's animation and sound without the attack, to bait the opponent.

## Reception and legacy

*Garou* arrived late in the Neo Geo's life, and its reputation grew afterwards. *Retro Gamer* called it "easily [SNK's] finest hour" and praised "one of the most balanced character rosters around" and "an insane amount of depth to anyone prepared to scratch below its gorgeous looking surface". In a later issue it called the game "considered the ultimate SNK fighter by many".

The home versions were hard to come by in Britain. *Retro Gamer* noted in 2008 that the Dreamcast and PlayStation 2 versions were never released there, and that home Neo Geo cartridges sold for more than £300. The Dreamcast version came out in Japan in September 2001 and in North America that November, published by Agetec. Sega Retro notes that it is the only release to carry the *Fatal Fury* name, and that Capcom borrowed Just Defend for one of the fighting styles in *Capcom vs. SNK 2*. The game has since been re-released on many later consoles and PCs.

## The wait for a sequel

SNK planned a *Garou 2*. In the same 2018 interview the designer Naoto Abe said he had read its scenario and that work had started on new characters and moves, but "old SNK was dissolved during development, so unfortunately it's all been archived."

For more than 25 years *Mark of the Wolves* remained the newest *Fatal Fury*. In March 2024 SNK announced *Fatal Fury: City of the Wolves*, a sequel that continues Rock Howard's story, and it was released on 24 April 2025 for the PlayStation 4 and 5, Xbox Series X and S, and PC.
