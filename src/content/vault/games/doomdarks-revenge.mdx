## Overview

The sequel to [*Lords of Midnight*](/vault/games/lords-of-midnight), published by [Beyond](/vault/companies/beyond-software) in 1985, running the same **landscaping** engine over a larger and more hostile world.

## Fast facts

- **Author:** [Mike Singleton](/vault/people/mike-singleton). **Publisher:** Beyond.
- **Same technique:** a stored map and a library of parts, with each view assembled on demand.
- **Different temperament:** more characters, more independent agendas, far less guidance.

The same two-year shape as its predecessor and the same migration into the Adventure chart — but note the ceiling. *Doomdark's Revenge* peaks at **#3** against *Lords of Midnight*'s **#2**, and then sits several places lower for the rest of its run.

That gap is the sequel's whole story in one number. It is a bigger, deeper, more ambitious game than the original, and it sold slightly less well for slightly less long, because **what it added was difficulty**.

## Harder on purpose

The first game teaches you its world. The second assumes you already know it and withholds the scaffolding: more lords with their own loyalties and objectives, a map you can get comprehensively lost in, and a much greater chance of putting a dozen hours into a position that turns out to be unwinnable.

This is the standard shape of an 8-bit sequel and it is worth naming, because the pattern recurs across this Vault. The people who buy a sequel are, by definition, people who finished or at least mastered the original — a self-selected audience of experts. Designing for them produces a game that is better on its own terms and **narrower**, and the chart position is the market registering exactly that.

*Kick Off 3* is the same lesson learnt the opposite way round: [changing](/vault/people/steve-screech) what made the original distinctive instead of intensifying it.
