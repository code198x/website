In *Suikoden*, meeting someone can change more than the next conversation. A new ally might fight beside you, work in your headquarters or help the rebel army. The aim is to gather the 108 Stars of Destiny, drawing on the band of outlaws in the Chinese novel *Water Margin*. [Konami](/vault/companies/konami) released the original game for [PlayStation](/vault/systems/sony-playstation) in 1995.

You begin as the son of an imperial general. The story takes you from service to the Scarlet Moon Empire into resistance against it, making family loyalties part of the conflict. The castle that becomes your headquarters gives that rebellion a place: its inhabitants grow with the group you recruit.

## Three scales of battle

The 108 allies are not 108 interchangeable fighters. *Computer & Video Games*' January 1997 review describes the army's need for cooks and other support roles as well as combatants. For ordinary encounters, you select a party of up to six. Characters with suitable ranged weapons can fight from the back row, and particular combinations can perform joint attacks through the Unite command.

Equipment also keeps a connection to the character using it. Warriors retain their own weapons, which blacksmiths improve for a fee. Better upgrades cost more, and eventually you need a smith capable of taking the weapon further. Buying a replacement sword is not the routine described by the magazine's review.

Larger confrontations bring the army together under strategist Mathiu. These use choices between forces such as archers, magicians and infantry. Duels form a third kind of encounter, bringing the conflict down to two opponents with their own health bars. The changes in scale let the story move between an individual's risk and the army's campaign without making every fight work alike.

## An early PlayStation RPG

The July 1996 *Computer & Video Games* import preview showed a game built around sprite characters and a camera that changed its view for combat. Its January 1997 review praised the story and music, while complaining about buildings whose furniture yielded no useful discoveries. That reception places it in the early PlayStation period, before the machine's RPG catalogue was fully established.

A Saturn edition also reached Japan: *Sega Saturn Magazine* discussed it among Konami's Japanese releases in October 1998. Later versions and the continuing series gave the game a life beyond its original console.

## A continuing world

*Suikoden II* followed in 1998. Konami's series history places the first five numbered games in different wars within a shared chronology; their numbering is not the order of events in that world.

Series creator Yoshitaka Murayama later led Rabbit & Bear Studios and wrote *Eiyuden Chronicle: Hundred Heroes*. The studio announced his death in February 2024, after he had completed its scenario.

On 6 March 2025, Konami released *Suikoden I & II HD Remaster* for consoles and PC. Original character designer Junko Kawano redrew the first game's portraits. The collection also adds a dialogue log and faster battle options, giving returning players changes beyond the higher-resolution artwork.
