*Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines* is a role-playing game in which you play a newly made vampire in a night-time Los Angeles. It was the third and last game by [Troika Games](/vault/companies/troika-games), the studio founded by three of *[Fallout](/vault/games/fallout)*'s makers, and it came out in November 2004, published by [Activision](/vault/companies/activision). Its reviews read like arguments with themselves. *Computer Gaming World* called it "a massive, hypnotic RPG" with "so many rough edges you could use the CDs as saw blades"; *PC Games*' Dirk Gooding, in Germany, called it a game you could not leave alone "despite hair-raising bugs". Troika closed three months later, but players never stopped patching it, and twenty-one years on it got a sequel.

## Night in Los Angeles

The game is set in the World of Darkness, the setting of White Wolf's *Vampire: The Masquerade* tabletop role-playing game. Its central rule is the masquerade: vampires hide their existence from humans. *Computer Gaming World*'s Robert Coffey explained how that kept a player in check. Break the masquerade too many times and other vampires call a blood hunt, and the game ends. Commit too many inhuman acts and your character's humanity drains away until you risk frenzy, losing control in a way that breaks the masquerade again.

You start by choosing one of seven clans, which changes how the game treats you. Coffey contrasted the Nosferatu, "so hideous they must move about a city through the sewers" because being seen by a human would break the masquerade, with the Ventrue, who get their way by "irresistible force of will". *Australian PC PowerPlay* noted that characters reacted to your clan and your sex. The Malkavians were the strangest choice: *PC Games*' clan guide warned beginners that they heard voices, had bizarre conversations and talked back to the television, which made the plot harder to follow.

The story starts in Santa Monica and draws you into a struggle between the Camarilla, arriving to impose order, and the Anarchs who had run the city. Your choices of allies led to one of five endings. Reviewers kept returning to the writing. *Australian PC PowerPlay* quoted the vampire Jeanette introducing herself: "I'm the fingers down your back when the lights go out." Coffey singled out "a haunted hotel and a crack house full of shambling dead", and the hotel quest, set in a house on the coast called the Ocean House, was named GameSpy's level of the year.

## Built on Source, shipped too soon

Troika built *Bloodlines* on [Valve](/vault/companies/valve)'s Source engine, the technology of *[Half-Life 2](/vault/games/half-life-2)*, which made it look like a first-person shooter. Coffey warned that it was not one: much of it rewarded stealth, talking and skills such as lock-picking, hacking and seduction, and its third-person melee combat was "awkward at best". *Edge* thought Troika had "the winning formula to give the Vampire RPG a Deus Ex treatment", and that it "still feels like it should have worked"; Gooding drew the same comparison with *[Deus Ex](/vault/games/deus-ex)*. Later, *Edge* and *PC Games* both noted that the final stages turned into much more fighting, which was hard going for characters built for talking.

The technical problems were in every review. Coffey reported loading times of close to two minutes, frame rates that could fall to about one a second, enemies running on the spot and a character who fell through the floor "into a black computer netherworld". *Edge* listed "spelling errors to crash bugs". According to *The Escapist*, Troika had worked with a prototype of Source that lacked Valve's own AI code, and Activision advanced more money in 2003 to get the game finished. Jason Anderson, one of Troika's founders, said the studio "just needed more time to test and polish", but that each game was "pulled out of our hands before we were through with it". The game went on sale on 16 November 2004, the same day as *Half-Life 2*.

*Computer Gaming World* still named it RPG of the Year. Troika released a final patch, its last code, and closed in February 2005 without a publisher for its next game.

## Patched by its players

The fixes did not stop with Troika. Fans went on patching *Bloodlines* for years; the best-known unofficial patch was carried on by Werner Spahl. GOG.com sells the game with these third-party fixes included, and in 2022 it updated its version to unofficial patch 11.1. In 2020 *Wireframe* described *Bloodlines* as a game that "withers on the vine, but lives on through community support and intense fandom".

## Bloodlines 2

The sequel came about after Paradox Interactive bought the World of Darkness. Hardsuit Labs' creative director, Ka'ai Cluney, asked Brian Mitsoda, lead writer of the original *Bloodlines*, to write it, and the studio pitched the game to Paradox in February 2016. Announced in 2019 for a 2020 release, it was delayed repeatedly, and in early 2021 Paradox removed Hardsuit Labs from the project. The Chinese Room finished it. *Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines 2*, set in Seattle, was released on 21 October 2025.
