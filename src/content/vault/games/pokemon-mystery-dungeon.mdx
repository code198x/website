Most [Pokémon](/vault/games/pokemon) games make the player a trainer who catches the creatures. *Pokémon Mystery Dungeon* made the player one of them. "You are a regular Trainer who wakes up one morning to discover you've been turned into a Pokémon!" is how The Pokémon Company describes the opening of the first games, *Red Rescue Team* and *Blue Rescue Team*. A personality quiz at the start decides which Pokémon you have become. You can now understand what other Pokémon say, a partner joins you, and the two of you set out into dungeons whose layout changes every time you go in.

The dungeons came from [Chunsoft](/vault/companies/chunsoft), which had been making them since 1993.

## From Torneko to Pokémon

Chunsoft's Mystery Dungeon games began with *Torneko's Great Adventure* (1993) on the Super Famicom, which gave a merchant from *[Dragon Quest](/vault/games/dragon-quest)* a dungeon of his own, and continued with *Shiren the Wanderer* (1995). They followed the rules of the old computer game *Rogue*, the founder of the [roguelike](/vault/genres/roguelike): a turn-based crawl through floors laid out afresh on every visit. Chunsoft's series was, in *Retro Gamer*'s words, "massively popular, providing an easy introduction to the sub-genre", and in 1997 *Edge* reported that Square's *Chocobo's Mystery Dungeon* was "based on Chunsoft's popular Siren Mystery Dungeon".

"Trust Nintendo to take an age-old sub-genre and give it a cute new spin," *Retro Gamer* wrote of *Red Rescue Team*.

## Red and Blue

Like the main Pokémon games, the first *Mystery Dungeon* came as a pair, but this pair was split across two machines: *Red Rescue Team* for the [Game Boy Advance](/vault/systems/game-boy-advance) and *Blue Rescue Team* for the [Nintendo DS](/vault/systems/nintendo-ds). They came out in Japan on 17 November 2005 and in North America on 18 September 2006.

The player and partner form a rescue team, taking on missions to help other Pokémon in trouble and recruiting new members along the way. Each time the team enters a dungeon, the floors are laid out differently and the items lie in different places. Fainting in a dungeon sends the team back to its base without the items it was carrying.

## Later games

Chunsoft kept the series going through two console generations. *Explorers of Time* and *Explorers of Darkness* (2007) and *Explorers of Sky* (2009) stayed on the DS. *Adventure Squad* (2009) came out for the Wii's WiiWare download service. *Gates to Infinity* (2012) moved to the 3DS. *Retro Gamer* gave it 62% and judged it "clearly aimed at kids with its trite storyline and cute visuals", but found "beneath its cheery exterior" a "surprisingly challenging Roguelike affair that'll offer you hours of gameplay if you're prepared to put the time in". It also used the 3DS's augmented reality. *Pokémon Super Mystery Dungeon* followed in 2015.

By then the developer had a new name: in April 2012 Chunsoft merged with Spike to become Spike Chunsoft. On 6 March 2020 *Pokémon Mystery Dungeon: Rescue Team DX* for Nintendo Switch "reimagines the very first entries in the *Pokémon Mystery Dungeon* series", in The Pokémon Company's words. It keeps the personality quiz, and a player who faints in a dungeon can ask other players to come to the rescue. In August 2024 the original *Red Rescue Team* and *Blue Rescue Team* became available on Nintendo Switch Online with the Expansion Pack.
