*FIFA International Soccer* showed football from an isometric angle, with the pitch running diagonally across the screen. *[Edge](/vault/magazines/edge)* admitted in its 1993 review that the view made the game slower, but said it also allowed "a lot more player detail", overhead kicks and back-heels, and a crowd that reacted to play: hit the crossbar, the magazine said, and "the roar immediately and seamlessly turns into a moan". [EA Sports](/vault/companies/ea-sports) put the governing body's name on the box, and the game became the start of a yearly series that carried the name for almost 30 years.

## Fast facts

- **First game:** *FIFA International Soccer*, [Mega Drive](/vault/systems/sega-mega-drive), December 1993 in the UK.
- **Developer:** Extended Play for the first game; by 1995 EA's own Canadian studio, EA Canada.
- **Publisher:** [Electronic Arts](/vault/companies/electronic-arts), under the [EA Sports](/vault/companies/ea-sports) label.
- **Reception:** *Edge* 8/10 for the Mega Drive original.
- **Sales:** more than 325 million copies by 2022, by EA's count.
- **Ultimate Team:** added as a download for *FIFA 09*.
- **Last under the FIFA name:** *FIFA 23* (September 2022). The series continued as *EA Sports FC*, starting with *EA Sports FC 24* in September 2023.

## The first game

*Edge* reviewed the Mega Drive cartridge in December 1993, the month of its UK release, and credited it to the developer Extended Play. It set the game against *Kick Off* and *[Sensible Soccer](/vault/games/sensible-soccer)*. Where *Sensible Soccer* gave "an adrenaline rush through pure speed and head-to-head competitiveness", *FIFA* gave "a real feeling of being there by setting the scene". Players were urged to plug the Mega Drive into an amplifier to hear the crowd's chants. One player against the computer, *Edge* admitted, was not as much fun as *Sensible Soccer*, mostly because of the difference in speed, but with EA's new Four Way Play adaptor four people could play at once. It gave the game eight out of ten.

Not everyone was won over. When the PC version arrived in 1994, Gary Penn in *PC Gamer* praised "the choice and use of the isometric viewpoint" but complained that the players "look and feel as though they are running on ice". The Super Nintendo version that year advertised play for up to five people through Hudson Soft's Super MultiTap adaptor, a new pass-power meter and "30 all new chants".

The public bought it anyway. In the summer of 1994 EA's advertising called *FIFA International Soccer* "the UK's biggest and fastest selling videogame of 1993", with "over half a million FIFA fanatics" across Europe and 2,000 frames of player animation. Behind the scenes the team was already looking at new hardware: *Edge* reported in April 1994 that the Canadian programmers behind the Mega Drive game had spent two months building a mock 3DO version "just to see what they could do", and that EA gave them the go-ahead as soon as it saw the demo.

## A new edition every season

The series became yearly, each edition numbered by year. According to *Nintendo Magazine* in 1997, *FIFA 95* on the Mega Drive remained the best-selling version to that date, and outsold *[Donkey Kong Country](/vault/games/donkey-kong-country)* to become the Christmas number one.

*FIFA 96* was the first big rebuild. The *Official UK PlayStation Magazine* explained in 1995 that it came from EA Canada, which had developed "Virtual Stadium" technology: a 3D stadium with a roving camera that could view the match from any angle and cut away when a player's view was blocked. The players were 3D models animated by a system EA called Motion Design. Real footballers were filmed, and the movement was reworked by hand. The magazine called it a hybrid, not true motion capture, and the [motion capture](/vault/techniques/motion-capture) entry explains why EA held back from full capture. The PlayStation version had 12 international leagues and over 3,000 real player names. That was the pattern for what followed: each new edition added leagues, teams, kits and names. The same article reported that EA Sports had sold 20 million games in its first four years, and named *FIFA International Soccer* alongside *John Madden Football* and *PGA Tour Golf* as its most successful series.

The yearly editions kept selling. *Edge* reported that over Christmas 1996 *FIFA 97*, "despite its obvious failings", beat *[Command & Conquer: Red Alert](/vault/games/red-alert)* and *[Tomb Raider](/vault/games/tomb-raider)* to the top of the UK charts.

## FIFA and its rival

The strongest challenge came from Konami. On the Nintendo 64 some developers preferred its *International Superstar Soccer 64*. "I always thought the Konami one was far better," Karl Hilton of Free Radical Design told *Edge* in 1999, "but you read the sales figures and you find out that FIFA sold four times as many. Is it down to a licence?" Konami's series became *[Pro Evolution Soccer](/vault/games/pro-evolution-soccer)*. In January 2003 *Edge*'s columnist RedEye complained that "every year, every Christmas, Fifa sold so unbelievably well", and claimed that *Pro Evolution Soccer 2* had beaten it in its first week of sales on the PlayStation 2.

## Ultimate Team

The series' most profitable addition came later. *FIFA Ultimate Team*, *Edge* explained in 2010, began as "a downloadable add-on to the boxed versions of *FIFA 09* and *FIFA 10*, allowing players to build up fantasy squads of the world's best players". Players could earn new players by winning online, "but they can also purchase booster packs to quickly build their rosters".

## The end of the name

In May 2022 EA and FIFA announced that their partnership of about 30 years was over. *FIFA 23*, released at the end of September 2022, was the last game in the series to carry the name, and the agreement ended on 31 December 2022. At the launch of *FIFA 23*, EA put the series' sales at more than 325 million copies. The series kept its real leagues and player names but lost the FIFA brand and World Cup rights, and continued as *EA Sports FC*, starting with *EA Sports FC 24* in September 2023.
