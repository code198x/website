## Overview

Written by **Denton Designs** for [Beyond](/vault/companies/beyond-software): a real-time squad rescue in which you command five characters aboard an enemy ship — and give every order through **icons**, with no typing at all.

## Getting rid of the parser

In 1985 a game with depth meant a game you **typed at**, and typing at a game meant guessing which verb the author had implemented. "GET LAMP" works; "PICK UP LAMP" does not; the player is playing the parser, not the world.

*Shadowfire* removes the problem by removing the words. Every action is an icon, every target is selected, and **nothing can be phrased wrongly**. What you lose is expressive range; what you gain is that the interface stops being an obstacle and the game can run in real time, which a typed game cannot, because typing is slow.

That combination — real time, no text, a squad with individual skills, and a clock counting down — is why it stands out from everything around it, and why Beyond could sell a strategy game to the general chart instead of the adventure one.

## Denton Designs

Denton were ex-[Imagine](/vault/companies/imagine-software) people who set up in Liverpool after that company's collapse, and they appear in the *CRASH* reader ranking of Ocean and Imagine talent as a **programming house in their own right**, credited with *The Great Escape* and *Where Time Stood Still*.

An icon interface, a real-time squad and two of the era's best-regarded Spectrum games is a strong body of work for a studio formed out of a bankruptcy.
