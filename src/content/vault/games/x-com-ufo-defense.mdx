*X-COM: UFO Defense* — *UFO: Enemy Unknown* to anyone who bought it in Britain — tasked players with defending Earth from alien invasion on two levels: strategic base management and tactical squad combat. Permadeath made every soldier precious. Procedural missions ensured variety. The combination created emergent stories of heroism and loss that players still recount decades later.

## Fast facts

- **Developer:** Mythos Games.
- **Publisher:** [MicroProse](/vault/companies/microprose).
- **Designer:** [Julian Gollop](/vault/people/julian-gollop).
- **Titles:** *UFO: Enemy Unknown* (UK/Europe), *X-COM: UFO Defense* (North America), *X-COM: Enemy Unknown* (PlayStation, 1995).
- **Spiritual predecessor:** [Laser Squad](/vault/games/laser-squad).

## Three titles, one game

The 1994 original shipped under three names, and which one someone uses tends
to say where and when they played it.

| Title | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ***UFO: Enemy Unknown*** | UK and Europe | The original MicroProse release, and overwhelmingly the most common name in the British press. |
| ***X-COM: UFO Defense*** | North America | The American retitling, and the name the series kept afterwards. |
| ***X-COM: Enemy Unknown*** | PlayStation, 1995 | The console release, merging the American prefix with the European subtitle. |

The third is the one usually forgotten, and it is neither a typo nor a fan
contraction — it is what MicroProse put on the PlayStation box. *Official UK
PlayStation Magazine* reviewed it under that title in its first issue, November
1995, listing publisher and developer as MicroProse and origin as UK.

⚠ It is also the title most often confused with Firaxis's 2012 *XCOM: Enemy
Unknown*, from which it differs by a single hyphen.

## The PlayStation version

*OPM*'s review opens by wondering whether a PC strategy review had been dropped
into the magazine by mistake, then argues the opposite case: that the game showed
"the true potential of the machine to break away from the traditional console
fare".

It reports the conversion as close to the PC original — "little changed, except
for some improved sound and the odd animated sequence" — concedes that the
visuals and audio are not the machine's best, and recommends using a mouse. Its
component verdicts run from 7 to 9: presentation "flat yet functional", gameplay
"absorbing rather than manic", lifespan "it'll outlive you".

The same issue reported plans to bring *X-COM: Terror From The Deep* to the
PlayStation during 1996.

## X-COM and XCOM: which is which

The franchise is easier to navigate once you notice that the punctuation does
the work. The **hyphenated** games are the originals, built on Gollop's design
and published by MicroProse in the 1990s. The **unhyphenated** ones are
Firaxis's reboot series, begun in 2012.

| Title | Year | Developer | Setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| *UFO: Enemy Unknown* / *X-COM: UFO Defense* | 1994 | Mythos Games | Global |
| *X-COM: Enemy Unknown* (PlayStation) | 1995 | MicroProse | Console conversion |
| *X-COM: Terror from the Deep* | 1995 | MicroProse | Underwater |
| *X-COM: Apocalypse* | 1997 | Mythos Games | A single city |
| *X-COM: Interceptor* | 1998 | MicroProse | Space combat |
| [*XCOM: Enemy Unknown*](/vault/games/xcom-enemy-unknown) | 2012 | Firaxis | Reboot |
| *XCOM 2* | 2016 | Firaxis | Resistance |

This entry covers the 1994 original and its conversions. The reboot era has
[its own entry](/vault/games/xcom-enemy-unknown), because it is a different
design by a different studio that happens to share a premise — and because
"XCOM-like" now describes a genre rather than a series.
