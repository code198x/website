In a *Shin Megami Tensei* game the monsters are demons, angels and gods taken from the world's religions and myths, and the player can stop a fight to talk to them. Say the right things, or hand over enough money, and a demon joins the party; put two together and they can be fused into a stronger one. Reviewing *Nocturne* in 2004, *Edge* summed the result up in a caption as "Pokemon with Tarot cards". The other thing that set the series apart was its moral choices. *Edge* noted that *Nocturne* had "no 'good' or 'evil', just different ideologies", and that the series let the player "throw your lot in with any of the game's disparate dogmas or strike out on your own moral path".

## Megami Tensei

The series grew out of *Digital Devil Story*, a set of novels by Aya Nishitani. In 1987 [Atlus](/vault/companies/atlus) developed *Digital Devil Story: Megami Tensei* for the Famicom, and [Namco](/vault/companies/namco) published it. Recruiting demons and fusing them were already part of the first game. *Megami Tensei II* followed in 1990, again under Namco's name, on a cartridge with Namco's 163 sound chip. In a 2006 readers' poll in Japan, printed in *Edge*, it still came 58th in a list of the hundred best games.

## Shin Megami Tensei (1992)

*Shin Megami Tensei* was published by Atlus itself, for the Super Famicom, in October 1992. It starts in Tokyo in "199X", where, in Gematsu's summary, "a mysterious murder leads to the emergence of demons and an escalating global conflict". The city is destroyed in a nuclear attack, and the game picks up again thirty years later in its ruins. The forces fighting over what is left are not good and evil but Law and Chaos, and the player's choices decide which side, if either, the hero ends up on. The demons were designed by Kazuma Kaneko, the series' long-serving artist. In the same 2006 poll *Shin Megami Tensei* came 59th, one place below *Megami Tensei II*.

It was converted to the PC Engine and the Mega-CD, remade for the PlayStation in 2001 and released on the Game Boy Advance in 2003. None of those versions left Japan. The first official English version arrived only in March 2014, for Apple's iOS devices, 22 years after the original.

## Too Japanese to export

The series' subjects made it a hard sell abroad. *Retro Gamer* pointed out that Japanese role-playing games such as *Shin Megami Tensei* were "rife with risqué humour, irreverent use of religious symbols, and blunt depictions of murder", and that Nintendo of America's content rules meant Japanese games of the time were often cut or changed. When *Nocturne* was finally confirmed for America in 2004, *Edge* listed what it expected to put people off: "devils, deaths and gods from every possible religion, a tyrannical creator and a slightly sympathetic portrayal of Lucifer Morningstar". The first *Megami Tensei* game sold in America was a spin-off, *[Revelations: Persona](/vault/games/persona)*, in 1996.

## Nocturne and after

*Shin Megami Tensei III: Nocturne* came out in Japan in 2003 on the PlayStation 2, and in America in 2004. As *Edge*'s review put it, "Twenty minutes into Nocturne, the world ends." The hero is reborn alone in a ruined Tokyo of desert and broken buildings, where nearly everything he meets is a possible companion: once "recruited, seduced or bribed" into the party, demons could be "crossbred with others" into new ones. Battles turned on hitting enemies' weaknesses, which earned extra actions while misses cost them; *Digital Devil Saga* carried the scheme over as the Press Turn system. The bosses could be brutal: *Edge* warned that many could "destroy an unprepared party in a single disastrous turn". The American release was based on the expanded *Maniacs* edition, with a guest appearance from Dante of Capcom's *Devil May Cry*. *Edge* gave it 7 out of 10.

Atlus kept adding branches as well as numbered games, among them *Soul Hackers* and *Digital Devil Saga*. In *Digital Devil Saga* the young soldiers of the party turn into demons themselves; *Electronic Gaming Monthly*'s reviewers liked its "speedy, simplified battles", though one missed "chatting with and fusing demons myself". *[Persona](/vault/games/persona)* became the best known of all.

The main series went on with *Shin Megami Tensei IV* on the 3DS (2013), whose English release *Retro Gamer* reported among Nintendo's announcements that year, and *Shin Megami Tensei V* on the Nintendo Switch in 2021. An expanded version, *Shin Megami Tensei V: Vengeance*, followed in June 2024 on the Switch, PlayStation, Xbox and PC, with a new storyline alongside the original. By then Atlus belonged to [Sega](/vault/companies/sega).
