Captain Commando was a mascot before he was a hero. In the late 1980s [Capcom](/vault/companies/capcom)'s American office used his name as a brand: its adverts listed "Captain Commando" as a trademark of Capcom U.S.A., and *ANALOG Computing* described the NES *[1942](/vault/games/1942)* in 1988 as the latest entry in Capcom's "'Captain Commando' action video game line". In 1991 Capcom finally gave him a body and a game, produced by [Yoshiki Okamoto](/vault/people/yoshiki-okamoto). They put him in a future Metro City, the city of *[Final Fight](/vault/games/final-fight)*, and surrounded him with an odd team: a knife-wielding alien mummy, a ninja and a baby in a robot.

## Fast facts

- **Developer and publisher:** [Capcom](/vault/companies/capcom), arcade, 1991, on the CP System board.
- **Players:** up to four at once, depending on the cabinet's settings.
- **Heroes:** Captain Commando, Mack the Knife, Ginzu the Ninja and Baby Head.
- **Setting:** Metro City in the year 2026.
- **Home versions:** Super Famicom and Super NES (1995); PlayStation (Japan, 1998).

## The Commando team

The four heroes play very differently. Captain Commando, the leader, fights with energy gloves. Mack the Knife is a mummy-like alien with a pair of knives; Ginzu is a ninja with a sword and the only one who throws shuriken. Baby Head is an infant genius who rides a robot. *Zero*'s arcade reviewer, early in 1992, thought that "any game starring characters like" the Ninja, Mack the Knife and Baby Head was "definitely worth a look". Baby Head, it noted, was not very fast, "but those robotic arms have a lethal clout".

The game runs on the same lines as *Final Fight*: walk to the right, beat up everyone, fight a boss. Its additions were the four-player cabinet and the robots, mechs left lying about the stages that any hero could climb into; they punched, threw flames or froze enemies. The stages start in Metro City and take in a museum, a circus, a ninja house and an aquarium, with a surfboard ride along the way, and the game ends in a fight with the villain Scumocide. Capcom ran it on the CP System, the board behind *Final Fight* and *[Street Fighter II](/vault/games/street-fighter-ii)*, with 4 MiB of game data.

## Reception

British reviewers were polite. *The One* saw it at a trade show in late 1991 and reported that "Capcom's own super hero Captain Commando, although up to the usual competent standard, did fail to inspire any great enthusiasm". *Zero* summed it up as "simple but not desperately exciting". At least one player was keener: in 1992 a *Mean Machines* reader wrote in to say he had tried it in an arcade, "and it was brilliant", and asked when it would reach the Mega Drive.

It never did. Capcom took it to Nintendo's console instead. The Super Famicom version was on show in Japan at the end of 1994 and came out in 1995, reaching the Super NES later the same year. It cut the game to two players, reduced the number of enemies on screen and took out the robots. A closer conversion for the PlayStation came out only in Japan, in 1998.

## After the game

The game had no sequel, but the Captain kept fighting. *Marvel vs. Capcom* (1998) brought him back as one of Capcom's fighters, alongside Mega Man and Strider, and *Edge* found that these resurrected heroes "work brilliantly as modern 2D fighters". In his special attack the Ninja and Baby Commando charge in to help him. He also appeared in the Japanese crossover game *Namco x Capcom* (2005), made with [Namco](/vault/companies/namco). The arcade game itself was reissued in Capcom's compilations, including *Capcom Classics Collection: Remixed* and *Capcom Classics Collection Volume 2* (both 2006), *Capcom Beat 'Em Up Bundle* (2018) and *Capcom Arcade Stadium* (2021).
