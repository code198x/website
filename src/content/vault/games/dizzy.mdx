Released in 1987 for the ZX Spectrum and Amstrad CPC, *Dizzy: The Ultimate Cartoon Adventure* introduced players to a cheery egg navigating perilous screens, collecting items, and solving puzzles. Its success spawned sequels, spin-offs, and boxed compilations that kept budget racks stocked for years.

## Fast facts
- **Developers:** Philip and Andrew Oliver, published by Codemasters.  
- **Format:** single-screen platformer with inventory-based puzzles and precise jumps.  
- **Releases of this title, in full:** ZX Spectrum, reviewed by *CRASH* in **November 1987**. The Commodore 64 version followed a year later — *ZZAP!64* reviewed it in **September 1988**. An Amstrad CPC version is usually listed alongside the Spectrum original, unconfirmed here from a contemporary review. A late **MSX2** release appears in 1992.
- **Not this title:** the Amiga, [Atari ST](/vault/systems/atari-st) and [NES](/vault/systems/nes) games people remember are **later Dizzy games**, not ports of the first one.

## What the press made of it

*CRASH*'s November 1987 reviewer was won over against his own taste: *"Dizzy may be a little too cute and cuddly for my liking, but there's a great game hidden within those small and furry folds… it's fab."* The same review lands a period observation about Codemasters' house style — the inlay *"goes a little overboard — the word 'brilliant' appears four times."*

## Cultural footprint
Dizzy’s approachable art and budget price made it a household name. The franchise proved that playful characters and clever puzzles could compete with action-heavy arcade conversions.
