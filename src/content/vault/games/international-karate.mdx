## Overview

***International Karate*** was [System 3](/vault/companies/system-3)'s answer to
[*Way of the Exploding Fist*](/vault/games/way-of-the-exploding-fist), released in
1985 and advertised with the confidence of a company that knew what it was up
against: "**you thought you'd seen a Karate game**... as you know, like the martial
arts, perfection is the only accepted standard, so play nothing until you play
System 3's *International Karate*."

Its sequel, [*International Karate+*](/vault/games/international-karate-plus),
is the one usually remembered. The original is the more interesting document.

## The critics could not agree

*International Karate* produced one of the sharper splits of the period.

**CRASH: 68%.** **Sinclair User: five out of five.**

That is not a small gap, and it is about a game now remembered as a classic of the
British 8-bit catalogue. Two magazines with the same readership, reviewing the
same cassette within weeks of each other, reached opposite conclusions — one
placing it below average, the other at the top of its scale.

It is worth holding on to whenever a period score is quoted as though it settled
something. The reviewer's verdict was one opinion in a market that had not yet
agreed what a fighting game was supposed to do, and *Way of the Exploding Fist*
had only established the genre a few months earlier.

## Recalled from America

The game's American history is a mess that had nothing to do with System 3.

In the United States it was published by **Epyx** as *World Karate Championship*.
**Data East** then sued Epyx — not over System 3's game as such, but over that
version's resemblance to Data East's arcade game *Karate Champ*.

On **10 June 1987**, US District Court Judge **William Ingram** ruled that Epyx
must cease reproduction and marketing, and

> recall all copies of both *World Karate Championship* and ***International
> Karate*** from all distributors, jobbers, representatives, and retailers.

The judge held the two games "substantially similar" and "qualitatively identical
in every respect".

So a British game was pulled from the American market by a judgment in a dispute
between a Japanese-owned publisher and an American one. System 3 appears in the
order only as the name on the other box.

⚠ This library holds the report of the June 1987 ruling and nothing on what
became of it afterwards. Copyright judgments of this kind were not always final;
treat the injunction as the documented event rather than the end of the story.
