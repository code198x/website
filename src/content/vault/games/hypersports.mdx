## The waggle

*Hypersports* belongs to the genre [*Daley Thompson's Decathlon*](/vault/people/christian-urquhart) had established a year earlier: **several events, each won by moving the joystick left and right as fast as physically possible.**

It is worth being precise about why this worked, because it looks crude and was not.

**It needs no explanation.** A child can be handed the controls and be competitive in four seconds. There is no learning curve at all.

**It is a physical contest, not a skill contest.** Two players at one machine are not comparing reflexes or knowledge — they are comparing how hard they are prepared to punish a £9.99 joystick, in front of each other.

**It made the hardware part of the game.** Joysticks broke. Microswitched sticks were sold on their ability to survive this genre. A game that consumes peripherals is a game with a merchandising tail nobody planned.

The waggler is the purest example in this Vault of a design that ignores the computer's strengths entirely and uses the **room** instead.
