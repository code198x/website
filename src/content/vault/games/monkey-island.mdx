Most adventure games of 1990 could kill you. In *The Secret of Monkey Island* the hero can get past a troll by giving it what it wants, buy a ship from a salesman who will not stop talking, and fight duels with insults instead of blades, and he almost never dies. [Ron Gilbert](/vault/people/ron-gilbert) made it at Lucasfilm Games with two young writers, [Tim Schafer](/vault/people/tim-schafer) and Dave Grossman, The idea, Gilbert said later, "started from being frustrated that Sierra sold way more games than we did": fantasy was selling, but he "didn't want to make a game about dragons", and pirates "seemed like a nice compromise". The result is the best-remembered of the company's [SCUMM](/vault/techniques/scumm) adventures, and the start of a series that has outlived the company that made it.

## Fast facts

- **Developer:** Lucasfilm Games, later [LucasArts](/vault/companies/lucasarts). US Gold published it in Britain.
- **Design:** Ron Gilbert, with Tim Schafer and Dave Grossman.
- **Released:** 1990 for the PC, in 16-colour EGA and 256-colour VGA versions; Amiga and Atari ST versions were reviewed in 1991, and a version for Sega's CD add-on followed by 1993.
- **Engine:** SCUMM, the scripting system Gilbert first built for *[Maniac Mansion](/vault/games/maniac-mansion)*.
- **Copy protection:** the Dial-A-Pirate [code wheel](/vault/techniques/code-wheels).

## How to become a pirate

Guybrush Threepwood arrives on Mêlée Island wanting to be a pirate. In the SCUMM Bar (a joke on the name of the engine, as *Computer Gaming World*'s Scorpia pointed out) the pirate leaders set him three trials: steal the Idol of Many Hands from the Governor, find the Legendary Lost Treasure of Mêlée Island, and defeat the Sword Master in single combat. The island is in a bad way, because the pirate LeChuck died and came back as a ghost, with a ghost ship and a ghost crew. When LeChuck kidnaps the Governor, Elaine Marley, and takes her to his hideout on Monkey Island, Guybrush has to buy a ship from Stan's Previously-Owned Vessels, find a crew and sail after him.

The humour was mostly in the dialogue, and much of it was deliberately out of period. "Being funny was more important than being authentic or piratey," Grossman told *Wireframe* in 2020; Stan was modelled partly on the Californian used-car salesman and TV personality Cal Worthington. The name Guybrush came, according to the team's own legend, from a joke by the artist Steve Purcell. The team was still planning a male and a female hero, and because [Deluxe Paint](/vault/tools/deluxe-paint) called an animated sprite a brush, Purcell suggested Guybrush and Girlbrush. The heroine was dropped; the name stayed. Threepwood was said to come from a Dungeons & Dragons character played by Grossman's brother.

## Insult sword fighting

The best-known puzzle turns a sword fight into a conversation. *Amiga Format* explained it in 1991: "Fights are won with words, not with wounds. The combatants fire insults and retorts at each other until one of them is stuck for a witty reply." The player learns insults and comebacks by losing fights to the pirates who wander Mêlée Island, then uses them against the Sword Master, who turns out to be a woman. The idea has two origin stories. *Wireframe* reported that it came from watching old Errol Flynn films at Skywalker Ranch; the designer Noah Falstein told *Retro Gamer* that he "contributed the core idea for Insult Swordfighting".

## Fair to the player

At Lucasfilm Games the approach of not punishing players developed over several games. Gilbert admitted that *Maniac Mansion* had "a lot more places where you die and the game can end in a no-win state" than *Monkey Island*: "Those were lessons learned." *Retro Gamer* later described one of his main design goals for *Monkey Island* as stopping players "getting stuck through lazy game design". In 1991 he argued the case at the Computer Game Developers' Conference against Lori Cole of [Sierra](/vault/companies/sierra), under the title "Death is Bad Drama"; Cole replied that without the risk of death "the game becomes mere amusement". Guybrush can die in one place: underwater, if the player leaves him there for more than ten minutes. (See [adventure game deaths](/vault/culture/adventure-game-deaths).)

The interface worked the same way. Scorpia liked that there was "no silliness about 'you're not close enough' to pick up an item": the player clicks on it and Guy walks over. She found the game "fairly easy", about "a step and a half up from *Loom*", and praised the music, "real tunes, not just sound effects". The game also detected the player's graphics and sound cards by itself.

## Making it

The team worked in Gary Winnick's office in a barn at Skywalker Ranch. Winnick was art director; the artists included Steve Purcell, Martin "Bucky" Cameron and [Mark Ferrari](/vault/people/mark-ferrari), who painted the town of Mêlée in EGA "during twilight and at night, because the strongest part of the EGA palette is its blues". The buildings narrow at the bottom, Ferrari explained, to fake a camera perspective without needing new animations as the screen scrolled. Michael Land wrote the music. Schafer later recalled that the whole game "took nine months to make".

## Reception

In Britain *Amiga Format* gave the Amiga version 92% in June 1991, calling it "Lucasfilm's best animated adventure to date, by miles", though it complained about disk swapping for owners without a hard drive and jerky scrolling. *AtariUser* enjoyed the joke copy protection: the player had to find a pirate on the Dial-A-Pirate wheel and enter the year he was hanged, "to be done with your tongue firmly planted in your cheek". According to Gilbert it sold only modestly, but well enough for Lucasfilm to approve a sequel.

## The series

After a two-week break the team started on the sequel. *Monkey Island 2: LeChuck's Revenge* (1991) introduced iMUSE, the music system by Michael Land and Peter McConnell that changes the music as the player moves between scenes, and ended with a scene set in a modern amusement park that fans still argue about. Gilbert meant to explain it in a third game, but, he said, "I decided to leave towards the end of Monkey Island 2, so it never got made."

Other teams carried the series on without him. Jonathan Ackley and Larry Ahern designed *The Curse of Monkey Island* (1997), the first with voices and the last LucasArts game on SCUMM. Mike Stemmle and Sean Clark made *Escape from Monkey Island* (2000), the first in 3D. The series then moved to [Telltale](/vault/companies/telltale), a company founded by former LucasArts staff: *Tales of Monkey Island* came out in five episodes from July to December 2009, directed by Grossman.

In the same month LucasArts released *The Secret of Monkey Island: Special Edition*, with new hand-drawn art, a remastered score and voices, and the original version a keypress away. *Edge* noted that "the original assets are included for stubborn acolytes", and *Retro Gamer* that Gilbert's old SCUMM scripts were still powering the special editions "from Xbox 360 to iPhone". After Disney bought Lucasfilm in 2012, Gilbert and Grossman returned: their *Return to Monkey Island*, developed by Terrible Toybox and published by [Devolver Digital](/vault/companies/devolver-digital), came out in September 2022. It begins on Mêlée Island, because, Gilbert said, "the story is kind of about Guybrush going back to unfinished business".
