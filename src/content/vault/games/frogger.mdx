*Frogger* asks one thing of the player: get across. A frog starts at the bottom of the screen, below five lanes of traffic moving at different speeds. Above the road is a river, and the only way over it is to ride logs and the backs of turtles, some of which dive. At the top are five homes, and when all five hold a frog the screen starts again, faster. It is played with a single four-way joystick and no buttons. In 1982 *Electronic Games* pointed out that *Pac-Man*, the most popular coin-op in America, had the simplest controls of any current hit, and that "this is also true of Frogger".

[Konami](/vault/companies/konami) made it, but almost nobody knew. [Sega](/vault/companies/sega) distributed it, and in America it carried the Sega/Gremlin name, so, as *Retro Gamer* later put it, anyone asked who made *Frogger* "would reply 'Sega'". It became, in the same magazine's words, "the must-have arcade title of 1981".

## Fast facts

- **Developer:** [Konami](/vault/companies/konami), 1981.
- **Distributor:** [Sega](/vault/companies/sega); Sega/Gremlin in North America.
- **Hardware:** a board descended from Namco's *[Galaxian](/vault/games/galaxian)*. A Z80 runs the game, and a second Z80 drives an [AY-3-8910](/vault/hardware/ay-3-8910) sound chip.
- **Controls:** one four-way joystick.
- **Home versions:** Parker Brothers held the cartridge rights; Sierra On-Line published disk versions for the Apple II, Atari 8-bit computers and Commodore 64. *Retro Gamer* counted over 20 official versions.

## Crossing the screen

The road comes first. Each lane moves at its own speed and in its own direction, so gaps open and close at different rates and a safe spot does not stay safe for long. Halfway up is a strip of pavement where the frog can rest. The river is the harder half: the frog drowns if it misses, so it has to ride a log or a row of turtles across and step off at the right moment. *Electronic Games* warned that the second row of turtles "dives at random intervals", and that the careful player watches where they like to go under. Crocodiles swim among the logs.

Bonus points come from catching a fly that appears in one of the homes, and from rescuing a lady frog. A timer runs down on every crossing. *Retro Gamer* noticed one side effect: with the time limit, players rarely heard more than half of the background tune before the frog ran out of time.

## Who sold the home versions

Parker Brothers bought the home rights from Sega almost by accident. It had just lost *Donkey Kong* to Coleco, its president Richard Stearns told *Video Games* in 1983, and at the time "Frogger was nothing. It was kind of a mediocre arcade game." Sega said the game was going elsewhere, so Parker Brothers raised its offer: "we called up David Rosen and almost heard his chin hit the table." It showed its *Frogger* cartridge at the Toy Fair the following February, and its Atari 2600 version carried Sega's name on the box. In February 1983 it was seventh in *Electronic Games*' readers' poll of the most popular cartridges.

The rights were split. Parker Brothers had the cartridges, and [Sierra On-Line](/vault/companies/sierra) published versions for the Apple II, the Atari 8-bit computers and the Commodore 64 under its Sierravision label. *Electronic Games* found Sierra's Apple II version "a faithful translation" with all the play, sounds and graphics of the original. In 1983 both companies were advertising their own *Frogger* for the Commodore 64 (see [Konami](/vault/companies/konami)). Coleco made a battery-powered tabletop *Frogger* for $70. *Retro Gamer* thought the official 2600 cartridge "crude-looking", and the official version for the Supercharger, a cassette add-on for the 2600, "hugely superior". It also counted the Cornsoft version for the [ZX81](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx81) as possibly the first licensed arcade conversion for a home computer. It split the playfield over two screens and drew a crocodile half the width of the screen.

## Frogs on the Spectrum

The [ZX Spectrum](/vault/systems/sinclair-zx-spectrum) never had a licensed version, and that did not stop anyone. The first issue of *CRASH*, in February 1984, reviewed a whole shelf of them: Rabbit's *Frogger*, Ocean's *Road Frog*, Anirog's *Frogrun*, DK'Tronics' *Road Toad*, C-Tech's *Frogger/Specman*, CDS's *Leapfrog* and DJL's *Froggy*, which it recommended for its turtles that sink under the frog's weight, its crocodiles in the homes and a "cruel" time limit. PSS sold *Hopper*, which added a railway line between the road and the river and gave the player ten frogs. A look back at the year in the same issue gave the "oddest frogger of the year award" to Virgin's *Yomp*, which swapped the frogs for paratroopers and laid a minefield on the far side of the road.

## Later history

Sega's hold on the name did not last. Konami took back the rights in the 1990s and licensed them to Hasbro Interactive, whose 1997 *Frogger* for the PlayStation and PC turned the single screen into a series of new levels. A straight conversion of the arcade game, published by Majesco, was among the last games released for the [Mega Drive](/vault/systems/sega-mega-drive) in North America in 1998. Konami has gone on publishing *Frogger* games under its own name, among them *Frogger: Ancient Shadow* and *Frogger Helmet Chaos* in 2005.

The machine also reached television. In "The Frogger", a 1998 episode of *Seinfeld*, George Costanza finds the cabinet in the pizza parlour he went to as a teenager, still showing his high score of over 860,000, and tries to carry it across a busy street without unplugging it.
