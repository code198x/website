**Bug-Byte Software** was a pioneering British video game publisher that operated from 1980 to 1985, playing a crucial role in the early development of the UK home computer gaming industry. Based in Liverpool, the company published some of the most memorable and influential games of the 8-bit era.

## Company Origins

Bug-Byte was founded in 1980 by Tony Baden and Tony Milner during the early days of the home computer revolution in the United Kingdom. The company emerged at a time when the ZX Spectrum and other 8-bit computers were creating new opportunities for independent game developers.

### Early Focus
- **Home Computer Market**: Targeting the growing ZX Spectrum user base
- **Independent Developers**: Supporting bedroom programmers and small studios
- **British Gaming Culture**: Helping establish distinctive UK gaming traditions
- **Affordable Entertainment**: Games priced for home computer budgets

## Notable Releases

Bug-Byte published several games that became classics of the 8-bit era:

### Manic Miner (1983)
- **Developer**: Matthew Smith
- **Platform**: ZX Spectrum
- **Significance**: One of the first successful platform games on home computers
- **Innovation**: Precise controls and challenging level design
- **Cultural Impact**: Established template for British platform games

### 3D Monster Maze (1981)
- **Developer**: Malcolm Evans
- **Platform**: ZX81
- **Significance**: One of the first 3D games for home computers
- **Technical Achievement**: Real-time 3D rendering on extremely limited hardware
- **Innovation**: First-person perspective horror gameplay

### Spectres (1982)
- **Platform**: ZX Spectrum
- **Genre**: Arcade-style action
- **Notable Features**: Impressive graphics for early Spectrum games
- **Technical Innovation**: Pushing hardware capabilities
