At the WESCON trade show in September 1975, MOS Technology stunned the industry by introducing the 6502 processor for just $25, when competing processors cost $179 or more.

## Industry Revolution

- **Price Disruption**: Forced Intel and Motorola to slash prices immediately
- **Market Access**: Made microprocessors affordable for hobbyists and small companies
- **Design Excellence**: Clean, efficient architecture despite low cost
- **Volume Production**: Demonstrated viability of high-volume, low-margin model

## Lasting Impact

The 6502 went on to power:
- Apple II (1977)
- Commodore PET (1977)
- Atari 8-bit computers (1979)
- BBC Micro (1981)
- Commodore 64 (1982)
- Nintendo Entertainment System (1983)

Chuck Peddle's philosophy of "computers for the masses" through aggressive pricing established the model for the entire personal computer industry.
