On 9th November 1989, the Berlin Wall fell after 28 years of dividing East and West Berlin. This momentous event marked the beginning of the end of the Cold War and had profound implications for the technology industry.

## Impact on Computing

The fall of the Berlin Wall and subsequent opening of Eastern Europe created new opportunities for the computer industry:

- **New Markets**: Western computer companies gained access to millions of potential customers
- **Talent Pool**: Eastern European programmers, many trained in mathematics and engineering, entered the global software market
- **Cultural Exchange**: Gaming and software development traditions from both sides of the Iron Curtain began to merge
- **Demoscene Growth**: The Eastern European demoscene, particularly strong in countries like Poland and Hungary, connected with Western communities

## Technology Context

By 1989, the personal computer revolution was in full swing:
- The Commodore 64 was still popular, especially in Europe
- The Amiga and Atari ST were competing for the 16-bit market
- Nintendo had revived the gaming industry with the NES
- The fall of the wall meant these technologies could reach new audiences

The event symbolised not just political freedom, but also the democratisation of technology and information that personal computers represented.
